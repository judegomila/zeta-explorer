# Full source-Cauchy anchor forcing: exact feasibility and root barrier

## 0. Exact outcome and claim boundary

Let `Q` be a finite real inversion-symmetric source-Cauchy matrix, let
`A=Q_+` be its normalized even block, and let `kappa` be a nonzero real even
anchor.  This note allows an arbitrary real inversion-symmetric
source-Cauchy perturbation `H` and asks whether

\[
 (Q-H)_+\kappa=\mu\kappa                                      \tag{0.1}
\]

can hold with `kappa` a simple lowest even eigenvector.

The linear eigenvector equation is always solvable: cancel `Q` down to a
scalar matrix.  The simple-lowest requirement is different.  It is
feasible exactly when the homogeneous raw anchor polynomial has `N`
**distinct real projective roots**.  Equivalently, the full source-Cauchy
freedom replaces the signed-Laplacian gate by the strict real-root target
for the anchor itself; it does not bypass that target.

More precisely, the positive source-Cauchy face annihilating `kappa` is the
cone generated by the Cauchy selector rays at the distinct real projective
roots of the anchor polynomial.  If there are `r` such roots, the largest
possible rank in that face is exactly `r`.  Thus every lowest-eigenvalue
realization has multiplicity at least `N+1-r`, and a simple ground exists
if and only if `r=N`.

This is a finite theorem and an exact semidefinite feasibility
classification.  It proves no strict real-root theorem for the actual
natural-cutoff prolate anchor on an unbounded schedule, no locally uniform
actual-ground-transform theorem, no equation (5.2) of
`DIRECT_RH_ATTACK.md`, no Riemann-hypothesis conclusion, and no Lambda
bound.  The formal project state remains `P0_INVALIDATED`.

The binding conventions remain those of `P0_SOURCE_LOCK.json` and
`P0_NORMALIZATION_AUDIT.md`: `L=2*log(lambda)`, centered `exp(-izt)`,
Lebesgue measure, zero extension, inversion symmetry, source
normalization, the inclusive inventory `1<n<=lambda^2`, and the minus sign
of the prime block.  The perturbations below are finite algebraic
auxiliaries, not new pinned arithmetic sources.

## 1. The complete linear eigenline family

Use the normalized even basis

\[
 e_0^+=V_0,
 \qquad e_n^+={V_n+V_{-n}\over\sqrt2}\quad(1\le n\le N),
                                                               \tag{1.1}
\]

and let `S_N` be the real vector space of even source-Cauchy blocks.  Write
an arbitrary `K in S_N` with independent diagonal variables `d_i` and
off-diagonal displacement variables `gamma_n` as

\[
 K_{00}=d_0,
 \qquad K_{0n}={\sqrt2\,\gamma_n\over n},
 \qquad K_{nn}=d_n,                                     \tag{1.2}
\]

\[
 K_{nm}=2{n\gamma_n-m\gamma_m\over n^2-m^2}
 \quad(n\ne m,\ n,m\ge1).                              \tag{1.3}
\]

These are exactly the source-Cauchy blocks: in raw parameters,

\[
 b_n^K=\gamma_n,
 \qquad a_0^K=d_0,
 \qquad a_n^K=d_n-{\gamma_n\over n}.                    \tag{1.4}
\]

The even block determines its full inversion-symmetric source-Cauchy
extension uniquely.

### Theorem 1.1 (all exact eigenline perturbations)

For a real scalar `mu`, equation (0.1) holds if and only if

\[
 \boxed{H^+=A-\mu I-K,
 \qquad K\in S_N,
 \qquad K\kappa=0.}                                   \tag{1.5}
\]

Every block in (1.5) has a unique full source-Cauchy extension, and every
full solution is obtained this way.

**Proof.**  Put `B=Q-H` and `K=B_+-mu*I`.  Since the source-Cauchy blocks
form a real vector space containing the identity, `K in S_N`.  Equation
(0.1) is exactly `K*kappa=0`.  Conversely, any such `K` gives
`B_+=mu*I+K` and hence (0.1).  Uniqueness of the raw extension follows from
the first row and diagonal reconstruction in (1.2)--(1.4).  \(\square\)

The original matrix `Q` therefore only translates the affine solution
set.  In particular `K=0` gives the always-available but maximally
degenerate solution `B_+=mu*I`.

### 1.2 Zero-coordinate compatibility

Let `C(gamma)` denote (1.2)--(1.3) with zero diagonal and let

\[
 Z=\{i:\kappa_i=0\}.                                  \tag{1.6}
\]

All solutions of `K*kappa=0` are described without hidden division as
follows:

\[
 \boxed{(C(\gamma)\kappa)_i=0\quad(i\in Z),}           \tag{1.7}
\]

\[
 \boxed{d_i=-{(C(\gamma)\kappa)_i\over\kappa_i}
 \quad(i\notin Z),}                                   \tag{1.8}
\]

while every `d_i` with `i in Z` is free.  Equation (1.7) is the exact
linear compatibility condition at a zero coordinate.  It is never a
solvability obstruction for the unrestricted eigenline problem because
`gamma=0`, `d=0` gives `K=0`.

When every coordinate is nonzero, `gamma_1,...,gamma_N` are free and
(1.8) determines the whole diagonal.  Thus the annihilator

\[
 \{K\in S_N:K\kappa=0\}                               \tag{1.9}
\]

has dimension `N`.  If the displacement of `Q` is `b_n^Q`, the diagonal
forcing of
`DIAGONAL_ANCHOR_FORCING_SIGNED_LAPLACIAN_STRIP_GATE.md` is the single
slice `gamma_n=b_n^Q`; allowing full source-Cauchy forcing varies these
`N` off-diagonal parameters as well.

In raw parameters the corresponding perturbation is explicit:

\[
 b_n^H=b_n^Q-\gamma_n,                                \tag{1.10}
\]

\[
 a_0^H=a_0^Q-\mu-d_0,
 \qquad
 a_n^H=a_n^Q-\mu-d_n+{\gamma_n\over n}.              \tag{1.11}
\]

### 1.3 Preserving the original commutator

For the raw mode operator `D`, distinct mode labels give

\[
 [D,H]_{nm}=(n-m)H_{nm}=b_n^H-b_m^H.                 \tag{1.12}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{[D,Q-H]=[D,Q]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 [D,H]=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_n^H=0
 \text{ for every }n.}                               \tag{1.13}
\]

Thus a commutator-preserving source-Cauchy perturbation is necessarily a
raw shell diagonal.  Under all-coordinate nonvanishing, (1.13) forces
`gamma=b^Q` and recovers the unique diagonal forcing exactly.  Full
source-Cauchy freedom is genuine only if one permits the auxiliary to
change the source displacement vector.

## 2. Homogeneous semidefinite feasibility

Normalize `kappa` to unit length and put

\[
 P_\perp=I-|\kappa\rangle\langle\kappa|.             \tag{2.1}
\]

For `B_+=mu*I+K`, the anchor is a lowest eigenvector exactly when

\[
 K\succeq0,
 \qquad K\kappa=0,                                   \tag{2.2}
\]

and it is a simple lowest eigenvector exactly when the kernel in (2.2) is
`R*kappa`.  Since the problem is homogeneous in `K`, a positive quotient
gap can be scaled to one.

### Theorem 2.1 (exact SDP)

There is a real source-Cauchy perturbation making `kappa` a simple lowest
even eigenvector if and only if the following closed semidefinite system is
feasible:

\[
 \boxed{
 K\in S_N,
 \qquad K\kappa=0,
 \qquad K\succeq P_\perp.}                          \tag{2.3}
\]

Every solution of (2.3), every `mu in R`, and (1.5) give such a
perturbation.  Conversely, if `K\succeq0` has simple kernel `R*kappa`, then
its least eigenvalue on `kappa^perp` is positive; rescaling `K` by its
reciprocal gives (2.3).

No norm or smallness condition on `H` is included in (2.3).  Adding such a
condition would be a separate source-dependent optimization.  Without it,
the SDP may cancel almost all of `Q` and is intentionally a feasibility
test rather than an arithmetic approximation theorem.

## 3. Selector face and the anchor polynomial

Write the real raw anchor coefficients as

\[
 \kappa_0=x_0,
 \qquad \kappa_n=\sqrt2x_n\quad(n\ge1),             \tag{3.1}
\]

and define

\[
 p_\kappa(y)=x_0\prod_{m=1}^N(y-m^2)
 +2y\sum_{n=1}^Nx_n
 \prod_{\substack{1\le m\le N\\m\ne n}}(y-m^2). \tag{3.2}
\]

This polynomial is nonzero whenever `kappa` is nonzero.  Indeed,

\[
 p_\kappa(0)=(-1)^N(N!)^2x_0,
 \qquad
 p_\kappa(j^2)=2j^2x_j
 \prod_{\substack{1\le m\le N\\m\ne j}}(j^2-m^2),             \tag{3.2a}
\]

so `p_kappa=0` first forces `x_0=0` and then every `x_j=0`.

For finite `tau`, use the cleared selector representative

\[
 q_0(\tau)=\prod_{m=1}^N(\tau+m^2),                  \tag{3.3}
\]

\[
 q_n(\tau)=\sqrt2\,\tau
 \prod_{\substack{1\le m\le N\\m\ne n}}
 (\tau+m^2),\qquad 1\le n\le N.                    \tag{3.4}
\]

The homogeneous degree-`N` continuation to `RP^1` is the same rational
normal selector curve as

\[
 w_\tau=e_0^++\sum_{n=1}^N
 {\sqrt2\,\tau\over\tau+n^2}e_n^+.                 \tag{3.5}
\]

At `tau=0` it is the central coordinate.  At `tau=-j^2`, (3.3)--(3.4)
give the `j`-th shell coordinate rather than a singular expression.  At
projective infinity its direction is

\[
 q(\infty)=(1,\sqrt2,\ldots,\sqrt2)=\eta_+.         \tag{3.6}
\]

Direct expansion gives the polynomial identity

\[
 \boxed{\langle\kappa,q(\tau)\rangle
 =(-1)^N p_\kappa(-\tau).}                          \tag{3.7}
\]

Because (3.7) is homogeneous, it includes multiplicities and the root at
infinity.  In particular:

- `tau=-j^2` is a selector root exactly when `x_j=0`, equivalently when
  `p_kappa(j^2)=0`;
- infinity is a selector root exactly when the leading coefficient
  `x_0+2*sum x_n` vanishes; and
- a repeated polynomial root gives one selector direction with repeated
  multiplicity, not several independent directions.

Let

\[
 C_N=\{K\in S_N:K\succeq0\}.                        \tag{3.8}
\]

The finite moment-cone theorem in
`SOURCE_CAUCHY_SELECTOR_CONE_PROJECTIVE_TRADEOFF.md` gives

\[
 C_N=\operatorname {cone}
 \{|q(\tau)\rangle\langle q(\tau)|:
     \tau\in\mathbb {RP}^1\}.                       \tag{3.9}
\]

The scaling of each projective representative is absorbed into its
nonnegative cone coefficient.

### Theorem 3.1 (annihilating selector face)

Let `Z_R(kappa)` be the set of **distinct real projective roots** of the
homogenization of `p_kappa(-tau)`.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 F_\kappa:=\{K\in C_N:K\kappa=0\}
 =\operatorname {cone}
 \{|q(\tau)\rangle\langle q(\tau)|:
       \tau\in Z_R(\kappa)\}.}                    \tag{3.10}
\]

If `r=card Z_R(kappa)`, then

\[
 \boxed{\max_{K\in F_\kappa}\operatorname {rank}K=r.} \tag{3.11}
\]

**Proof.**  Write a member of `C_N` as a positive atom sum using (3.9).
If it annihilates `kappa`, then

\[
 0=\langle\kappa,K\kappa\rangle
  =\sum_j\theta_j|\langle\kappa,q(\tau_j)\rangle|^2.
                                                               \tag{3.12}
\]

Every nonnegative summand vanishes, so (3.7) places every support point in
`Z_R(kappa)`.  The converse is immediate, proving (3.10).

Under the invertible congruence in the moment-cone theorem, `q(tau)` is the
degree-`N` rational normal curve.  Any at most `N+1` distinct projective
points on that curve are linearly independent.  Therefore a sum supported
on `r` distinct roots has rank at most `r`, while a positive sum using all
`r` roots has rank exactly `r`.  This proves (3.11).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (simple-ground/root equivalence)

The following are equivalent:

1. the SDP (2.3) is feasible;
2. some real source-Cauchy `Q-H` has `kappa` as a simple lowest even
   eigenvector;
3. the degree-`N` homogenization of `p_kappa` has `N` distinct roots in
   `RP^1`.

When the roots are `tau_1,...,tau_N` in the selector parameter, every
simple-ground shifted block is of the form

\[
 \boxed{
 K=\sum_{j=1}^N\theta_j
 |q(\tau_j)\rangle\langle q(\tau_j)|,
 \qquad \theta_j>0,}                                \tag{3.13}
\]

up to the harmless choice of projective representatives.  Together with
(1.5), equation (3.13) is the complete solution cone.

**Proof.**  A simple ground requires `rank K=N`.  Equations (3.10)--(3.11)
make this possible exactly when `r=N`.  A nonzero homogeneous polynomial
of degree `N` has `N` complex-projective roots counted with multiplicity;
hence `r=N` exactly when all of them are real and distinct.  If it holds, a
positive sum over all roots has rank `N`, annihilates `kappa`, and has
kernel precisely `R*kappa`.  Every rank-`N` member of the face must use all
`N` root directions with positive total weight.  \(\square\)

If every projective root is real but some are repeated, the transform of
`kappa` is already cross-rooted, but (3.11) forces a multiple lowest
eigenspace for every source-Cauchy realization.  If a nonreal root occurs,
no choice of `H` can make `kappa` a simple lowest vector.  This is also
immediate from the exact transform

\[
 \widehat\kappa(z)
 ={\sqrt L\over\pi}{\sin(\pi s)\over s}
 {p_\kappa(s^2)\over\prod_{m=1}^N(s^2-m^2)},
 \qquad s={zL\over2\pi}:                              \tag{3.14}
\]

`H` changes neither `kappa` nor its zeros.  Thus full source-Cauchy forcing
is equivalent to the strict real-root target, rather than a proof of it.

## 4. Exact annihilating-face counterexample

Take `N=2` and the raw anchor direction

\[
 (x_0,x_1,x_2)=(1,-1,1).                              \tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{p_\kappa(y)=y^2+y+4,\qquad \operatorname {disc}p=-15.} \tag{4.2}
\]

There is no real projective root, so Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
 \boxed{F_\kappa=\{0\}.}                             \tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently, if a source-Cauchy block has this `kappa` as a lowest
eigenvector at `mu`, then `K=B_+-mu*I=0`; the even block is scalar and the
ground multiplicity is three.  A simple ground is impossible.

There is also a direct exact SDP check.  Put
`S=diag(1,sqrt(2),sqrt(2))` and `L_K=S*K*S`.  With displacement variables
`u=gamma_1`, `v=gamma_2`, the equation `L_K*(1,-1,1)^T=0` forces

\[
 d_0=2u-v,
 \qquad d_1={u+4v\over3},
 \qquad d_2={-4u+5v\over6}.                         \tag{4.4}
\]

On the basis `(1,1,0)`, `(-1,0,1)` of `(1,-1,1)^perp`, the determinant of
the restricted form is

\[
 \boxed{-6(4u^2-7uv+4v^2).}                         \tag{4.5}
\]

The quadratic in parentheses is positive definite because its discriminant
is `49-64=-15`.  Positive semidefiniteness therefore forces `u=v=0`, and
(4.4) forces `K=0`.

This is the same fixed-rank prolate-direction obstruction already isolated
in the selector and diagonal-forcing notes.  It is not a
projection-convergent natural-cutoff counterexample.  It does prove sharply
that no source-Cauchy perturbation theorem valid for every finite prolate
projection can force the desired ground.

## 5. Remaining actual-source obligation

For the actual natural-cutoff anchor, the finite SDP is now completely
decided by the roots of `p_kappa`.  The existing closed negative-real
interval theorem, fixed-compact convergence, and coordinate asymptotics do
not prove that all `N` projective roots are real and distinct on a growing
schedule.  Proving that condition would already supply axis-rooted finite
transforms and, together with the separately required locally uniform
limit, the surviving RH-strength zero geometry.

Allowing arbitrary `H` therefore has only two outcomes:

1. preserve the pinned commutator, in which case `H` is diagonal and the
   original signed-Laplacian gate returns; or
2. change the displacement and use (3.13), which is possible exactly after
   the strict real-root target has already been proved.

No actual-source construction satisfying either unbounded gate is supplied
here.  The formal state remains `P0_INVALIDATED`.
