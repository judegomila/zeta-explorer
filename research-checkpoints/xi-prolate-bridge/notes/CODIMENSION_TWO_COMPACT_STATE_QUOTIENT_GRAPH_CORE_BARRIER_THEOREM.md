# Codimension-two compact-state quotient and graph-core barrier theorem

## 0. Exact outcome and boundary

Fix `lambda>1`.  On the even, time-limited source class, write

\[
 I(f)=\int_{-\lambda}^{\lambda}f(x)\,dx,
 \qquad v(f)=f(0),
 \qquad T_\lambda=C_\lambda {\cal E}.                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Let `psi_lambda` be the locked even, zero-integral origin bump supported
strictly inside `(-lambda^(-1),lambda^(-1))`, normalized by
`psi_lambda(0)=1`.  This note proves the following narrow statements.

1. On the full zero-integral source class, the map

   \[
    R_\lambda f=f-v(f)\psi_\lambda                  \tag{0.2}
   \]

   is an exact retraction onto the codimension-two class, and

   \[
    \boxed{T_\lambda R_\lambda f=T_\lambda f.}       \tag{0.3}
   \]

   Thus the two classes have exactly the same compact central-state range,
   not merely the same closure.  The equality survives inversion parity,
   fixed Fourier projection, and fixed anchor projection.
2. This exact result uses the augmented source family containing the bump.
   In a pure modal prefix, the origin-null family generated by the columns
   `g_n` has one dimension less than the zero-integral mixed prefix.  The
   corrected augmented range has not lost that dimension; it places it in
   the nonmodal column `R_lambda b_lambda`.
3. At each fixed `lambda`, the union of the pure modal codimension-two
   spaces is dense in the full zero-integral source hyperplane in `L2` and
   in the modal exterior seminorm.  Consequently it has the same range
   after every fixed finite Fourier/parity/anchor projection, once a
   sufficiently large but non-effective modal prefix is allowed.
4. No conclusion about the growing-prefix Toeplitz floor or a Weil graph
   core follows.  The density proof uses arbitrarily high modal labels and
   has no rate on the locked growing schedule.  The invisible bump has the
   wrong exterior scale on the bridge, while `L2` and exterior-seminorm
   convergence do not control the complete signed form or the absolute
   zero-sampling norm.

Therefore the origin condition can be imposed without changing a compact
central state, but it does **not** bypass the single bridge in the existing
floor proof.  The augmented route still needs the coherent raw-row
Plemelj/closability theorem.  The pure-modal route instead needs a
quantitative graph-norm approximation theorem on the locked growing
schedule.  Neither input is currently frozen.  This note proves no new
complete-form floor, zero theorem, or Riemann hypothesis.

Let

\[
 H_\lambda=L^2_{\rm even}(-\lambda,\lambda),
 \qquad
 V_Q=\operatorname {span}\{h_0,h_2,\ldots,h_{4Q+2}\},
 \qquad V_{\rm fin}=\bigcup_{Q\geq0}V_Q.             \tag{1.1}
\]

The modes are the locked real even prolate basis, zero-extended outside the

\[
 Z=\{f:I(f)=0\},
 \qquad Z_0=\{f:I(f)=v(f)=0\},                       \tag{1.2}

\[
 Z_Q=V_Q\cap\ker I,

The locked bump is

\[
 \psi_\lambda(x)=\psi(2\lambda x),
 \qquad I(\psi_\lambda)=0,
 \qquad v(\psi_\lambda)=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname {supp}\psi_\lambda
       \subset(-\lambda^{-1},\lambda^{-1}).          \tag{1.4}

Only the support, integral, and value properties in (1.4) are used in the

## 2. Exact compact-state quotient

### Theorem 2.1 (invisible-origin retraction)

On `Z`, the operator (0.2) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{R_\lambda^2=R_\lambda,
        \quad\operatorname {ran}R_\lambda=Z_0,
        \quad\ker R_\lambda=\mathbb C\psi_\lambda.} \tag{2.1}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{T_\lambda R_\lambda=T_\lambda\quad\hbox{on }Z.} \tag{2.2}
\]

**Proof.**  Equations (1.2)--(1.4) give

\[
 I(R_\lambda f)=0,
 \qquad v(R_\lambda f)=v(f)-v(f)v(\psi_\lambda)=0.  \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus `R_lambda` maps `Z` into `Z_0` and is the identity on `Z_0`, proving
the range and idempotence statements.  If `R_lambda f=0`, then
`f=v(f)psi_lambda`; the reverse inclusion is immediate, proving the kernel
statement.

For `u in [lambda^(-1),lambda]` and every integer `n>=1`, one has
`nu>=lambda^(-1)`, outside the support in (1.4).  Hence
`E(psi_lambda)(u)=0`, which proves (2.2).  \(\square\)

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{T_\lambda(Z)=T_\lambda(Z_0).}                \tag{2.4}
\]

There may be other invisible sources, so no claim that
`ker(T_lambda|_Z)=C psi_lambda` is made.  The exact state quotient is

\[
 \boxed{
 Z/(Z\cap\ker T_\lambda)
 \simeq Z_0/(Z_0\cap\ker T_\lambda).}                \tag{2.5}
\]

If `B` is any downstream linear map defined on the compact state range,
then

\[
 BT_\lambda(Z)=BT_\lambda(Z_0).                     \tag{2.6}
\]

This applies, in particular, to either inversion parity, a fixed `P_N`, and
a fixed orthogonal anchor projection.  Because the compact vectors are
literally equal, every norm or graph norm defined only on those compact
vectors also gives equality.  This observation does not identify their
different outer Poisson representatives.

## 3. Augmented corrected prefix versus the pure modal prefix

The modal inventory (1.1) has dimension `2Q+2`.  With
`I_n=I(h_n)` and `v_n=v(h_n)`, the two rows are independent because

\[
 \Delta=I_0v_2-I_2v_0
       =v_0v_2(\chi_0+\chi_2)>0.                    \tag{3.1}
\]

For every even label `4<=n<=4Q+2`, define

\[
 A_n={I_nv_2-I_2v_n\over\Delta},
 \qquad B_n={I_0v_n-v_0I_n\over\Delta},             \tag{3.2}
\]

\[
 g_n=h_n-A_nh_0-B_nh_2.                              \tag{3.3}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{G_Q=\operatorname {span}\{g_4,g_6,\ldots,g_{4Q+2}\},
        \quad\dim G_Q=2Q,
        \quad\dim Z_Q=2Q+1.}                        \tag{3.4}
\]

The missing modal direction can be represented by the bridge

\[
 b_\lambda=h_2-{I_2\over I_0}h_0,
 \qquad v(b_\lambda)={\Delta\over I_0}\ne0,         \tag{3.5}
\]

so that

\[
 Z_Q=G_Q\mathbin\oplus\mathbb C b_\lambda.          \tag{3.6}
\]

The bump is not in `V_Q`: it vanishes on an open subinterval where a finite
prolate combination is analytic, but it is not identically zero.  Therefore
`R_lambda` is injective on `Z_Q`, and

\[
 \boxed{
 R_\lambda Z_Q
 =G_Q\mathbin\oplus\mathbb C R_\lambda b_\lambda,
 \qquad
 (R_\lambda Z_Q)\cap V_Q=G_Q.}                      \tag{3.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \dim R_\lambda Z_Q=2Q+1,
 \qquad T_\lambda(R_\lambda Z_Q)=T_\lambda(Z_Q).} \tag{3.8}
\]

This is the essential distinction.  The augmented corrected prefix has the
full state range and stores the bridge in a nonmodal bump-corrected column.
The pure modal `g_n` prefix has really discarded one source dimension.

For `lambda=j+1/2`, the frozen unmatched-breakpoint theorem says that each

\[
 S_\pm A_\lambda:V_Q\longrightarrow
 L^2([\lambda^{-1},\lambda],du/u)                   \tag{3.9}
\]

is injective.  Hence at the same finite prefix

\[
 \dim S_\pm A_\lambda Z_Q=2Q+1,
 \qquad \dim S_\pm A_\lambda G_Q=2Q,                \tag{3.10}
\]

and the lost modal bridge is an actual lost continuous physical direction.
For fixed `lambda`, fixed `V_Q`, and a fixed sign, the frozen theorem also
gives an index `N_0(lambda,V_Q,sign)` after which
`P_NS_\pm A_lambda` is injective on `V_Q`.  It supplies no effective or
uniform bound on `N_0`, and it says nothing by itself after a further anchor
projection.  At the same prefix, a downstream map `B` removes the loss
exactly when

\[
 B b_\lambda\in B(G_Q),                              \tag{3.11}
\]

which must be proved for that map and is not implied by parity or anchoring.

## 4. Fixed-lambda `L2` density of the pure modal family

The origin row is deliberately singular in `L2`.  The next lemma makes the
density mechanism precise.

### Lemma 4.1 (unbounded tail origin row)

For every fixed `Q`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{\substack{q\in V_{\rm fin}\cap V_Q^\perp\cap\ker I\\
                  \|q\|_2=1}}
 |v(q)|=+\infty.}                                    \tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.**  First, point evaluation is unbounded on `V_fin` in the `L2`
norm.  Indeed, let `A_c` be the fixed-`lambda` prolate Sturm--Liouville
operator and let `Pi_M` be its even spectral projection.  For an even
`rho in C_c^infinity(-lambda,lambda)`, spectral calculus gives
`Pi_M rho -> rho` in every power graph norm of `A_c`.  Interior elliptic
regularity then gives uniform convergence near zero, in particular

\[
 v(\Pi_M\rho)\longrightarrow\rho(0).                \tag{4.2}
\]

If `v` were `L2` bounded on `V_fin`, (4.2) would imply
`|rho(0)|<=C||rho||_2` for every such `rho`.  Applying this to
`rho_epsilon(x)=rho(x/epsilon)` with `rho(0)=1` contradicts
`||rho_epsilon||_2=epsilon^(1/2)||rho||_2`.

Removing the finite-dimensional head `V_Q` cannot make the remaining
origin row bounded.  Also `I` is nonzero on the tail: every locked even
prolate mode has

\[
 I_{2j}=(-1)^j\chi_{2j}v_{2j}\ne0.                  \tag{4.3}
\]

Suppose now that `v` were bounded on the tail intersection with `ker I`.
Choose one tail vector `e` with `I(e) != 0`.  Every tail vector has the
decomposition

\[
 f=\left(f-{I(f)\over I(e)}e\right)+{I(f)\over I(e)}e. \tag{4.4}
\]

The first term lies in `ker I`; boundedness of `I` and the assumed bound
would make `v` bounded on the whole tail, a contradiction.  This proves
(4.1).  \(\square\)

### Theorem 4.2 (fixed-lambda source and exterior closure)

In `H_lambda`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \overline{G_{\rm fin}}^{\,L^2}
 =\overline{V_{\rm fin}\cap\ker I}^{\,L^2}
 =\ker I.}                                           \tag{4.5}
\]

Moreover, if

\[
 D_\lambda\!\left(\sum_j a_jh_{2j}\right)
   =\sum_jd_{2j}|a_j|^2,
 \qquad 0<d_{2j}<1,                                  \tag{4.6}
\]

is the modal exterior-defect form, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \overline{G_{\rm fin}}^{\,D_\lambda}
 =\overline{V_{\rm fin}\cap\ker I}^{\,D_\lambda}.} \tag{4.7}
\]

**Proof.**  The prolate basis is complete and `I` is a bounded nonzero
functional on `H_lambda`.  Correcting arbitrary finite-modal approximants
by one fixed vector with nonzero integral proves that
`V_fin cap ker I` is dense in `ker I`.

It remains to impose the origin condition.  Fix
`f in V_fin cap ker I`, and choose `Q` containing its modal support.  By
Lemma 4.1 there are unit finite-tail vectors `r_k in ker I` with
`|v(r_k)| -> infinity`.  Put

\[
 q_k={v(f)\over v(r_k)}r_k,
 \qquad g_k=f-q_k.                                   \tag{4.8}
\]

Then `g_k in G_fin` and

\[
 \|g_k-f\|_2={|v(f)|\over|v(r_k)|}\longrightarrow0. \tag{4.9}
\]

This proves (4.5).  Equation (4.6) gives
`D_lambda(q)<=||q||_2^2`, so the same sequence proves (4.7).  \(\square\)

The theorem is purely fixed-parameter.  The correction works because its
point value stays fixed while its `L2` norm tends to zero; this is exactly
the singular behavior which prevents a quantitative conclusion in a
stronger topology.

## 5. Fixed finite physical projections

Let `E_N` be a fixed finite centered Fourier space, and let

\[
 B=P_{\rm anchor}P_NS_\pm A_\lambda:
 H_\lambda\longrightarrow E_N                       \tag{5.1}
\]

with either parity and any fixed orthogonal anchor projection.  The frozen
source estimate makes `A_lambda` bounded at fixed `lambda`; all subsequent
maps are contractions.  Theorem 4.2 therefore gives

\[
 \overline{B(G_{\rm fin})}=\overline{B(\ker I)}.     \tag{5.2}
\]

The left side is already closed: `B(G_fin)` is a linear subspace of the
finite-dimensional space `E_N`.  Thus

\[
 \boxed{B(G_{\rm fin})=B(\ker I).}                   \tag{5.3}
\]

Because the spaces `G_Q` are nested, a finite basis of the range in (5.3)
is contained in one of them.  Hence there is a non-effective index

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_0=Q_0(\lambda,N,\text{sign},P_{\rm anchor})
 \quad\hbox{such that}\quad
 B(G_Q)=B(\ker I)\quad(Q\geq Q_0).}                  \tag{5.4}
\]

There is no conflict with (3.10).  Equation (3.10) compares `G_Q` with
`Z_Q` at the same source cutoff.  Equation (5.4) permits arbitrarily higher
modal labels to reproduce a fixed finite collection of physical
coordinates.  No frozen estimate places `Q_0` below `C_0L`, or below any
other locked growing cutoff.

## 6. Why the Toeplitz floor does not transfer

There are two distinct representatives and two distinct failures.

### 6.1 The augmented exact representative

The exact correction of the bridge is

\[
 R_\lambda b_\lambda
 =b_\lambda-v(b_\lambda)\psi_\lambda.               \tag{6.1}
\]

It gives the identical compact state, but it is not a modal column.  Its
bump has

\[
 \|v(b_\lambda)\psi_\lambda\|_2^2
 \asymp {|v(b_\lambda)|^2\over\lambda},             \tag{6.2}
\]

whereas the raw modal bridge has

\[
 D_\lambda(b_\lambda)\asymp d_2
 \asymp\lambda^5e^{-4\pi\lambda^2}.                 \tag{6.3}
\]

The strict-PASS bridge-domain pair pinned in Section 1 proves that this
scale mismatch prevents inheritance of the modal quarter floor.  The
Xi-over-s pair proves that estimating the separated beta/bump carrier is an
RH-strength obstruction.  Exact state equality licenses only the complete
coherent cancellation, not deletion or separate estimation of either row.

### 6.2 The pure modal approximants

The vectors `q_k` in (4.8) use labels escaping every fixed modal prefix.
The proof gives no bound on their largest label and no convergence in the
fixed-lambda logarithmic form norm.  In particular,

\[
 D_\lambda(q_k)\longrightarrow0                     \tag{6.4}
\]

does not imply that a pole, Archimedean, prime, or complete signed
quadratic form involving `q_k` tends to zero.  The existing Toeplitz floor
is a theorem on a controlled low-prefix inventory in its pulled-back full
metric; it is not a continuity theorem for arbitrary `D_lambda`-small
high-label sequences.  Equations (4.7) and (5.4) therefore cannot transport
that floor to the bridge.

## 7. Graph topology and the precise missing alternatives

At fixed `lambda` and fixed `N`, the target is finite dimensional, so (5.3)
settles every target norm.  The two graph-core questions used later in the
program are stronger.

1. The unprojected fixed-`lambda` semilocal form has a logarithmically
   weighted Fourier domain.  `L2` convergence of the central states does
   not imply convergence in that form norm.
2. The widening global passage to the Weil criterion uses the absolute
   zero-sampling norm from the frozen `q`-shift core theorem.  Neither
   source `L2` convergence nor the exterior seminorm controls that norm.

Thus this note proves no identity

\[
 \overline{T_\lambda(G_{\rm fin})}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{\rm graph}}
 =\overline{T_\lambda(Z)}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{\rm graph}}, \tag{7.1}
\]

and no growing analogue in the absolute zero-sampling norm.  The augmented
family has exact equality of compact vectors by (2.4), so a graph property
depending only on those vectors transfers tautologically.  What does not
transfer is the **proof of the floor**, because that proof resolves the
outer representative into modal and beta/bump rows before the needed
coherent cancellation has been established.

There are therefore two honest missing alternatives.

- **Augmented route.**  Prove a cutoff-uniform complete raw-row
  finite-part/Plemelj closability identity, keeping pole, Archimedean,
  prime, beta, bump, and every cross term coherent, with bridge-negative
  cost at the required defect-relative scale.
- **Pure modal route.**  Prove a quantitative approximation of the bridge
  by `G_Q` in the relevant semilocal or absolute zero-sampling graph norm,
  with `Q` inside the locked growing schedule and with the pulled-back full
  exterior metric retained.

The first alternative is exactly the missing gate isolated by the pinned
bridge-domain and Xi-over-s pairs.  The second is strictly stronger than
Theorems 4.2 and 5.1.  Without one of them, imposing `f(0)=0` does not bypass
the mixed bridge.

## 8. Fail-closed conclusion

The question has a split answer.

- **Finite compact state:** yes, exactly, after the invisible bump is
  admitted.
- **Pure modal prefix at the same cutoff:** no; one bridge dimension is
  lost, and continuous parity injectivity detects it.
- **Fixed finite projected range after arbitrarily enlarging the prefix:**
  yes, non-effectively, by fixed-`lambda` `L2` density.
- **Exterior Toeplitz floor or Weil graph core on the locked growing
  schedule:** not proved.

No beta/bump row has been discarded, no graph continuity has been assumed,
and no complete pole--Archimedean--prime cancellation has been promoted
from compact-state equality.  The project state remains `P0_INVALIDATED`,
and the standard eight are unchanged.
