## 2. Strengthened raw BV radical lemma

The origin bump used in the existing radical note is unnecessary for zero
evaluation itself.  The following extension is useful because it leaves the
raw prolate Fourier tail and its exact exterior metric unchanged.

### Lemma 2.1 (zero integral is enough for the BV radical)

Suppose `f` is even, compactly supported, piecewise `C^1`, of bounded
variation, and bounded at zero.  If

\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\,dx=0,                                \tag{2.1}
\]

then

\[
 {\cal E}(f)(u)=u^{1/2}\sum_{n\geq1}f(nu)                 \tag{2.2}
\]

is `O(u^(1/2))` at zero, vanishes for sufficiently large `u`, belongs to
`L^2(R_+^*,du/u)`, and satisfies

\[
 \widehat{{\cal E}(f)}(z)
 =\zeta\!\left({1\over2}-iz\right)m_f(z),\qquad
 m_f(z)=\int_0^\infty f(v)v^{1/2-iz}{dv\over v},            \tag{2.3}
\]

throughout `Im z > -1/2`.  Consequently both Mellin evaluations at every
nontrivial-zero parameter vanish.  No condition `f(0)=0` is needed.

**Proof.**  The bounded-variation Riemann-sum estimate gives

\[
 u\sum_{n\geq1}f(nu)=\int_0^\infty f(v)\,dv+O(u)=O(u).       \tag{2.4}
\]

Thus (2.2) is `O(u^(1/2))`.  Compact support handles infinity, proving the
`L^2` assertion and making the left side of (2.3) holomorphic for
`Im z>-1/2`.

For `Im z>1/2`, absolute convergence and `v=nu` prove (2.3) term by term.
Because `f` is bounded at zero, `m_f` itself is holomorphic for
`Im z>-1/2`.  The only zeta pole in that half-plane is at `z=i/2`, and it
is removed by

\[
 m_f(i/2)=\int_0^\infty f(v)\,dv=0.                         \tag{2.5}
\]

The identity theorem proves (2.3) on the larger half-plane.  If
`rho=1/2+is` is a nontrivial zero, then `-1/2<Im s<1/2`; both `s` and
`\bar s` lie in this domain, and the two zeta factors vanish exactly.
\(\square\)

The absolutely convergent spectral-tail argument in Section 4 of the
frozen radical note now applies verbatim.  Indeed, after inversion
symmetrization the global vector has zero evaluations, its compact
log-Fourier projection `p_N` has Mellin transform `O(1/|s|)`, and the
remainder evaluates to `-p_N` at every zero.  Each polarized zero summand is
therefore `O(1/|s|^2)`, which is summable by the classical zero-counting
bound.  Hence the raw zero-integral source in (0.3) is a legitimate
two-sided spectral radical.

This extension does not delete the `-f(0) beta_lambda` term from the Poisson
outer decomposition.  It only removes the need to change the raw source by
an invisible origin bump.

## 3. Exact conductor and divisor identity

Let `T=[-lambda,lambda]`, put

\[
 B_m=[-m\lambda,m\lambda],\qquad
 F_m={\bf1}_{B_m^c}H,\qquad g_m={\cal F}_a^{-1}F_m,          \tag{3.1}
\]

and define the wider concentration operator on `L^2(T)` by

\[
 K_m=P_T{\cal F}_a^{-1}P_{B_m}{\cal F}_aP_T.                \tag{3.2}
\]

Since `f=P_Tf`, the exact inside residual is

\[
 r_m=P_Tg_m=(I-K_m)f.                                      \tag{3.3}
\]

Set

\[
 S_m=\|F_m\|_2^2=\|g_m\|_2^2,\qquad
 S=\sum_{m\geq1}{S_m\over m},\qquad D=S_1.                  \tag{3.4}
\]

For `z>=1`, use the step functions

\[
 H_{\lfloor z\rfloor}=\sum_{m\leq z}{1\over m},\qquad
 L_{\lfloor z\rfloor}=\sum_{m\leq z}{\log m\over m}.        \tag{3.5}
\]

Tonelli gives the divisor identities

\[
 S=\int_{|v|\geq\lambda}
 H_{\lfloor |v|/\lambda\rfloor}|H(v)|^2\,dv,
                                                                    \tag{3.6}
\]

\[
 \sum_{m\geq1}{\log m\over m}S_m
 =\int_{|v|\geq\lambda}
 L_{\lfloor |v|/\lambda\rfloor}|H(v)|^2\,dv.                \tag{3.7}
\]

In particular `S>=D`.

Let

\[
 C_{\rm B}=\log|x|+{\cal F}_a^{-1}\log|x|{\cal F}_a        \tag{3.8}
\]

be Burnol's conductor.  With the normalizations (0.6)--(0.8), the exact
Mellin/additive conversion is

\[
 2\langle R_m\phi,a(D)R_m\phi\rangle
 ={1\over4m}\langle F_m,C_{\rm B}F_m\rangle.               \tag{3.9}
\]

Here are all normalization factors.  On the positive half-line use the
unitary logarithmic map

\[
 ({\cal U}_+q)(t)=\sqrt2\,e^{t/2}q(e^t),                    \tag{3.9a}
\]

and put

\[
 G_m(x)=\sqrt\lambda\,{\bf1}_{\{|x|\geq1\}}H(m\lambda x).
                                                                    \tag{3.9b}
\]

Direct substitution into (0.6) gives

\[
 {\cal U}_+G_m=2\sqrt2\,R_m\phi.                            \tag{3.9c}
\]

Burnol's even gamma factor says exactly that
`{\cal U}_+C_{\rm B}{\cal U}_+^{-1}=a(D)`.  If
`{\cal D}_Aq(x)=\sqrt A\,q(Ax)` is unitary dilation, then

\[
 G_m=m^{-1/2}{\cal D}_{m\lambda}F_m.                       \tag{3.9d}
\]

The conductor is dilation invariant.  Consequently

\[
 8\langle R_m\phi,a(D)R_m\phi\rangle
 =\langle G_m,C_{\rm B}G_m\rangle
 ={1\over m}\langle F_m,C_{\rm B}F_m\rangle,
\]

which is (3.9).  The same change of variables gives

\[
 \|\phi\|_2^2={1\over4}\int_\lambda^\infty|H(v)|^2\,dv
 ={D(f)\over8},
\]

and independently locks (0.7).

The prime equality in (0.10) is exactly

\[
 -{1\over2}\int_{|v|\geq\lambda}
 L_{\lfloor |v|/\lambda\rfloor}|H(v)|^2\,dv.                \tag{3.10}
\]

Equations (3.7), (3.9), and Plancherel therefore give

\[
 \mathfrak E_\lambda(f)
 ={1\over4}\sum_{m\geq1}{1\over m}
 \left\{
  \langle F_m,\log|v|F_m\rangle
 +\langle g_m,\log|x|g_m\rangle
 -2\log m\,S_m\right\}.                                  \tag{3.11}
\]

Define the nonnegative inside penalty and outside surplus

\[
 J_m=\int_{|x|<\lambda}\log{\lambda\over|x|}|r_m(x)|^2\,dx,
                                                                    \tag{3.12}
\]

\[
 U_m=\int_{|x|\geq\lambda}\log{|x|\over\lambda}|g_m(x)|^2\,dx.
                                                                    \tag{3.13}
\]

Then, exactly,

\[
 \langle g_m,\log|x|g_m\rangle
 =\log\lambda\,S_m-J_m+U_m.                               \tag{3.14}
\]

Using `log|v|=log(lambda)+log(|v|/lambda)` in (3.11), put

\[
 J=\sum_{m\geq1}{J_m\over m},\qquad
 U=\sum_{m\geq1}{U_m\over m}.                             \tag{3.15}
\]

The complete exact identity is

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak E_\lambda(f)
  ={}&{L\over4}S
  +{1\over4}\int_{|v|\geq\lambda}
   \left\{H_{\lfloor z\rfloor}\log z
                -2L_{\lfloor z\rfloor}\right\}|H(v)|^2\,dv\\
  &-{1\over4}J+{1\over4}U,
  \qquad z={|v|\over\lambda}.
 \end{aligned}}                                             \tag{3.16}
\]

No pseudodifferential replacement or smoothing of a hard cutoff occurs in
(3.16).

## 4. The arithmetic bracket is nonnegative

For an integer `M>=1`, let

\[
 A_M=H_M\log M-2L_M.                                      \tag{4.1}
\]

One has `A_1=0`, and direct subtraction gives

\[
 A_{M+1}-A_M
 =H_M\log\left(1+{1\over M}\right)
  -{\log(M+1)\over M+1}.                                  \tag{4.2}
\]

The elementary bounds

\[
 H_M\geq\log(M+1),\qquad
 \log\left(1+{1\over M}\right)\geq{1\over M+1}           \tag{4.3}
\]

show that (4.2) is nonnegative.  If `M=floor(z)`, then

\[
 H_{\lfloor z\rfloor}\log z-2L_{\lfloor z\rfloor}
 \geq H_M\log M-2L_M=A_M\geq0.                            \tag{4.4}
\]

Dropping this bracket and `U` from (3.16) yields the sharp reduction

\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak E_\lambda(f)\geq {L\over4}S-{1\over4}J.} \tag{4.5}
\]

## 5. Moving central split

Take

\[
 R={\lambda\over L},\qquad h=\log{\lambda\over R}=\log L. \tag{5.1}
\]

For `|x|<lambda`,

\[
 \log{\lambda\over|x|}
 \leq h+{\bf1}_{\{|x|<R\}}\log{R\over|x|}.                 \tag{5.2}
\]

Define

\[
 J_R=\sum_{m\geq1}{1\over m}
 \int_{|x|<R}\log{R\over|x|}|r_m(x)|^2\,dx.              \tag{5.3}
\]

Because `P_T` is an orthogonal projection,

\[
 \|r_m\|_2^2\leq\|g_m\|_2^2=S_m.                         \tag{5.4}
\]

Thus

\[
 J\leq(\log L)S+J_R.                                      \tag{5.5}
\]

The entire endpoint/glancing annulus `R<=|x|<lambda` has now been retained
without a commutator: its cost is the first term on the right of (5.5).
Combining (4.5), (5.5), and `S>=D` gives, for large `L`,

\[
 \mathfrak E_\lambda(f)
 \geq\left({L\over4}-{1\over4}\log L\right)D-{1\over4}J_R.
                                                                    \tag{5.6}
\]

It remains only to prove `J_R=O(D)` in the nonstationary central cone
`|x|/lambda<=1/L`.

## 6. Central defect spectral kernel and the `m=1` term

Scale the prolate functions to `[-1,1]`:

\[
 u_n(y)=\sqrt\lambda\,h_{n,\lambda}(\lambda y),\qquad
 \|u_n\|_{L^2(-1,1)}=1.                                   \tag{6.1}
\]

They solve

\[
 -((1-y^2)u_n')'+c^2y^2u_n=\mu_n(c)u_n.                   \tag{6.2}
\]

The locked inputs give

\[
 n(n+1)\leq\mu_n(c)<3c^2\qquad(n<n_*),                   \tag{6.3}
\]

the global elementary bound

\[
 \|u_n\|_\infty\leq\sqrt{24}\,c,                         \tag{6.4}
\]

and, on the leading side `0<=n<=S-1`,

\[
 d_n\leq10\exp\left(-{S-n-7\over\beta_\lambda}\right).  \tag{6.5}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 (uniform central amplitude above half Shannon)

There are absolute `delta>0`, `C_0`, and `lambda_0` such that

\[
 \sup_{|y|\leq\delta}|u_n(y)|^2\leq C_0                  \tag{6.6}
\]

whenever `lambda>=lambda_0` and `S/2<=n<n_*`.

**Proof.**  By (6.3),

\[
 {c^2\over\pi^2}\leq\mu_n(c)<3c^2.                       \tag{6.7}
\]

Fix

\[
 a={1\over4\pi},\qquad I=[-a,a],\qquad I_0=[-a/2,a/2],
\]

and set

\[
 v_n(y)=\sqrt{1-y^2}\,u_n(y).                              \tag{6.8}
\]

Direct differentiation of (6.2) gives

\[
 v_n''+\Omega_n^2v_n=0,\qquad
 \Omega_n^2={\mu_n-c^2y^2\over1-y^2}
             +{1\over(1-y^2)^2}.                           \tag{6.9}
\]

Because `a^2=1/(16\pi^2)`, (6.7) implies on `I`

\[
 {\sqrt{15}\over4\pi}c\leq\Omega_n(y)\leq2c,\qquad
 |(\log\Omega_n)'(y)|\leq C_I                             \tag{6.10}
\]

for all sufficiently large `c`, with an absolute constant `C_I`.
Indeed, direct differentiation of (6.9) bounds the numerator of
`(\log\Omega_n)'` by `O(c^2)`, while its denominator obeys
`\Omega_n^2\geq15c^2/(16\pi^2)`.  In Prüfer variables

\[
 v_n=\rho_n\sin\vartheta_n,\qquad
 v_n'=\rho_n\Omega_n\cos\vartheta_n,                      \tag{6.11}
\]

one has

\[
 {\rho_n'\over\rho_n}
 =-{\Omega_n'\over\Omega_n}\cos^2\vartheta_n,\qquad
 \vartheta_n'=\Omega_n
 +{\Omega_n'\over\Omega_n}\sin\vartheta_n\cos\vartheta_n.
                                                                    \tag{6.12}
\]

Hence

\[
 e^{-2aC_I}\leq{\rho_n(y)\over\rho_n(y_0)}
 \leq e^{2aC_I}\qquad(y,y_0\in I).                         \tag{6.12a}
\]

Put `c_2=\sqrt{15}/(8\pi)`.  Equations (6.10) and (6.12) give

\[
 c_2c\leq\vartheta_n'(y)\leq3c\qquad(y\in I)              \tag{6.12b}
\]

for all sufficiently large `c`.  Thus the phase length across `I` is at
least `2a c_2 c`.  Change variables from `y` to `\vartheta_n`.  The
elementary bound

\[
 \int_\alpha^\beta\sin^2\theta\,d\theta
 \geq{\beta-\alpha\over2}-{1\over2}                       \tag{6.13}
\]

and the upper bound in (6.12b) then give, once `2a c_2 c\geq2`,

\[
 \int_I|v_n(y)|^2\,dy\geq c_3\rho_n(y_0)^2                \tag{6.14}
\]

for every `y_0\in I_0`, with an absolute `c_3>0`.  Since
`\int_I |v_n|^2 \leq \int_{-1}^1 |u_n|^2=1`, this bounds
`\rho_n(y_0)^2`.  Finally
`|u_n(y_0)|^2=|v_n(y_0)|^2/(1-y_0^2)` and `1-y_0^2` is bounded below on
`I_0`; hence (6.6) follows with `\delta=a/2`.  \(\square\)

Define the defect-weighted central spectral function

\[
 K_\lambda(y)=\sum_{0\leq n<n_*}d_n|u_n(y)|^2.             \tag{6.15}
\]

For `n<S/2`, (6.4)--(6.5) give

\[
 \sum_{n<S/2}d_n|u_n(y)|^2
 \leq240c^2S\exp\left(-{S/2-7\over\beta_\lambda}\right)
 =o(1).                                                     \tag{6.16}
\]

For `S/2<=n<S`, the geometric sum in (6.5) is `O(beta_lambda)`, and
there are exactly `b_lambda` indices in the transition buffer
`S<=n<n_*`.  Lemma 6.1 therefore proves

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{|y|\leq\delta}K_\lambda(y)
 \leq C_4(\beta_\lambda+b_\lambda+1)\leq C_5L.}            \tag{6.17}
\]

For `m=1`, the concentration operator is diagonal in the prolate basis:

\[
 r_1=(I-K_1)f=\sum_{n\in I}d_na_nh_{n,\lambda}.             \tag{6.18}
\]

Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and (6.17) give, whenever `|x|/lambda<=delta`,

\[
 |r_1(x)|^2
 \leq D\sum_{n\in I}d_n|h_{n,\lambda}(x)|^2
 \leq {C_5L\over\lambda}D.                                \tag{6.19}
\]

Since `R/lambda=1/L<delta` eventually and

\[
 \int_{-R}^{R}\log{R\over|x|}\,dx=2R,                    \tag{6.20}
\]

the central first-cutoff penalty satisfies

\[
 \boxed{J_{R,1}\leq2C_5D.}                                \tag{6.21}
\]

The hard endpoint and its possible Weber transition are contained exactly
in (6.18); no endpoint localization of `log<D>` has been used.

## 7. Exact hard cutoffs `m>=2`

For one even prolate mode and `t>lambda`, the exterior equation is

\[
 ((t^2-\lambda^2)H_n')'+(k^2t^2-\mu_n)H_n=0,
 \qquad k=2\pi\lambda.                                     \tag{7.1}
\]

On `t>=2lambda`, put

\[
 p(t)=t^2-\lambda^2,\qquad w_n=\sqrt p\,H_n,                \tag{7.2}
\]

\[
 w_n''+\Omega_n^2w_n=0,\qquad
 \Omega_n^2=k^2+{c^2-\mu_n\over p}+{\lambda^2\over p^2}. \tag{7.3}
\]

The transition outer theorem gives

\[
 {k^2\over3}<\Omega_n^2<{3k^2\over2},\qquad
 \operatorname {Var}_{[2\lambda,\infty)}\log\Omega_n
 <\log(9/2),                                               \tag{7.4}
\]

and, with

\[
 w_n=r_n\sin\theta_n,\qquad w_n'=r_n\Omega_n\cos\theta_n,
                                                                    \tag{7.5}
\]

\[
 {r_n'\over r_n}=-{\Omega_n'\over\Omega_n}\cos^2\theta_n,
 \qquad {2A_n\over9}\leq r_n\leq{9A_n\over2},
 \qquad A_n={|h_{n,\lambda}(\lambda)|\over\pi}.           \tag{7.6}
\]

The endpoint estimate is

\[
 A_n^2\leq2592\lambda d_n.                                \tag{7.7}
\]

The phase derivative needs one additional elementary BV observation.  Set

\[
 u={t\over\lambda},\qquad
 \delta_n={c^2-\mu_n\over c^2}\in(-2,1],\qquad
 \varepsilon_\lambda={1\over4\pi^2\lambda^4},             \tag{7.8}
\]

\[
 W_n(u)={\Omega_n(\lambda u)^2\over k^2}
 =1+{\delta_n\over u^2-1}
   +{\varepsilon_\lambda\over(u^2-1)^2}.                   \tag{7.9}
\]

For `u>=2`, (7.4) bounds `W_n` away from zero and infinity.  Direct
differentiation of the rational function (7.9), uniformly for
`delta_n in (-2,1]`, gives

\[
 \int_2^\infty\left|
 {d\over du}\log W_n(u)\right|du\leq C_6,
 \qquad
 \int_2^\infty\left|
 {d^2\over du^2}\log W_n(u)\right|du\leq C_6.              \tag{7.10}
\]

Thus, for `q_n=Omega_n'/Omega_n`,

\[
 \int_{2\lambda}^\infty|q_n(t)|\,dt\leq C_6,\qquad
 \operatorname {Var}q_n\leq{C_6\over\lambda}.             \tag{7.11}
\]

Let

\[
 b_n(t)={r_n(t)\over\sqrt{t^2-\lambda^2}}.                 \tag{7.12}
\]

Equations (7.6), (7.11), and monotonicity of `p^(-1/2)` imply, for
`a=m lambda`, `m>=2`,

\[
 \|b_n\|_{L^\infty(a,\infty)}
 +\operatorname {Var}_{[a,\infty)}b_n
 \leq {C_7A_n\over m\lambda}.                              \tag{7.13}
\]

Moreover

\[
 \theta_n'=\Omega_n+q_n\sin\theta_n\cos\theta_n.          \tag{7.14}
\]

Equations (7.4), (7.10)--(7.11), and (7.14) give

\[
 \operatorname {Var}_{[a,\infty)}\theta_n'\leq C_8k.       \tag{7.15}
\]

Indeed, `Var Omega_n=O(k)`, while differentiating
`q_n sin(theta_n)cos(theta_n)` costs `Var q_n` plus
`O(k)\int|q_n|`.

For `|x|\leq R`, put `\omega=2\pi x`.  Then `|\omega|/k\leq1/L`, so for all
sufficiently large locked `lambda`,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \inf_{t\geq a}|\theta_n'(t)\pm\omega|
 &\geq {k\over\sqrt3}-{4\over3\lambda}-{k\over L}\\
 &\geq c_9k.
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{7.16}
\]

The first line uses `|q_n|\leq8/(3\lambda)` and
`|\sin\theta_n\cos\theta_n|\leq1/2`; the small Prüfer correction is
therefore retained rather than silently replacing `\theta_n'` by
`\Omega_n`.  From (7.15)--(7.16),

\[
 \left\|{1\over\Psi'}\right\|_\infty\leq{C\over k},\qquad
 \operatorname {Var}\left({1\over\Psi'}\right)\leq{C\over k},
 \qquad \Psi'=\theta_n'\pm\omega.                           \tag{7.17}
\]

Indeed, the variation on the left is at most
`Var(Psi')/(inf|Psi'|)^2=O(k)/k^2`.  Stieltjes integration by parts
with
`\Psi'=\theta_n'\pm\omega` gives

\[
 \left|\int_a^\infty b_n(t)e^{i\Psi(t)}\,dt\right|
 \leq2\left\|{b_n\over\Psi'}\right\|_\infty
      +\operatorname {Var}\left({b_n\over\Psi'}\right)
 \leq {C_{10}A_n\over m\lambda k}.                          \tag{7.18}
\]

Since

\[
 g_{m,n}(x)=2\int_{m\lambda}^\infty
 H_n(t)\cos(2\pi xt)\,dt,                                  \tag{7.19}
\]

expanding `\sin(\theta_n)\cos(\omega t)` into four exponentials and using
`k=2\pi\lambda` proves

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{|x|\leq R}|g_{m,n}(x)|
 \leq {C_{11}A_n\over m\lambda^2}.}                         \tag{7.20}
\]

This treats the exact exponential hard cutoff.  There is no commutator
between a spatial partition and `log<D>`.

For one same-phase residue class below `n_*`, the number of available labels
is at most `(3/2)lambda^2` for all sufficiently large locked `lambda`.
This includes the singleton row.  Indeed the count is at most
`ceil(n_*/4)`.  At `p=5` it is nine.  For odd `p>=7`,

\[
 b_\lambda
 \leq7+{2\log20\over\pi^2}\log(50p^2+25)
 \leq{p^2\over2}-3,                                        \tag{7.21}
\]

where the last difference is positive at seven and has positive derivative.
Since `p^2=1 mod 8`, this implies
`ceil(n_*/4)<=3p^2/8=(3/2)lambda^2`.
Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz, (7.7), and (7.20) therefore give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |g_m(x)|^2
 &\leq D\sum_{n\in I}{|g_{m,n}(x)|^2\over d_n}\\
 &\leq {C_{12}\over m^2\lambda}D,
 \qquad |x|\leq R,\quad m\geq2.                            \tag{7.22}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Using (6.20),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{m\geq2}{1\over m}
 \int_{|x|<R}\log{R\over|x|}|r_m(x)|^2\,dx
 \leq C_{13}{R\over\lambda}D
 ={C_{13}\over L}D.}                                       \tag{7.23}
\]

Here `r_m=g_m` on `T`, and `R<lambda`.

## 8. Completion and exact congruence statement

Equations (6.21) and (7.23) prove

\[
 J_R\leq C_{14}D.                                          \tag{8.1}
\]

Substitution into (5.6) proves (0.11).

The result is a genuine matrix inequality on the raw sampler-equality
subsystem.  If `C_I` is any coefficient synthesis into the admitted modal
set **whose range lies in the zero-integral hyperplane**, let
`Q_{\rm eq}` denote the Hermitian matrix obtained by polarizing (0.10) on
its columns.  The actual anchor-deflated LOW_BLOCK columns have this
property.  Then

\[
 D_{\rm full}=C_I^*\operatorname {diag}(d_n)C_I            \tag{8.2}
\]

is its exact full exterior Gram and

\[
 Q_{\rm eq}
 \succeq\left({L\over4}-{1\over4}\log L-C\right)D_{\rm full}.
                                                                    \tag{8.3}
\]

More explicitly, define the exterior-tail synthesis by

\[
 \Phi x=\phi_{C_Ix},                                      \tag{8.3a}

with `\phi` as in (0.6).  Polarization of (0.7) gives the exact isometry

 8\|\Phi x\|_2^2=x^*D_{\rm full}x.                       \tag{8.3b}

Thus (8.3) retains the full exterior metric; it does not replace it by a
diagonal or anchor-split approximation.

Let `eta(f)=f(lambda)` be the outgoing endpoint row and retain label `8` as

makes every rest column endpoint-null.  Applying (8.4) to the whole raw

## 9. Transfer to the locked origin-corrected sampler

This section proves (0.13), while retaining the exact locked bump rather
than changing radical representative.

For the plus phase `n=0 mod 4`, the zero-integral condition and the exact
prolate integral formula give

\[
 0=\sum_{n\in I}a_n\chi_nh_{n,\lambda}(0),\qquad
 A=f(0)=\sum_{n\in I}a_n(1-\chi_n)h_{n,\lambda}(0).         \tag{9.1}
\]

Since

\[
 {(1-\chi_n)^2\over d_n}
 ={1-\chi_n\over1+\chi_n}
 ={d_n\over(1+\chi_n)^2}\leq d_n,                          \tag{9.2}
\]

weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and the central kernel bound (6.17) imply

\[
 \boxed{|A|^2\leq {C L\over\lambda}D.}                     \tag{9.3}
\]

Put `b(x)=A\psi(2\lambda x)`, so
`\widetilde f=f-b`.  Its full Fourier norm is

\[
 \|{\cal F}_ab\|_2^2=\|b\|_2^2
 ={|A|^2\over2\lambda}\|\psi\|_2^2
 =O\!\left({L\over\lambda^2}D\right).                      \tag{9.4}
\]

If

\[
 \widetilde D
 =\int_{|v|\geq\lambda}|{\cal F}_a\widetilde f(v)|^2\,dv,
                                                                    \tag{9.5}
\]

the reverse triangle inequality in the exterior space gives

\[
 |\sqrt{\widetilde D}-\sqrt D|
 \leq\|{\cal F}_ab\|_2,
 \qquad
 \widetilde D
 =D\left(1+O\!\left({\sqrt L\over\lambda}\right)\right).   \tag{9.6}
\]

It remains to compare the central penalties.  Write
`\Psi={\cal F}_a\psi` and define the fixed scaled hard tails

\[
 G_m(s)={\cal F}_a^{-1}
       \left({\bf1}_{\{|\eta|\geq m/2\}}\Psi(\eta)\right)(s).
                                                                    \tag{9.7}
\]

Fourier scaling gives exactly

\[
 r_{m,b}(x)=A\,G_m(2\lambda x).                             \tag{9.8}
\]

This is the exact locked source correction (3.2) of the frozen radical
note, not a newly chosen representative.  The sampler formed from
`{\cal F}_a\widetilde f` is therefore the actual corrected sampler.  Its
Poisson formula has no separate beta term because `\widetilde f(0)=0`.
No identity between the Fourier tail of the bump and `\beta_\lambda` is
asserted.

Because `\psi` is fixed, smooth, and compactly supported, `\Psi` is
Schwartz.  Plancherel and the elementary Fourier `L^1` bound give the
finite constant

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_\psi={}&\sum_{m\geq1}{1\over m}\left[
  \|G_m\|_2^2+
  \int_{\mathbb R}\log_+{1\over|s|}|G_m(s)|^2\,ds\right]
 <\infty .                                                  \tag{9.9}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, Tonelli rewrites the first sum with the integrable Schwartz weight
`H_{\lfloor2|\eta|\rfloor}|\Psi(\eta)|^2`.  For the second sum,

\[
 \|G_m\|_\infty
 \leq\int_{|\eta|\geq m/2}|\Psi(\eta)|\,d\eta=O_N(m^{-N}),
                                                                    \tag{9.9a}
\]

and `\int_{|s|<1}\log(1/|s|)\,ds=2`; hence that sum converges as well.

Equations (9.8)--(9.9) and
`\log(Y/|s|)\leq\log Y+\log_+(1/|s|)` on `|s|<Y` give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_R(b)
 &={|A|^2\over2\lambda}\sum_{m\geq1}{1\over m}
   \int_{|s|<2\lambda R}
       \log{2\lambda R\over|s|}|G_m(s)|^2\,ds\\
 &\leq {C_\psi|A|^2\over2\lambda}
       \{1+\log(2\lambda R)\}\\
 &=O\!\left({L^2\over\lambda^2}D\right)=o(D).              \tag{9.10}
 \end{aligned}
\]

The direct sum over `m`, with measure
`m^{-1}\log(R/|x|)\,dx`, is a Hilbert space.  Its triangle inequality,
(8.1), and (9.10) yield

\[
 \sqrt{J_R(\widetilde f)}
 \leq\sqrt{J_R(f)}+\sqrt{J_R(b)},\qquad
 J_R(\widetilde f)=O(D).                                   \tag{9.11}
\]

The exact conductor identity (3.16) and central split (5.6) apply to
`\widetilde f` without change.  Substituting (9.6) and (9.11) gives

\[
 \widetilde{\mathfrak E}_\lambda(\widetilde f)
 \geq\left({L\over4}-{1\over4}\log L-C\right)D,
                                                                    \tag{9.12}
\]

because `L^{3/2}/\lambda=o(1)`.  This proves (0.13).  The correction
has removed the Poisson beta term, but it has not supplied any of the
non-equality pieces excluded in Section 0.
