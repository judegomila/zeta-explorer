## Generic obstruction and form-domain qualification

For scalar D=epsilon>0 and x=0, b=sqrt(epsilon) has Euclidean residual tending to0 but energy correction r*D^-1r=1. Choosing b=epsilon^(1/4) gives residual tending to0 and correction epsilon^-1/2 diverging. Therefore a small unweighted residual alone cannot certify a Schur reserve; an actual lower metric or dual-energy residual bound is needed. This is generic linear algebra, not a theta counterexample.

For an unbounded-operator formulation, assume D is densely defined nonnegative self-adjoint, or start from a densely defined closed nonnegative quadratic form and its associated operator. Let b be in the Hilbert space. The finite variational supremum over X in dom(D^1/2) of2 Re<b,X>-||D^1/2 X||^2 occurs exactly when b is in ran(D^1/2); then it equals ||D^-1/2 b||^2. The inverse is the spectral inverse on ker(D)^perp, with the unique orthogonal representative. The exact completion residual is D^-1/2 b-D^1/2 X. No DX need exist for a form-domain trial X; writing b-DX instead would require the stronger X in dom D and appropriate residual inverse domain.

A nonzero kernel component in b makes the supremum infinite. That is not the only obstruction: b orthogonal to the kernel but outside ran(D^1/2) also yields infinite supremum through low-spectrum sequences. Finite supremum need not be attained when the range is not closed; attainment requires b in ran D (up to arbitrary kernel components of the optimizer). The finite positive-definite3x3 computation has none of these domain ambiguities.
