## 1. Why the eighth-power norm proof is valid

On the actual logarithmic support interval, the prime operator is a finite sum of paired compressed translations with positive weights log(p)/sqrt(n). It is bounded and selfadjoint; positivity as a quadratic form is not assumed. Its eighth power has a nonnegative finite path kernel. Exact Fraction states (q,lo,hi) preserve every partial-path support intersection, and merging identical states adds their positive interval weights. Zero-length intersections are irrelevant to the L2 operator.

With positive Schur weight cosh(2(x-W/2)/3), the row ratio on each interval between path endpoints is A+B tanh(2(x-W/2)/3). Its maximum occurs at an endpoint limit. The enumerator evaluates both limits using outward Arb arithmetic, then takes the maximum of exact upper endpoints. Weighted Schur bounds ||P^8|| by that row bound. Selfadjoint spectral mapping gives ||P^8||=||P||^8, so the positive eighth root bounds the entire prime norm. This remains valid whether P has negative spectrum. The source differs from the previously audited seventh-power enumerator only in the number of steps, root and corresponding output names; it does not mistakenly retain a seventh root. The local variable name `fourth` is cosmetic.

All exact-X phase data and active prime powers remain those at X=161/10. There is no support change, no new prime activation, and no old-X16001/1000 moment substitution. The scalar norm receipt is checked finite and strictly below the chosen exact envelope, with a margin far larger than its serialization radius.
