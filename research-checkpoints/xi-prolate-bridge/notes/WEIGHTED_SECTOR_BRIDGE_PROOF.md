# Draft: resolvent-weighted inversion-sector bridge

This note sharpens only the finite-dimensional eigenvector estimate in the
inversion-sector bridge. It is a conditional linear-algebra statement. It
does not establish the needed inequalities on an unbounded schedule and does
not constitute a proof of RH.

## Theorem

Let `H` be a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, with inner product
conjugate-linear in the first argument. Let

\[
 J=J^*=J^{-1},\qquad Q=Q^*,\qquad QJ=JQ,
\]

and write `H=H_+ direct-sum H_-`, where `H_+=ker(J-I)` and
`H_-=ker(J+I)`. Fix a unit vector `kappa in H_+` and put

\[
 q=\langle\kappa,Q\kappa\rangle,\qquad
 b=(Q-qI)\kappa,\qquad r=\|b\|,
\]

and `K=H_+ intersect kappa^perp`. Relative to
`H_+=C kappa direct-sum K`, write

\[
 Q_+=\begin{bmatrix}q&b^*\\ b&A\end{bmatrix}.       \tag{W1}
\]

Assume `K` is nonzero and define

\[
 \alpha=\min\sigma(A),\qquad
 \omega=\min\sigma(Q|_{H_-}),                       \tag{W2}
\]

with `omega=+infinity` if `H_-={0}`. When `r>0`, set

\[
 w=b/r,\qquad c=\langle w,Qw\rangle,
\]

and let

\[
 u=\frac{q+c-\sqrt{(c-q)^2+4r^2}}2.                \tag{W3}
\]

When `r=0`, set `u=q`. Suppose

\[
 u<\alpha,\qquad u<\omega.                         \tag{W4}
\]

Then `Q` has a unique global ground eigenspace, it is inversion even, and its
unit vector `xi` has a unique phase for which
`a=<kappa,xi>` is positive real. If

\[
 \tau=\left\|(A-uI)^{-1}b\right\|,                 \tag{W5}
\]

then

\[
 \|\xi-\kappa\|
 \le D(\tau)
 :=\sqrt{2-\frac{2}{\sqrt{1+\tau^2}}}
 \le\tau
 \le\frac{r}{\alpha-u}.                            \tag{W6}
\]

Thus the resolvent-weighted quantity `tau`, rather than the uniform operator
norm estimate `r/(alpha-u)`, is a valid finite eigenvector-error bound. It can
be much smaller when `b` lies mainly in high-energy spectral directions of
`A`.

If `delta` is a bounded linear functional, either of the optional conditions

\[
 |\delta(\kappa)|>\|\delta\|D(\tau)                \tag{W7a}
\]

or, writing `delta_K` for its restriction to `K`,

\[
 |\delta(\kappa)|>\|\delta_K\|\,\tau               \tag{W7b}
\]

implies `delta(xi) != 0`. Under the Riesz identification of a functional with
a vector `d`, one has `||delta_K||=||P_K d||`. Condition (W7b) is sharper
than the older tau-based condition obtained by replacing `||delta_K||` with
`||delta||`; (W7a), which uses the smaller chordal bound, is a separate
sufficient test, and neither (W7a) nor (W7b) uniformly dominates the other.
For the exact Weil matrices both remain only independent sufficient checks:
the source commutator argument from the simple-even ground-state hypotheses
is the primary boundary-normalization route.

If `K={0}`, then `b=0`, the even sector is `C kappa`, and no resolvent is
needed. In that degenerate case `omega>q` makes `kappa` the unique global
ground vector, with zero eigenvector error.

## Proof

The commutation relation makes the even and odd sectors reducing subspaces
for `Q`. Moreover, `b in H_+` and

\[
 \langle\kappa,b\rangle
 =\langle\kappa,Q\kappa\rangle-q=0,
\]

so `b in K` and (W1) is the correct Hermitian block representation, including
when its entries are complex.

Let

\[
 \mu=\min\sigma(Q_+).
\]

For `r>0`, the compression of `Q_+` to the orthonormal pair
`{kappa,w}` is

\[
 \begin{bmatrix}q&r\\r&c\end{bmatrix}.             \tag{W8}
\]

Indeed, Hermiticity and `w=b/r` give
`<kappa,Qw>=<Qkappa,w>=<b,w>=r`, a positive real number; no real-vector
assumption is being made. The lower eigenvalue of (W8) is `u`, hence
Rayleigh--Ritz gives `mu<=u`. If `r=0`, the matrix in (W1) is block diagonal,
`kappa` is a `q`-eigenvector, and (W4) gives `mu=q=u`.

Cauchy interlacing between `Q_+` and its codimension-one compression `A`
gives

\[
 \lambda_2(Q_+)\ge\lambda_1(A)=\alpha>u\ge\mu.    \tag{W9}
\]

Thus the even ground eigenvalue is simple. Write its unit vector as

\[
 \xi=a\kappa+v,\qquad v\in K,
\]

and multiply by a unit complex scalar so that `a>0`. The coefficient `a`
cannot vanish: otherwise the Rayleigh quotient of `xi in K` would be at
least `alpha>mu`. The complementary block of the eigenvalue equation is

\[
 (A-\mu I)v=-ab.                                   \tag{W10}
\]

Since `mu<=u<alpha`, both resolvents in

\[
 t:=\frac{\|v\|}{a}
   =\left\|(A-\mu I)^{-1}b\right\|                 \tag{W11}
\]

are well defined. The comparison `t<=tau` needs a little care. It does not
follow merely from a generic implication of the form `0<=X<=Y` implying
`||Xb||<=||Yb||`, which is false without additional structure. Here the two
resolvents are commuting scalar functions of the same Hermitian operator
`A`. If `nu_b` is the spectral measure of `A` generated by `b`, then

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 t^2
 &=\int_{[\alpha,\infty)}\frac{1}{(x-\mu)^2}
       \,d\nu_b(x)\\
 &\le\int_{[\alpha,\infty)}\frac{1}{(x-u)^2}
       \,d\nu_b(x)
 =\tau^2,                                           \tag{W12}
 \end{aligned}
\]

because `mu<=u<alpha<=x`. The same formula gives

\[
 \tau^2\le\frac{1}{(\alpha-u)^2}
              \int d\nu_b
          =\frac{r^2}{(\alpha-u)^2}.               \tag{W13}
\]

This proves the last two inequalities in (W6).

Normalization of `xi` and (W11) give

\[
 a=(1+t^2)^{-1/2},\qquad
 \|\xi-\kappa\|^2=2-2a.
\]

The function `D(s)=sqrt(2-2/sqrt(1+s^2))` is increasing for `s>=0`, so
`||xi-kappa||=D(t)<=D(tau)`. Also `D(s)<=s`: on putting
`y=sqrt(1+s^2)>=1`, the squared difference is

\[
 s^2-D(s)^2
 =\frac{(y-1)^2(y+2)}{y}\ge0.                     \tag{W14}
\]

This proves the eigenvector estimate in (W6), including its chordal form.

Every odd eigenvalue is at least `omega>u>=mu`; together with (W9), this
proves that the simple even ground vector is the unique global ground vector.
Finally, (W10)--(W12) imply `||v||<=a tau`, and hence

\[
 |\delta(\xi)|
 \ge a|\delta(\kappa)|-|\delta(v)|
 \ge a\bigl(|\delta(\kappa)|-\|\delta_K\|\tau\bigr)>0
\]

under (W7b). Alternatively, (W7a) follows immediately from

\[
 |\delta(\xi)|\ge |\delta(\kappa)|
                  -\|\delta\|\,\|\xi-\kappa\|.
\]

This completes the proof.

In the exact-zero branch `b=0`, one has `r=0`, `u=q`, `tau=0`, and (W1) is
block diagonal. Conditions (W4) put both the rest of the even spectrum and
the entire odd spectrum strictly above `q`, so all conclusions follow with
`xi=kappa`. A numerical interval merely containing zero is not enough to
invoke this branch; `b=0` must be established exactly or by a zero-radius
certificate.

## Fail-closed interval certificate

Point evaluations of (W5) are diagnostics. A finite rigorous certificate can
use the following outward-rounded data.

1. Certify the Hermitian sector decomposition and the exact unit/even
   hypotheses for `kappa`. Obtain lower bounds
   `alpha_L<=alpha` and `omega_L<=omega` for the complete even-complement and
   odd-sector compression floors.
2. Obtain a rigorous scalar upper bound `U>=u` for the actual lower Ritz
   value, not just a rounded display value. Require the two strict
   inequalities

   \[
   g_+:=\alpha_L-U>0,\qquad g_-:=\omega_L-U>0.      \tag{W15}
   \]

   They imply `mu<=u<=U<min(alpha,omega)`.
3. Bound the shifted solve at the conservative shift `U`. Since the
   resolvents are again ordered through the spectral calculus,

   \[
   \tau
   \le\left\|(A-U I)^{-1}b\right\|.                \tag{W16}
   \]

   A verified linear solve may enclose this vector directly. Equivalently,
   for any approximate vector `z_0`, certify outward-rounded bounds

   \[
   Z_0\ge\|z_0\|,\qquad
   \rho_U\ge\|b-(A-U I)z_0\|.
   \]

   Positivity in (W15) then proves

   \[
   \tau\le T_U:=Z_0+\frac{\rho_U}{g_+}.             \tag{W17}
   \]

   This residual correction is an independently checkable inclusion proof;
   a floating-point inverse without such an enclosure is not sufficient.
   Choosing `z_0=0` recovers the uniform fallback
   `T_U=r_U/g_+` from any certified upper bound `r_U>=r`.

The certified phase-aligned distance bound is `D(T_U)`, with outward rounding
through the monotone function `D`; the simpler bound `T_U` is also valid. For
small arguments the cancellation-free identity
`D(s)=sqrt(2)*s/sqrt(y*(y+1))`, `y=sqrt(1+s^2)`, should be used.
The two optional boundary reserves are certified respectively by
`|delta(kappa)|_L-||delta||_U D(T_U)>0` and
`|delta(kappa)|_L-||delta_K||_U T_U>0`. All endpoints used in strict
comparisons must be directed-rounding bounds. A sampled eigenvalue, an
unverified linear solve, or a binary64 reconstruction of decimal source
parameters does not establish these conditions.

If the interval for `r` includes zero but does not certify `b=0`, the exact
zero branch must not be selected. One may instead use any separately
certified variational upper bound `U>=mu` (for example an upper bound on the
Rayleigh quotient `q`) in (W15)--(W17), or leave the weighted certificate
unresolved.

For auditability, the exact diagnostic `tau`, the certified upper bound
`T_U`, the uniform comparison `r/(alpha-u)`, the two shifted gaps, the solve
residual `rho_U`, and the projected functional norm `||delta_K||` should be
serialized separately. A finite weighted certificate must not overwrite the
signed legacy gaps or turn an uncertified asymptotic estimate into a theorem.

## Conditional asymptotic replacement of equation (5.2)

For a diagonal sequence in Section 5 of `DIRECT_RH_ATTACK.md`, let

\[
 e_j=\|P_{N_j}k^+_{\lambda_j}-k^+_{\lambda_j}\|,
\]

and suppose the two strict sector comparisons are rigorously certified at
each index. Let `T_j` be any proved upper bound for the weighted quantity
`tau_j` obtained from (W16)--(W17), and put `d_j=D(T_j)`. The normalization
identity and (W6) give

\[
 \left\|\|P_{N_j}k^+_{\lambda_j}\|\,\xi_j
             -k^+_{\lambda_j}\right\|
 \le e_j+\|P_{N_j}k^+_{\lambda_j}\|d_j.            \tag{W18}
\]

Consequently the resolvent-weighted replacement for (5.2) is

\[
 \sqrt{\log\lambda_j}\,\lambda_j^\eta
 \left(e_j+\|P_{N_j}k^+_{\lambda_j}\|d_j\right)
 \longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\eta<1/2).                                 \tag{W19}
\]

Because `d_j<=T_j`, the more conservative condition obtained by replacing
`d_j` with `T_j` is also sufficient. Using the exact diagnostic `tau_j` in a
finite table is not an asymptotic proof; (W19) still requires analytic or
certified uniform control on an explicit unbounded sequence, together with
the other hypotheses of the main bridge.
