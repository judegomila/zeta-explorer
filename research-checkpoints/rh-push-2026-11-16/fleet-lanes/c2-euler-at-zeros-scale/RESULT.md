## Question

For S = {p <= P}, zeta_S(s) = prod_(p<=P) (1 - p^-s)^-1 and A_S(T,H) = sum over zeros gamma in [T, T+H] of |zeta_S(1/2 + i gamma)|^2 w(gamma): how does A_S grow in P and T against (i) N_w M_P, M_P = prod_(p<=P)(1 - 1/p)^-1 (N_w = number of zeros in the window, equal to H/(2 pi) log(T/2 pi) to O(log T)), and (ii) the same sum at shifted points gamma + delta. Is the ratio one-sidedly bounded, uniformly in height, in every window, and does any control share that.

## Answer (one statement)

MEASURED: the zero-sampled ratio against reference (i) is below 1 in every window measured (max 0.904 over 500-zero windows, 0.782 over 2000-zero windows, heights 1e3 to 1e8, P = 10 to 1e5), but the bound is not height-independent at fixed P: the ratio is a function of x = (log P - gamma_E)/log(T/2 pi) only, equal to 1 - 2x for x <= 0.25, and it tends to 1 as T grows with P fixed. Against reference (ii) with shifts inside one mean spacing the ratio exceeds 1 in some windows (max 1.172). The controls share the suppression: the Davenport-Heilbronn formal partial product at DH zeros (on-line and off-line), and the Liouville twist at its own zeros rho/2, are suppressed by the same law in the mean. The lead is not raised to deepen status.

## Law fitted (zeta)

Landau-Gonek (CLASSICAL, unconditional for fixed x): sum_(gamma<=T) x^(i gamma) has main term -(T/2 pi) Lambda(x)/sqrt(x). Expanding |zeta_S|^2 over S-smooth m, n and keeping n/m a prime power gives

  A0/(N_w M_P) = 1 - 2 c_P / L + (lower order), c_P = sum_(p<=P) log p/(p-1) = log P - gamma_E + o(1), L = log(T/2 pi).

The same computation for points gamma + delta averaged over delta in [0, 2 pi/L] gives U/(N_w M_P) = 1 - (2/L) sum_(p<=P, k) (log p / p^k) sin(u)/u, u = 2 pi k log p / L, which tends to 1/2 for log P >> L. So reference (ii) with shifts inside one spacing is itself suppressed (it weights t by the number of zeros in the preceding spacing); it is not an independent reference. The continuous t-mean is the independent one.

MEASURED (Table 1):
- x <= 0.26 (6 points): |Rref - (1 - 2 c_P/L)| <= 0.014; at P = 10 the agreement is 0.0003 to 0.003 for T0 >= 1e5.
- All 30 (T0, P) points collapse on one curve in x within about 15 percent. x >= 0.6 (15 points): Rref = 0.0220 x^(-2.87), max log-residual 0.11; Rcont = 0.034 x^(-2.0). The first-order formula is negative there and does not apply (the Landau-Gonek error terms dominate once P exceeds about T^(1/2) in these window lengths).
- The exponents -3 and -2 are those of the heuristic |zeta_S(rho)| ~ |zeta'(rho)|/(e^gamma_E log P) for log P > L (Gonek-Hughes-Keating splitting) with mean |zeta'(rho)|^2 ~ L^3/12 and mean |zeta(1/2+it)|^2 ~ L: constants predicted 1/(12 e^(3 gamma_E)) = 0.0148 and 1/(12 e^(2 gamma_E)) = 0.0263 against fitted 0.022 and 0.034. HEURISTIC, constants not matched.
- U/(N_w M_P) follows the sinc formula within 0.035 for x <= 0.42 and falls below it for larger x.

## Verdict

Dead as a discriminator. The suppression of |zeta_S|^2 at zeros is the diagonal term of the Landau-Gonek explicit formula: it is a statement that a truncation of a Dirichlet series is small at that series' zeros, with first-order size fixed by the coefficients of -F'/F on prime powers. It holds for the DH combination, which has no Euler product, at on-line and off-line zeros alike, so it carries no information about zero location. At fixed P it tends to 1 with height; in the scaled variable x it is a height-independent curve, but that curve is shared.

Pivot (one): the large-P regime x > 0.6, where the first-order formula fails, was checked for a zeta-specific law. Result: power law x^-3 consistent with the |zeta'(rho)|/log P heuristic; DH on-line zeros follow the same shape within a factor 2. No separation.

## Requirement flags

- R1 (multiplicativity essential): false. DH's formal product (not an Euler product) shows the same effect; the first-order term uses only the prime-power coefficients of -F'/F.
- R2 (height-uniform, every window): false. Rref < 1 in every window measured (interval-certified in 6 windows), but the bound at fixed P decays as 2(log P - gamma_E)/log(T/2 pi); RU exceeds 1 in 500-zero windows at T0 >= 1e5.
- R3 (fails for every control for a structural reason): false. DH and Liouville-at-rho/2 share it; only Liouville-at-rho (opposite sign) and random twists (no log P growth) separate.
- R4 (reaches the critical line): false. Finite sums over computed zeros; no statement about zero location.

## Three weakest steps

1. DH zeros are float64 with an argument-principle count, not interval-certified; the DH tables are a screen. The DH reference M^DH_P is the mean-square of the formal series, compared with zeta only in shape, not through a derived DH formula.
2. The collapse in x and the x^-2.87 fit rest on 30 points with window lengths H between 2181 and 12905; for P > H the short-window mean of |zeta_S|^2 is itself below M_P (column 10 of Table 1 falls to 0.35), so Rref at large x mixes the zero effect with the window effect. Rcont separates them and has a different exponent.
3. Heavy tails: for the Liouville twist at sigma = 1/4 and for random twists at P >= 1e3 the second-moment ratios are dominated by few points (window sd up to 1); statements for those cells rest on the log statistic G and on the P = 10, 100 cells.
