# RH attempt, round 3 (1 November 2026): a non-equivariant Heegner-height dictionary for ζ_K = ζ·L(·, χ_D), and the line where it fails

**Outcome: the attempt fails. No proof of RH.**

Internal working note. Not externally refereed, nothing committed. Round-1/2 context: `2026-11-01-synthesis.md` and
`2026-11-01-r2-synthesis.md`. The direct parent is the round-2 modular-tower note `2026-11-01-r2-modular-tower.md`. Its
§10 item 2 left "non-equivariant maps built from Heegner points" uncovered, and the round-2 synthesis (§4, item 3)
proposed this attempt.

**What the attempt does.** Imaginary quadratic CM points carry ζ_K = ζ·L(·, χ_D) in two places. Hecke's toric formula
puts ζ_K(s)/ζ(2s) into the Eisenstein (continuous-spectrum) part of every Green function evaluated on CM divisors.
Kronecker's limit formula puts γ_K, the constant term of ζ_K′/ζ_K at s = 1, into their regularized heights. Gross and
Zagier write the Néron–Tate height of a Heegner divisor as a sum of such archimedean local heights and non-archimedean
ones, and Néron–Tate positivity is a theorem. The attempt (§3) builds Heegner combinations x_F from a test function F by
a rule that is not Hecke-equivariant, so the round-2 transcendence obstruction (Theorem 4.13 there) does not apply. From
ĥ(x_F) ≥ 0 it derives a family of inequalities in which ζ enters through its values on the critical line (Proposition
H4; valid). Step H5 then claims that this family forces Weil positivity for ζ_K, or the Hardy-space form of the
Nyman–Beurling criterion for ζ.

## The line where it fails

§3, **Step H5 (transfer)**. Neither branch holds.

1. **The global height retains no information about the zeros of ζ_K (Theorems C and D, §4).** On every Heegner span
   the Néron–Tate form is diagonal in the (f, ψ) eigenbasis, with weights proportional to L′(½, f_K ⊗ ψ) (multiplicity
   one plus Gross–Zagier). So ĥ depends only on cuspidal data. The continuous-spectrum ζ_K-content of the archimedean
   local height is therefore offset exactly by the finite, Maass and constant terms (Theorem C). The term-by-term
   spectral form of this cancellation, with the height series m ↦ ⟨c, T_m d⟩ a holomorphic cusp form, is argued [A]. The inequalities that
   positivity supplies form exactly the cone generated by {L′(½, f_K ⊗ ψ) ≥ 0}. These are central derivatives of
   cusp-form L-functions, each a statement at the single point s = ½: the completed function has no real zero to the
   right of the centre. ζ is the Eisenstein constituent and does not occur.
2. **What enters from ζ_K′/ζ_K is one Li coefficient (Theorem B).** By Kronecker's limit formula and Hecke's formula,
   for every fundamental D < 0,
   (1/h)Σ_τ log(√(Im τ)·|η(τ)|²) = A − ½λ₁(ζ_K), where A is independent of D and
   λ₁(ζ_K) = Σ_ρ 1/ρ = 1 + ½log|D| − log 2π + L′/L(1, χ_D).
   The ζ-part is the fixed number λ₁(ζ) = 1 + γ/2 − ½log 4π = 0.0230957…, and λ₁ > 0 holds for every configuration of
   zeros in the open strip, since Re(1/ρ) > 0 there. Li's criterion needs λ_n for every n; only λ₁ is a height. Checked
   numerically for 14 discriminants (§6.1).
3. **The inequalities point the wrong way (Theorem E).** In the arithmetic intersection form the archimedean Dirichlet
   energy enters with a negative sign. So every Hodge-index inequality is a lower bound on a Green energy, i.e. on the
   nonnegative functional ∫ρ_ζ(t)|B_x(½+it)|²dμ(t), where ρ_ζ(t) = |ζ(½+it)|²/|ζ(1+2it)|² and
   dμ = dt/(2π(¼+t²)). Adding zeros in Re s > ½ by Z ↦ Z·B/B(1), with B a finite Blaschke product, multiplies ρ_ζ by
   1/|B(1+2it)|² ≥ 1 at every t. It moves the interior constants by O(δ/γ₀²). So the family H4 never excludes off-line
   zeros. The RH-equivalent statements that use the same weight dμ as the Arakelov Green function are upper bounds:
   Balazard–Saias–Yor, E_μ log|ζ(½+it)| = Σ_{β>½} log|ρ/(1−ρ)|, and its Hardy-space Nyman–Beurling form,
   inf_A E_μ|Z·A|² = Π_{β>½}|ρ/(1−ρ)|². With the functional equation imposed (a Lehmer pair displaced off the line),
   ρ_ζ rises by factors 10⁴–10⁸ near the displaced ordinate γ̄. It falls by at most 0.2%, 4.3% and 13% (δ = 0.05, 0.25,
   0.45) in a unit window near γ̄/2. That window is where 1 + 2it reaches height γ̄, so the fall is an edge effect of
   size about δ² (§4.5).
4. **The Hardy branch is invalid as a deduction (Lemma 4.7).** The H4 identities for torsion Heegner combinations
   (ĥ = 0) do give upper bounds. But Heegner and Hecke amplitudes grow like (√|D|/2)^s and m^{Re s − ½} along the real
   axis. So they are not in the Smirnov class of Re s > ½, and the Hardy-space criterion (T5) does not apply to them.
   For example Z(s)e^{a(s−1)} has value 1 at s = 1 and E_μ-norm² = e^{−a}(log 2π − γ) < 1. In any case this branch does
   not use positivity.

So the attempt stops at representability, as the tower did, but for a different reason. In the tower the map into the
cuspidal part had to be built from equivariant data, and it could see only the poles. Here the map is free and the ζ_K
data are present in the local heights, but they cancel out of the only quantity that positivity controls.

## Decisions requested by the task

* **First test (round-2 synthesis §4 item 3): does the archimedean Gross–Zagier term see the zeros of ζ with a sign?**
  No. ζ_K′/ζ_K enters the regularized archimedean heights only through its constant term at s = 1, equivalently through
  λ₁(ζ_K) (Theorem B). ζ_K(½+it) enters the continuous spectrum only as |ζ_K(½+it)|²/|ζ(1+2it)|², with no sign, and
  that term cancels in the global height (Theorem C).
* **Davenport–Heilbronn excluded explicitly.** The DH function is not ζ_K for any K and has no Heegner theory. Step H1
  (Hecke's formula, Σ_Q Z_Q = w·ζ_K) uses unique factorization of ideals in O_K, which DH lacks. That exclusion does not
  help, because the step that has to carry RH (H5) cannot tell ζ from a twin with off-line zeros. The half-plane twin
  satisfies every H4 inequality (Theorem E). The Heegner amplitude span itself contains DH-type brackets. The amplitude
  of τ₋₇ − τ₋₈ is ζ(s)/ζ(2s) times (√7/2)^s L(s, χ₋₇) − √2^s L(s, χ₋₈). That bracket equals an elementary factor times
  𝔇(s) = Λ(s, χ₋₇)/√7 − Λ(s, χ₋₈)/√8, a Davenport–Heilbronn-type function with 𝔇(s) = 𝔇(1−s). It vanishes at
  s = 1.92574583 + 7.35195194i (Proposition F, §6.4). Positivity and the height identities hold uniformly on the span.
* **Wall 3 (finite support).** Each generator of the positivity cone is a central-derivative statement for one
  L-function, at height 0. The Eisenstein constant is λ₁, one fixed low-height Weil functional. Moving the evaluation
  point to height T needs the twist |N(·)|^{iT}, which is not an algebraic Hecke character for T ≠ 0; algebraic
  anticyclotomic twists change the L-function instead (Proposition G). In verified-height terms:
  * the cuspidal generators are height-0 statements about cusp forms;
  * the ζ_K constant is λ₁, which is > 0 for any zeros in the strip;
  * the remaining ζ-content (H4) consists of lower bounds that adding off-line zeros cannot violate (Theorem E), apart
    from an O(δ²) edge effect once the functional equation is imposed (Prop. E′).

RH is open. Platt–Trudgian Λ ≤ 0.2 stands. No Λ bound and no zero-free region changes. NO QED.

### 1.1 Notation

* D < 0 is a fundamental discriminant, K = Q(√D), χ_D = (D/·), h = h(D), w = w_D ∈ {2, 4, 6}, u = w/2.
* ζ_K(s) = ζ(s)L(s, χ_D). Λ_K(s) = (√|D|/2π)^s Γ(s) ζ_K(s), and ξ_K(s) = s(s−1)Λ_K(s) is entire of order 1 with
  ξ_K(s) = ξ_K(1−s). Its zeros are the nontrivial zeros of ζ together with those of L(s, χ_D).
* Λ_K(n) := Σ_{N𝔞 = n} Λ(𝔞): Λ_K(p^k) = 2 log p if p splits; = log p if p ramifies; = 2 log p for even k and 0 for
  odd k if p is inert.
* μ := dt/(2π(¼ + t²)) on R. It is a probability measure: the harmonic measure of the half-plane Re s > ½ seen from
  s = 1.
* Z(s) := ((s−1)/s)·ζ(s). Then Z(1) = 1, and |Z(½+it)| = |ζ(½+it)| because |(s−1)/s| = 1 on Re s = ½.
* ρ_ζ(t) := |ζ(½+it)|²/|ζ(1+2it)|², the "Heegner density" (§2.6).

### 1.2 Target statements

* **(T1) [P]** GRH(ζ_K) ⟹ RH, because every nontrivial zero of ζ is a zero of ξ_K (L(s, χ_D) is entire).

* **(T3) [L: Li 1997; Bombieri–Lagarias 1999, Thm 1]** GRH(ζ_K) ⟺ λ_n(ζ_K) := Σ_ρ [1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n] ≥ 0 for every

## 2. Objects and the facts used

### 2.1 Heegner points [L: Gross–Zagier 1986, §I]

Fix N ≥ 1 and D < −4 such that every p | N splits in K (the Heegner condition; N = 1 is allowed). Fix 𝔫 ⊂ O_K with
O_K/𝔫 ≅ Z/N. For each class [𝔞] ∈ Cl(K) let x_𝔞 ∈ X₀(N)(H) be the Heegner point (C/𝔞 → C/𝔞𝔫^{−1}), where H is the
Hilbert class field. Gal(H/K) ≅ Cl(K) permutes the x_𝔞 simply transitively (Shimura reciprocity). Put
y_𝔞 := (x_𝔞) − (∞) ∈ J₀(N)(H). On X(1) these are the h points τ_Q = (−b + i√|D|)/(2a), Q = [a, b, c] reduced of
discriminant D.

### 2.2 Néron–Tate height and Hecke operators [L]

ĥ is a positive definite quadratic form on J₀(N)(Q̄) ⊗ R. The T_m with (m, N) = 1 are self-adjoint for ⟨·,·⟩_NT.
J₀(N) is isogenous to Π_f A_f^{m_f} over the newforms f of level dividing N; e_f denotes the f-isotypic projector.

### 2.3 Gross–Zagier, local heights, Gross–Kohnen–Zagier [L]

* **GZ formula** [Gross–Zagier 1986, Thm I.6.3]. For f a newform of weight 2 and level N, ψ ∈ Cl(K)^∧,
  c_ψ := Σ_σ ψ̄(σ) y^σ and c_{ψ,f} := e_f c_ψ:
  L′(f, ψ, 1) = (8π²(f, f)/(h u² √|D|))·ĥ(c_{ψ,f}).
  Here L(f, ψ, s) is the Rankin product of f with the weight-1 theta series θ_ψ, i.e. L(f_K ⊗ ψ, s), with centre s = 1
  in this normalization (s = ½ analytically). Under the Heegner condition its sign is −1.
* **Local decomposition** [GZ §§II–IV]. ⟨c, T_m d⟩ = Σ_v ⟨c, T_m d⟩_v.
  * The archimedean term is a regularized sum of Green-function values of X₀(N)(C) at s → 1 over the T_m-translates.
  * The term at p is minus a local intersection multiplicity on the regular model, times log p.
  * In the assembled finite part, log p occurs only for p not split in K. The divisor function in GZ's formula is
    σ′(n) = Σ_{d|n} ε(d) log(n/d²), and its local factor vanishes at split p: Σ_{j=0}^{k}(k − 2j) = 0.
  * The singular-moduli data of §6.2 involve only primes non-split in both fields [E].
* **GKZ** [Gross–Kohnen–Zagier 1987]. For degree-0 Heegner classes c, d, the numbers m ↦ ⟨c, T_m d⟩ are the
  coefficients of a cusp form in S₂(Γ₀(N)). As D varies, the traced f-components lie on one line of A_f(Q) ⊗ R, with
  coordinates given by Fourier coefficients of a Jacobi form.

### 2.4 Faltings–Hriljac and the sign of archimedean classes [L: Faltings 1984; Hriljac 1985; Gillet–Soulé; Moriwaki 1996]

* For x of degree 0 with admissible Green current and vertical correction Φ(x): ĥ(x) = −(x̂ + Φ(x))².
* In Ĉh¹(𝒳)_R the purely archimedean classes a(φ) = (0, φ) satisfy (a(φ)·a(φ)) = −c_A ∫|∇φ|² dA with c_A > 0.
* The arithmetic intersection form has exactly one positive direction (arithmetic Hodge index).

Consequently, writing the archimedean contribution of a degree-0 class through its Green energy, the energy always
enters the self-intersection with a minus sign.

### 2.5 Spectral expansion of the Green function [L: Iwaniec, *Spectral methods of automorphic forms*, Ch. 7]

On Γ = Γ₀(N) take a finite signed measure x on Γ\H of total mass 0 with smooth density. Let g be the automorphic Green
function of −Δ at eigenvalue 0 with the constant eigenfunction removed. Then

    E(x) := ∫∫ g(z, w) dx(z) dx(w) = Σ_j |⟨x, u_j⟩|²/λ_j + Σ_𝔞 (1/4π) ∫_R |⟨x, E_𝔞(·, ½ + it)⟩|² dt/(¼ + t²),

with u_j the Maass cusp forms and E_𝔞 the Eisenstein series of the cusps 𝔞. Note (1/4π) dt/(¼ + t²) = ½ dμ(t).
For point divisors the archimedean local height uses g off the diagonal and the metric on the diagonal [GZ §II;
Arakelov]. The continuous part ½∫|A_x(½+it)|² dμ(t) converges: by Cauchy–Schwarz and fourth moments of ζ and L(·, χ_D),
∫_T^{2T}|ζ(½+it)L(½+it, χ_D)|² dt ≪_D T^{1+ε}, so dyadic pieces are ≪ T^{−1+ε}, and 1/|ζ(1+2it)| ≪ log t. Pointwise
convexity alone would not suffice. The
divergence of the point self-energies sits in the Maass sum and is removed by the regularization. The regularized Maass
sum 𝓜_reg(x) need not be ≥ 0 [A]; it belongs to the explicit side of H4.

### 2.6 Hecke's toric formula [P; checked in §6.1]

On SL₂(Z)\H, with E(z, s) = Σ_{Γ_∞\Γ} (Im γz)^s, for ψ ∈ Cl(K)^∧ and all s (continuation from Re s > 1):

    A_ψ(s) := Σ_{Q ∈ Cl(D)} ψ(Q) E(τ_Q, s) = (√|D|/2)^s · u · L(s, ψ)/ζ(2s),   L(s, 1) = ζ_K(s).

*Proof.* With τ = τ_Q one has b² + |D| = 4ac, so |mτ + n|² = (an² − bmn + cm²)/a = Q(n, −m)/a, and
Im τ = √|D|/(2a). Hence (Im τ)^s/|mτ+n|^{2s} = (√|D|/2)^s Q(n, −m)^{−s}. Summing over (m, n) ≠ (0, 0) gives
2ζ(2s)E(τ_Q, s) = (√|D|/2)^s Z_Q(s), where Z_Q(s) = Σ′Q(m, n)^{−s} is Epstein's zeta function. Each ideal of norm n in
the class of Q has exactly w representations, so Σ_Q ψ(Q) Z_Q(s) = w·L(s, ψ). ∎

For a real combination x = Σ_Q c_Q τ_Q (over one or several discriminants) the **Eisenstein amplitude** is
A_x(s) := Σ c_Q E(τ_Q, s). If x is a class function on each Cl(D_i), then by the formula

    A_x(s) = ζ(2s)^{−1} Σ_i Σ_ψ ĉ_i(ψ) (√|D_i|/2)^s u_i L(s, ψ).

Write B_x(s) := ζ(s)^{−1}ζ(2s)A_x(s) when only trivial characters occur. Then
|A_x(½+it)|² = ρ_ζ(t)·|B_x(½+it)|², with B_x(s) = Σ_i ĉ_i (√|D_i|/2)^s u_i L(s, χ_{D_i}). On X₀(N) the same holds for
Heegner points with E_∞^{(N)}, up to Euler factors at p | N [A: standard, not re-derived here].

### 2.7 Kronecker's limit formula [L]

For the normalization E(z, s) = Σ_{Γ_∞\Γ}(Im γz)^s used here,

    E(z, s) = 3/(π(s − 1)) + (6/π)(γ − log 2 − ζ′(2)/ζ(2) − log(√y·|η(z)|²)) + O(s − 1),

with η the Dedekind eta function. This is the classical formula for Σ′ y^s|mz+n|^{−2s} = 2ζ(2s)E(z, s), divided by
2ζ(2s); the constant (6/π)(γ − log 2 − ζ′(2)/ζ(2)) is the tower's C₀ = 0.8671324277. A first draft of this note
omitted the ζ′(2)/ζ(2) term. The §6.1 check then showed a D-independent offset of exactly −ζ′(2)/ζ(2) = 0.56996, and
the formula was corrected.

## 3. The argument

This section is written as the argument was intended. Its one unproved step is H5. Every other step is graded.

### H0. Reduction [P-L]

Fix one imaginary quadratic field, e.g. K = Q(√−7) (h = 1). By (T1) it suffices to prove GRH(ζ_K), i.e. (T2) for
ζ_K. Alternatively, prove (T5) for ζ directly.

### H1. ζ_K in the CM amplitudes [P]

Let x be a real degree-0 combination of CM points of discriminants D₁, …, D_r, constant on each Galois orbit
(trivial-character part). By §2.6,

    A_x(s) = ζ(s)·B_x(s)/ζ(2s),   B_x(s) = Σ_i c_i u_i (√|D_i|/2)^s L(s, χ_{D_i}).

* (a) B_x(1) = (π/2)·deg x = 0, so A_x is regular at s = 1. By the class number formula,
  u(√|D|/2)L(1, χ_D) = πh/2, and a degree-0 combination has Σ_i c_i h_i = 0.
* (b) |A_x(½+it)|² = ρ_ζ(t)·|B_x(½+it)|².
* (c) A_x vanishes at every zero ρ of ζ with ζ(2ρ) ≠ 0 (absorption). This includes every zero with β ≥ ½, since then
  Re 2ρ ≥ 1, and in particular every zero on the line. §6.1 checks this at ρ₁ for four discriminants.

### H2. ζ_K in the archimedean local height [P-L; the regularization is A]

The archimedean local height of x has two pieces that carry ζ_K.

* (i) The continuous-spectrum part ½∫_R ρ_ζ(t)|B_x(½+it)|²κ_N(t) dμ(t) (§2.5). For N = 1, κ₁ ≡ 1. For squarefree N
  every E_𝔞 is a combination Σ_{M|N} α_{𝔞,M}(s)E(Mz, s), and the points Mτ are again CM points of discriminant D. So
  each cusp contributes the same factor ζ(s)/ζ(2s), and κ_N(t) := Σ_𝔞 |Σ_M α_{𝔞,M}(½+it)|² > 0 [A: not written out].
* (ii) The regularized diagonal (point) terms. By Kronecker's limit formula (§2.7) these involve log(√y|η(τ)|²) at the
  CM points. Summed over a Galois orbit they produce γ_K = γ + L′/L(1, χ_D), the constant term of ζ_K′/ζ_K at s = 1.
  This is "the ζ_K′/ζ_K of the archimedean Gross–Zagier term" [L: GZ §§II, IV]. Theorem B in §4.1 makes the statement
  exact.

### H3. Positivity [L]

ĥ(x) ≥ 0 for every x ∈ J₀(N)(Q̄) ⊗ R. With the Gross–Zagier local decomposition ĥ = Arch + Σ_p ⟨·,·⟩_p this reads

    Arch(x) ≥ Fin(x),    Fin(x) := −Σ_p ⟨x, x⟩_p = Σ_p (x, x)_p log p.

**Remark H3 (direction).** "Finite ≤ archimedean" is the shape of Weil positivity (prime side ≤ archimedean side plus
poles) and of the one-sided Landau bound (arithmetic foundation §2). In rounds 1–2 the needed one-sided inequality was
always the unproved step. Here it is a theorem, and the archimedean side carries ζ_K.

### H4. Proposition (the Heegner lower bounds) [P-L, with the regularization A]

Fix N with g₀(N) ≥ 1, discriminants D_i satisfying the Heegner condition, and a real degree-0 combination x of Heegner
points (constant on Galois orbits). Fix a Hecke polynomial P = Σ_m c_m T_m with (m, N) = 1, and put x_P := P·x. Then

    ½ ∫_R ρ_ζ(t)·|P̂(t)|²·|B_x(½+it)|²·κ_N(t) dμ(t)  ≥  𝔏(x_P) := Fin(x_P) − 𝓜_reg(x_P) − 𝒦(x_P),

with equality if and only if ĥ(x_P) = 0. The notation:
* P̂(t) := Σ_m c_m λ_t(m), where λ_t(m) = Σ_{ad=m} (a/d)^{it} is the normalized eigenvalue of T_m on E(·, ½+it);
* 𝓜_reg is the regularized Maass part;
* 𝒦 is the Kronecker (diagonal) part, which contains γ_{K_i} (H2(ii));
* κ_N > 0 is the cusp weight of H2(i). The continuous parts of all cusps are on the left, and all of them depend on ζ.

*Proof.* ĥ(x_P) = Arch(x_P) − Fin(x_P) ≥ 0 by H3. By §§2.5–2.7 and H2,
Arch = (continuous part, all cusps) + 𝓜_reg + 𝒦. T_m acts on E(·, ½+it) by λ_t(m), so the continuous amplitude of x_P is
P̂(t)·A_x(½+it). Then use H1(b). ∎

The left side depends on ζ (through ρ_ζ) and on the L(s, χ_{D_i}) (through B_x). The right side is built from finite
intersection numbers (log p for p non-split, §2.3), the Maass spectrum at CM points, and Kronecker constants.

**D-averaging ("the averaged Heegner amplitude is ζ itself") [L for the averages; A for the use].** By orthogonality of
quadratic characters, (1/#𝒟_X) Σ_{D∈𝒟_X} L(s, χ_D) → ζ(2s)𝔓(s) for Re s > 1, where 𝔓 is an explicit Euler product
and 𝒟_X is the set of negative fundamental discriminants with |D| ≤ X. On the critical line a second (polar) term from
the functional equation appears [L: Jutila 1981; Goldfeld–Hoffstein 1985]. So with constant weights the averaged
bracket is ≈ ζ(2s)𝔓(s), and the averaged amplitude is ≈ ζ(s)𝔓(s). Its square on the line, |ζ(½+it)|²|𝔓|², grows
like log t on average, as does the gamma symbol of the Weil form. This is the reason to expect H5a(i).

### H5. Transfer (claimed; this is where the argument fails, §4)

**(H5a) Weil branch.** Choose the data non-equivariantly as functions of F: discriminants D_i ∈ 𝒟_X, weights
c_i = c_i(F), and a Hecke polynomial P_F. Claim: as X → ∞, with a normalization c_X > 0,

* (i) ½∫ρ_ζ|P̂_F|²|B_{x_F}|²κ_N dμ = c_X·[archimedean side + pole side of W_K at F ⋆ F̌] + o(c_X);
* (ii) 𝔏(x_{F,P_F}) = c_X·2Σ_n Λ_K(n) n^{−1/2}(F ⋆ F̌)(log n) + o(c_X).

H4 then gives W_K(F ⋆ F̌) ≥ −o(1), hence (T2), hence RH. The motivation for (i) is the D-averaging above. The
motivation for (ii) is that the finite local heights are prime sums with weights log p (§2.3, §6.2).

**(H5b) Hardy branch.** Take x with ĥ(x) = 0. These are every degree-0 x on X(1), where the genus is 0. On X₀(N) they
are the combinations that kill every f-component; by GKZ the traced f-components of the y_D lie on one line per f, so
there are many such x once there are more than g₀(N) discriminants. For these x, H4 is an identity. Bounding the
regularized Maass part below by its explicit diagonal piece, 𝓜_reg ≥ −𝓜_diag [A], gives the upper bound (written
at level 1, where κ₁ = 1; on X₀(N) replace dμ by κ_N dμ)

    E_μ|A_x(½ + i·)|² ≤ 2U(x),    U(x) := Fin(x) − 𝒦(x) + 𝓜_diag(x).

Write A_x = Z·Ã_x with Ã_x(s) := s·B_x(s)/((s−1)ζ(2s)), which is regular at s = 1 by H1(a), and Ã_x(1) = A_x(1). The
claim is that there are x_n with 2U(x_n)/|A_{x_n}(1)|² → 1. Then (T5), applied to A = Ã_{x_n}/Ã_{x_n}(1), gives
Π_{β>½} |ρ/(1−ρ)|² ≤ 1, i.e. RH.

### H6. Conclusion (conditional on H5)

Either branch of H5 gives RH by (T1)/(T2) or (T5). The Davenport–Heilbronn function is excluded at H1: Hecke's formula
uses the Euler product of ζ_K (ideal counting in O_K), and DH is not ζ_K for any K and has no CM points.

## 4. The attack: why H5 fails

### 4.1 Theorem B (the ζ_K′/ζ_K content of CM heights is the first Li coefficient) [P from §§2.6–2.7; checked M]

For every fundamental D < 0 put S(D) := (1/h) Σ_{Q ∈ Cl(D)} log(√(Im τ_Q)·|η(τ_Q)|²). Then

    S(D) = A − ½ λ₁(ζ_K),    A := ½γ − ½log 2 + ½(1 − log 2π) = −0.4769042910…,
    λ₁(ζ_K) := Σ_ρ 1/ρ = ξ_K′/ξ_K(1) = 1 + ½log|D| − log 2π + L′/L(1, χ_D) = λ₁(ζ) + λ₁(L(·, χ_D)),
    λ₁(ζ) = 1 + γ/2 − ½log 4π = 0.0230957089…,  λ₁(L(·, χ_D)) = ½log(|D|/π) − ½γ + L′/L(1, χ_D).

*Proof.*
1. Sum Kronecker's formula (§2.7) over Cl(D):
   Σ_Q E(τ_Q, s) = 3h/(π(s−1)) + (6/π)[h(γ − log 2 − ζ′(2)/ζ(2)) − h·S(D)] + O(s−1).
2. Expand Hecke's formula (§2.6) at s = 1, using:
   * ζ_K(s) = r_K[1/(s−1) + γ_K + O(s−1)] with r_K = L(1, χ_D) = 2πh/(w√|D|) and γ_K = γ + L′/L(1, χ_D);
   * (√|D|/2)^s = (√|D|/2)(1 + (s−1)log(√|D|/2) + …);
   * 1/ζ(2s) = ζ(2)^{−1}(1 − 2(s−1)ζ′(2)/ζ(2) + …).

   The prefactor u(√|D|/2)r_K/ζ(2) equals 3h/π, so
   Σ_Q E(τ_Q, s) = (3h/π)[1/(s−1) + γ_K + log(√|D|/2) − 2ζ′(2)/ζ(2)] + O(s−1).
3. Compare the constant terms. The ζ′(2)/ζ(2) terms cancel, leaving S(D) = γ − ½log 2 − ½γ_K − ¼log|D|.
4. Since ξ_K = s(s−1)(√|D|/2π)^sΓ(s)ζ_K(s),
   ξ_K′/ξ_K(1) = 1 + log(√|D|/2π) + ψ(1) + γ_K = 1 + ½log|D| − log 2π + L′/L(1, χ_D).
   By the Hadamard product with ρ ↔ 1 − ρ pairing, ξ_K′/ξ_K(1) = Σ_ρ 1/(1−ρ) = Σ_ρ 1/ρ.
5. Substitute into step 3. ∎

**Check [M, §6.1].** 14 discriminants, −3 to −47, class numbers 1 to 5: max |S(D) − A + ½λ₁(ζ_K)| = 3.4·10⁻²¹.
λ₁(ζ_K) runs from 0.0797 (D = −3) to 0.725 (D = −40). γ_K runs from 0.945 (D = −3) to −0.064 (D = −47).

**Consequences.**
* (i) The CM η-heights depend on ζ only through the single D-independent number λ₁(ζ) (and the constants γ, log 2,
  log π). The same holds for the regularized diagonal terms of the archimedean Gross–Zagier heights (H2(ii)), and, via
  Chowla–Selberg, for the Faltings heights of CM elliptic curves. "The archimedean GZ term carries ζ_K′/ζ_K" means
  exactly this.
* (ii) [P] λ₁ > 0 for every multiset of zeros in the open strip that is symmetric under ρ ↦ 1 − ρ and ρ ↦ ρ̄. Each pair
  contributes 1/ρ + 1/ρ̄ = 2β/|ρ|² > 0. An inequality λ₁(ζ_K) ≥ c with c ≤ 0, which is what height positivity
  produces, therefore already follows from the zeros lying in the strip. RH(ζ_K) ⟺ λ_n(ζ_K) ≥ 0 for every n (T3).
  Heights give n = 1 only. (Over function fields, Yun–Zhang interpret every higher central derivative as an intersection
  number of Heegner–Drinfeld cycles; over Q only the first derivative has a cycle interpretation [L].)
* (iii) Faltings heights. With Colmez's form of Chowla–Selberg, h_Fal(E_CM) = −½L′/L(0, χ_D) − ¼log|D| + const [L],
  and the functional equation L′/L(0) = −L′/L(1) − log(|D|/π) + γ + log 2, one gets h_Fal(E_CM) = ½λ₁(ζ_K) + c₀ with
  c₀ independent of D [A: c₀ not re-derived]. Lower bounds on Faltings heights, the positivity direction (Bost), are
  lower bounds on λ₁(ζ_K). These are harmless for Siegel zeros, since a real zero β near 1 makes L′/L(1, χ_D) ≈ 1/(1−β)
  large and positive. The useful direction is an upper bound (Granville–Stark: uniform abc ⟹ no Siegel zeros), and that
  is not a positivity statement.

### 4.2 Theorem C (the continuous-spectrum ζ_K content cancels in the global height) [P-L; one A]

Let x be a real degree-0 combination of Heegner points on X₀(N), for any D_i satisfying the Heegner condition, after
any Hecke polynomial P has been applied.
* (i) ĥ(x) depends only on the coordinates of x in the cuspidal (f, ψ)-eigenspaces and on the numbers L′(f, ψ, 1)
  (Theorem D).
* (ii) Hence, in ĥ(x) = Arch(x) − Fin(x), the ζ-dependent continuous term is exactly offset:
  Fin(x) − 𝓜_reg(x) − 𝒦(x) = ½∫ρ_ζ|P̂ B_x|²κ_N dμ − ĥ(x).
* (iii) [A] As functions of the Hecke index (pairings ⟨c, T_m d⟩), the archimedean local height is
  Σ_j λ_j(m)α_j + (1/4π)∫λ_t(m)β(t) dt/(¼+t²) + (Eisenstein constant-term contributions). Here β(t) contains
  ζ_K(½+it)ζ_K(½−it)/|ζ(1+2it)|². The global pairing is Σ_f a_f(m)H_f, the coefficients of a weight-2 cusp form
  (GZ, GKZ). So the Maass and Eisenstein components of Arch(m) and Fin(m) cancel term by term. Writing this out needs
  linear independence of the sequences (λ_j(m)), (λ_t(m)) and (a_f(m)) in a topology where the sums converge; that is
  not done here.

*Proof.* (i) is Theorem D. (ii) rearranges the identity in H4. (iii) is GKZ plus §2.5 plus the action of T_m on Maass
forms and Eisenstein series. ∎

*Reading.* Theorem C extends the tower's Eisenstein confinement (Theorem 4.5 there: ζ only in the degrees) from NS to
local heights. Wherever ζ_K enters a local height with a spectral parameter, as |ζ_K(½+it)|², it leaves again before
the global height is formed.

### 4.3 Theorem D (the positivity cone of Heegner spans) [P-L]

Take N prime (so no weight-2 oldforms) and D satisfying the Heegner condition. Let V ⊂ J₀(N)(H) ⊗ R be the real span of
{T_m y^σ : (m, N) = 1, σ ∈ Gal(H/K)}, and V_C its complexification, with the Hermitian extension of ⟨·,·⟩_NT.

* (i) V_C = ⊕_{(f,ψ)} C·c_{ψ,f}, an orthogonal sum over newforms f of level N and ψ ∈ Cl(K)^∧, each summand of
  dimension ≤ 1.
* (ii) For x = Σ c_{m,σ} T_m y^σ:
  ĥ(x) = h^{−2} Σ_{(f,ψ)} |Σ_{m,σ} c_{m,σ} a_f(m) ψ(σ)|² · ĥ(c_{ψ,f}),
  where ĥ(c_{ψ,f}) = (h u² √|D|/(8π²(f, f)))·L′(f, ψ, 1).
* (iii) The linear inequalities among the numbers ⟨T_m y^σ, T_{m′} y^{σ′}⟩ that follow from "ĥ ≥ 0 on V" are exactly
  the nonnegative combinations of {L′(f, ψ, 1) ≥ 0 : c_{ψ,f} ≠ 0}.
* (iv) Across discriminants, for traced points y_D^{tr} ∈ J₀(N)(Q) ⊗ R: by GKZ e_f y_D^{tr} = c_f(D) y_f for a fixed
  y_f. So the Gram matrix of the f-parts is the rank-one matrix c_f(D)c_f(D′)ĥ(y_f), and positivity on traced spans
  over all D is equivalent to {ĥ(y_f) ≥ 0}. [A] For untraced points of different fields the cross terms involve only
  characters that factor through H_D ∩ H_{D′}.

*Proof.*
* (i) On e_f J ⊗ C, T_m acts by the scalar a_f(m) (Eichler–Shimura), and Gal(H/K) acts on Heegner points through
  Cl(K) (Shimura reciprocity). The (f, ψ)-isotypic part of V_C is therefore spanned by the single vector c_{ψ,f}, the
  Hecke-eigen projection of the ψ-isotypic projection of y. Distinct (f, ψ) are orthogonal: T_m is self-adjoint and
  ⟨·,·⟩_NT is Galois invariant.
* (ii) Expand x in this basis.
* (iii) A diagonal form Σ_k w_k|ℓ_k(x)|² with independent ℓ_k is ≥ 0 on V iff every w_k ≥ 0 (test x on a dual basis).
  The cone of linear consequences is generated by the w_k ≥ 0 (Farkas).
* (iv) is GKZ Theorem C. ∎

*Reading (wall 3).* Néron–Tate positivity on Heegner spans is exactly central-derivative positivity of the cusp forms in
the support. Each L′(f, ψ, 1) ≥ 0 follows from "the completed L(f_K ⊗ ψ, s) has no zero on the real axis to the right
of the centre": the completed function is real on the real axis, vanishes at the centre to odd order, and is positive
for large real s. That is a height-0 statement. ζ is the Eisenstein member of the family and has no (f, ψ)-component;
its central analogue, L(½, χ_D) ≥ 0, is open (it follows from GRH; Chowla conjectured L(½, χ) ≠ 0). Heights do not
give it, because the Eisenstein part of J₀(N) is torsion (Manin–Drinfeld).

### 4.4 Proposition H (abstract Heegner dictionaries) and the natural constructions

* **(i) [P-L] Abstract existence.** If GRH(ζ_K) holds, there are linear maps F ↦ x_F^{(n)} into Heegner spans (prime
  levels N_n → ∞) and c_n > 0 with ĥ(x_F^{(n)})/c_n → W_K(F ⋆ F̌) for every real even F ∈ C_c^∞(R). Conversely, such
  maps give W_K ≥ 0 on even F, hence GRH(ζ_K), using the even-sector argument of tower Lemma 4.11(iii) [A].
  *Proof.*
  1. Under GRH, W_K(F ⋆ F̌) = 2Σ_{k≥1} F̂(γ_k)² for even F, over the positive ordinates of ξ_K with multiplicity.
  2. For each n choose k(n) → ∞ distinct newforms f_1, …, f_{k(n)} of prime level N_n with ĥ(y_{D, f_k}) > 0, i.e.
     L′(½, f_K) ≠ 0. Level-aspect non-vanishing results for central derivatives are cited for this [L, recalled, not
     re-verified: Kowalski–Michel–VanderKam 2000; averages of Gross–Zagier heights, Michel–Ramakrishnan 2007]. The
     construction needs only that k(n) → ∞ such forms exist at some levels.
  3. Put x_F^{(n)} := c_n^{1/2} Σ_{k ≤ k(n)} √2·F̂(γ_k)·ĥ(y_{D, f_k})^{−1/2}·e_{f_k} y_D.
  4. Then ĥ(x_F^{(n)})/c_n = 2Σ_{k ≤ k(n)} F̂(γ_k)² → W_K(F ⋆ F̌). ∎

  So (H5a) can be satisfied only by putting the zeros of ζ_K into the (f, ψ)-coordinates by hand. A Heegner dictionary
  has content only if x_F is built from the arithmetic without reading off the zeros.
* **(ii) [P + Atlas] Hecke-translate constructions** x_F = Σ_m F(log m) m^{−1/2} T_m x fail the support test. Take F_ε
  even about c = log 2.5 and supported in (c − ε, c + ε) ⊂ (log 2, log 3). Then x_{F_ε} = 0 for every N. But
  W_K(F_ε ⋆ F̌_ε) = W_K(F_ε(·+c) ⋆ F_ε(·+c)ˇ) = 2log(1/ε) + O_D(1) > 0 for small ε, by tower Lemma 4.6.1(i) applied to
  both factors of ζ_K.
* **(iii) [P] Discriminant-sampling at fixed level** x_F = Σ_{D ∈ 𝒟_X} F(log(|D|/X)) a_D y_D^{tr}. By (iv) of
  Theorem D, ĥ(x_F) = Σ_f ĥ(y_f)·|Σ_D F(log(|D|/X)) a_D c_f(D)|² is a sum of at most g₀(N) squares of linear
  functionals of F. Any limit form therefore has rank ≤ g₀(N), while W_K on even F has infinite rank (F̂ separates
  infinitely many ordinates). With N → ∞ the rank grows, but each f contributes one square whose F-dependence passes
  through the GKZ coefficients c_f(D). These are Fourier coefficients of a half-integral-weight form attached to f, with
  no access to the zeros of ζ [A for the last clause].
* **(iv)** Lemma 4.6 (§4.6): the finite side 𝔏 is supported on non-split primes, the prime side of W_K on split ones.

### 4.5 Theorem E (sign: positivity gives only lower bounds, and lower bounds cannot detect zeros) [P; numbers M]

Notation. Let 𝒵 be the class of functions holomorphic on Re s > ½, continuous up to the line, with value 1 at s = 1.
Z(s) = ((s−1)/s)ζ(s) ∈ 𝒵. For f ∈ 𝒵 put ρ_f(t) := |f(½+it)|²/|f(1+2it)|². Then ρ_Z = ρ_ζ·|1+2it|²/(4t²), and the
extra factor does not depend on f.

* **(a)** Every inequality supplied by H4 has the form Φ_x(ρ_ζ) ≥ 𝔏(x), where Φ_x(ρ) = ½∫ρ(t)w_x(t) dμ(t) with
  w_x(t) = |P̂(t)B_x(½+it)|²κ_N(t) ≥ 0. So Φ_x is a nonnegative linear functional of the density. The right side 𝔏(x) does
  not involve ρ_ζ. It involves ζ only through the Kronecker constants of Theorem B (through λ₁(ζ)); the finite
  intersection numbers are arithmetic.
* **(b)** Let ρ_j = ½ + δ_j + iγ_j (0 < δ_j < ½) be finitely many points. Let B(s) := Π_j (s − ρ_j)/(s − 1 + ρ̄_j) be
  their Blaschke product for Re s > ½, and Z♭ := Z·B/B(1). Then:

* **(d)** More generally, take any family of inequalities Φ(|f(½+i·)|) ≥ c or Ψ(ρ_f) ≥ c, with Φ and Ψ nondecreasing in

* **(e) Identities are different.** For x with ĥ(x) = 0, H4 is an equality, and the twin violates it strictly. This

  RH needs the arithmetic side 𝔏(x), i.e. the finite intersection numbers and the Maass terms, evaluated at all scales
  to square-root precision. That is atlas closing-line item 1 ("a genuine all-scale signed arithmetic estimate"), and
  the Hardy-space route through them is blocked by Lemma 4.7.

### 4.6 Lemma 4.6 (prime-support mismatch) [L for (a); P for (b), (c)]

* **(a)** In the finite local heights of Heegner/CM divisors, log p occurs only for primes p not split in K (for two
  discriminants, not split in either field). At split p the CM points reduce to ordinary points, and distinct Heegner
  points have distinct reductions [GZ Ch. III; Deuring]. At inert or ramified p the reduction is supersingular, and
  intersections are counted by optimal embeddings into maximal orders of the quaternion algebra B_{p,∞} [Gross–Zagier
  1985; Gross–Keating]. At the level of formulas: the divisor function σ′(n) = Σ_{d|n} ε(d) log(n/d²) of GZ has local
  factor Σ_{j=0}^{k}(k − 2j) = 0 at split p. Its Dirichlet series is ζ(w)L(w, ε)·(L′/L(w, ε) − ζ′/ζ(w)), whose prime
  weights are (1 − ε(p)) log p ∈ {0, log p, 2 log p}. [E, §6.2: in all 20 singular-moduli pairs every prime of J(d₁,d₂)²
  is non-split in both fields.]
* **(b)** In the explicit formula of ζ_K the first-power prime terms are Λ_K(p)p^{−1/2}Φ(log p) with
  Λ_K(p) = (1 + χ_D(p)) log p: 2 log p at split p, log p at ramified p, 0 at inert p.
* **(c)** So at first order the finite side of H4 runs over the primes with weight 1 − χ_D(p) (the prime content of
  ζ²/ζ_K), and the prime side of W_K over those with weight 1 + χ_D(p) (the prime content of ζ_K). Apart from ramified
  primes the two supports are disjoint. (H5a)(ii) cannot hold term by term. It could hold only as an equality of totals
  forced by the choice of x_F, which is (Rep) (Proposition H(i)).

### 4.7 The Hardy branch: (T5) and why Heegner amplitudes are not admissible [P-L; P]

*Proof of (T5).*
1. E_μ|Z|² = log 2π − γ < ∞ (Plancherel for ζ(s)/s = −∫₀^∞{1/x}x^{s−1}dx; checked in §6.3).
2. Z lies in the Smirnov class N⁺ of Re s > ½, and its inner factor is the Blaschke product B_ζ of the zeros with
   β > ½ [L: BSY 1999]. So Z ∈ H²(μ) and Z = B_ζ·O with O outer.
3. For A ∈ H^∞ with A(1) = 1, the sub-mean-value property for harmonic measure from 1 gives
   E_μ|ZA|² = E_μ|OA|² ≥ |(OA)(1)|² = |O(1)|² = |B_ζ(1)|^{−2} = Π_{β>½}|ρ/(1−ρ)|².
4. O is outer, so {OA : A ∈ H^∞} is dense in H² (Beurling). Choose OA_k → O(1) in H². Evaluation at 1 is continuous,
   so A_k(1) → 1, and A_k/A_k(1) attains the bound in the limit. ∎

The two RH-equivalent statements recalled here in the weight μ, which is the weight of the Arakelov Green function, are
thus **upper** bounds:
E_μ log|Z| ≤ 0 (T4), or "some admissible A has E_μ|ZA|² ≤ 1 + ε" (T5). H4 supplies lower bounds (Theorem E).

**Lemma 4.7 (mean type) [P].** H5b's deduction is invalid even if its upper bounds were sharp.
* (i) Let |D| > 4 and let x be degree 0 with nonzero trivial-character coefficient on the Galois orbit of D. Along the
  real axis, A_x(s) grows like (√|D|/2)^s: the y^s term of E(τ, s) at the principal form, where Im τ = √|D|/2 > 1.
  Distinct discriminants give distinct rates, so the growth cannot cancel. Hence A_x ∉ N⁺(Re s > ½) and
  A_x/Z ∉ H^∞.
* (ii) Hecke translates add type log m. The normalized eigenvalue Σ_{ad=m}(a/d)^{s−½} is invariant under s ↦ 1 − s and
  grows like m^{Re s − ½}.
* (iii) For such functions the bound of (T5) fails. For example f(s) = Z(s)e^{a(s−1)}, a > 0, has f(1) = 1 and
  E_μ|f|² = e^{−a}E_μ|Z|² = e^{−a}(log 2π − γ), which is < 1 for a > 0.2316. In general, for f = e^{as}g with
  g ∈ N⁺, only E_μ|f|² ≥ e^{−a}|f(1)|²Π_{β>½}|ρ/(1−ρ)|² survives. That is vacuous unless a → 0, and for Heegner
  amplitudes a ≥ log(√|D|/2).
* (iv) The only admissible amplitudes come from D ∈ {−3, −4} (√|D|/2 ≤ 1). In degree 0 that is a one-dimensional
  space, with nothing to optimize.
* (v) The log version (T4) is blocked the same way. Jensen's inequality E_μ log|A| ≥ log|A(1)|, valid in N⁺, fails
  for A = e^{a(s−1)}: E_μ log|A| = −a/2 < 0 = log|A(1)|. So E_μ log|Z| cannot be bounded above through Heegner
  amplitudes either.

*Proof.* (i): the Fourier expansion E(τ, s) = y^s + φ(s)y^{1−s} + O(e^{−2πy}) as s → +∞ along R; N⁺ functions satisfy

### 4.9 Proposition G (algebraicity; wall 3) [L for the cited facts; A for the conclusion]

The known height formulas for CM and Heegner cycles express heights through L′(½, π_K ⊗ χ). Here π is of cohomological
type at ∞, and χ is a Hecke character of K of algebraic type: infinity type (k, −k), k ∈ Z, for self-duality.
[L: Gross–Zagier 1986; Zhang 1997 (higher weight); Yuan–Zhang–Zhang 2013; Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna 2013.]
* The Eisenstein shadow of such a formula involves the Hecke L-functions L(s, χ) of type (k, −k). For k ≠ 0 these are
  not ζ_K and do not contain ζ. For k = 0, χ is a class-group character; the trivial character gives ζ_K, evaluated at
  the fixed points s ∈ {0, 1} (Theorem B).
* The twist ζ_K(s + iT) = L(s, |N(·)|^{iT}) would need χ = |N(·)|^{iT}, which for T ≠ 0 is neither algebraic nor
  self-dual.
* Higher weight 2k moves the centre to s = k, outside the critical strip of ζ_K for k ≥ 2. Positivity of the relevant
  Beilinson–Bloch heights is conjectural.

So heights of algebraic cycles evaluate ζ_K only at finitely many fixed points. This is wall 3 in its sharpest form: not
a finite verified height, but height 0 (Theorem D) and the single functional λ₁ (Theorem B). It parallels the tower's
Theorem 4.13 (exactly equivariant maps carry only the Tate characters e^{λx}, λ ∈ Q). Non-equivariant maps built from
cycles carry only algebraic Hecke characters, whose infinity types are discrete, while reaching an ordinate γ needs a
continuous twist parameter.

### 4.11 Verdict on H5

* **H5a (Weil branch): unproved, and refuted for each natural construction.** By Theorem D, (i) and (ii) together would
  make W_K a limit of normalized Néron–Tate heights, i.e. of nonnegative combinations of cuspidal central derivatives.
  By Proposition H(i) that is possible if and only if GRH(ζ_K), and only by encoding the zeros. Hecke-translate
  constructions fail the support test (H(ii)). Fixed-level discriminant sampling has finite rank (H(iii)). The prime
  supports are disjoint at first order (Lemma 4.6).
* **H5b (Hardy branch): invalid as a deduction** (Lemma 4.7). It also does not use positivity: it uses the ĥ = 0
  identities and Maass ≥ 0, which is spectral positivity of the Laplacian.
* **H4 as a constraint on ζ: lower bounds only, and twin-stable** (Theorem E, Proposition E′).
* **The ζ_K′/ζ_K content is λ₁** (Theorem B). **The global height has no ζ_K content** (Theorem C).

**Exact failing line: §3, Step H5.** No other step is in doubt beyond its grade. H1 is [P]. H2 is [P-L], with the
point-mass regularization [A]. H3 is [L]. H4 is [P-L], with the same [A].

## 5. The four walls

| Wall | Why the attempt is not blocked a priori | Where the attempt actually ends up |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Flow-blindness, SL2 (no flow-stable hypothesis on Φ proves RH) | Its inputs are heights of algebraic cycles (Néron–Tate on J₀(N), Gross–Zagier, GKZ). They are not hypotheses on the profile Φ and have no heat-flow deformation | The ζ-content of the output (H4) is a family of lower bounds with slack 2ĥ(x) on nonnegative functionals of ρ_ζ. Such inequalities persist under small deformations of ζ: under the backward heat flow (ρ_{ζ_ε} → ρ_ζ locally uniformly; dominated convergence against the weights [A]), and under the Blaschke and Lehmer twins (Theorem E, Prop. E′). In SL2's sense the ζ-content is flow-stable, so it cannot prove RH. The arithmetic content (cuspidal central derivatives) does not involve ζ |
| (2) NS-RIGID (on the native span every arithmetic input is flow-stable or RH itself) | The inputs are not on the twin-ladder span | The attempt lands in NS-RIGID's "loophole" class: function-side information on the critical line. Theorem E closes that class for Heegner inputs by a sign argument. The abstract dictionary (Prop. H) is the "RH itself" branch |
| (3) Verified height / finite support (Route I ceiling, crossover 2a = 7.764) | H4 holds for all N, D and P; the family is infinite and not a fixed-support statement | Sharper than a finite height. Each generator of the positivity cone is a central-derivative statement for one L-function, at height 0 (Theorem D). The ζ_K constant is the single functional λ₁ (Theorem B). Algebraic twists only, so no evaluation point moves to height T (Prop. G). What reaches ζ is λ₁, which is > 0 for any zeros in the strip, and lower bounds that adding off-line zeros cannot violate (Theorem E), up to an O(δ²) edge effect under the functional equation |
| (4) Pointwise / MV-CAP | MV-CAP is proved for zero-side statistics and not for function-side mean values (route-42 referee correction 2). H4 is a function-side statement | Theorem E is a sign version of MV-CAP for function-side lower bounds. They are preserved exactly under adding off-line zeros (half-plane twin). With the functional equation they can register a displacement only through the edge factor, with relative size O(δ²). Upper bounds would detect zeros (T4, T5), but positivity does not supply them, and for Heegner amplitudes they are not admissible (Lemma 4.7) |
| Vortex trapping (global box winding = RH) | not used | not involved |

The attempt avoids walls 1 and 2 at the level of its inputs: Néron–Tate positivity is arithmetic and not flow-stable.
It fails to transfer that positivity to ζ, and what reaches ζ is flow-stable (wall 1). It meets wall 3 more sharply than
any earlier route, at height 0 instead of at a finite verified height. It meets wall 4 in a form not covered before
(function-side lower bounds), and Theorem E handles that form.

   by up to 13% near γ̄/2 (§4.5, Prop. E′). Theorem E is therefore stated for the half-plane twin. The FE version is
   stated with its edge caveat.

## 7. Self-attack (same author; not a fresh-context referee)

1. **"Theorem D makes the attempt trivial: Heegner positivity is just L′(½, f_K ⊗ ψ) ≥ 0."** Yes, that is its whole
   content. The attempt hoped that the ζ_K-content of the local heights would add something. Theorem C says it does not
   survive into the global height, and Theorem E says that, read through the local decomposition, it only bounds a
   ζ-functional from below.
2. **"Theorem E holds 𝔏 fixed while ζ changes, but ζ determines the primes and hence 𝔏."** The twin test checks the
   inference "H4 ⟹ RH", the same device as the DH, F₅ and MT twins of rounds 1–2. A proof through H5 would have to use
   more information linking ζ to the primes in 𝔏. That information is the GZ identity with its exact cuspidal term
   (Theorems C, D), not positivity.
3. **"Prop. E′ shows lower bounds can detect off-line zeros."** Only through the edge factor, with relative effect
   O(δ²) and weights concentrated near γ̄/2, and only if the slack is below δ². Excluding all δ needs relative slack → 0,
   i.e. upper bounds on ĥ(x_n). The reading is graded [A].
4. **"Lemma 4.7 is an artefact of normalization: divide by (√|D|/2)^s."** That factor is not a Hecke or CM operation,
   and dividing by it changes A_x(1). With several discriminants no single factor removes all the growth rates, and
   Hecke translates add two-sided growth. Within the Heegner span, the admissible amplitudes come from D ∈ {−3, −4}
   only.
5. **"Theorem B is classical (Kronecker, Chowla–Selberg)."** Yes. Only the Li-coefficient reading, and its use as the
   wall-3 statement, belong to this note. No novelty is claimed.
6. **"The zero of 𝔇 at Re s = 1.93 is a numerical root."** Located to |B| = 2.6·10⁻²⁶ at 25 digits, with the identity
   and the functional equation checked. Not interval-certified. The argument needs only that the span contains
   amplitudes with zeros off the line.
7. **"The point-mass regularization is [A]."** It affects 𝓜_reg and 𝒦 in H4. It does not affect the sign of the
   continuous term, which comes from a positive kernel, so Theorem E stands. Theorem D does not use it.
8. **"Is the sign of Arch in ĥ right?"** Faltings–Hriljac (§2.4): archimedean classes have negative self-intersection,
   and ĥ = −(x̂ + Φ)². So the Green energy enters ĥ positively. The Arakelov and hyperbolic Green functions differ by
   h(z) + h(w) + c, which cancels on degree-0 divisors (tower §6.1), and the hyperbolic Green function has the spectral
   expansion of §2.5 with a positive continuous part. At genus 0 (§6.2) both sides of the singular-moduli identity are
   positive, consistent with Arch = Fin.
9. **"Lemma 4.6(a) at level N is cited, not derived."** It is cross-checked on the singular-moduli data (level 1, two
   fields) and on the local factor of σ′. The level-N statement is [L: GZ Ch. III].
10. **"Could a non-equivariant x_F evade Theorem D?"** No. Theorem D concerns the quadratic form on the span, not the map
    F ↦ x_F. Any x in a Heegner span has ĥ(x) = Σ (≥ 0)·|coordinate|². The map only chooses the coordinates, and
    Prop. H shows that choosing them to reproduce W_K is GRH(ζ_K) put in by hand.
11. **"The ĥ = 0 identities do distinguish ζ from Z♭, so Theorem E is not the end."** Correct, and recorded as
    Theorem E(e). Those identities are explicit formulas, true for ζ whatever its zeros, and they contain no positivity.
    Using them toward RH means evaluating their arithmetic side at all scales (atlas item 1), and the Hardy-space use is
    blocked by Lemma 4.7. The failing step stays H5: positivity, the attempt's distinctive input, contributes nothing
    beyond {L′(½, f_K ⊗ ψ) ≥ 0}.

## 8. What survives

* **Theorem B** [P, checked to 3.4·10⁻²¹]: the CM η-heights equal A − ½λ₁(ζ_K) with A = ½γ − ½log 2 + ½(1 − log 2π).
  "The archimedean GZ term carries ζ_K′/ζ_K" means: it carries the first Li coefficient. λ₁ > 0 holds for every zero
  configuration in the strip.
* **Theorem C** [P-L; one A]: the continuous-spectrum ζ_K content of local heights cancels in the global height.
  Eisenstein confinement extends from degrees to local heights.
* **Theorem D** [P-L]: the positivity cone of Heegner spans is exactly {L′(½, f_K ⊗ ψ) ≥ 0}, a family of height-0
  statements.
* **Theorem E** [P]: function-side lower bounds of the H4 type are preserved under adding off-line zeros (Blaschke
  twin). The proof of (T5) [P-L] shows that RH-equivalent statements in the Arakelov weight are upper bounds.
* **Prop. E′** [P/M]: the FE twin raises ρ_ζ by 10⁴–10⁸ near the displaced ordinate and lowers it by ≤ 0.2%, 4.3% and
  13% near γ̄/2 (an edge effect, ≈ δ²).
* **Lemma 4.6** [L/P]: the finite heights weight primes by 1 − χ_D(p), the ζ_K prime side by 1 + χ_D(p).
* **Lemma 4.7** [P]: Heegner and Hecke amplitudes have positive mean type, so they are not admissible in Hardy-space RH
  criteria (one-sided versus two-sided exponential sums).
* **Prop. F** [P/M]: a Davenport–Heilbronn twin inside the Heegner span. The amplitude of τ₋₇ − τ₋₈ is ζ(s)/ζ(2s) times
  a DH-type 𝔇 with the functional equation 𝔇(s) = 𝔇(1−s) and a zero at 1.92574583 + 7.35195194i.
* **Prop. G** [L/A]: heights see only algebraic Hecke characters, which is wall 3 at height 0.
* **Prop. H** [P-L]: abstract Heegner dictionaries exist if and only if GRH(ζ_K).
* **Tools:** a Hecke/Kronecker checker, a singular-moduli checker (20 pairs, 60 digits, exact factorization), twin
  calculators, and a root finder for the DH bracket.

**Directions this leaves (proposals, not run).**
1. The useful direction for heights is an upper bound. Granville–Stark show that uniform abc gives an upper bound on CM
   heights and hence no Siegel zeros. An analogue at the critical line would need upper bounds on Heegner heights at
   relative precision → 0 (Prop. E′); that is a Lindelöf/abc-type input, not positivity.
2. Higher Li coefficients λ_n(ζ_K), n ≥ 2, correspond to higher derivatives at the centre. Over function fields these
   are intersection numbers (Yun–Zhang); over Q no cycle-theoretic interpretation is known. Constructing one would move
   wall 3.
3. Hardy-space criteria need one-sided Dirichlet series (Lemma 4.7). Any arithmetic positivity used through T4 or T5
   must produce one-sided amplitudes. Self-adjoint (Hecke) and FE-symmetric (toric) constructions produce two-sided ones.
