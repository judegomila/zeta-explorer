## 2. The construction and what is proved about it

### 2.1 The Euler-completed multiplier

**Definition.** M_X is the multiplication operator (1/2π-normalized Fourier multiplier) on L²(ℝ) with symbol

  σ_X(t) = α(t) + c_X(t) + 2Re (ζ_X′/ζ_X)(½ + it),   c_X(t) = 2Re[(X^{1/2+it} − 1)/(½ + it)].   (2.1)

Equivalently (PROVED, two lines in 2.3):

  σ_X(t) = 2 ∂_σ log|ξ_X(σ + it)| |_{σ=½} + 2Re[X^{1/2+it}/(½ + it)].   (2.2)

The second term is the pole of ζ at s = 1 seen through the cutoff X. The first is twice the normal derivative of the
modulus of the completed finite Euler product across the critical line. For the true ξ that derivative is identically 0
(functional equation and reality). Expanding the Euler factors, 2Re ζ_X′/ζ_X(½ + it) = −Σ_{p≤X} Σ_{k≥1} log p p^{−k/2}
2cos(kt log p). So M_X is the full-line convolution by α̌ + e^{|r|/2} 1_{|r|≤W} − Σ_{p≤X, k≥1} log p p^{−k/2}
(δ_{k log p} + δ_{−k log p}). σ_X is real-analytic, bounded below and ≍ log|t| at infinity, so M_X is self-adjoint and
bounded below.

The zero-extended (sharp) symbol is σ^♭_X(t) = α(t) + c_X(t) − Σ_{n<X} Λ(n) n^{−1/2} 2cos(t log n). In the 5/4
normalization it is the completed symbol V_L of atlas §9 (L = ℓ). The two differ by the atoms beyond the support:

  T_X(t) := σ^♭_X(t) − σ_X(t) = 2Re Σ_{p≤X≤p^k} log p · p^{−k(½+it)},   |T_X(t)| ≤ T_X(0) = log X + O(1)  (Mertens).

### 2.2 Theorem A (compression identity; PROVED + NUMERIC)

**Theorem A.** For every f in the form domain of H_λ, λ² = X: q_λ[f] = (1/2π) ∫ σ_X(t)|f̂(t)|² dt = (1/2π) ∫ σ^♭_X(t)
|f̂(t)|² dt. That is, H_λ is the compression of M_X (and of M^♭_X) to L²_even[−ℓ, ℓ], in the form sense.

*Proof.* Insert R_f(r) = (1/2π)∫|f̂|² e^{itr} dt into (1.1). The continuum term is ∫_{−W}^{W} e^{|r|/2} R_f(r) dr =
(1/2π)∫ c_X|f̂|². Each prime-power term is Λ(n) n^{−1/2}(R_f(log n) + R_f(−log n)) = (1/2π) ∫ Λ(n) n^{−1/2} 2cos(t log n)
|f̂|². The atoms of T_X sit at k log p ≥ W. There R_f vanishes, because supp R_f ⊂ [−W, W] and R_f(±W) = 0 for
f ∈ L². So they contribute 0. Every symbol involved is bounded except α, which is the same on both sides. ∎

NUMERIC check (`v2-compression.json`). For the box at X = 30 and 200, (1/2π)∫α|ê₀|² = (2/W)(aW + T(W)) to 7·10⁻⁶. For a
smooth modulated bump at frequency 12, the x-space value of the continuum-minus-prime part equals the t-space integral of
σ_X − α to 1.4·10⁻⁸. That integral includes the atoms beyond the support, and they integrate to zero as Theorem A says.

### 2.3 Theorem B (spectral identity; PROVED from K-T + NUMERIC)

**Theorem B.** For all real t, with X not a prime power (X a prime power: average the two one-sided limits),

  σ^♭_X(t) = Z_X(t) + τ_X(t),   σ_X(t) = Z_X(t) − T_X(t) + τ_X(t),   (2.3)

  Z_X(t) := 2Re Σ_ρ X^{ρ−½−it}/(ρ − ½ − it),   τ_X(t) := −2Re Σ_{k≥1} X^{−2k−½−it}/(2k + ½ + it),  |τ_X| ≤ X^{−5/2}.

Under RH, Z_X(t) = Σ_γ 2 sin((γ − t) log X)/(γ − t): the zero measure convolved with the Dirichlet kernel
D_L(u) = 2 sin(uL)/u, L = log X, whose Fourier transform is 2π 1_{[−L,L]}.

*Proof.* Apply K-T at s = ½ + it, x = X, and take 2Re. ξ(½ + it) is real, so Re(ξ′/ξ)(½ + it) = 0. Since
Re[1/s + 1/(s−1)] = 0 on the critical line and ψ(z+1) = ψ(z) + 1/z, this gives
α(t) + 2Re ζ′/ζ(½ + it) = Re[1/(¼ + it/2)] = 4/(1 + 4t²). Also c_X(t) − 2Re[X^{1/2−it}/(½ − it)] = −2Re[1/(½ + it)] =
−4/(1 + 4t²), because X^{1/2+it}/(½ + it) and X^{1/2−it}/(½ − it) are complex conjugates. The two rational terms cancel.
What remains is (2.3) for σ^♭; subtract T_X for σ_X. For (2.2), expand 2Re ξ_X′/ξ_X(½ + it) in the same way. ∎

The term-wise reading has no singularity at t = γ: Re[X^{iu}/(iu)] = sin(uL)/u. The pole of ζ′/ζ sits in the imaginary
parts, which cancel.

NUMERIC check (`v2-identity.json`). For X ∈ {10², 10³, 10⁴} and t ∈ {0, 5, γ₁ + 4.4934/log X, 20, 50, 101.3}, σ_X(t) −
(Z_X(t) − T_X(t)) is ≤ 6.3·10⁻⁵ in absolute value. Z_X was summed over 2,001,052 zeros with a mean-density tail.

**Corollary B1 (Hilbert–Pólya in measure form; PROVED, classical in substance).** If RH holds, then
σ_X(t) dt → 2π Σ_γ δ_γ vaguely as X → ∞: the symbols of the Euler-completed multipliers converge to the zero-counting
measure, a positive measure. If RH fails with a zero of real part ½ + δ, then for every p < 2δ and every C, m there are
arbitrarily large X with inf_t σ_X(t) < −C(1 + log X)^m X^{p/2}.

*Proof.* Fix φ ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ). ∫ T_X φ = 2Re Σ_{p≤X≤p^k} log p p^{−k/2} φ̂(k log p) → 0, because φ̂ decays faster than any
power and k log p ≥ log X. Under RH, ∫ D_L(γ − t) φ(t) dt → 2πφ(γ) for each γ. For the zeros outside a neighbourhood of supp φ, write
1/(γ − t) = 1/γ + t/(γ(γ − t)). The second piece contributes O(1/(Lγ²)) after one integration by parts, which is
summable. The first piece sums to 2Im[φ̂(L) Σ_γ e^{iγL}/γ] (symmetric sum). Under RH, Σ_γ e^{iγL}/γ =
−i(ψ(e^L) − e^L)e^{−L/2} + O(1) = O(L²). Since φ̂(L) = O(L^{−3}), this tends to 0. So ∫ σ_X φ → 2π Σ_γ φ(γ). For the
second statement:
Theorem A gives inf_t σ_X(t) ≤ ε₀(√X), since a compression is bounded below by the infimum of the symbol. The
contrapositive of Theorem C (Section 2.4) says that ε₀(λ) ≥ −C(1 + log λ)^m λ^p fails along a sequence λ → ∞ whenever a
zero has real part > (1+p)/2. ∎

*Remark (not used).* Off RH one expects σ_X dt to have no vague limit at all: a test function resonant with X^{−it} picks
up X^{δ} from an off-line zero. A clean proof has to control the resonant terms of all far off-line zeros at once (they
are weighted only by 1/|γ − γ₀|), and this note does not write one. HEURISTIC.

**Corollary B2 (the averaged identity; PROVED).** For f supported in [−ℓ, ℓ], (1/2π)∫ D_L(γ − t)|f̂(t)|² dt = |f̂(γ)|²
exactly, because the Fourier transform of D_L is 2π 1_{[−L,L]} and that indicator is 1 on supp R_f. By K-W, the compression of
M_{Z_X + τ_X} is exactly the zero side of the explicit formula, q_λ[f] = Σ_ρ f̂(γ_ρ) \overline{f̂(\bar γ_ρ)}. τ_X compresses
to 0, because its kernel X^{−2k−½} e^{−(2k+½)(|r|−W)} 1_{|r|>W} lives outside the support. For an on-line zero the term
is visibly |f̂(γ)|².
The pointwise negativity of σ_X (Section 4) is the negative lobes of D_L. The support constraint removes those lobes
exactly. Section 4.3 measures this loss.

### 2.4 Theorem C (floor ⇒ zero-free half-plane; PROVED-A from Suzuki §1 and arithmetic foundation §2)

**Theorem C.** Let 0 ≤ p < 1 and C, m ≥ 0. If ε₀(λ) ≥ −C(1 + log λ)^m λ^p for all λ ≥ λ₀, then ζ(s) ≠ 0 for
Re s > (1+p)/2. With p = 0 this is RH.

*Proof.* The normalized box e₀ = W^{−1/2} 1_{[−W/2, W/2]}, W = 2 log λ, is even, has ‖e₀‖ = 1 and lies in the form domain
(ê₀ = O(|t|^{−1})). So q_W[e₀] ≥ ε₀(λ). By (A-S), Ψ(W) = (W/2) q_W[e₀] ≥ −(W/2) C (1 + W/2)^m e^{pW/2}. With
Ψ = aW + T − D and 0 ≤ T ≤ T_∞,

  D(W) = aW + T(W) − Ψ(W) ≤ |a| W + T_∞ + (W/2) C (1 + W/2)^m e^{pW/2} ≤ C′ (1 + W)^{m+1} e^{(p/2) W}   (W ≥ W₀).

Suzuki's D and the D of arithmetic foundation §1 are the same function (an endpoint hinge contributes zero, and
4e^{W/2} − 2W − 4 = 4(e^{W/2} − 1) − 2W). Apply (A-L) with α = p/2 < ½: no zeros in Re s > ½ + p/2. ∎

This is atlas §10's V7 statement (p = 3/4 gives 7/8, p = 0 gives RH). The proof above uses one direction only, the box,
so a floor on that single direction already suffices. That direction is the Suzuki scalar criterion of atlas §11, with
polylogarithmic negativity allowed.

### 2.5 Theorem D (the floor is the complement floor; PROVED-A from spectral head T1)

Fix r > 0. By (A-H), for λ ≥ λ_r: F_λ ⊂ Dom(H_λ) and ‖H_λ F‖ ≤ η_λ ‖F‖ on F_λ, with η_λ = C_r (1 + log λ)^{−r}. Let P be
the physical L² orthogonal projector onto F_λ, and κ(λ) := inf{q_λ[y]/‖y‖² : y ≠ 0 in the form domain, Py = 0}.

**Theorem D.** min(κ(λ), 0) − 2η_λ ≤ ε₀(λ) ≤ κ(λ) for λ ≥ λ_r. Hence for every F ≥ 0, a floor ε₀ ≥ −F − O(η) holds
exactly when κ ≥ −F − O(η). The V7 floor of atlas §10 and the missing complement bound of spectral head §8 are the same
problem up to O((log λ)^{−r}).

*Proof.* The upper bound is restriction. For f in the form domain write f = x + y with x = Pf ∈ Dom(H_λ), y = f − x.
Then q[f] = ⟨x, H_λ x⟩ + 2Re⟨H_λ x, y⟩ + q[y] ≥ −η‖x‖² − 2η‖x‖‖y‖ + κ‖y‖² ≥ (min(κ,0) − 2η)‖f‖², using
‖x‖² + 2‖x‖‖y‖ ≤ 2‖f‖² and ‖y‖ ≤ ‖f‖. ∎

By T2 the head has dimension ≥ λ² − (4/π²)(log λ)² − O(log λ log log λ). By Corollary B2, q[y] = Σ_ρ ŷ(γ_ρ)
\overline{ŷ(\bar γ_ρ)}. So the complement block is where the zeros at heights up to about 2πλ² are
seen. NUMERIC illustration (`v2-ritz-*.json`, box basis, x-space quadrature): the number of Ritz values below 10⁻³ is 2,
7, 11, 22, 38, 46 at λ² = 4, 9, 16, 25, 100, 400 (24–64 modes). The lowest Ritz values are 0 within the quadrature error
(|q₀₀ − (2/W)Ψ(W)| = 6·10⁻⁶ … 5.4·10⁻⁵).

## 3. The argument

The aim is Theorem HP via the multiplier M_X. Steps 1–3 and 5 are proved. Step 4 is the step that has to carry the
arithmetic, and it fails (Section 4).

### 3.1 Step 1: compression bound (PROVED)

By Theorem A, ε₀(√X) ≥ inf_t σ_X(t) and ε₀(√X) ≥ inf_t σ^♭_X(t).

### 3.2 Step 2: reduction to a pointwise Euler-product inequality (PROVED)

By Step 1 and Theorem C, RH follows from

  **(EP_m)** there is C with σ_X(t) ≥ −C(1 + log X)^m for all real t and all X ≥ 2,

or from the same statement for σ^♭_X. By (2.2), (EP_m) says that the completed finite Euler product cannot lose modulus
too fast as σ increases through ½:
2∂_σ log|ξ_X(σ + it)|_{σ=½} ≥ −2Re[X^{1/2+it}/(½ + it)] − C(1 + log X)^m.

### 3.3 Step 3: the archimedean range (PROVED)

Put S_X := 2Σ_{p≤X} log p/(√p − 1) = Σ_{p≤X} max_θ |2 log p Re[p^{−1/2}e^{iθ}/(1 − p^{−1/2}e^{iθ})]|. By the prime
number theorem S_X = 4√X(1 + o(1)). Let t\*(X) := 2π exp(S_X + 3). For t ≥ 1, α(t) ≥ log(t/2π) − 2 (Stirling for
Re ψ(5/4 + it/2); checked numerically on [1, 10⁶]). Also |c_X(t)| ≤ 2(√X + 1)/t and |2Re ζ_X′/ζ_X| ≤ S_X. So for
|t| ≥ t\*(X), σ_X(t) ≥ log(|t|/2π) − 2 − S_X − 2(√X + 1)/|t| > 0. The same holds for σ^♭_X with S_X replaced by
2Σ_{n<X} Λ(n) n^{−1/2}.

So (EP_m) only has to be shown on the compact range |t| ≤ t\*(X) = e^{(4+o(1))√X}.

### 3.4 Step 4: the Euler–Kronecker balance (THE STEP TO BE PROVED; FAILS)

**Claim (Step 4).** For |t| ≤ t\*(X): −2Re (ζ_X′/ζ_X)(½ + it) ≤ α(t) + c_X(t) + C(1 + log X)^m.
