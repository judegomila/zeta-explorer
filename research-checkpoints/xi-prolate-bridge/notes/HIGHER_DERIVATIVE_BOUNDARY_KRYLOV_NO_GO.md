# Higher derivatives, boundary Krylov closure, and the full odd gate

## 0. Scope and outcome

This note asks whether the first derivative direction in
`notes/DERIVATIVE_RADICAL_ODD_SPLIT.md` can be enlarged into a coframe that
proves

\[
 q<\mu_-:=\lambda_{\min}(Q_-).
\]

There are three exact conclusions.

1. Every odd derivative of an even vector has an explicit finite
   endpoint-jet commutator formula.  If all positive Fourier coefficients
   of the vector are nonzero, the first `N` odd derivatives form a
   Vandermonde basis of the `N`-dimensional odd sector.  Positivity of the
   complete cross-derivative form matrix is then equivalent to the desired
   odd sign.
2. There is a cancellation-preserving alternative which does not repeatedly
   differentiate the residual.  The entire Krylov chain generated by
   `A=Q_- -qI` from `x=D\kappa` is exactly the derivative image of the
   boundary-zero Krylov chain generated by the oblique even compression.
   If `x` is cyclic, one finite Hankel moment matrix gives both the full
   transverse floor and the inverse-weighted endpoint coupling.
3. Neither identity supplies a new sign.  A fixed derivative or Krylov list
   misses an orthogonal complement, while a complete `N`-vector list is a
   congruent rewriting of `A>0`.  An exact positive Fourier-evaluation Gram
   with `N=2` has two linearly independent odd derivatives whose two
   individual shifted energies are positive, but whose full cross Gram has
   negative determinant.

Thus higher derivatives identify an exact independently replayable finite
gate, but they do not prove that gate on an unbounded arithmetic schedule.
No zero table, global Weil positivity, zero-reality assumption, signed
sector floor, equation (5.2) of `DIRECT_RH_ATTACK.md`, determinant limit, or
Riemann-hypothesis conclusion is used or proved.  The formal project state
remains `P0_INVALIDATED`.

All formulas use the complete source matrix after combining the pole,
archimedean, and minus-prime blocks.  In particular, the minus sign in
source equation (3.19) and the inclusive endpoint `n<=lambda^2` have not
been changed.

## 1. Exact finite setup

Use the source modes `V_n`, `-N<=n<=N`, and put

\[
 DV_n=nV_n,
 \qquad
 \eta=\sum_{|n|\leq N}V_n,
 \qquad
 \beta=\sum_{|n|\leq N}b_nV_n.
\]

The finite source matrix satisfies

\[
 [D,Q]=|\beta\rangle\langle\eta|
       -|\eta\rangle\langle\beta|.                 \tag{1.1}
\]

Let `H_+` and `H_-` be the inversion-even and inversion-odd sectors.  In
the normalized parity bases,

\[
 e_0^+=V_0,
 \qquad
 e_n^+={V_n+V_{-n}\over\sqrt2},
 \qquad
 e_n^-={V_n-V_{-n}\over\sqrt2},                    \tag{1.2}
\]

the plus-to-minus part of `D` obeys

\[
 De_0^+=0,
 \qquad
 De_n^+=ne_n^-.
\]

Fix a real unit even state `kappa`, and define

\[
 q=\langle\kappa,Q_+\kappa\rangle,
 \qquad
 A=Q_- -qI,
 \qquad
 x=D\kappa.                                          \tag{1.3}
\]

The target `q<mu_-` is exactly `A>0`.

## 2. All odd derivatives and their endpoint jets

For every positive integer `m`, the elementary commutator identity is

\[
 [D^m,Q]
 =\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}D^{m-1-r}[D,Q]D^r.               \tag{2.1}
\]

If `p` is even and `m` is odd, parity removes half of the terms.  With

\[
 R_p=(Q_+-qI)p,
 \quad
 \delta_r(p)=\langle D^r\eta,p\rangle,
 \quad
 \epsilon_r(p)=\langle D^r\beta,p\rangle,
\]

equations (1.1)--(2.1) give

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 A D^m p={}&D^mR_p
 -\sum_{\substack{0\leq r<m\\ r\ \mathrm{even}}}
   D^{m-1-r}\beta\,\delta_r(p)\\
 &+\sum_{\substack{0\leq r<m\\ r\ \mathrm{odd}}}
   D^{m-1-r}\eta\,\epsilon_r(p).
\end{aligned}}                                      \tag{2.2}
\]

For `m=1`, this is the endpoint-residual identity already used at the
frontier,

\[
 A Dp=DR_p-(\delta p)\beta.                         \tag{2.3}
\]

For `m=3`, new endpoint jets appear:

\[
 AD^3p=D^3R_p-D^2\beta\,\delta_0(p)
       -\beta\,\delta_2(p)+D\eta\,\epsilon_1(p).   \tag{2.4}
\]

The scalars in (2.2) are endpoint/source jets: in the centered Fourier
basis, `delta_r(p)` is the corresponding weighted endpoint coefficient
sum.  Subtracting the constant mode makes `delta_0` vanish, but it does not
make the higher jets in (2.4) vanish.  Consequently repeated differentiation
does not turn (2.3) into a boundary-free estimate.  The terms on the right
of (2.2) must remain combined.

## 3. Full derivative Vandermonde coframe

Write

\[
 \kappa=\kappa_0e_0^++\sum_{n=1}^N\kappa_ne_n^+,
 \qquad
 x_j=D^{2j+1}\kappa,
 \quad 0\leq j<N.                                   \tag{3.1}
\]

Relative to the odd basis in (1.2), the synthesis matrix whose `j`th
column is `x_j` has entries

\[
 S_{nj}=\kappa_n n^{2j+1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 \det S=\left(\prod_{n=1}^N\kappa_n n\right)
 \prod_{1\leq m<n\leq N}(n^2-m^2).}                \tag{3.2}
\]

In particular, the odd derivatives in (3.1) form a basis of `H_-` if and
only if every `kappa_n` is nonzero.  Define the complete derivative form
matrix

\[
 \mathcal H^{\rm der}_{ij}
 =\langle x_i,A x_j\rangle,
 \qquad 0\leq i,j<N.                                \tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (full derivative congruence)

If every positive-mode coefficient of `kappa` is nonzero, then

\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal H^{\rm der}=S^*AS,
 \qquad
 \operatorname {inertia}(\mathcal H^{\rm der})
 =\operatorname {inertia}(A),}                      \tag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 q<\mu_-
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal H^{\rm der}>0.}                          \tag{3.5}
\]

Also

\[
 \det\mathcal H^{\rm der}=|\det S|^2\det A.        \tag{3.6}
\]

This is an exact all-vector derivative-frame criterion.  Checking only the
diagonal quantities
`<D^(2j+1)kappa,A D^(2j+1)kappa>` is not enough; every cross term in (3.3)
is required.

Coefficient zeros are not a fundamental algebraic obstacle.  One may use
any auxiliary even vector `g` with all positive-mode coefficients nonzero,
form `D^(2j+1)g`, and retain the original scalar `q` in `A`.  The target is
then unchanged.  Equivalently, at a fixed finite `N` one may perturb the
coframe vector by

\[
 u=\sum_{n=1}^N2^{-n}e_n^+,
 \qquad g_\varepsilon=\kappa+\varepsilon u,          \tag{3.7}
\]

and choose an arbitrarily small nonzero `epsilon` outside the finite set
`{-2^n kappa_n:1<=n<=N}`.  This makes every positive-mode coefficient
nonzero.  The coframe need not be normalized.

Recomputing the Rayleigh scalar after such a perturbation is not equivalent.
If `r=(Q_+-qI)kappa`, then the exact orientation is

\[
 q_{g_\varepsilon}-q
 ={2\varepsilon\operatorname {Re}\langle u,r\rangle
   +\varepsilon^2\langle u,(Q_+-qI)u\rangle
  \over\|\kappa+\varepsilon u\|^2}.                 \tag{3.8}
\]

Positivity of `Q_- -q_(g_epsilon)I` implies the original target only when
this quotient is nonnegative, and no current source theorem forces that
orientation.  Retaining the original `q` avoids the loss but makes the
construction explicitly an auxiliary congruence, rather than a new
comparison state.

The auxiliary-vector repair gives no asymptotic gain.  The new form matrix
is still congruent to `A`, and a small coefficient introduced to repair a
zero appears multiplicatively in (3.2).  Thus any quantitative coframe
floor degenerates with that repair unless a separate coefficient lower
bound is proved.

## 4. Boundary-corrected Krylov closure

There is a cleaner chain which differentiates only once.  Define

\[
 \delta f=\langle\eta,f\rangle,
 \qquad
 H_0=\ker\delta\subset H_+,
 \qquad
 R_0=I-|e_0^+\rangle\delta,                         \tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
 T_q=R_0(Q_+-qI)|_{H_0}.                             \tag{4.2}
\]

The map `D_0=D|_(H_0):H_0->H_-` is an isomorphism.  The source commutator
gives the exact similarity

\[
\boxed{D_0T_q=AD_0.}                                 \tag{4.3}
\]

Put

\[
 f_0=R_0\kappa,
 \qquad x=Df_0=D\kappa.
\]

Assume `x!=0`; if `x=0`, this Krylov chart is unavailable and the shifted
odd block has to be checked by another full coframe.

Induction in (4.3) gives the all-order cancellation-preserving identity

\[
\boxed{
 A^j x=D_0T_q^jf_0
 \qquad(j\geq0).}                                   \tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the full odd Krylov chain is exactly an even boundary-zero oblique
Krylov chain.  In contrast with (2.2), no growing list of separated endpoint
jets appears.

Define its combined moments

\[
 \mu_j=\langle x,A^jx\rangle
      =\langle Df_0,D T_q^jf_0\rangle.               \tag{4.5}
\]

Let `d` be the cyclic dimension of `(A,x)`.  Before breakdown, put

\[
 G_d=(\mu_{i+j})_{0\leq i,j<d},
 \qquad
 H_d=(\mu_{i+j+1})_{0\leq i,j<d}.                   \tag{4.6}
\]

The first matrix is the ordinary Gram of
`x,Ax,...,A^(d-1)x`, and the second is the matrix of the `A` form on that
same Krylov space.

### Theorem 4.1 (cyclic moment closure)

One has `G_d>0`.  If `d=N`, so that `x` is cyclic for `A`, then

\[
\boxed{
 A>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_N>0,
 \qquad
 \det A={\det H_N\over\det G_N}.}                  \tag{4.7}
\]

Under the same cyclicity hypothesis `d=N`, if `A` is invertible, set

\[
 g=(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_{N-1})^T.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
 \langle x,A^{-1}x\rangle=g^*H_N^{-1}g.}           \tag{4.8}
\]

Under `A>0`, the endpoint Schur scalar from
`notes/RELATIVE_ODD_GAP_ENDPOINT_SCHUR_GATE.md` is therefore

\[
\boxed{
 a-\langle c,B_\perp^{-1}c\rangle
 ={\mu_0\over g^*H_N^{-1}g}.}                       \tag{4.9}
\]

Formula (4.9) includes both the transverse floor and the inverse-weighted
coupling, not only the Rayleigh value along `x`.

There is also a direct transverse version.  For `1<=j<N`, put

\[
 z_j=A^jx-{\mu_j\over\mu_0}x.
\]

These vectors form a basis of `x^perp` when `x` is cyclic.  Let

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{ij}={}&\mu_{i+j+1}
 -{\mu_j\mu_{i+1}+\mu_i\mu_{j+1}\over\mu_0}
 +{\mu_i\mu_j\mu_1\over\mu_0^2},\\
 h_i={}&\mu_{i+1}-{\mu_i\mu_1\over\mu_0},
 \qquad 1\leq i,j<N.                               \tag{4.10}
\end{aligned}
\]

Then `C=(<z_i,Az_j>)`, `h=(<z_i,Ax>)`, and

\[
\boxed{
 B_\perp>0\Longleftrightarrow C>0,
 \qquad
 \langle c,B_\perp^{-1}c\rangle
 ={h^*C^{-1}h\over\mu_0}.}                         \tag{4.11}
\]

Consequently the exact endpoint gate is

\[
\boxed{
 q<\mu_-
 \Longleftrightarrow
 C>0\quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \mu_1-h^*C^{-1}h>0.}                              \tag{4.12}
\]

If `d<N`, the cyclic space reduces `A`, and every moment in (4.5) is blind
to its orthogonal reducing complement.  Even the complete infinite moment
sequence of `(A,x)` then cannot prove the full odd sign.  This is the exact
cyclicity failure already seen abstractly in earlier odd-Krylov audits.

## 5. Exact source-Cauchy coefficient-zero obstruction

The coefficient issue can occur inside the exact Cauchy algebra.  Let
`N>=2`, take `b_n=0` for every mode, and choose the positive diagonal source
matrix

\[
 Qe_0^+=e_0^+,
 \qquad
 Qe_n^+=3e_n^+,
 \quad Qe_n^-=3e_n^-\quad(1\leq n<N),
 \qquad
 Qe_N^+={3\over2}e_N^+,
 \quad Qe_N^-={3\over2}e_N^- .                     \tag{5.1}
\]

More explicitly, every `+/-n` complex mode has the displayed diagonal and
every off-diagonal entry is zero.  This has exact source Cauchy form and
rank-two displacement with `beta=0`.

For

\[
 \kappa={e_0^++e_1^+\over\sqrt2},
 \qquad q=2,
\]

all odd derivatives are multiples of `e_1^-`, where `Q_- -qI` has value
one.  They never see `e_N^-`, where the same shifted block has value
`-1/2`.  Thus every derivative and Krylov scalar generated by this state is
positive while the full target fails.  This is a logical model, not the
arithmetic Weil matrix.

## 6. Exact positive evaluation-Gram obstruction

The need for all cross terms persists even when coefficient zeros are
absent and the complete matrix is a genuine positive Fourier-evaluation
Gram.

Take `L=2*pi`, modes `-2<=n<=2`, and the reflected real multiset

\[
 \Gamma=\{\pm1/4,\ \pm3/2,\ \pm5/2\},              \tag{6.1}
\]

each with multiplicity one.  With the locked centered modes, assemble

\[
 Q_{nm}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
          \widehat\phi_n(\gamma)\widehat\phi_m(\gamma).
                                                               \tag{6.2}
\]

After multiplying by the harmless positive scalar `pi`, every entry is
rational.  The six evaluation rows contain a nonsingular `5 by 5` Cauchy
minor, so `Q` is strictly positive definite.  Reflection gives inversion
symmetry, and the partial-fraction identity gives the exact source Cauchy
off-diagonal form.

Use the real even vector whose complex mode coefficients in the order
`(-2,-1,0,1,2)` are

\[
 \kappa=(-3,1,-1,1,-3).                              \tag{6.3}
\]

Its positive-mode coefficients are both nonzero, and its Rayleigh scalar
for `pi Q` is

\[
 q={195904\over11025}.                               \tag{6.4}
\]

In the normalized odd parity basis, the operator matrix of `pi Q_-` is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  36608/3675&-512/135\\
  -512/135&23552/1323
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore

\[
 A=\pi Q_- -qI
 =\begin{pmatrix}
  -17216/2205&-512/135\\
  -512/135&1088/33075
 \end{pmatrix},                                     \tag{6.5}
\]

and

\[
 \det A=-{3203231744\over218791125}<0.              \tag{6.6}
\]

After removing a common positive normalization, the first two odd
derivatives are

\[
 x_0=(1,-6)^T,
 \qquad x_1=(1,-24)^T.                              \tag{6.7}
\]

They form a basis.  Nevertheless,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \langle x_0,Ax_0\rangle&={20416\over525}>0,\\
 \langle x_1,Ax_1\rangle&={709952\over3675}>0,\\
 \langle x_0,Ax_1\rangle&={135616\over1225},
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{6.8}
\]

while their complete derivative form matrix has

\[
 \boxed{
 \det\mathcal H^{\rm der}
 =-{12812926976\over2701125}<0.}                    \tag{6.9}
\]

Thus positive evaluation-Gram structure, exact Cauchy displacement,
reflection, integer multiplicity, nonzero Fourier coefficients, and
positive energy along every displayed derivative basis vector do not imply
the shifted odd sign.  The missing information is exactly the oriented
cross Gram in (3.3).

## 7. Exact conclusion

Higher derivatives and boundary Krylov vectors do give complete finite
coordinates for the frontier Schur gate:

- the derivative Vandermonde criterion (3.5) is available whenever a full
  coefficient coframe is used;
- the cyclic moment criterion (4.12) reconstructs the transverse block and
  inverse-weighted coupling whenever `D kappa` is cyclic; and
- an auxiliary all-nonzero vector repairs coefficient zeros without changing
  the target shift.

But completeness costs `N` vectors and moments through order `2N-1`.  At
that point the matrices are congruent to the original shifted odd block.
Any shorter chain needs an independent positive theorem on its unseen
complement.  The source commutator supplies exact endpoint jets and
similarity, not their sign.  The remaining arithmetic obligation is still a
source-specific proof of the full cross-moment matrix, or an equivalent
transverse floor plus inverse-weighted endpoint orientation, on one common
unbounded schedule.
