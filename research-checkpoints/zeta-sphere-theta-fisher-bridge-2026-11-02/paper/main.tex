\begin{abstract}
We organize a computational and analytic research program around a paired theta
kernel whose all-order positive-definiteness problem is tied to the Riemann
hypothesis. Let $H_{\mathrm{cut}}$ denote a spatial lower integration limit.

The principal finite theorem is analytic rather than a giant matrix
calculation.  For the normalized bridge density $\Lambda_a$, every nonzero real
polynomial $p$ of degree at most $3\cdot10^{10}$ satisfies, uniformly for
$0\le a\le1/2$,
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}p(t)^2\Lambda_a(t)\,dt
 >\bigl(0.00172-e^{-2\cdot10^{11}}\bigr)
   \int_{-4}^{4}p(t)^2\,dt>0.
\]
The proof combines a certified central theta reserve, the exact localization
of a possible negative zero-pair halo, the published Platt--Trudgian critical-line
verification through height $3\cdot10^{12}$, and an explicit gamma-decay plus
polynomial-tail estimate.  The external zero computation is a dependency and
was not rerun.  The standard outer-shift argument then gives positivity through
the same finite degree for every $a>0$ at $H_{\mathrm{cut}}=0$, including the
normalized $a=0$ limit.  For complex polynomials the statement uses $|p|^2$;
it is not an order statement for the bilinear expression $p^2$.

A separate direct interval certificate proves the even block on degrees
$0,2,4,6,8,10$ and the inherited odd block on degrees $1,3,5,7,9$ are positive
for every $0\le a\le1/2$ and $0\le H_{\mathrm{cut}}\le1/8$.  Thus the actual
degree-at-most-ten Gram is positive on that compact rectangle.  This direct
$H_{10}$ theorem retains positive-cutoff content that the much larger
verified-zero degree theorem does not provide.  The corrected odd-degree-11
interval run is inconclusive in every shift slab; an earlier six-channel
adaptation had an unfilled row and column and is invalid, not negative evidence.

The program has also rigorously disproved a stronger shortcut: arbitrary spatial
cumulative tails need not be positive, even at the explicit positive shift
$a=10^{-4}$. A positive-time refinement exhibits the correct mechanism on a
restricted actual band: the tail is negative, the central contribution is
positive, and their complete sum is positive.

The information-relaxation layer gives the genuine bridge Fisher bound
$I_a\le 2/17$, the regular endpoint bound $I_{a^2}(0)\le4/867$, and exponential
decay $I'(h)\le-14I(h)$ for the complete theta orbit law. These estimates enter
exact signed identities rather than serving as positivity theorems. At the
existing odd-19 endpoint, interval evaluation proves that innovation pays
coefficient motion. Newman payment is certified at degree $2$ and, after an
exact zero-signed-integral old-feature gauge, at degree $5$ for
$a=1/4$, $0\le\lambda\le1$. A separate odd same-parity degree-nine endpoint
certificate retains positive regional reserve at $a=1/4$.

At the normalized endpoint, an exact complete-theta identity writes every
quadratic contact $h[t^2-\alpha]$ as a square plus a positive curvature
remainder. The all-real bound $4k^2+k''>1254$, with
$k=(\log\psi)''$, pays this whole quadratic family. At the already-known odd
degree-seven rung, a split at $|u|=1/8$ produces a negative region-optimized
core, but variation forced by the globally selected regression coefficients
pays it with certified positive surplus. These are finite compensation
mechanisms, not a new hierarchy order or degree extension.

An exact inverse-residual replay reproduces the known normalized odd
degree-seven Schur bound with explicit lower-metric conditioning. A modular
Bessel/divisor identity exhibits certified signed cancellation: at one
frequency a negative unweighted section coexists with a positive full bridge
transform. The exact normalized-endpoint dyadic comparison threshold is
$107521$, with exhaustive integer margin $11$ through $2^{21}$ and sharp
failure at $107520$. These results retain the signed pieces; none is an
all-degree positivity theorem.

An all-finite-degree theorem controls every raised spatial moment in the
complete-theta positive-parent norm, but gives no signed Schur reserve. Exact
block transport and modular reflection reduce one normalized-endpoint route to
one negative direction, while a canonical source cancels inverse feedback.  An
exact Hardy prefix identity compresses all subset debits to one translation
norm, and an exact canonical flux retains every root-deletion cost; neither has
a known degree-uniform sign.  Polynomial localization proves that a negative
open interval in the actual fixed-shift density, if one exists, must eventually
defeat both parity ladders.  A degree-20 selected residual also refutes an
uncorrected local canonical-sign shortcut, without determining its integrated
pivot.  The finite signed core beyond the new threshold, all-degree closure,
and the positive-cutoff hierarchy beyond degree ten remain unproved.  Nothing
here is an RH proof or a claim of literature novelty.

\section{The paired theta bridge}

Let $\psi$ be the complete even positive theta profile in the campaign
normalization, and write
\begin{equation}
  F(z):=\Xi(z)=\int_0^\infty \psi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
  \qquad z=t+ia.
\end{equation}
For $a>0$ define the positive parent and its parameter derivative
\begin{align}
 K_a(v)&=\int_0^\infty \cosh(2ah)\psi(h-v)\psi(h+v)\,dh,\\
 C_a(v)&=\frac12\partial_a K_a(v)
       =\int_0^\infty h\sinh(2ah)\psi(h-v)\psi(h+v)\,dh.
\end{align}
With Fourier convention $\widehat f(k)=\int_\R f(v)e^{-ikv}\,dv$,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat K_a(2t)=|F(t+ia)|^2=:M(t,a),\qquad
 \widehat C_a(2t)=\frac12\partial_a M(t,a)=:W(t,a).
 \label{eq:bridge-fourier}
\end{equation}
The parent $K_a$ is a positive Gram for every $a$. The derivative $C_a$ is the
target and need not inherit positivity merely from its parent. Nonnegativity of
$W(t,a)$ for every real $t$ and every positive shift in the critical strip is
the horizontal-modulus formulation of the RH target, consistent with the
classical logarithmic-derivative criteria of Lagarias and the associated-kernel
literature \cite{Lagarias1999,Lagarias2005,SondowDumitrescu2010,Csordas2015}.

The exact paired-coordinate derivation is essential. An ordinary
two-dimensional integral receives no nonzero mass from the line $u=v$; that
line has two-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. The repository's corrected
measure audit replaces the withdrawn ``positive diagonal channel'' with the
actual paired $(r,s)$ identity. Nothing in this paper uses the stale diagonal
argument.

For normalized moments put
\begin{equation}
 \mu_n(a)=(-1)^n\frac{C_a^{(2n)}(0)}{a},
\end{equation}
with the removable $a=0$ limit. The associated Hankel blocks are finite tests
of the full positivity hierarchy. They are useful footholds, but no fixed
finite order can discharge the universal quantifiers in RH.

Throughout, $H_{\mathrm{cut}}$ denotes a real spatial lower cutoff, whereas
$H_n$ denotes a finite hierarchy label. In particular,
$H_{\mathrm{cut}}=0$ is not the hierarchy label $H_0$. The normalized
$a=0$ object below is always the divided-by-$a$ removable limit; the raw bridge
$C_0$ vanishes.

\section{Heat, Ornstein--Uhlenbeck, and Newman evolution}

\subsection{Spatial heat preserves the original zeros}

Let $\psi_s=e^{s\partial_u^2}\psi$. Its transform is
\begin{equation}
 F_s(z)=e^{-sz^2}F(z),
\end{equation}
so every zero and its multiplicity are unchanged. With
$E=e^{-2s(t^2-a^2)}$,
\begin{equation}
 M_s=EM,\qquad W_s=E(W+2saM).
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 C_s=e^{2a^2s}e^{(s/2)\partial_v^2}(C_0+2saK_0).
\end{equation}
The positive term $2saK_0$ is a Gaussian reserve. Subtracting it gives
\begin{equation}
 C_s-2saK_s=e^{2a^2s}e^{(s/2)\partial_v^2}C_0,
\end{equation}
whose Fourier sign is equivalent to the original bridge sign. The reserve
cannot cure an off-axis zero: near a simple zero the negative term in $W$ is
linear in transverse displacement, whereas the reserve is quadratic.
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck relaxation likewise rescales nonreal zeros rather than
making them real.

\subsection{Newman forcing remains signed}

For the de Bruijn--Newman deformation \cite{RodgersTao2020},
\begin{equation}
 F_\lambda(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{\lambda u^2}\psi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
\end{equation}
define
\begin{equation}
 W[G](t,a):=\Re\!\left(iG'(t+ia)\overline{G(t+ia)}\right),
 \qquad W_\lambda:=W[F_\lambda].
\end{equation}
Then one has spectral backward heat
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\lambda F_\lambda=-\partial_z^2F_\lambda.
\end{equation}
Product differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\lambda W_\lambda
 =-\partial_t^2W_\lambda+2W[F_\lambda'].
 \label{eq:newman-forcing}
\end{equation}
The derivative-ladder term $2W[F_\lambda']$ has no proved favorable sign.
It cannot be suppressed under the name of entropy dissipation.

For a polynomial $p$, define the signed quadratic forms
\begin{align}
 Q_\lambda[p]&:=\int_\R |p(t)|^2W[F_\lambda](t,a)\,dt,\\
 Q_\lambda^{[1]}[p]&:=\int_\R |p(t)|^2W[F_\lambda'](t,a)\,dt.
\end{align}
For the monic $Q_\lambda$-Schur residual polynomial $p_\lambda^*$, exact heat
transport of the test polynomial yields
\begin{equation}
 S_\lambda'=2\left(
 Q_\lambda^{[1]}[p_\lambda^*]
 -Q_\lambda[(p_\lambda^*)']\right).
 \label{eq:schur-flow}
\end{equation}
A safe positive Sobolev envelope pays both signed terms, leading to a finite
certificate template: certify the lower block along the interval, certify a
terminal reserve, and prove that the integrated positive energy budget is
smaller than that reserve. This template has not been closed uniformly in
degree.

It has, however, been closed at one actual finite order. For the theta kernel
at degree $n=2$, $a=1/4$, and $0\le\lambda\le1$, an independent
$60000\times1000$ Arb rectangle certificate proves, before the common
$\pi/2$ normalization,
\begin{align}

 2\int_0^1 B_{\mathrm{pair}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda
   &<3.1520578244214.
 \label{eq:paid-newman-n2}
\end{align}
The paid residual therefore exceeds $0.4922103693903$ unscaled and exceeds
$0.773162230542629$ after multiplication by the common $\pi/2$ factor. The
certificate retains derivative-square cancellation and reflection before
taking a positive-part bound. It validates the paid mechanism at an
already-known finite order; it proves no new positivity order, all-shift

The same mechanism closes at degree $5$ only after retaining a legal

 \label{eq:paid-newman-n5}
\end{align}
The simpler certified bounds are $S(1)>375000$, budget $<330000$, and reserve

There is also an exact forcing reduction. For the real phase-normalized

\begin{equation}
 \int_{\R}u^2FK\,du=-\int_{\R}u(K^2+FL)\,du.
\end{equation}
Actual signed lower same-parity orthogonality removes the $uFL$ term, yielding
\begin{equation}
 D=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(
     \int_{\R}u^3F(u)^2\,du

 \label{eq:orthogonality-forcing}

This is an exact identity, not a sign theorem. The second integral is an
opposite-parity bridge energy whose sign needs an independent proof: the

higher-degree comparison has been paid, and a local positive-part estimate can
be more expensive than the paired formula.

\section{Exact block transport and the modular one-direction reduction}

\subsection{Joint endpoint feedback before positive parts}

In common endpoint coordinates, let $A_0$ be the initial old block,
$e_1=(c_1,1)$ the terminal residual coefficient vector, and
$r_0=A_0c_1+b_0$. For each spatial block $I$, exact expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 \Delta s_I
 =e_1^T(G_{I,1}-G_{I,0})e_1
 +2r_0^TA_0^{-1}r_{I,0}
 -r_0^TA_0^{-1}A_{I,0}A_0^{-1}r_0.
 \label{eq:local-feedback}
\end{equation}
The local terms are signed. Only after assembling the complete expression may
a positive part be taken. Summation over all blocks yields
\begin{equation}
 \sum_I\Delta s_I
 =e_1^T(H_1-H_0)e_1+r_0^TA_0^{-1}r_0.
 \label{eq:joint-feedback}
\end{equation}
Thus the global positive quadratic is produced by cancellation among signed
local feedback terms, not by bounding those terms separately.

\subsection{Modular reflection leaves one signed direction}

only by its known pivots. If $R$ is polynomial reflection, $X_N$ the skew

 \qquad r_N=2a(N+1)(-1)^N.

 =4a\left\|z_\perp-\frac{z_Nw_\perp}{4a}\right\|^2

 \qquad

 \label{eq:one-negative}
\end{equation}
For $N\ge1$, this form has exactly $N$ positive directions and one negative
direction; for $N=0$ it is zero. This is an inertia statement under
congruence, not an eigenvalue-size bound in the target coordinates and not a
payment theorem.

\subsection{Signed pieces of the canonical source}

 \label{eq:boundary-squares}
\end{equation}

 \label{eq:trace-boundary}
\end{equation}
Thus the boundary correction has a sign; $R_{jj}$ and

Only the boundary correction and the coefficient $\gamma_N$ are signed. The

\section{From the correlated scalar residual to a finite arithmetic core}

\subsection{Scalar positivity is not a Gram theorem}

Retain the complete theta density $\Lambda$ from the preceding subsection. For
$d>0$ define its scalar resolvent
\begin{equation}
 J(d)=\int_{\R}\frac{\Lambda(t)}{t^2+d^2}\,dt.
\end{equation}
The reviewed positive-primitive identity is
\begin{equation}
 J(d)=\frac{\pi}{2d}\iint_{\R^2}(u-v)^2\psi(u)\psi(v)
       e^{-d|u+v|}\,du\,dv>0.
 \label{eq:scalar-resolvent}
\end{equation}
For every $R>0$ it also gives the explicit lower bound
\begin{equation}
 J(d)\ge\frac{\pi}{d}e^{-2dR}M_0(R)M_2(R)>0,
 \label{eq:scalar-resolvent-lower}
\end{equation}
where $M_0,M_2$ are the corresponding positive truncated orbit moments.

An earlier base-$2$ construction supplies a positive theta primitive
$\chi_2=2x^{5/4}\Theta_4'(x)$ and an exact reflected inverse relation for
$a<1/2$. It produces signed energy identities, not a hidden reserve: at the
raw endpoint the apparently positive pieces cancel exactly, and on every
region the scalar-tail bulk cancels its boundary. Likewise, no
degree-uniform strict reflected contraction can hold in the actual even
polynomial ladder. These reviewed limitations motivate retaining the complete
correlated residual below.

This scalar sign does not imply positivity of a higher rational Gram. Put
$s=d^2$ and, assuming only the current even and odd prefix blocks are positive,
separate the constant coefficient of a polynomial $p$. Exact square completion
has the form
\begin{equation}
 I[p]=(z+p_0C^{-1}b)^*C(z+p_0C^{-1}b)+|p_0|^2\Delta_N(s),
 \qquad
 \Delta_N(s)=J(d)-b^TC^{-1}b.
 \label{eq:rational-square}
\end{equation}
Here $C$ is the known current rational Gram and $b$ its constant-term column.
The actual monic current polynomial $P_N$ satisfies $P_N(-s)\ne0$ without
assuming the next pivot, and
\begin{equation}

       \frac{P_N(t^2)^2\Lambda(t)}{t^2+s}\,dt}
      {P_N(-s)^2}.
 \label{eq:delta-rational}
\end{equation}
Thus $J(d)>0$ is a useful input, not a replacement for the missing correlated
residual. A fixed scalar floor is also insufficient at all degrees: under
indefinitely positive prefixes, the Gauss resolvent converges to $J$ while the
available fixed lower floor stays strictly below it.

\subsection{Adaptive resolvent and correlated conditional mean}

\end{equation}

 \label{eq:correlated-mean}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the first expression is $4\pi\E[C_N(R_s)]$ for

 \label{eq:gauss-source}

This is an exact current-prefix cancellation. Reflected finite-orbit bounds

\begin{equation}
 A_f(R)=\int_{\R}f(R+w)f(R-w)\,dw,\qquad
 C_f(R)=\int_{\R}w^2f(R+w)f(R-w)\,dw.
\end{equation}
If $d(k)$ is the divisor count and
\begin{equation}
 \ell_2(k)=\sum_{n\mid k}\left[\log\left(\frac{n^2}{k}\right)\right]^2,
\end{equation}
then the complete, untruncated theta kernel satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C(r)=\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-1/2}\left[
 d(k)C_f\!\left(r+\tfrac12\log k\right)
 +\frac{\ell_2(k)}4A_f\!\left(r+\tfrac12\log k\right)
 \right].
 \label{eq:divisor-grouping}
\end{equation}
The identity follows by grouping orbit pairs $nm=k$ and shifting the midpoint;
the odd cross term integrates to zero. Absolute integrated convergence pays
the grouping and every fixed derivative used below. No finite-orbit
approximation occurs in \eqref{eq:divisor-grouping}.

\subsection{Twin-parity arithmetic remainder}

For the even ladder, positivity of the actual two-parity prefix implies that
the monic polynomial $P_N$ has positive roots $x_\ell$ and therefore
\begin{equation}
 L_N=P_N(-D^2/4)^2
     =\prod_\ell(D^2/4+x_\ell)^2
     =\sum_{j\ \mathrm{even}}a_jD^j,\qquad a_j\ge0.
 \label{eq:positive-operator-coefficients}
\end{equation}
Put $K=4N$ and
\begin{equation}
 W(S)=\sum_j a_j(2S)^j=P_N(-S^2)^2,
\end{equation}
and define the degree-only constants
\begin{align}
 B_K&=6400(K+2)^2(K+3)^3,\nonumber\\
 R_K&=(2K+7)(3K+7),\nonumber\\
 z_K&=\max\left\{64\pi,\ K(9+2K),\ \frac{R_K}{4},\
                \frac12\log(24B_K)\right\},
 \qquad
 k_K=\left\lceil\frac{z_K}{\pi}\right\rceil.
 \label{eq:degree-only-cutoff}
\end{align}
The reviewed central/noncentral comparison retains the whole positive
coefficient polynomial $W$, which cancels from the cutoff estimate. Every even
divisor summand with $k\ge k_K$ is then strictly positive for every $r\ge0$.
Thus the omitted infinite remainder after the finite signed core is positive.
The cutoff is $O(N^2)$; for $K\ge4$ one may use the conservative bound
\begin{equation}
 k_K\le\left\lceil\frac{100K^2}{\pi}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
The actual coefficients remain inside the finite core; only their contribution
to the cutoff location has cancelled.

There is a parity reversal. For the adjacent odd target,
\begin{equation}
 C_N^{\mathrm{odd}}
 =-\frac{D^2}{4}Q_N(-D^2/4)^2C=-A_NC,
 \label{eq:odd-sign-reversal}
\end{equation}
where $A_N$ has nonnegative even-derivative coefficients and highest order
$K=4N+2$. The same high-index single-orbit kernels are positive before the
leading minus sign. Hence all sufficiently high odd arithmetic summands are
strictly negative. They must be paid, not discarded as a reserve.

Let $U_k$ denote the actual signed divisor summand in the parity under
consideration, and define the corresponding partial sum
$S_J=\sum_{k=1}^{J-1}U_k$. For an integer
\begin{equation}
 J\ge\max\left\{k_K,e^2,\frac{R_K}{4\pi},
                         \frac{4(K+3)}{\pi}\right\},
\end{equation}
the complete absolute remainder is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Tail}_K(J)=\frac{4B_K}{\pi}J^{1/4}
 \left(1+\frac{\log^2J}{4}\right)
 W(2\pi J)e^{-\pi J/2}.
 \label{eq:arithmetic-tail-payment}
\end{equation}
In the normalized contact convention this gives
\begin{align}
 \frac{h_N^{\mathrm{even}}}{4\pi}
   &\in[S_J,S_J+\operatorname{Tail}_K(J)],\nonumber\\
 \frac{h_N^{\mathrm{odd}}}{4\pi}
   &\in[S_J-\operatorname{Tail}_K(J),S_J].
 \label{eq:twin-tail-enclosures}
\end{align}
For the even ladder, a positive exact finite partial sum beyond the sign cutoff
certifies the pivot. For the odd ladder, positivity requires the stronger
payment $S_J>\operatorname{Tail}_K(J)$. The even procedure is a complete
semidecision if the true pivot is positive, but no uniform termination or
effort bound is proved. No new pivot was computed in v30.

\subsection{Dyadic localization of the eta payment}

For an actual inverse-selected residual $p$, the eta-shift correlations collapse
into the same divisor kernels as the target. If $L=\log2$, $q=2^{-1/2}$,
$b_m=q^mC_m$, and $X_v$ is the exact valuation-$v$ arithmetic shell, then
\begin{equation}
 b_m=\sum_{v\ge m}(v-m+1)X_v,
 \qquad b_m-2b_{m+1}+b_{m+2}=X_m.
 \label{eq:dyadic-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Beyond the degree-only sign cutoff, the even $b_m$ are positive, decreasing,
and discretely convex; the odd $b_m$ are negative, increasing to zero, and
discretely concave. For each fixed degree the omitted cost tail has an explicit
superexponential bound in $2^m$. An exact divisor-variance comparison also
shows that sufficiently high even target terms pay their eta subtraction
termwise. In odd parity the inequality reverses, so the negative arithmetic
remainder remains a real cost.

The v32 arithmetic refinement makes the dyadic comparison sharp. If
$v_2(j)$ is the dyadic valuation, then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\ell_2(j)}{d(j)\log^22}
 =\frac13\sum_{p^\alpha\parallel j}
   \alpha(\alpha+2)(\log_2p)^2.
 \label{eq:dyadic-prime-powers}
\end{equation}
The least integer $T$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\ell_2(j)}{d(j)}
 \ge\log^22\,[6+5v_2(j)]
 \qquad\text{for every }j\ge T
 \label{eq:sharp-dyadic-threshold}
\end{equation}
is exactly
\begin{equation}
 T=107521.
\end{equation}
For $107521\le j\le2^{21}$, put
$q_p=\lfloor\log_2(p^4)\rfloor$. Independent prime-power accumulation checks
the exact integer inequality
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{p^\alpha\parallel j}\alpha(\alpha+2)q_p^2
 \ge48[6+5v_2(j)]
\end{equation}
with minimum margin $11$. The analytic estimate covers $j\ge2^{21}$, while
$107520=2^{10}\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7$ fails by exact power inequalities, proving
sharpness.

This threshold is for the normalized $a=0$ coefficient inequality and applies
only after the separate degree-dependent derivative-sign cutoff. It lets even
high-index target terms dominate their same-residual eta terms; odd high terms
remain negative and require an absolute payment. It proves neither the finite
signed core nor a positive-shift or all-degree theorem.

The low shells stay signed. In particular, valid known-rung replays show an
actual odd residual with negative dyadic aggregate $D$ and an actual even
residual with negative remaining aggregate $Y$, although both complete target
contacts are positive. Thus separate laws $D\ge0$ and $Y\ge0$ cannot be the
uniform proof. The exact correlated identity survives; its low finite terms
still require joint payment.

The central open arithmetic lemma is therefore concrete: prove the actual
finite signed divisor core positive in the even ladder, and large enough to pay
\eqref{eq:arithmetic-tail-payment} in the odd ladder, uniformly in degree. The
positive even infinite remainder and the explicit odd payment do not determine
that core.

\section{A positive death chain and a still-signed bridge}

The orbit marginals admit an exact time-inhomogeneous pure-death process. For
$m<n$ set
\begin{equation}
 q_{n,m}(h)=p_m(h)(S_m(h)-S_n(h)),
\end{equation}
with no upward jumps. The forward equation is precisely
$p_n'=p_n(S_n-\E S)$. This supplies a positive Markov realization and a
nonnegative jump energy, while retaining the signed moving-feature drift.

For a finite observable $V$, the compensated process has exact covariance cost
in any already-certified lower metric $A\pd$:
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_u(V)(n):=\sum_{m<n}q_{n,m}(u)
 [V(m)-V(n)][V(m)-V(n)]^*\succeq0.
\end{equation}
Writing $M_t$ for the compensated jump martingale,
\begin{equation}
 \E[M_t^*A^{-1}M_t]
 =\int_s^t\E\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}\Gamma_u(V))\,du.
\end{equation}
At a random spatial horizon with the genuine bridge density, the same process
gives an exact forcing representation for the Schur residual. It also gives an
exact conditional mismatch identity in which positive innovation and initial
or horizon covariance are subtracted from the raw second moment.

The pair-chain common-atom estimate proves a degree-independent conditional
innovation fraction greater than $0.508$ from $0$ to $1/16$ and greater than
$0.9955$ from $0$ to $1/4$. These are fractions of each terminal feature's own
conditional orbit covariance. They are not fractions of the bridge Schur
surplus, whose covariance and spatial means may grow with degree.

\section{All-degree analytic reductions and obstruction tests}

The finite theorem \eqref{eq:v33-30b} changes the practical frontier but not
the logical all-degree problem. The v33 continuation develops several exact
reductions and then stress-tests the shortcuts they might suggest. The useful
distinction is between an identity that keeps every signed term and an estimate
that actually pays those terms.

\subsection{Hardy prefix compression and a shrinking critical band}

Fix a real-rooted polynomial $p$ and let $q_S$ delete the labeled roots in a
nonempty subset $S$. If $M_q(b)$ is the positive norm of $q$ on the horizontal
line $b$ and $H_p(b)$ is the corresponding Hardy norm of $p(t+ib)$, then the
finite triangular expansion is exact:
\begin{equation}
 J_a[p]=H_p'(a)-\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}
 \left(2|S|a^{2|S|-1}M_{q_S}(a)+a^{2|S|}J_a[q_S]\right).
 \label{eq:v33-hardy-budget}
\end{equation}
For an actual inverse-selected residual, prefix positivity makes every
displayed debit positive. The source $H_p'(a)$ is itself positive, but that
fact alone does not prove that it dominates the deletion budget. At $a=0$,
the divided limit of \eqref{eq:v33-hardy-budget} is exactly the earlier
root-deletion identity; the apparent new source has not removed the old cost.
All subset costs can also be compressed to one actual-prefix translation norm
using the finite nilpotent differentiation matrix. This is a cleaner exact
target, not a payment theorem.

One tempting universal-factor shortcut is unavailable: the complete theta
profile has a flat, nonremovable boundary at $u=i\pi/4$. After the alternating
modular transformation it vanishes faster than every power on approach to that
point, so a holomorphic continuation would force it to vanish identically.
Multiplication by an entire nonvanishing Gaussian-like factor cannot repair
this boundary. This refutes that proposed factorization criterion only; it
does not rule out strip-holomorphic or theta-specific estimates for
\eqref{eq:v33-hardy-budget}.

There is nevertheless a degree-dependent analytic entry into the critical
shift region. For each fixed $n$, an explicit tail inequality chooses
$R_n=O(n\log n)$ and
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_n=\min\!\left(\frac14,
       \frac{1}{6\log(R_n+3)}\right).
 \label{eq:v33-critical-band}
\end{equation}
The Mossinghoff--Trudgian--Yang zero-free region
\cite{MossinghoffTrudgianYang2024}, the paired halo formula
\eqref{eq:v33-zero-pair}, and complete-theta contour decay then prove the
degree-at-most-$n$ global form positive for
$a\ge1/2-\epsilon_n$. This supplies no fixed interior shift valid at every
degree: $\epsilon_n\to0$. It is uncut spatially, depends on a published
zero-free theorem, and asserts neither a practical decimal threshold nor RH.

\subsection{Fixed-shift localization and the signed-flux remainder}

At a fixed interior shift, the exponentially decaying bridge density is
continuous. If it is negative on any open interval, polynomial concentration
constructs a finite even test and a finite odd test with negative quadratic
form. Conversely, pointwise nonnegativity immediately makes every polynomial
form nonnegative. Thus all-degree polynomial positivity at a fixed shift is
equivalent to pointwise nonnegativity of the actual density there. The result
does \emph{not} assert that the actual theta density has a negative interval;
it proves that an exponentially tiny remote negative interval could not be
paid at all degrees merely by bounding its total mass.

The normalized endpoint has an exact canonical flux for every real-rooted
parity polynomial. It retains every deletion and produces a polynomial flux
of degree at most $2n+3$. The higher remainder has denominator only $T_0^2$,
independent of $n$, and an exact Bell-type recurrence. These are genuine
structural gains. But the Taylor radius is exactly $\pi/2$, forcing
factorial-order coefficient growth, and the central canonical remainders take
both signs infinitely often in the larger real-rooted test class. Therefore
neither a geometric absolute-coefficient estimate nor generic local
nonnegativity closes the target. The sign for the actual inverse-selected
coefficients after integration remains open.

The associated nonlinear theta/Bezout module is also exact: it gives a
rank-one polynomial recurrence and a divergence-certificate framework, with
base cases $n=0,1$. A fixed rational-coefficient linear ODE closure is
impossible because the theta profile has infinitely many boundary
singularities. This excludes that finite linear closure, not nonlinear,
infinite, or nonrational approaches.

\subsection{Selected degree-20 and dual-transport cautions}

For the actual normalized even degree-20 inverse-selected residual, the
uncorrected canonical local factor is rigorously negative near $u=14/25$:
\begin{equation}
 -2697.34<f_{20}(14/25)<-2697.17.
 \label{eq:v33-selected20-local}
\end{equation}
Hence a proof that simply declares this canonical local remainder nonnegative
cannot work. Scalar proportional gauges fail locally, and a two-point check
excludes every real one-dimensional proportional gauge on a small
neighborhood. Neither calculation annihilates the full cross-moment vector,
so neither is a negative optimized-dual witness or an integrated pivot sign;
the degree-20 target moment was not computed.

The normalized selected orthogonality problem does have an attained dual
minimum with an atomic optimizer using at most $N+1$ atoms. Its positivity is
unproved in general. Enlarging the selected residual to arbitrary polynomial
multiples satisfying finitely many orthogonality constraints is not a safe
shortcut: an actual-theta constrained negative energy exists in that larger
class. Likewise, rigid supporting-plane transport can fail even in known
positive settings. Adaptive reoptimization yields conditional fixed-degree
continuation and eventual large-shift positivity, but its optimizer norm can
grow while the fractional margin decays. Frozen raw linear-program outputs
remain diagnostics; they are not uniform SOS or atomic-repair certificates.

\section{Two endpoint reductions and the global remaining obligation}

 \label{eq:sharpened-endpoint}
\end{equation}
The old inverse-feedback term has already cancelled. What remains unproved is

ladder needs its finite partial sum to exceed the explicit negative-tail bound

criteria, but no proof establishes them uniformly in degree. They are not
equivalent to \eqref{eq:sharpened-endpoint}, and neither endpoint route alone is
equivalent to the full all-shift problem.

The constants must remain uniform as the degree grows.
