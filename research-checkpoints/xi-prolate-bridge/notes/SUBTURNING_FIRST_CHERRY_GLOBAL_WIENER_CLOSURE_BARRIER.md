# Subturning first-cherry global Wiener closure barrier

## 0. Exact outcome and claim boundary

Retain the two fixed labels `j=0,4`, the action

\[
 D_c(s)=c\left\{\sqrt{1+(s/2c)^2}-1
 -{s\over2c}\operatorname {arsinh}(s/2c)\right\},
 \qquad c\longrightarrow\infty,                                  \tag{0.1}
\]

and the exact Riccati coefficient

\[
 \alpha_j(s)={s+2\over s+1}{b_j(s+4)\over b_j(s)}
 \exp\{D_c(s+j+3)-D_c(s+j-1)\}.                                  \tag{0.2}
\]

This note closes one narrower proposal for the all-order Closure Lemma
`CL`.  It does **not** close or disprove `CL` itself.  The exact result is
that the first nonlinear cherry cannot be returned, with a constant
independent of `c`, to the global positive-support Laplace/Wiener atom
class generated by the exact Green atoms.

More precisely, let `X_(j,u)` be the minimal Green response to the
Laplace atom `exp(-u(s-3))`.  If the first-cherry response were an exact
superposition

\[
 Y_{j;u,v}(s)=\int_{[0,\infty)}X_{j,q}(s)\,d\nu_{j,c;u,v}(q)          \tag{0.3}
\]

with uniformly bounded total variation, then at `u=v=0` one would have

\[
 \|\nu_{j,c;0,0}\|_{\rm TV}
 \geq\{|I|^2+o(1)\}c^{2/3},
 \qquad
 |I|^2=0.349831425170034047\ldots.                                 \tag{0.4}
\]

Thus neither total variation nor total variation plus first moment is
uniformly bounded.  The obstruction is detected at the turning height
`Im(s)=2c`, but analytic continuation transports it into a contradiction
with an exact global Laplace representation even when (0.3) is first
asserted only on the open subturning band

\[
 \operatorname {Re}s>3,
 \qquad |\operatorname {Im}s|<2c^{3/4}.                            \tag{0.5}
\]

This does not contradict the low-band atom or bilinear pointwise bounds.
Those bounds are context only: pointwise control of one cherry is weaker
than returning that cherry to the atom class with bounded coefficient
measure.  A genuinely band-local tail norm, a non-Laplace class, or an
approximate terminal representation remains possible.  No all-tree
bound, Birkhoff connection estimate, arithmetic sign, eta decay, RH
implication, or de Bruijn--Newman bound follows.  The project state
remains `P0_INVALIDATED`.

## 2. Exact atoms and the first-cherry representation test

Let `Sg(s)=g(s+2)` and

\[
 L_j=I+\alpha_jS,
 \qquad
 A_{j,n}(s)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha_j(s+2k),
 \qquad A_{j,0}=1.                                                   \tag{2.1}
\]

For `u>=0`, put

\[
 e_u(s)=e^{-u(s-3)},
 \qquad
 K_{j,s}(u)=\sum_{n\geq0}(-1)^nA_{j,n}(s)e^{-2nu},
 \qquad
 X_{j,u}(s)=e_u(s)K_{j,s}(u).                                      \tag{2.2}
\]

At `u=0` the action decay makes the series normally convergent on every
compact subset of the right half-plane.  Shifting its index gives the
exact identity

\[
 K_{j,s}(u)+e^{-2u}\alpha_j(s)K_{j,s+2}(u)=1,                       \tag{2.3}
\]

and therefore

\[
 L_jX_{j,u}=e_u,
 \qquad
 \alpha_jSX_{j,v}=e_v-X_{j,v}.                                    \tag{2.4}
\]

The quadratic Riccati cherry with inputs `u,v` has forcing and minimal
response

\[
 h_{j;u,v}=\alpha_jX_{j,u}SX_{j,v}
 =X_{j,u}(e_v-X_{j,v}),
 \qquad
 Y_{j;u,v}={\cal E}_jh_{j;u,v}.                                    \tag{2.5}
\]

A bottom-up atom proof would next attach `Y_(j;u,v)` to a third leaf.
For that step it needs (0.3), with for example

\[
 \|\nu_{j,c;u,v}\|_{W_1}
 :=\int_{[0,\infty)}(1+q)\,d|\nu_{j,c;u,v}|(q)\leq C.              \tag{2.6}
\]

Applying `L_j` to (0.3), and using (2.4), shows that (2.6) requires

\[
 h_{j;u,v}(s)=
 \int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-q(s-3)}\,d\nu_{j,c;u,v}(q).                  \tag{2.7}
\]

For `u=v=0`, equations (2.2) and (2.5) reduce this necessary condition
to

\[
 h_{j;0,0}(s)=K_{j,s}(0)\{1-K_{j,s}(0)\}.                          \tag{2.8}
\]

This is the first exact class-invariance test.  A uniform pointwise
bilinear Green bound does not imply the measure estimate (2.6).

## 3. Turning expansion of the exact coefficient

Fix `sigma>=0` and set

\[
 s_{c,\sigma}=3+\sigma+2ic,
 \qquad
 d_{j,\sigma,k}=k+{j+4+\sigma\over2}.                              \tag{3.1}
\]

All branches are continued from the positive real half-plane.  At the
upper turning point,

\[
 \operatorname {arsinh}(i+\epsilon)
 ={i\pi\over2}+(1-i)\sqrt\epsilon+O(\epsilon^{3/2}),
 \qquad \epsilon\downarrow0.                                     \tag{3.2}
\]

Equivalently, uniformly for `1<=a<=Mc^(1/3)`,

\[
 D_c(2ic+a)=D_c(2ic)-{i\pi a\over4}
 +{(-1+i)a^{3/2}\over3\sqrt{2c}}+O_M(a^2/c).                      \tag{3.3}
\]

Centering the four-unit action difference in (0.2) at
`2ic+2d_(j,sigma,k)` and using the rational factor there gives

\[
 \log\{-\alpha_j(s_{c,\sigma}+2k)\}
 =-2(1-i)\sqrt{d_{j,\sigma,k}/c}+\varepsilon_{j,c,k},              \tag{3.4}
\]

where the logarithm is the branch tending to zero and, for every fixed
`M`,

\[
 \sup_{0\leq n\leq Mc^{1/3}}
 \left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{j,c,k}\right|=o_M(1).          \tag{3.5}
\]

For completeness, (3.5) follows from the uniform bound

\[
 |\varepsilon_{j,c,k}|
 \leq C_{j,M}\left\{c^{-1}
 +c^{-1/2}(1+k)^{-3/2}
 +c^{-3/2}(1+k)^{3/2}\right\}.                                    \tag{3.6}
\]

The first term includes the fixed rational factor.  The second is the
symmetric four-unit averaging error in (3.2), and the third is the next
turning term.

There is also a useful exact telescoping check.  Put

\[
 q=s+j+1,
 \qquad
 P_{j,n}(s)=
 \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}{s+2k+2\over s+2k+1}
 {b_j(s+2n)b_j(s+2n+2)\over b_j(s)b_j(s+2)}.                       \tag{3.7}
\]

Then

\[
 A_{j,n}(s)=P_{j,n}(s)
 \exp\{D_c(q+2n-2)+D_c(q+2n)-D_c(q-2)-D_c(q)\}.                   \tag{3.8}
\]

For `n=O(c^(1/3))`, `P_(j,n)(s_(c,sigma))=1+o(1)`.  If

\[
 T_{j,c,n}=(-1)^nA_{j,n}(s_{c,\sigma}),                            \tag{3.9}
\]

then (3.3), or equivalently the sum of (3.4), gives uniformly for `x`
in compact subsets of `[0,infinity)`,

\[
 T_{j,c,\lfloor c^{1/3}x\rfloor}
 \longrightarrow
 \exp\left\{-{4\over3}(1-i)x^{3/2}\right\}.                     \tag{3.10}
\]

Elementary real-part bounds in (3.2) give constants `a,C>0`, independent
of the two labels, such that

\[
 |T_{j,c,n}|\leq C\exp\{-a n^{3/2}/\sqrt c\},
 \qquad 0\leq n\leq c,                                             \tag{3.11}
\]

while the large-real-part action gives a summable stronger tail for
`n>=c`.  Thus (3.11) supplies the domination needed below; this is not
only a fixed-`n` expansion.

## 4. Exact scaled Green limit and numerical regressions

Equations (2.2), (3.10), and (3.11) turn the sum into a Riemann integral:

\[
 c^{-1/3}K_{j,s_{c,\sigma}}(0)\longrightarrow I,
 \qquad
 I=\int_0^\infty
 e^{-(4/3)(1-i)x^{3/2}}\,dx.                                       \tag{4.1}
\]

The substitution `y=x^(3/2)` evaluates the constant exactly:

\[
 I={2\over3}\Gamma(2/3)
 \left\{{4\over3}(1-i)\right\}^{-2/3}
 =0.5122241392959975833
  +0.2957327447079685694i.                                         \tag{4.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
 |I|^2=0.3498314251700340475\ldots,
 \qquad
 c^{-2/3}h_{j;0,0}(s_{c,\sigma})\longrightarrow-I^2.               \tag{4.3}
\]

Direct high-precision recursion of
`T_(n+1)=-alpha_j(s_(c,0)+2n)T_n` gives:

| `c` | `j=0`: `|h_(0;0,0)|/c^(2/3)` | `j=4`: `|h_(4;0,0)|/c^(2/3)` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| `10^3` | 0.256914836842099 | 0.193599553122674 |
| `10^6` | 0.336392206868318 | 0.320882142887322 |
| `10^9` | 0.348353066802846 | 0.346451402680278 |

These values are regressions of the exact coefficient, not evidence used
in place of (3.2)--(4.3).  The same limit holds for every fixed positive
`sigma`, which is the form needed for the measure argument.

## 5. Analytic continuation and the Wiener lower bound

Assume that the effective-terminal representation (0.3) holds on the
open band (0.5) for a finite complex measure on `[0,infinity)`.  Applying
`L_j` gives (2.7) there.  Both sides of (2.7) are holomorphic on the
connected half-plane

\[
 \mathbb H_3=\{s:\operatorname {Re}s>3\}.                           \tag{5.1}
\]

The identity theorem therefore extends (2.7) from the band to all of
`H_3`.  Fix `sigma>0` and evaluate it at
`s=s_(c,sigma)=3+sigma+2ic`.  Since the measure has positive support,

\[
 |h_{j;0,0}(s_{c,\sigma})|
 \leq\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\sigma q}\,d|\nu_{j,c;0,0}|(q)
 \leq\|\nu_{j,c;0,0}\|_{\rm TV}.                                  \tag{5.2}
\]

Combining (4.3) and (5.2) proves the sharp scaled obstruction

\[
 \liminf_{c\to\infty}c^{-2/3}
 \|\nu_{j,c;0,0}\|_{\rm TV}\geq |I|^2,
 \qquad j=0,4.                                                       \tag{5.3}
\]

The first-moment norm in (2.6) dominates total variation, so one
`t` derivative cannot repair the failure.  This argument also explains
why merely observing that `2c` lies outside (0.5) is insufficient: exact
global Laplace representation is a holomorphic identity, not a
frequency-truncated equality.

## 6. Exact stopping point

The global positive-support Laplace/Wiener terminal class was attractive
because `S` acts on its atoms by `e^(-2q)` in `[0,1]`; it excludes the
alternating mode `e^(i*pi*s/2)`, on which `S=-1`.  The present result shows
that this global class is nevertheless not invariant under even the first
Riccati cherry with a uniform norm.  The failure is already in total
variation, before the first moment needed for one `t` derivative.

Nothing here refutes the restricted low-band atom and bilinear estimates.
Nothing here proves that every band-local all-order norm fails.  In
particular, a norm defined directly by restricted alternating tails need
not admit a global positive-support measure representation, and an
approximate terminal decomposition need not satisfy the identity theorem
hypothesis used in Section 5.  Proving or refuting such a genuinely
band-local all-tree Closure Lemma `CL` remains the exact open step.
