# Growing positive degree prefixes do not synchronize the Laplacian

## 0. Exact outcome and claim boundary

Checkpoint 0.84 proves that, in every RH-false right-edge case, there are
strict natural-grid lengths `L_j` and integers `K_j -> infinity` for which

\[
 d_k(L_j)>0\qquad(0\leq k\leq K_j).                 \tag{0.1}
\]

This note identifies exactly what (0.1) can and cannot do at the next
matrix step.  A positive degree says only that the corresponding vertex has
at least one positive incident edge in the **full** graph.  It does not put
that edge inside the displayed prefix and it controls only a one-by-one
principal minor of the signed Laplacian.

Two exact source-Cauchy fixtures prove the sharp non-implications.

1. A four-vertex fixture has every degree positive while its positive-edge
   graph has two connected components.
2. An all-odd-`N` family has every one of its `N+1` degrees positive, has a
   connected positive-edge graph, and agrees with the admitted prolate
   fixed-mode pattern on every fixed index.  Nevertheless its fixed
   `{0,1}` principal Laplacian has negative determinant.  The associated
   minimum-norm routing norm is therefore strictly larger than one.  The
   defect persists when the perturbation is arbitrarily small relative to
   the rank-one leading term, even though `d_1/d_0 -> 4` and
   `w_(01)/d_0 -> 2`.

These are synthetic combined-displacement matrices.  They are not the
pinned pole--archimedean--minus-prime source and are not generated by the
actual zeta zeros.  They prove that source-Cauchy algebra, fixed-mode anchor
asymptotics, displacement smallness, positive degrees, and even positive
connectivity do not imply the anchored Dirichlet gate.  A successful
actual-source argument must prove a new relative matrix inequality.

The positive centered-zero Gram decomposition is RH-conditional and cannot
be inserted into the RH-false branch.  The unconditional centered-zero
factorization is recorded below, but its off-line summands have no positive
Gram sign.  Nothing here proves an actual-source minor, graph connectivity,
routing contraction, finite hyperbolicity, equation (5.2) of
`DIRECT_RH_ATTACK.md`, a Lambda bound, or the Riemann hypothesis.  The
formal project state remains `P0_INVALIDATED`.

The binding finite conventions remain those of `P0_SOURCE_LOCK.json` and
`P0_NORMALIZATION_AUDIT.md`.

## 1. What a positive degree really gives

Let `w_(ij)=w_(ji)` be real signed edge weights and let `L_w` be their
row-sum-zero Laplacian,

\[
 (L_w)_{ij}=-w_{ij}\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
 d_i=(L_w)_{ii}=\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}.                \tag{1.1}
\]

If `d_i>0`, then at least one edge incident to `i` is positive.  This is the
only graph conclusion available from that scalar inequality: the positive
neighbor can lie beyond a controlled prefix, and positive degrees do not
prevent the positive graph from having several nontrivial components.

The first missing principal-minor condition is already visible on two
vertices.  Since `(L_w)_(ij)=-w_(ij)`, positivity of the principal block on
`{i,j}` requires

\[
 \boxed{d_i d_j-w_{ij}^2\geq0.}                     \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus synchronizing the signs of `d_i` and `d_j` does not synchronize their
off-diagonal coupling.  For the complete strict gate, choose any anchor
vertex `r` and delete its row and column.  The exact equivalence is

\[
 \boxed{
 L_w\succeq0,\quad\ker L_w=\mathbb C\mathbf1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 L_w^{(r)}\succ0,}                                  \tag{1.3}
\]

where `L_w^(r)` is the reduced Laplacian.  Equivalently, all Schur pivots
in one Sylvester ordering of `L_w^(r)` must be positive.  Degree positivity
checks only the first level of this hierarchy.

## 2. Source-Cauchy serialization

Retain the exact weighted-Loewner notation

\[
 t_i=i^2,\qquad c_0=0,\qquad c_n=nb_n\quad(n\geq1), \tag{2.1}
\]

and let every `p_i` be nonzero.  The anchored weights are

\[
 \boxed{
 w_{ij}=-p_i p_j{c_i-c_j\over t_i-t_j}\quad(i\ne j).} \tag{2.2}
\]

This is exactly the real inversion-symmetric source-Cauchy class, not a
free signed graph.  Indeed, put

\[
 \alpha_0=1,\qquad\alpha_n=\sqrt2\ (n\geq1),
 \qquad\kappa_i={p_i\over\alpha_i}.                 \tag{2.3}
\]

Then the even source block has off-diagonal entries

\[
 A_{ij}=\alpha_i\alpha_j{c_i-c_j\over t_i-t_j},     \tag{2.4}
\]

and `w_(ij)=-A_(ij)*kappa_i*kappa_j`.  Arbitrary even
source diagonals may be added; they cancel from the forced anchored
Laplacian exactly.

### Theorem 2.1 (positive degrees do not force positive connectivity)

Take four vertices and the rational source-Cauchy data

\[
 t=(0,1,4,9),\qquad
 p=(2,3,-2,3),\qquad
 c=(0,-73,-77,0).                                  \tag{2.5}
\]

Equation (2.2) gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
ij&01&02&03&12&13&23\\ \hline
w_{ij}&438&-77&0&-8&-657/8&462/5.
\end{array}                                         \tag{2.6}
\]

All four degrees are strictly positive:

\[
 \boxed{
 (d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3)
 =\left(361,{2783\over8},{37\over5},{411\over40}\right).} \tag{2.7}
\]

The only positive edges are `01` and `23`.  Hence the positive graph has
the two components `{0,1}` and `{2,3}`.

**Proof.**  Equations (2.6)--(2.7) follow by direct rational substitution
in (2.2) and (1.1).  Their signs give the component statement.  \(\square\)

Multiplying all `c_i` by any positive scalar makes every displacement
coefficient arbitrarily small without changing a sign or a component.
This fixture is not asserted to have the actual prolate anchor direction;
its role is to disprove connectivity from source-Cauchy algebra and degree
signs alone.

## 3. A growing all-degree family still has a negative fixed minor

The next family retains the actual fixed-mode direction and already grants
positive connectivity.

### Theorem 3.1 (all degrees positive, connected, but indefinite)

Let `N>=3` be odd, let `q>0`, and let `0<epsilon<=q`.  Define

\[
 p_0=1,\qquad
 p_i=2(-1)^i\quad(1\leq i<N),\qquad
 p_N=-1.                                            \tag{3.1}
\]

Then `sum_(i=0)^N p_i=0`.  Put

\[
 t_i=i^2,\qquad
 c_i=q i^2+\epsilon\mathbf1_{\{i=2\}},             \tag{3.2}
\]

and define the exact source-Cauchy weights by (2.2).  Then:

\[
 \boxed{d_i>0\qquad(0\leq i\leq N),}               \tag{3.3}
\]

the positive-edge graph is connected, but

\[
 \boxed{
 L_w[\{0,1\}]
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 q-\epsilon/2&-2q\\
 -2q&4q+4\epsilon/3
 \end{pmatrix},}                                   \tag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \det L_w[\{0,1\}]
 =-{2\over3}\epsilon(q+\epsilon)<0.}              \tag{3.5}
\]

Consequently `L_w` is indefinite.  If `B_+` and `B_-` are the positive-
and negative-edge incidence factors, respectively, and

\[
 S=B_+^\dagger B_-,                                \tag{3.6}
\]

is the minimum-norm routing through the positive graph, then

\[
 \boxed{\|S\|_{\rm op}>1.}                          \tag{3.7}
\]

**Proof.**  Split (3.2) into its linear part `q*t_i` and the single
perturbation at vertex `2`.  Since `sum_i p_i=0`, the linear part has
weights `-q*p_i*p_j` and degrees `q*p_i^2`.  For `i!=2`, the perturbation
changes only the edge to vertex `2`, giving

\[
 d_i=q p_i^2+{2\epsilon p_i\over i^2-4}.            \tag{3.8}
\]

In particular,

\[
 d_0=q-\epsilon/2\geq q/2,\qquad
 d_1=4q+4\epsilon/3.                                \tag{3.9}
\]

For `i>=3`, a positive `p_i` makes the correction in (3.8) positive.  A
nonterminal negative coordinate gives

\[
 d_i=4q-{4\epsilon\over i^2-4}\geq{16q\over5},      \tag{3.10}
\]

while the moving terminal coordinate gives

\[
 d_N=q-{2\epsilon\over N^2-4}\geq{3q\over5}.       \tag{3.11}
\]

At the perturbed vertex,

\[
 d_2=4q+\epsilon
 \sum_{\substack{0\leq j\leq N\\j\ne2}}
 {2p_j\over j^2-4}.                                \tag{3.12}
\]

The `j=0,1` contribution to the sum is `5/6`.  Dropping every positive
even term and bounding the terminal half-weight by the full odd tail gives

\[
 \sum_{\substack{0\leq j\leq N\\j\ne2}}
 {2p_j\over j^2-4}
 >{5\over6}-
 \sum_{\substack{j\geq3\\j\ \mathrm{odd}}}
 {4\over j^2-4}
 ={5\over6}-{4\over3}=-{1\over2}.                  \tag{3.13}
\]

Here

\[
 {4\over j^2-4}={1\over j-2}-{1\over j+2}
\]

telescopes on odd `j`.  Thus `d_2>4q-epsilon/2>0`, proving (3.3).

Every pair of opposite signs not involving vertex `2` has the positive
weight `-q*p_i*p_j`.  If vertex `2` is paired with a negative vertex `j`,
then

\[
 w_{2j}=-2p_j\left(q-{\epsilon\over j^2-4}\right)>0, \tag{3.14}
\]

with the same statement interpreted at `j=1`, where the parenthesis is
`q+epsilon/3`.  Hence the positive graph contains the complete bipartite
graph between the two sign classes and is connected.

No perturbation touches edge `01`, so `w_(01)=2q`.  Equations
(3.9) and (1.1) give (3.4), and direct expansion gives (3.5).  A negative
principal minor supplies a negative trial vector supported on `{0,1}`.

For a connected positive graph, the exact routing inertia formula gives

\[
 n_-(L_w)=#\{\rho\in\operatorname {spec}(S^*S):\rho>1\}. \tag{3.15}
\]

Since `L_w` has a negative direction, (3.15) proves (3.7).  \(\square\)

This is stronger than a growing-prefix counterexample: here one may take
`K_N=N`, so every vertex degree is positive.  The anomaly `p_N=-1` moves
to infinity.  For every fixed index `i`, eventually

\[
 p_0=1,\qquad p_i=2(-1)^i,                            \tag{3.16}
\]

which is exactly the admitted prolate fixed-mode direction after a common
normalization.

### Corollary 3.2 (the rank-one asymptotics remain sign-blind)

Put `r=epsilon/q`.  As `r downarrow 0`, equations (3.4)--(3.5) give

\[
 {d_1\over d_0}\longrightarrow4,\qquad
 {w_{01}\over d_0}\longrightarrow2,                 \tag{3.17}
\]

while

\[
 {d_0d_1-w_{01}^2\over q^2}
 =-{2\over3}r(1+r)<0.                               \tag{3.18}
\]

Thus the leading rank-one limits put the first two-by-two minor exactly on
the boundary; its first relative correction can have the wrong sign.

Multiplying all `p_i` by `a_N>0` and all `c_i` by `tau_N>0` multiplies
`L_w` by `a_N^2*tau_N` and leaves every sign and the routing norm unchanged.
One may take `a_N` at the prolate `L^(-1/2)` scale and choose `tau_N` so
small that

\[
 \max_{1\leq n\leq N}|b_n|
 =\max_{1\leq n\leq N}{|c_n|\over n}               \tag{3.19}
\]

beats any prescribed absolute rate.  Absolute displacement smallness
therefore cannot repair (3.5).

## 4. Why the centered-zero identities do not close the RH-false branch

The unconditional centered-zero moment formula gives, with
`a_n=2*pi*n/L`,

\[
 A_n(L)=-2\sum_{\alpha\in Z_+}
 {\cosh(\alpha L)-1\over\alpha^2+a_n^2}.             \tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently its divided difference is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 D_{ij}=-2\sum_{\alpha\in Z_+}
 {\alpha^2\{\cosh(\alpha L)-1\}
  \over(\alpha^2+a_i^2)(\alpha^2+a_j^2)}.}          \tag{4.2}
\]

On RH, `alpha=i*gamma`, and (4.2) reorganizes into the positive
zero-evaluation Gram matrix already recorded in
`CENTERED_ZERO_GRAM_SIMULTANEOUS_VERTEX_SUBSEQUENCE.md`.  In the RH-false
branch, conjugate pairing keeps the final matrix real, but the individual
rank-one coefficients and features in (4.2) have no positive sign.  The RH-
conditional identity `G=V*V^T>=0` is therefore unavailable precisely in the
case where (0.1) is being used.

Even granting an abstract positive-Gram row-sum form would not turn degrees
into matrix positivity: `FINITE_GRAM_DEFECT_TRANSVERSE_FRAME_BARRIER.md`
gives positive-definite `G`, an arbitrarily small row-sum residual, positive
degrees, and connected positive edges while
`G-diag(G*1)` is indefinite.  Its exact missing condition is the oriented
transverse second-frame inequality.  The source-Cauchy family of Section 3
shows the same failure directly in the weighted-Loewner class.

## 5. Exact surviving matrix target

The checkpoint-0.84 degree subsequence supplies the diagonal inequalities

\[
 d_k>0\qquad(0\leq k\leq K_j),                       \tag{5.1}
\]

but no relative control of `w_(ij)`.  The first genuinely new actual-source
statement is already

\[
 \boxed{d_0d_1\geq w_{01}^2}                        \tag{5.2}
\]

on the same sequence.  Strict inequality is necessary for positive
definiteness of that fixed principal block.  Higher matrix synchronization
then requires
positive Schur pivots of a reduced Laplacian, equivalently (1.3).

In routing language the complete target remains

\[
 \boxed{
 G_+\text{ connected},\qquad
 \lambda_{\max}\!\left(
 W_-^{1/2}C_-^*L_+^\dagger C_-W_-^{1/2}
 \right)<1.}                                       \tag{5.3}
\]

Neither the growth of a positive degree prefix nor fixed-index rank-one
asymptotics controls (5.2) or (5.3).  An actual advance must estimate an
off-diagonal coupling relative to the synchronized degrees, or prove the
equivalent reduced-Laplacian/collective-routing matrix inequality directly.
