**The exact missing lemma (BR-3).** Let abs(alpha_p) <= 1 for every prime p and let G(s) = prod over p of (1 - alpha_p p^-s)^-1 for Re s > 1. Suppose G extends to a meromorphic function on C of finite order with (s - 1) G(s) analytic near Re s >= 1/2 apart from finitely many poles, and G satisfies the functional equation of zeta. Suppose sum over p of abs(1 - alpha_p) p^-sigma diverges for some sigma > 1/2. Then G = zeta; or else an explicit G not equal to zeta with these properties is displayed.

Its companion in regime 2 (BR-2): for abs(alpha_p) = p^(1/2) on a set S of primes with sum over S of p^-sigma finite for every sigma > 1/2 and infinite at 1/2 (alpha_p = 1 off S; this drops the bound abs(alpha_p) <= 1), either the product has the line Re s = 1/2 as a natural boundary, or a twin is constructed. The lane text argues a natural boundary and does not prove it.
