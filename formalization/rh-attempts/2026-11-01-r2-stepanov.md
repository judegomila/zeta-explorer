# RH attempt R2, 1 November 2026: a Stepanov-type auxiliary for the one-sided Chebyshev bound

**Outcome: the attempt fails. No proof of RH. NO QED.**

**Goal.** Prove RH by proving the one-sided bound

  (★)  S(X) := Σ_{n≤X} Λ(n) n^{−1/2} ≤ 2√X + C(log X)^m  for all X ≥ X₀,

which implies RH by Landau's lemma (Theorem 1.1). The tool is a transplant of the Bombieri–Stepanov point-counting method
from curves over F_q to Spec Z. It uses dimension counts only: no Riemann–Roch, no Hodge index, no inertia.

**What the attempt establishes.**

1. **The Frobenius substitution has a lossless replacement over ℚ.** It is Legendre's formula in the form Λ ∗ 1 = log
   (Lemma 3.2). It turns "the auxiliary vanishes at every prime power m ≤ x" into |J_x| ≤ 2√x linear inequalities on the
   coefficients of an auxiliary Dirichlet polynomial C(s) = Σ_k c_k k^{−s}. The polynomial is normalized to agree with
   1/ζ to first order at s = 1 (Section 3.4).
   * Valid normalized auxiliaries exist for every x (Lemma 4.4, PROVED). They have exact degree ψ(x) and ℓ¹-mass
     (6/π² + o(1))x.
   * With O(√x) factorials (MEASURED, x ≤ 10⁶), valid auxiliaries of exact degree ψ(x) exist at N ≈ 5–8√x
     (unnormalized, Table 6.1), and valid normalized ones exist at N ≈ 3√x (Table 6.3).
2. **Two steps have no replacement.** One is Stepanov's multiplicity (S2), which rests on additivity of the r-th power
   map in characteristic p. The other is a degree bounded by the construction (S5).
3. **The argument fails at Step 5 (Section 4.5), on both branches.**
   * **Branch S (Type-I / Selberg auxiliary): false in the class covered.**
     * Theorem P with Remark 5.1′: every Type-I majorant of level ≤ X·exp(−C_A(log log X)²) with coefficient sum
       Σ|c_u|/u ≤ (log X)^A certifies at best S(X) ≤ (4 − o(1))√X. That is the sieve parity barrier in the (★)
       normalization, with an error of at least (2 − o(1))√X.
     * Theorem P′, conditional on the 7/8 zero-free half-plane input, extends this to coefficient sums ≤ X^{η/8−ε} at
       level X^{1−η}.
     * Larger coefficient sums are covered only by the parity heuristic.
     * The characteristic-0 analogue of multiplicity, through s-derivatives, runs backwards. The Λ_k certificate
       overshoots by a factor tending to k (Proposition 5.3), whereas Stepanov's excess falls like 1/r (Proposition 2.1).
   * **Branch C (Legendre auxiliary, full level): at least as hard as the target.**
     * Claim 5C (ℓ¹-mass ≤ C√x(log x)^A) implies RH.
     * More generally, ν₁(x) ≪ x^{θ′}(log x)^A gives no zeros in Re s > θ′. In the other direction,
       ν₁ ≥ (ψ(x) − x)₊/K(x) forces the exponent to be ≥ Θ := sup Re ρ (Proposition 6.6).
     * The dual feasible set contains the rescaled true primes (Proposition 6.3), and multiplicity is vacuous
       (Proposition 6.7).
     * The degree overhead is the archimedean lattice-point sawtooth. It vanishes identically in the genus-0 function
       field F_q[t], where the same Legendre substitution gives ψ_q(n) = qⁿ exactly (Proposition 6.8).
4. **Structural reading.** Stepanov's method needs (S2) and (S5) together.
   * Type-I auxiliaries over ℚ have (S5) but not (S2). The cost is the parity factor 2.
   * Legendre auxiliaries have the effect of (S2), since they are lossless, but not (S5). The cost is control of the
     archimedean sawtooth, which is Mertens-type cancellation.
   * This corrects the synthesis's expected stop: what is missing is not the Frobenius substitution itself.

## 1. Target and reduction

### 1.1 Notation

* Λ is the von Mangoldt function; ψ(x) = Σ_{n≤x} Λ(n) and θ(x) = Σ_{p≤x} log p.
* μ is the Möbius function, λ(n) = (−1)^{Ω(n)} the Liouville function, M(y) = Σ_{k≤y} μ(k), m(y) = Σ_{k≤y} μ(k)/k.
* w(n) = n^{−1/2} log n, and

  S(X) = Σ_{n≤X} Λ(n) n^{−1/2},  S_P(X) = Σ_{p≤X} w(p),  G(X) = S(X) − 2√X.

* J_x = {⌊x/m⌋ : 1 ≤ m ≤ x}. This set has at most 2√x elements, contains every integer j with j(j+1) ≤ x, and
  meets (x/2, x] only in ⌊x⌋.

### 1.2 The target

**(★)** There are C, m, X₀ with S(X) ≤ 2√X + C(log X)^m for all X ≥ X₀.

**Theorem 1.1** (PROVED-A: round-1 operator attempt, Theorem E; arithmetic foundation §2). (★) implies RH. Conversely,
RH implies G(X) = O(log² X).

The proof is Landau's lemma applied to f = C′(1 + log x)^m − G ≥ 0.
* The Mellin transform of G is −ζ′/ζ(s + ½)/s − 2/(s − ½).
* It is holomorphic at every real s > 0, because ζ(σ) ≠ 0 for real σ > ½.
* Positivity of f moves the abscissa of convergence to 0.

The converse uses G(X) = −2 + (ψ(X) − X)/√X + ½∫₁^X (ψ(u) − u)u^{−3/2} du. Under RH the integral is O(1) by the
explicit formula, and the middle term is O(log² X). Full text: operator attempt §4.2.

**Lemma 1.2** (PROVED). Let (★u) be the unweighted one-sided bound ψ(u) ≤ u + C√u(log u)^m for u ≥ u₀. Then (★u)
implies (★) with exponent m + 1, and (★) ⟹ RH ⟹ (★u) with m = 2. So (★), (★u) and RH are equivalent.

*Proof.* Stieltjes integration gives S(X) = ψ(X)X^{−1/2} + ½∫₁^X ψ(u)u^{−3/2} du. Insert (★u) for u ≥ u₀, and
ψ(u) ≤ 2u below u₀. The result is

  S(X) ≤ √X + C(log X)^m + (√X − 1) + (C/(2(m+1)))(log X)^{m+1} + O(1).

The rest is Theorem 1.1 and the RH bound ψ(u) = u + O(√u log² u). ∎

Sections 4 and 6 work with (★u), because floor-function auxiliaries are natural for ψ. Lemma 1.2 transfers every
statement to (★).

**Remark 1.3 (why the one-sided bound is the right Stepanov target over ℚ).**
* Over F_q, Stepanov's method proves only the upper bound N_n ≤ qⁿ + 1 + Cq^{n/2}.
* That bound misses a real ghost: a real Frobenius root β > √q lowers N_n (route 27 §4.3, table of fakes; operator §4.4,
  the F₅ model 1 − 5T + 5T²). The lower bound needs a separate covering and twisting argument.
* Over ℚ there is no real ghost. (1 − 2^{1−σ})ζ(σ) = Σ(−1)^{n−1}n^{−σ} > 0 on (0, 1), so ζ(σ) < 0 there.
* A ℚ-analogue of Stepanov's one-sided bound would therefore give RH outright. In this respect (★) asks less than Weil's
  theorem asks over F_q, not more.

**Remark 1.4 (check against atlas §7).**
* Change finitely many Λ(n). Then S(X) moves by a bounded amount for all X, and the Dirichlet series moves by a finite
  Dirichlet polynomial, which is entire.
* So (★) survives with a larger C, and the Landau argument still applies.
* Weil positivity is not stable in this way (atlas §7, "Accepted obstruction").
* A dimension-count method may therefore treat finitely many primes crudely. The obstruction in Section 5 comes from an
  infinite perturbation, the Liouville twist.

## 2. Bombieri–Stepanov: what each ingredient does

### 2.1 The argument (STANDARD; Stepanov 1969; Bombieri, Sém. Bourbaki 430, 1973)

Setup:
* C/F_q is smooth, projective and geometrically irreducible, of genus g; q = p^e; x₀ ∈ C(F_q); N = #C(F_q).
* r = p^b with 1 ≤ r ≤ q, and ℓ(m) = dim L(m x₀).
* Riemann's inequality gives ℓ(m) ≥ m − g + 1, with equality for m ≥ 2g − 1.
* Fix A < q/r and B. Choose bases {a_i} of L(Ax₀) and {b_j} of L(Bx₀) whose pole orders n_i ≤ A and m_j ≤ B at x₀
  are pairwise distinct.

The five steps:
* **(S1) Frobenius substitution.** For P ∈ C(F_q) ∖ {x₀} and u regular at P, u(P)^q = u(P).
* **(S2) Multiplicity (additivity of the r-th power).** Since r is a power of p = char F_q,
  (Σ γ_i u_i)^r = Σ γ_i^r u_i^r. So f := Σ c_{ij} a_i^r b_j^q equals H^r, where
  H := Σ c_{ij}^{1/r} a_i b_j^{q/r} ∈ L((A + (q/r)B) x₀).
* **(S3) Dimension count.** The F_q-linear map c ↦ G := Σ c_{ij} a_i^r b_j ∈ L((rA + B)x₀) has a nonzero kernel
  element whenever ℓ(A)ℓ(B) > ℓ(rA + B).
* **(S4) Independence.** If A < q/r, the products a_i^r b_j^q have pairwise distinct pole orders r(n_i + (q/r)m_j). So
  f ≠ 0, and hence H ≠ 0, for c ≠ 0.
* **(S5) Degree bounds the zero count.** For rational P ≠ x₀, (S1) gives
  H(P)^r = f(P) = Σ c_{ij} a_i(P)^r b_j(P) = G(P) = 0. So H vanishes at N − 1 points, and
  N − 1 ≤ deg(H)_∞ ≤ A + (q/r)B.

**Proposition 2.1 (multiplicity ladder; PROVED, elementary bookkeeping on 2.1).**

  N ≤ 1 + min{A + (q/r)B : 0 ≤ A < q/r, (A − g + 1)(B − g + 1) > rA + B − g + 1, rA + B ≥ 2g − 1}.

At r = 1 the choice A = 2g + 1, B = g + 1 gives N ≤ (g + 1)q + 2g + 2. At r = √q (e even) the minimum is
q + O(g√q), the Bombieri–Stepanov bound.

**Reading.**
* The excess is ≈ g·q/r for r ≪ √q.
* Without multiplicity (r = 1) the method is a constant-factor method: N ≤ (g + 1)q.
* The sharp main term needs r → ∞, and the square-root error needs r ≍ √q.
* Genus 0 needs no multiplicity: the excess is 1 for every r.

### 2.2 What must be replaced over ℚ

| Step | What it does | Why it works over F_q |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | compresses "H vanishes at N ≈ q points" into "G ≡ 0", i.e. ℓ(rA + B) linear conditions | all rational points share one Frobenius u ↦ u^q |
| S2 | divides the degree by r: deg H = deg f / r | the r-th power map is additive in characteristic p |
| S3 | more unknowns than conditions | Riemann's inequality (only counts of monomials are needed) |
| S4 | the auxiliary is nonzero | pole orders at x₀ separate the products |
| S5 | the degree of H is bounded **by the construction**, independently of N | x₀ is non-archimedean: the degree of Σ γ_{ij} a_i b_j^{q/r} is at most max_{ij}(n_i + (q/r)m_j), and each monomial's degree is an exact integer, whatever the number of terms and the coefficients |

Over ℚ an attempt needs three things:
* (i) a substitution that compresses all the vanishing conditions;
* (ii) a multiplicity gain or a substitute for it;
* (iii) a degree bounded by the construction.

Section 3 finds (i). Sections 5 and 6 show that (ii) and (iii) are where the attempt fails.

## 3. Dictionary and substitutions over ℚ

### 3.1 Dictionary

| L(mx₀), ℓ(m) ≈ m − g + 1 | coefficient space ℝ^N against the number of conditions |
| Frobenius substitution (S1) | Legendre substitution Λ ∗ 1 = log (F2); divisor substitution (F1); Fermat (F3), point evaluation (F3′) |
| multiplicity (S2) | no replacement (Sections 5.3, 6.7) |
| non-archimedean degree (S5) | archimedean degree: Stirling with a sawtooth error per term (Sections 6.2, 6.8) |

### 3.2 The substitutions

**Lemma 3.1 (F1, divisor substitution; PROVED).** Let p > D be prime and (λ_d)_{d≤D} real. Then
Σ_{d∣p, d≤D} λ_d = λ₁. The weight W(n) = (Σ_{d∣n, d≤D} λ_d)² with λ₁ = 1 is ≥ 0 everywhere and equals 1 at every prime
p > D.

*Proof.* The only divisor of p that is ≤ D is 1. ∎

This is the sieve shadow of (S1). A high-complexity weight collapses to a constant at every point, and the existence
problem has D free parameters. It is Selberg's Λ² sieve.

**Lemma 3.2 (F2, Legendre substitution; PROVED).** For real c₁, …, c_N put f_c(j) = Σ_{k≤N} c_k ⌊j/k⌋ and
log M_c := Σ_{k≤N} c_k log⌊x/k⌋!. Then:

* (a) log K! = Σ_{m≥1} Λ(m)⌊K/m⌋ (Chebyshev's identity);
* (b) log M_c = Σ_{m≤x} Λ(m) f_c(⌊x/m⌋) = Σ_{j∈J_x} L_j f_c(j), where
  L_j := Σ_{m: ⌊x/m⌋=j} Λ(m) = ψ(x/j) − ψ(x/(j+1)) ≥ 0;
* (c) if c ∈ ℤ^N, then ord_p(M_c) = Σ_{i≥1} f_c(⌊x/p^i⌋). So if f_c ≥ 1 on J_x, then lcm(1, …, x) ∣ M_c.

*Proof.*
* (a) log K! = Σ_{n≤K} Σ_{m∣n} Λ(m).
* (b) Use (a) and ⌊⌊x/k⌋/m⌋ = ⌊x/(km)⌋, then group m by j = ⌊x/m⌋.
* (c) Legendre gives ord_p(K!) = Σ_i ⌊K/p^i⌋. Hence ord_p M_c = Σ_k c_k Σ_i ⌊⌊x/k⌋/p^i⌋ = Σ_i f_c(⌊x/p^i⌋). For
  p^i ≤ x the argument lies in J_x, so the term is ≥ 1. For p^i > x the argument is 0, and f_c(0) = 0. ∎

**This is the ℚ-replacement of (S1).**
* "M_c vanishes at every point p ≤ x, to the order of the prime-power count" is lcm(1..x) ∣ M_c, equivalently f_c ≥ 1
  on J_x.
* That is x conditions (one per m ≤ x) compressed into |J_x| ≤ 2√x linear inequalities.
* The substitution is uniform in the point, as (S1) is. It uses only unique factorization in ℤ.
* For real c the inequality ψ(x) ≤ log M_c still holds by (b), since L_j ≥ 0. Integrality is not needed.

**Lemma 3.3 (F3, Fermat substitution; PROVED for the congruence, ARGUED for the cost).**
* For a prime p ∤ a, a^p ≡ a (mod p). For p ∈ (X − H, X], a^X ≡ a^{X−p+1} (mod p), and the reduced exponent lies in
  [1, H].
* The exponent depends on the point. Uniformity is restored only on a short interval.
* The "Frobenius-twisted" element a^X costs X log a ≥ X log 2 in degree, against a target mass θ(X) − θ(X − H) ≈ H < X.
  So (F3) never compresses at a profit.
* This is the ℚ-form of route 27's obstruction (O2): there is no single Frobenius, because the residue fields F_p have
  different sizes.

**Remark 3.3′ (F3′, point evaluation; PROVED).** Let F ∈ ℤ[t] be nonzero of degree d, and suppose p ∣ F(a) for every
a ∈ ℤ and every p in a set of primes P.
* For p > d with p ∤ cont(F), the reduction F mod p is a nonzero polynomial of degree < p. It cannot vanish on all of
  F_p.
* Hence P ⊂ {p ≤ d} ∪ {p ∣ cont(F)}.
* A polynomial auxiliary that vanishes on every fibre point over p therefore needs degree ≥ p, and certifies nothing
  beyond log d! (Pólya: the fixed divisor of a primitive degree-d polynomial divides d!).
* This is (O2) again. The residue fields grow with p, whereas over F_q all points share one field.

### 3.3 Completeness of the Legendre substitution

**Theorem 3.4 (PROVED).**
* (i) For every x ≥ 1 and 1 ≤ j ≤ x, Σ_{k≤x} μ(k)⌊j/k⌋ = 1. Hence c = μ|_{[1,x]} is valid with f_μ ≡ 1 on J_x, and
  log M_μ = ψ(x). Equivalently,

  lcm(1, 2, …, x) = ∏_{k≤x} ⌊x/k⌋!^{μ(k)}.

* (ii) For every valid c (f_c ≥ 1 on J_x), log M_c = ψ(x) + Σ_{j∈J_x} L_j (f_c(j) − 1) ≥ ψ(x). Equality holds iff

* (iii) For N ≥ x the linear program min{log M_c : c ∈ ℝ^N valid} has value ψ(x). Every point y ≥ 0 of its dual

*Proof.*
* (i) Σ_{k≤j} μ(k)⌊j/k⌋ = Σ_{n≤j} Σ_{k∣n} μ(k) = 1.
* (ii) Lemma 3.2(b), together with Σ_j L_j = ψ(x).

  Σ_j y_j = Σ_j y_j f_μ(j) = Σ_k μ(k) Σ_j y_j ⌊j/k⌋ = Σ_k μ(k) log⌊x/k⌋! = ψ(x). ∎

**Reading.** The perfect auxiliary exists: it is lcm(1..x), the ℚ-analogue of x^q − x on ℙ¹. The full floor matrix

### 3.4 The Legendre auxiliary as a Dirichlet polynomial

For c ∈ ℝ^N put C(s) := Σ_{k≤N} c_k k^{−s}. Then:

* f_c(j) = Σ_{n≤j} (c ∗ 1)(n): validity is a one-sided condition on the summatory coefficients of C(s)ζ(s) at the
  points of J_x. In words, "C(s)ζ(s) ≥ 1" in floor-summatory form.
* α(c) := Σ c_k/k = C(1) and β(c) := Σ c_k (log k)/k = −C′(1). The normalization α = 0, β = −1 is C(1) = 0, C′(1) = 1.
  These are the value and the derivative at s = 1 of 1/ζ(s) = (s − 1) − γ(s − 1)² + …. The Möbius certificate is the
  truncation of 1/ζ.
* log M_c = Σ_{n≤x} (c ∗ log)(n), since Λ ∗ (c ∗ 1) = c ∗ log. Its Dirichlet series is −C(s)ζ′(s). The residue at s = 1
  of −C(s)ζ′(s)x^s/s is C(1)·x(log x − 1) + C′(1)·x, which equals x under the normalization.

So the auxiliary of the synthesis's description ("an auxiliary Dirichlet polynomial vanishing to high order at prime
powers up to X") is a one-sided mollifier of ζ. Claim 5C (Section 4.5) asks for one whose coefficient ℓ¹-norm, an upper
bound for |C| on Re s = 0, is ≤ C√x(log x)^A.

The Branch-S auxiliary is the classical Selberg mollifier Σ_{d≤D} λ_d d^{−s}.

## 5. Attack on Branch S: the parity cap in the (★) normalization

### 5.1 Theorem P and its extensions

A **Type-I majorant of level L** for the primes in (√X, X] is a function W(n) = Σ_{u∣n, u≤L} c_u (n ≤ X) such that
W(n) ≥ 0 for all n ≤ X and W(p) ≥ 1 for all primes p ∈ (√X, X]. It certifies
Σ_{√X<p≤X} w(p) ≤ B(W) := Σ_{n≤X} W(n) w(n).

**Theorem P (PROVED).** This is Selberg's parity principle, restated in the w-weighting with an explicit error. No
novelty is claimed.

Fix ε ∈ (0, 1) and A ≥ 0. There are c = c(ε) > 0 and X₁ = X₁(ε, A) such that, for X ≥ X₁ and every Type-I majorant W
of level L ≤ X^{1−ε} with Σ_u |c_u|/u ≤ (log X)^A,

  B(W) ≥ 2 Σ_{√X<p≤X} W(p) w(p) − O_{ε,A}(√X (log X)^{A+1} e^{−c√(log X)}) ≥ (4 − o(1))√X.

So the bound for S(X) obtained from W has error ≥ (2 − o(1))√X, which is larger than any C(log X)^m. **Claim 5S is
false in this class.**

*Proof.*
1. Since 1 − λ(n) ∈ {0, 2} and W ≥ 0, Σ_{n≤X} W(n)w(n)(1 − λ(n)) ≥ 2 Σ_{√X<p≤X} W(p)w(p), because λ(p) = −1. Hence
   B(W) ≥ 2Σ_p W(p)w(p) − |T|, where T := Σ_{n≤X} W(n) w(n) λ(n).
2. λ is completely multiplicative, so T = Σ_{u≤L} c_u λ(u) u^{−1/2} Σ_{m≤X/u} λ(m) m^{−1/2} (log u + log m).
3. Unconditionally (de la Vallée Poussin–Landau), Σ_{m≤y} λ(m) ≪ y e^{−c₀√(log y)}. Partial summation gives
   Σ_{m≤Y} λ(m) m^{−1/2}(log u + log m) ≪ Y^{1/2} log(3uY) e^{−c₀√(log Y)/√2} + Y^{1/4} log(3uY). To see this, split the
   Stieltjes integral at √Y and use |Σ_{m≤t} λ(m)| ≤ t below √Y.
4. Here Y = X/u ≥ X^ε and uY = X. Hence |T| ≪ √X log X e^{−c₀√(ε log X/2)} Σ_u |c_u|/u. Take c = c₀√(ε/2).
5. Finally W(p) ≥ 1, and by the prime number theorem with classical error,
   Σ_{√X<p≤X} w(p) = 2√X − 2X^{1/4} + O(√X e^{−c√(log X)}). ∎

**The cap is sharp.**
* Selberg's weights satisfy the hypotheses. At level L = D² they have |λ_d| ≤ 1 (checked: max |λ_d| = 1 at
  D = 100, 316, 1000), c_u = Σ_{[d₁,d₂]=u} λ_{d₁}λ_{d₂}, and
  Σ_u |c_u|/u ≤ Σ_{d₁,d₂≤D} gcd(d₁,d₂)/(d₁d₂) ≪ (log D)³.
* They give B(W_λ) = (2√X log X − 4√X)/(log D + 1.3326…)·(1 + o(1)). So B/(2√X) → 2/θ when D² = X^θ.
* Hence factor 2 is the exact limit, as θ → 1, of Type-I majorants with polylogarithmic coefficient sums.

### 5.3 Multiplicity through the s-derivative: the ladder runs backwards

The closest analogue of the derivative trick D(x^q) = 0 differentiates in s, since d/ds n^{−s} = −(log n)n^{−s}. The
k-th order object is Λ_k := μ ∗ log^k, with Dirichlet series (−1)^k ζ^{(k)}/ζ. It has Λ_k(p) = log^k p, which is
"multiplicity k" at primes. Bombieri's asymptotic sieve evaluates Σ a_nΛ_k(n) from level-x^{1−ε} Type-I data for k ≥ 2,
but not for k = 1 (STANDARD; Bombieri 1976; Friedlander–Iwaniec, *Opera de Cribro*, the chapters on the asymptotic sieve
and on parity).

**Proposition 5.3 (the Λ_k ladder; (i) PROVED, (ii) STANDARD and MEASURED).**
* (i) Λ_{k+1} = Λ_k·log + Λ_k ∗ Λ for k ≥ 1. Hence Λ_k ≥ Λ·log^{k−1} ≥ 0. This gives the "multiplicity-k certificate"

  Σ_{n≤X} Λ(n) log^{k−1}(n) n^{−1/2} ≤ Σ_{n≤X} Λ_k(n) n^{−1/2}.

* (ii) As X → ∞, Σ_{n≤X} Λ(n)log^{k−1}(n) n^{−1/2} ~ 2√X (log X)^{k−1} and Σ_{n≤X} Λ_k(n) n^{−1/2} ~ 2k√X (log X)^{k−1}.
  So the multiplicity-k certificate overshoots by a factor tending to k.

*Proof.*
* (i) Write F_k = (−1)^k ζ^{(k)}/ζ, so F_k ζ = (−1)^k ζ^{(k)}. Differentiating gives F_{k+1} = −F_k′ + F_k·(−ζ′/ζ). In
  coefficients, −F_k′ ↔ Λ_k log and F_k(−ζ′/ζ) ↔ Λ_k ∗ Λ. Induction from Λ₁ = Λ gives the rest.
* (ii) The polar parts at s = 1 are F_k = k!(s−1)^{−k}(1 − γ(s−1) + (γ² + γ₁)(s−1)² − …) and
  (−1)^{k−1}(d/ds)^{k−1}(−ζ′/ζ) = (k−1)!(s−1)^{−k} + (regular). Perron's formula with the classical zero-free region
  gives the main terms. The leading term of a pole a_{−k}(s−1)^{−k} in the n^{−1/2}-weighted sum is
  2a_{−k}√X(log X)^{k−1}/(k−1)!. ∎

### 5.4 What breaks parity, and why at frequency 0 it is the Mertens problem (ARGUED)

* **Dirichlet-series form of parity.**
  * Σ_n W(n)λ(n)n^{−s} = (Σ_u c_uλ(u)u^{−s})·ζ(2s)/ζ(s).
  * ζ(2s)/ζ(s) is regular at s = 1, where it vanishes. So the λ-twisted Type-I sums have no main term, and Type-I data
    cannot distinguish w from w(1 − λ) at level < X.
  * The Legendre program escapes this, because its data are exact integers at full level (Theorem 3.4(iii): no duality
    gap at N ≥ x).
* **Parity breakers.** Parity is broken only by bilinear (Type-II) information: Vinogradov, Vaughan, Heath-Brown,
  Friedlander–Iwaniec.
* **Frequency 0.** For the non-oscillatory sum Σ Λ(n)n^{−1/2}, Vaughan's identity (arithmetic foundation §4) leaves
  Type-II pieces Σ_{m>U, n>V, mn≤X} (μ_{>U}∗1)(m)Λ(n)w(mn).
  * Bilinear bounds by Cauchy–Schwarz and the large sieve exploit an additive oscillation e(αmn).
  * At α = 0 there is none, and the only cancellation available is that of μ over long ranges.
* **Type-I main terms.** The Type-I pieces contain truncated Möbius series Σ_{d≤U} μ(d)/d^s. These cancel against the
  Type-II main term only up to the error of those partial sums.
* **Conclusion.** The parity-breaking input that (★) needs is Möbius cancellation at all scales up to X. This agrees with
  arithmetic foundation §4. It is the content of Branch C's degree step (Sections 6.2, 6.4). The two branches meet at
  the same object.
  * Branch S fails at every level ≤ X·exp(−C(log log X)²) for polylogarithmic coefficient sums (parity), and for
    polynomially large ones at level X^{1−η} under the 7/8 input (Theorem P′).
  * Branch C is exact at level X but needs Mertens-strength control.

### 6.2 The degree of a normalized auxiliary, and why Claim 5C is RH-hard

**Proposition 6.2 (PROVED).** Let N ≤ x, and let c ∈ ℝ^N be valid (f_c ≥ 1 on J_x) and normalized (α(c) = 0,
β(c) = −1). Put φ_k := lgamma(x/k + 1) − log⌊x/k⌋! ∈ [0, log(x/k + 1)) and
K(x) := ½ log(2πx) + log(x + 1) + 1/12. Then, with some ϑ_k ∈ (0, 1),

  ψ(x) ≤ log M_c = x + Σ_{k≤N} c_k [½ log(2πx/k) + ϑ_k k/(12x) − φ_k] ≤ x + ‖c‖₁ K(x).

Consequently:
* (a) Claim 5C ⟹ (★u) ⟹ RH.
* (b) ν₁(x) ≥ (ψ(x) − x)₊ / K(x) for every x.
* (c) Unconditionally, ν₁(x) ≥ c√x log log log x/log x for infinitely many x (Littlewood). This is compatible with
  Claim 5C. If RH fails and Θ = sup Re ρ > ½, then ν₁(x) = Ω(x^{Θ−ε}) for every ε > 0 (Landau), so Claim 5C fails.

*Proof.*
* By Lemma 3.2(b), ψ(x) ≤ Σ_k c_k log⌊x/k⌋! = Σ_k c_k[lgamma(x/k + 1) − φ_k].
* Stirling with y = x/k ≥ 1 gives lgamma(y + 1) = y log y − y + ½ log(2πy) + ϑ/(12y).
* The main terms sum to x(log x − 1)α(c) − xβ(c) = x.
* Each bracket is a difference of two nonnegative numbers, so its absolute value is at most
  max(½ log(2πx) + 1/12, log(x + 1)) ≤ K(x). This proves the display.
* (a) follows with Lemma 1.2 and Theorem 1.1, and (b) is the display rearranged.
* (c) uses Landau's oscillation theorem, ψ(x) − x = Ω₊(x^{Θ−ε}) when Θ > ½, and Littlewood's
  ψ(x) − x = Ω±(√x log log log x) (both STANDARD). ∎

The identity in the display was checked term by term on the exact certificates of Lemma 4.4, with residual ≤ 4·10⁻¹⁰
at x = 10⁴, 10⁵ and 10⁶ (R3). The two values of 12y·(Stirling remainder) slightly above 1 at large y are cancellation
error in double precision.

**The archimedean overhead (the precise ℚ-failure of S5).**
* For an exact normalized auxiliary (log M_c = ψ(x), for example Lemma 4.4) the display is an identity:

  ψ(x) − x = Σ_k c_k [½ log(2πx/k) − φ_k] + O(Σ_k |c_k| k/x).

  The whole zero oscillation ψ(x) − x = −Σ_ρ x^ρ/ρ + … sits in a weighted sawtooth sum, with
  φ_k = {x/k} log(x/k) + O(1).
* Over F_q the analogous overhead is zero. Each monomial's pole order is an exact integer, additive in products. The
  degree of a sum is at most the maximum, because x₀ is non-archimedean (Section 2.2, S5).
* Over ℚ the degree of M_c is additive in its factors as well. But each factor's degree log⌊x/k⌋! differs from its
  smooth profile by the sawtooth φ_k, and these errors add with weights c_k. The construction bounds them only by
  ‖c‖₁ K(x).
* So the dimension count, which controls the number of conditions, controls nothing that bounds the degree. The ℓ¹ mass
  of the certificate replaces Stepanov's pole-order bookkeeping, and bounding it is the problem itself. Proposition 6.8
  identifies the sawtooth as the lattice-point term of the archimedean place.

**Remark 6.2′ (sums are nearly ultrametric; PROVED, elementary).**
* For integers a_i and real γ_i, log|Σ_{i≤n} γ_i a_i| ≤ max_i log|a_i| + log Σ_i |γ_i|. So an additive (Thue–Siegel
  shaped) ℚ-auxiliary M = Σγ_i a_i does have a degree bounded by the construction, up to log Σ|γ_i|.
* The failure there is elsewhere. The vanishing conditions p ∣ M are congruences, and no substitution compresses them:
  the compressing substitutions found, (F1)–(F2), are multiplicative.
* (S4) also has no replacement. Minkowski's theorem gives a short γ ≠ 0, with |γ|_∞ ≤ Q^{1/n}, satisfying
  M ≡ 0 mod Q := ∏p. But the lattice {γ : M ≡ 0 mod Q} contains the rank-(n−1) sublattice {M = 0}.
  * When n Q^{1/n} max|a_i| < Q, the short vector must give M = 0, because a nonzero M would have
    Q ≤ |M| ≤ n Q^{1/n} max|a_i|.
  * Any γ with M ≠ 0 has |M| ≥ Q, which is the trivial bound. So there is no zero estimate and no gain.
* So the first pass's phrase "deg(Σ) ≤ max deg fails at ∞" is corrected: the archimedean place loses only log Σ|γ_i| on
  sums. Branch C loses through the floor function inside each factor.

### 6.3 Duality: the dimension count cannot bound the degree without bounding ψ(x) − x

The program ν₁(x) = min{‖c‖₁ : f_c ≥ 1 on J_x, α(c) = 0, β(c) = −1} has the dual

  ν₁(x) = max{ Σ_{j∈J_x} y_j − v : y ≥ 0, u, v ∈ ℝ, |Σ_j y_j ⌊j/k⌋ + u/k + v(log k)/k| ≤ 1 for all k ≤ N }.

**Proposition 6.3 (PROVED).** The rescaled true prime measure is dual-feasible.
* Take y_j = L_j/K(x), u = −x(log x − 1)/K(x), v = x/K(x).
* Then Σ_j L_j⌊j/k⌋ = log⌊x/k⌋! (Lemma 3.2(b) with c = e_k).
* Stirling makes each constraint entry equal to [½ log(2πx/k) + ϑ_k k/(12x) − φ_k]/K(x) ∈ [−1, 1].
* The dual value of this point is (ψ(x) − x)/K(x). ∎

Checked numerically at x = 10⁴, N = 2000: the largest constraint entry is 0.373, and the dual value is
0.904 = (ψ(x) − x)/K(x).

So Claim 5C states that every nonnegative "pseudo-prime" block measure y, whose floor sums match the Stirling profile of
a mass-v measure to within ±1 at each scale x/k, has Σ_j y_j − v ≤ C√x(log x)^A. The true primes are one such measure.
Hence:
* any proof of Claim 5C bounds ψ(x) − x;
* the method has no route to Claim 5C other than the target;
* Step 4 (feasibility) is a statement about the primal and gives no information about the dual optimum.

HEURISTIC reading. The dual is a Chebyshev–Beurling inverse problem: a nonnegative measure ν with ν ∗ 1 ≈ log in
floor-summatory form. On it the zeros of ζ are invisible to the data, since convolving with 1 multiplies the Dirichlet
series by ζ and cancels the poles of ζ′/ζ. They reappear only through the Möbius inversion that recovers ν.

Prime-free sufficient (or equivalent) conditions for RH are classical. Redheffer's matrix has det R(n) = M(n), and
RH ⟺ det R(n) = O(n^{1/2+ε}) (STANDARD). Claim 5C is another one. Being prime-free is not evidence of tractability.

**Remark 6.3′ (the Cramer form of Claim 5C).**
* A basic optimal solution has at most |J_x| + 2 ≤ 2√x + 2 nonzero entries. Each is a ratio of maximal minors of the
  matrix [⌊j/k⌋ | 1/k | (log k)/k] (rows j ∈ J_x plus two normalization rows).
* So Claim 5C would follow from O((log x)^A) bounds on these ratios.
* The full square floor matrix has determinant 1 and a Möbius inverse with entries in {−2, …, 2} (Section 3.3). Its
  J_x-restricted minors are not controlled by anything in the construction.

### 6.4 Forced head, Mertens tail (structure of exact auxiliaries)

**Proposition 6.4 (PROVED).** Let s = s(x) := max{j : j(j+1) ≤ x}, so that {1, …, s} ⊂ J_x. If c ∈ ℝ^N with
s ≤ N ≤ x satisfies f_c ≡ 1 on J_x, then:
* (i) c_k = μ(k) for k ≤ s;
* (ii) for every j ∈ J_x with j > s,

  Σ_{s<k≤min(N,j)} c_k ⌊j/k⌋ = r_j := Σ_{s<k≤j} μ(k)⌊j/k⌋ = Σ_{i≥1, j/i>s} (M(j/i) − M(s)).

*Proof.*
* (i) f_c(j) − f_c(j−1) = Σ_{k∣j} c_k must equal [j = 1] for j ≤ s, so c ∗ 1 = δ on [1, s].
* (ii) Subtract from f_c(j) = 1 the identity Σ_{k≤j} μ(k)⌊j/k⌋ = 1, and write ⌊j/k⌋ = Σ_{i≤j/k} 1. ∎

So the tail of every exact auxiliary solves a linear system whose right-hand side is a vector of Mertens-function
increments at the scales j/i ≤ x. Bounding the tail in ℓ¹ is a statement about M(y) − M(y′) for y, y′ ≤ x, and
M(y) = O(y^{1/2+ε}) is equivalent to RH (STANDARD). This identifies the cancellation that Section 5.4 needs.

Equivalently, for the Möbius head alone, f_{μ|[1,s]}(j) = j·m(s) − Σ_{k≤s} μ(k){j/k}. The validity of a short head at
large j is a statement about m(s) and a Möbius-weighted sawtooth.

### 6.6 Exponent transfer

**Proposition 6.6 (PROVED, using the STANDARD oscillation theorems).** Let Θ := sup{Re ρ : ζ(ρ) = 0} and
ν* := limsup_{x→∞} log ν₁(x)/log x. Then:
* (a) ½ ≤ Θ ≤ ν* ≤ 1;
* (b) if ν₁(x) ≤ C x^{θ′}(log x)^A for all large x, with ½ ≤ θ′ < 1, then ζ(s) ≠ 0 for Re s > θ′.

*Proof.*
* (a) ν* ≤ 1 is Lemma 4.4. For Θ ≤ ν*:
  * If Θ > ½, Landau's theorem gives ψ(x) − x = Ω₊(x^{Θ−ε}) for every ε > 0. Otherwise the Landau lemma of arithmetic
    foundation §2, applied to x^{Θ−ε}(log x) − (ψ(x) − x), would clear the half-plane Re s > Θ − ε. Combined with
    Proposition 6.2(b), ν₁(x) ≥ x^{Θ−ε}/K(x) for arbitrarily large x.
  * If Θ = ½, Littlewood's Ω₊(√x log log log x) gives ν* ≥ ½.
* (b) Proposition 6.2 gives ψ(x) − x ≤ C x^{θ′}(log x)^A K(x) for large x.
  * Put f(x) = C′x^{θ′}(log x)^{A+1} − (ψ(x) − x) for x ≥ x₀ and f = 0 before; then f ≥ 0. Its Mellin transform is
    C′(A+1)!/(s − θ′)^{A+2}, plus ζ′(s)/(sζ(s)) + 1/(s − 1), plus an entire term from the omitted initial segment.
  * This is holomorphic on the real half-line (θ′, ∞): ζ has no real zeros in (0, 1), and the pole at s = 1 cancels.
  * Landau's lemma puts the abscissa at ≤ θ′, so ζ′/ζ is holomorphic in Re s > θ′. ∎

**Reading.**
* Branch C converts certificate exponents into zero-free half-planes one for one. Conversely the true Θ bounds every
  achievable exponent from below.
* A half-plane input θ (7/8 Lean-checked; B = 0.87488665 conditional) already gives ψ(x) − x ≪ x^{θ+ε} directly.
  Certificates can never beat ψ(x) itself (Theorem 3.4(ii)).
* To improve on θ the method would need a family of exponent θ′ < θ. Nothing in it builds one (Proposition 6.3,
  Remark 6.3′).
* That no amplification is possible at all is ARGUED, not proved. It is the Branch-C analogue of round 1's bootstrap
  Theorem S (capacity-form inputs do not improve themselves).
* The first pass's sentence "Branch C then returns at best log M_c − x ≪ x^{7/8+ε} for Möbius-type certificates" is
  withdrawn. No such certificate was constructed, and the true statement is (a)–(b).

### 6.7 Multiplicity is vacuous in the Legendre family

**Proposition 6.7 (PROVED).** For r > 0, f_{rc} = r f_c, log M_{rc} = r log M_c, α(rc) = rα(c), β(rc) = rβ(c) and
‖rc‖₁ = r‖c‖₁.

So "vanishing to order r at every prime power" (f ≥ r on J_x, normalized by β = −r) is exactly r times a valid
normalized certificate, and it certifies the same bound ψ(x) ≤ log M_{rc}/r. Integrality plays no role either: Lemma
3.2(b) holds for real c, and rational c = c′/r with integral c′ is multiplicity r for c′. ∎

In Stepanov's method the gain is nonlinear. f = H^r vanishes to order r at each point, but is cut out by ℓ(rA + B)
conditions rather than r·N. Over ℚ the valuation ord_p(M_c) and the degree log M_c are both linear in c, so vanishing
order and degree scale together and multiplicity buys nothing. This is the Branch-C counterpart of Proposition 5.3.

### 6.8 The sawtooth is archimedean: exactness in the genus-0 function field

**Proposition 6.8 (PROVED; classical in substance).** Work in the ring F_q[t]. For monic f put Λ(f) = deg P if f = P^j with P
monic irreducible, and Λ(f) = 0 otherwise. Let ψ_q(n) := Σ_{deg g = n} Λ(g). Then:
* (a) Σ_{g∣f} Λ(g) = deg f (monic divisors), the analogue of Λ ∗ 1 = log;
* (b) the analogue of Chebyshev's identity is Σ_{deg f = m} deg f = m qᵐ = Σ_{d=1}^{m} q^{m−d} ψ_q(d);
* (c) hence ψ_q(n) = qⁿ for every n ≥ 1, exactly, with no error term.

*Proof.*
* (a) Unique factorization in F_q[t].
* (b) Sum (a) over monic f of degree m. The number of monic f of degree m divisible by a monic g of degree d ≤ m is
  q^{m−d}, exactly.
* (c) Put S_m := Σ_{d≤m} q^{m−d} ψ_q(d) = m qᵐ. Then ψ_q(m) = S_m − q S_{m−1} = m qᵐ − (m − 1)qᵐ = qᵐ. ∎

Brute-force check (R5): counting monic irreducibles over F₂ (n ≤ 10) and F₃ (n ≤ 6) gives ψ_q(n) − qⁿ = 0 in every case.

**Reading.**
* Over F_q[t] the Legendre/Chebyshev substitution of Section 3 proves the genus-0 statement exactly by triangular
  inversion. The reason is that #{h monic : deg h = n} = qⁿ is exact: the absolute value at ∞ takes values in q^ℤ, and
  its balls are F_q-vector spaces.
* Over ℤ the corresponding count is #{n ≤ y} = ⌊y⌋ = y − {y}. The fractional parts {x/k} are the sawtooth φ_k of
  Proposition 6.2, and for exact certificates the weighted sawtooth sum is ψ(x) − x.
* So the overhead that blocks (S5) over ℚ is the lattice-point error of ℤ in archimedean intervals. It is not the
  triangle inequality (Remark 6.2′).
* For a curve of genus g over F_q the count of effective divisors of degree n is h(q^{n+1−g} − 1)/(q − 1) only for
  n > 2g − 2 (STANDARD). Finitely many low-degree deviations carry the 2g Frobenius roots.
* Over ℚ the deviation {y} occurs at every scale. This is the Chebyshev-side form of route 27's (O1), S₀ = #zeros = ∞.

### 6.9 Classical precedents and an analogy

**Remark 6.9.**
* **Chebyshev–Sylvester–Diamond–Erdős (STANDARD, cited from memory, not re-verified).**
  * Chebyshev (1852) and Sylvester used fixed finite factorial-ratio auxiliaries ∏⌊x/k⌋!^{c_k}. Each fixed combination
    certifies ψ(x) ≤ (1 + δ)x with some δ > 0.
  * Diamond and Erdős (*L'Enseignement Math.* 26 (1980) 313–321) showed that for every δ > 0 some finite combination
    certifies (1 − δ)x ≤ ψ(x) ≤ (1 + δ)x for large x, but their existence proof uses the prime number theorem.
  * That is the constant-level version of Propositions 6.3 and 6.6. The method certifies what the prime distribution
    allows, and finding the certificate needs the prime distribution.
  * Claim 5C is the x-dependent, square-root-level form of the same question.
* **Beurling analogy (HEURISTIC).** The dual of Claim 5C asks whether a nonnegative pseudo-prime measure with
  ordinary-integer Chebyshev data at the scales x/k must satisfy Ψ(x) − x = O(√x polylog).
  * In Beurling's theory, integer-side regularity does not force prime-side regularity. Diamond–Montgomery–Vorhauer
    (*Math. Ann.* 334 (2006)) constructed generalized prime systems with N(x) = cx + O(x^θ), θ < 1, whose prime error is
    no better than de la Vallée Poussin's (as recalled; not re-verified).
  * The analogy is imperfect. Here the integers are the ordinary ones and the data are exact up to O(1) at specified
    scales. It is not used as an argument.
