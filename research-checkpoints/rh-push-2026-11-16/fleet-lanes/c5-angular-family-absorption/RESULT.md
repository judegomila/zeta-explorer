**Verdict: precise obstruction; the decisive test kills the candidate.** The absorption identity does not hold for
the angular family or for ring-class characters. Both families are groups of characters trivial on the ideles of Q;
their epsilon factor is identically 1 (the evaluation of the family parameter at a fixed element), and the transform
in the row variable is Fourier inversion on a compact group with no Gauss sum in it. The coefficient of the Moebius
variable must therefore be mu itself, the rows are Moebius sums of the family, and the two representations of the
7/8 method coincide. The coherent set of exponent 1/2 (rays) exists, but it couples the two variables (s must be
divisible by conj(c)), which puts the reciprocal at shift 1/2: net gain 0, abscissa 1. In the unconditional ledger
the abscissa is >= 1 for every K and Y. In the model ledger the abscissa is (1 + kappa)/2 with K = Z^kappa, and the
low estimate that gives it contains the conclusion at the same abscissa as its k = 0 component. No pivot was run:
the general row asked for in the assignment is delivered instead (section 5). No statement about any zero is made.
7/8 (unrefereed manuscript) stands; RH open. NO QED.

## 2. Definitions

F = Q(sqrt(-3)), O = Z[omega], class number 1, six units. lambda^(6k)(alpha) = (alpha/abs(alpha))^(6k)
= (alpha/conj(alpha))^(3k) is a function of the ideal (alpha); it is a Hecke character of conductor 1 and
infinity type 6k, trivial on rational numbers. L(s, lambda^(6k)) is the L-function of a CM form of weight 6|k| + 1
and level 3. Completed function: Lam(s) = (sqrt(3)/2 pi)^s Gamma(s + 3|k|) L(s, lambda^(6k)).

Klein involution: alpha -> conj(alpha). lambda^(6k)(conj(alpha)) = lambda^(-6k)(alpha). The rays arg(alpha) in
(pi/3) Z are its fixed locus on C modulo units and are the ideals generated by rational integers.

Ring-class twin: G_f = (ideals prime to f)/(principal ideals with a generator in Z + fO), a finite group of order
about f/3 times a product over p | f; its characters psi are the ring-class characters of conductor dividing f.
Common description of both families: idele class characters of F that are trivial on the ideles of Q; equivalently
psi(conj(a)) = psi(a)^(-1). Write G for the compact group (the circle of angles mod pi/3, or G_f) and G^ for the
family (Z, or the dual of G_f). The common kernel of the family is H = {ideals generated by rational numbers}
(angular) or the ideals with a generator in Z + fO (ring-class).

Probe, transplanted from the manuscript's (6.2) as in `ap-order-two` section 3, with the row variable k:

  A_k = Y^(-1) sum_s W1(Ns/Y) lambda^(6k)(s),   B_k = Z^(-1/2) sum_c x_c lambda^(6k)(c) w(Nc/Z),
  I(K) = K^(-1/2) sum_k Om(k/K) A_k B_k,

s, c over nonzero ideals, W1, w smooth on (1, 2), Om smooth even with Om(0) = 1, x_c = mu(c) eta(c) for the target
(eta a fixed Hecke character; eta = 1 gives 1/zeta_F = 1/(zeta L(., chi_(-3)))). Normalisation as in the source:
generic abs(A_k) is Y^(-1/2), generic abs(B_k) is 1, K plays the role of Q = XY, so X = K/Y. Write K = Z^kappa,
Y = Z^ly.

## 3. Decisive test: the absorption identity

### 3.1 The transform in the row variable has no Gauss sum [PROVED]

For arbitrary coefficients a_s, b_c,

  sum_k Om(k/K) (sum_s a_s lambda^(6k)(s)) (sum_c b_c lambda^(6k)(c)) = sum_(s,c) a_s b_c Omhat_K(6 arg(sc)),

Omhat_K(theta) = sum_k Om(k/K) e^(ik theta), concentrated on abs(theta mod 2 pi) << 1/K. For G_f the same holds
with Omhat the indicator of the trivial class of [s][c] (exact, Om = 1). The transfer kernel is a function of the
class of sc in G only. It contains no factor depending on the prime factorisation of c. In the sextic case the
corresponding kernel is g(chi_a) chi_a(h)^(-1)/sqrt(Na), and the Gauss sum is what converts theta coefficients
into mu.

### 3.2 The epsilon factor is identically 1 [PROVED for lambda^(6k); CLASSICAL/RECALLED in general]

**Lemma 1 [PROVED].** Lam(s, lambda^(6k)) = Lam(1 - s, lambda^(6k)) for every k: root number +1.
Proof. P(alpha) = alpha^(6k) is harmonic and homogeneous of degree 6k, so the Fourier transform of
P(x) e^(-pi abs(x)^2) is i^(-6k) P(xi) e^(-pi abs(xi)^2). The dual lattice of O is (2/sqrt 3) i O and
P(i alpha) = i^(6k) P(alpha). The two factors multiply to 1. Poisson summation gives
Theta_k(1/t) = t^(6k+1) Theta_k(t) for Theta_k(t) = sum_alpha alpha^(6k) exp(-2 pi N(alpha) t/sqrt 3), and the Mellin
transform gives the statement (L(s, lambda^(-6k)) = L(s, lambda^(6k)) by the Klein involution). QED
Check [MEASURED, `checks.log` N1]: k = 1, 2, 3, 4 at t = 0.8, relative difference below 1e-39 at 40 digits.

**Lemma 2 [RECALLED: Froehlich-Queyrut 1973; not re-read].** For K/F quadratic and chi a character of K_v^*
trivial on F_v^*, eps(chi, psi_F o Tr, 1/2) = chi(delta) for any delta in K with trace 0. Globally, for an idele
class character psi of F trivial on the ideles of Q, W(psi) = prod_v psi_v(delta) = psi(delta) = 1.
Consistent with Lemma 1: the archimedean factor is (i/(-i))^(3k) = (-1)^k and the factor at sqrt(-3) is
lambda^(6k)(sqrt(-3)) = (-1)^k. For ring-class characters W = 1 also follows from the Epstein functional equation
(each partial zeta function of a class satisfies the functional equation with sign +1 and zeta_g = zeta_(g^-1));
CLASSICAL.

Reading. As a function of the family parameter psi in G^, the epsilon factor is psi -> psi(delta): a homomorphism
G^ -> S^1, i.e. evaluation at one fixed element of G (here the trivial class). Multiplying the rows by W(psi) and
summing over the family translates the kernel of 3.1 by that element. The functional equation of the rows followed by
the family sum is the family sum. This is the statement "Poisson maps the probe to itself" of `ap-order-two`, with
the Weil index eps_c replaced by 1.

### 3.3 Forced coefficients [PROVED]

**Lemma 3.** Let c_a be coefficients such that, after the transform of 3.1, the coefficient of a on the class
g in G is mu(a) xi_g(a) with xi_g a Hecke character times a function of the class. Then c_a = mu(a) F(a) with F
of the same type, and every row is a combination of Moebius sums of the family:

  sum_a c_a psi(a) w(Na/Z) = sum_(psi') Fhat(psi') sum_a mu(a) (xi psi psi')(a) w(Na/Z).

Proof. The transfer kernel is 1 on its support (3.1), so the dual coefficient on a class equals c_a. The family is a
group, so psi psi' is in the family. QED

Consequence. There is no choice of automorphic coefficients (coefficients with a functional equation of their own,
as the cubic theta coefficients in the sextic case) whose dual carries mu. The rows have Dirichlet series
sum_j kappa_j / L(s, xi psi_j); their only "functional equation" is that of L in the denominator, and a reflection
crosses the zeros. Absorption fails. The epsilon factor does not carry mu; it is a character of the family
parameter.

## 4. Transplant of Theorem 1 of `r3-method-limit`: h/r and the range of K

**Proposition 4 (the probe is the Moebius sum) [PROVED-sketch; MEASURED].** For arbitrary bounded x_c and
K <= Y^(1/2 - eps): I(K) = K^(-1/2) A_0 B_0 + O_A(Y^(-A)), A_0 = Y^(-1) sum_s W1(Ns/Y) = c_W + O(Y^(-A)), and
B_0 = Z^(-1/2) sum_c x_c w(Nc/Z).
Proof. For k != 0, A_k = (2 pi i)^(-1) int W1~(v) Y^(v-1) L(v, lambda^(6k)) dv with L entire; shift to Re v = -A and
use Lemma 1: abs(L(-A + it)) << ((3|k| + |t|)^2)^(A + 1/2), so A_k << |k| (k^2/Y)^A Y^(-1). QED
Check (`checks.log` N2, N3; Y = 20000, Z = 5000, x = mu on Z[omega], 72486 and 18150 lattice points):
abs(A_k) < 1e-13 for 1 <= k <= 120 (k/sqrt Y <= 0.85) and abs(A_k) sqrt Y between 0.11 and 0.87 for k >= 200;
sum_k Om A_k B_k equals the k = 0 term to 1e-12 for K <= 160 and departs from it for K >= 400.

**Proposition 5 (ray regime) [PROVED].** An element alpha off the rays has abs(Im alpha) >= sqrt(3)/2 after a unit
rotation, so abs(6 arg alpha mod 2 pi) >= 3 sqrt(3)/sqrt(N alpha) (1 + o(1)) (`checks.log` N5: minimum of
abs(6 arg) sqrt N is 5.1963 for N <= 4000). For K >= (YZ)^(1/2 + eps) and Om Schwartz the family sum is supported on
sc in H. For squarefree c with no rational prime factor, sc is rational iff s = conj(c) t, t rational. The count of
such s with Ns about Y is (Y/Nc)^(1/2). Hence

  I(K) = K^(1/2) Y^(-1/2) Z^(-1/2) sum_c x_c Nc^(-1/2) w_1(Nc/Z) (1 + lower-order terms from rational factors of c).

The pairs (s, c) on the rays number (YZ)^(1/2): this is the coherent set of exponent 1/2. Each c enters with weight
Nc^(-1/2): the Moebius sum appears at shift 1/2. With M(Z) = sum x_c of size Z^beta the ray term is
K^(1/2) Y^(-1/2) Z^(beta - 1), against the Cauchy-Schwarz level (K/Y)^(1/2): ratio Z^(beta - 1). Net gain 0;
detection only for beta >= 1. This is the mechanism of map row Z21 (reciprocal at shift 1/2 cancels the gain
1/2), with the cause visible: in the quadratic case the coherent rows m = t^2 are coherent for every pair (s, c);
here coherence is a relation between s and c.

**Theorem 6 (ledger) [PROVED for (a), (c); MODEL for (b)].**
(a) Unconditional. sum_k abs(A_k)^2 is of order K/Y + 1 and sum_k abs(B_k)^2 <= C (K + Z) for bounded coefficients
(count of pairs c, c' with angles within 1/K; sharp for general coefficients). The k = 0 row has abs(A_0) of order 1
and B_0 = Z^(-1/2) M(Z). Cauchy-Schwarz detects the signal only if Z^(2 beta - 1) > (1 + K/Y)(K + Z) >= Z, i.e.
beta > 1. Abscissa >= 1 for every K and Y.
(b) Model ledger: take sum_k abs(B_k)^2 = K Z^eps (generic size; measured mean of abs(B_k)^2 is 0.14 to 0.16 for
K = 50 to 1500 at Z = 5000, `checks.log` N4, against abs(B_0)^2 = 0.0006). Then for K <= Y the abscissa is
B = (1 + kappa)/2, i.e. h/r = (1 - kappa)/2 in the notation sigma_0 = 1 - h/r. For K >= Y it is
B = 1/2 + kappa - ly/2.
(c) The model hypothesis contains its output: sum_k abs(B_k)^2 <= K Z^eps includes abs(B_0)^2 <= K Z^eps, which is
M(Z) << Z^((1 + kappa)/2 + eps), the same abscissa. At K = 1 it is the conclusion itself.
(d) Range of K. Exact ray support needs kappa >= (1 + ly)/2. A-norm at the level K/Y in (b) needs kappa <= ly.
Positive gain needs kappa < 1. The first two give ly >= 1 and kappa >= 1: the three conditions are inconsistent.
The large sieve without loss needs K >= Z, the same wall. In the ray regime both ledgers give abscissa >= 1.

Ring-class twin [PROVED-sketch]. The same statements hold with K replaced by the order of G_f (about f/3), exact
orthogonality instead of a smooth weight, conductor 3 f^2 in place of k^2 in Proposition 4, and
sum_psi A_psi B_psi = abs(G_f) sum_g A(g^(-1)) B(g) (Parseval over classes). Kernel counts (`checks.log` N6):
elements of Z + fO prime to f with N <= 4000, divided by 6, fall from 388 (f = 5) to 21 (f = 101), the last equal
to the rational integers alone (2 * 63/6); lattice prediction (2 pi/sqrt 3)(4000/f)/6.

Comparison with `n4-spectral-window-moebius` (cap A <= 1, abscissa 1/2 + 1/(2A) >= 1). Same cap by the same
cause: a Moebius polynomial longer than what the family's orthogonality handles needs Moebius cancellation against
the family (here: in sectors of angle 1/K, or in ring classes), which is the Riemann hypothesis for the family.
Here the admissible length is Z <= K (family size, the square root of the conductor k^2), and the output is
abscissa >= 1 in both lanes. The normalisations of A differ and were not reconciled.

## 5. General row

**Theorem 7 (families whose epsilon factor is a character of the family parameter) [PROVED for the three families
listed; the formulation for an abstract family is ARGUED].** Let chi(p, a) be a row pairing: for each row p a Hecke
character of the Moebius variable a. Let tau_h(a) be the kernel of the transform in the row variable at frequency h.
Suppose tau_h(a) = e(a) theta_h(a) on its support, with theta_h a Hecke character or the indicator of a class in a
group through which the whole family factors, and e unimodular with values in a fixed finite set and periodic
(a finite combination of characters). Then:
(i) a coefficient c_a whose dual carries mu(a) xi(a) (xi Hecke times periodic) is c_a = mu(a) xi'(a) with xi' of the
same type (divide by e theta_h);
(ii) every row is a combination of smoothed partial sums of 1/L(s, xi_j chi_p): the direct side is the Moebius side;
(iii) each term of the row transform is again a bilinear form with coefficients mu times (Hecke times periodic):
the second representation is of the same form as the first;
(iv) with Cauchy-Schwarz, the large sieve for the family, convexity, the functional equation of smooth rows and the
row transform, the abscissa is >= 1; any low estimate giving abscissa B < 1 contains the bound
sum mu xi chi_p(a) w(Na/Z) << Z^(B + eps) for a row p carrying the target.

Cases.
* Quadratic rows (m/c): tau_h(c) = (h/c) eps_c, eps_c in {1, i} periodic mod 4 (`ap-order-two` Lemma 0).
* Angular rows lambda^(6k): tau = class indicator on G = circle, e = 1 (3.1); root number 1 (Lemma 1).
* Ring-class / anticyclotomic rows: tau = class indicator on G_f, e = 1; W(psi) = psi(delta) = 1 (Lemma 2).
* Not covered: orders r >= 3, where tau_h(a) = conj(chi_a(h)) g(chi_a)/sqrt(Na) and g(chi_a)/sqrt(Na) is not of the
  form e theta.

**Remark 8 (where mu comes from at order >= 3, and why not at order 2) [CLASSICAL identities; reading ARGUED;
Weil 1952 RECALLED].** As a function of the character, the normalised Gauss sum W(chi) = g(chi)/sqrt q has
2-cocycle W(chi) W(rho)/W(chi rho) = J(chi, rho)/sqrt q when chi, rho, chi rho are all ramified. The Hecke
character attached to Jacobi sums is a -> prod_(pi | a) (-J_pi) (Weil), with the sign -1 at each prime, so
prod_(pi | a) J_pi = mu(a) times a Hecke character. This is the identity of `zf-higher-rows` Thm 1. The sign is the
sign of odd-degree cohomology: -J is a Frobenius eigenvalue on H^1 of the Fermat curve x^r + y^r = 1, of genus
(r-1)(r-2)/2. For r = 2 the curve is a conic, H^1 = 0, chi rho is unramified, and J(chi, chi) = -chi(-1) has
modulus 1: the cocycle is a root of unity, W is a homomorphism up to a periodic sign, and no mu appears. For
characters trivial on the base field the cocycle is identically 1 (Lemma 2). Checked (`jacobi.log`, 15 primes
p = 1 mod 3 up to 997): J(chi_3, chi_3) = a + b omega with norm p and (a, b) = (2, 0) mod 3 in every case
(-J = 1 mod 3, the normalised Hecke value), and J(chi_2, chi_2) = -chi_2(-1) in every case.
Row for the map: **a family absorbs mu only through a nontrivial weight-1 cocycle of its epsilon factor (Jacobi
sums, order >= 3). Families with epsilon a character of the family parameter (quadratic; angular; ring-class; any
family trivial on the ideles of a subfield of index 2) have rows equal to Moebius sums, model abscissa 1 in the
unconditional ledger, and effective order 2 only with a low estimate that contains the conclusion.**

Geometric side. The Klein involution alpha -> conj(alpha) inverts every member of both families; the families are
exactly the characters of the quotient of the idele classes of F by those of Q (a circle times a profinite group).
The family sum is the projection onto the fixed locus of the involution (the rays). On R x S^1 (log-norm times angle)
the explicit formula for the family is Fourier analysis in the S^1 direction, which commutes with the Euler product
in the R direction; nothing in the S^1 direction sees the prime factorisation. The spinor weight 6k enters only
through the gamma factor Gamma(s + 3|k|) (Bessel kernel J_(6k)), which sets the transition k of order sqrt(Y) in
Proposition 4 and nothing else.

## 6. Controls

The lane produces identities and a ledger, not an argument about zeros, so controls are run against the architecture.

* **DH (Epstein).** sum_psi conj(psi(g)) L(s, psi) = abs(G_f) zeta_g(s), the Epstein zeta function of the class g,
  which for a class group with more than one class per genus has no Euler product and has zeros off the line
  (Davenport-Heilbronn 1936, CLASSICAL). Step where the architecture fails for it: the signal identification
  "row = sum mu(c) psi(c) w(Nc/Z) = partial sum of 1/L(s, psi)". It uses complete multiplicativity of psi. For the
  class sum, sum over c in g of mu(c) Nc^(-s) = abs(G)^(-1) sum_psi conj(psi(g))/L(s, psi), which is not
  1/zeta_g(s) and is regular at the zeros of zeta_g. The probe carries no pole at an Epstein zero. Same step as map
  row G20. Since h(O) = 1 for F, the Epstein control exists only for f with more than one class per genus.
* **THIN** (completely multiplicative unimodular twist with a zero at b, `c4-thin-twist-control-78`). Proposition 4
  holds for arbitrary coefficients, so with x_c = mu(c) eps(c) the probe equals the Moebius sum of the control
  and the low estimate is false for it. No step separates zeta from THIN, because the only step that could is the
  missing one: an unconditional low estimate from a second representation.
* **TW-** (signed twist): same as THIN; the identities are coefficient-blind.
* **HEAT, EA, NR, Beurling, phase-rotated transfer operator, theta^w:** not formulable against an identity; not run.

## 7. Requirement flags

* R1 (multiplicativity essential): **not established.** Propositions 4, 5 and Theorem 6 hold for arbitrary bounded
  coefficients x_c. Multiplicativity enters only in naming the row as a partial sum of 1/L.
* R2 (height-uniform, every window): **not established.** The identities are exact but carry no estimate.
* R3 (fails for every control at a named step): **not established.** DH fails at a named step (section 6); THIN and

* R4 (reaches the critical line): **not established.** Abscissa >= 1 unconditionally; 1/2 only with the conclusion
  as input.

## 8. Three weakest steps

1. Theorem 7 for an abstract family is a formulation, not a classification. It is proved for the three listed
   families by exhibiting the kernel. A family whose row transform is not Fourier inversion on a group or Poisson
   summation modulo the conductor (for example rows indexed by automorphic forms on a nonabelian group, where the
   "epsilon factor" is a matrix) is not covered. Remark 8 (weight of the cocycle, Fermat-curve H^1) is a reading of
   classical identities with Weil's theorem recalled from memory; it is not used in any proof here.
2. Lemma 2 (Froehlich-Queyrut) is recalled, not re-read. Only its consequence W = 1 is used; for lambda^(6k) that is
   proved independently (Lemma 1, checked numerically) and for ring-class characters it follows from the Epstein
   functional equation. The local form eps = chi(delta) at ramified primes p | f was not checked.
3. Proposition 5 and Theorem 6(a) are written for squarefree c without rational prime factors and with error terms
   at the level of standard counting; the lower-order terms from rational factors of c and the constant in the
   angular large sieve were not written out. The mixed regime Y^(1/2) < K < (YZ)^(1/2) (strips abs(Im(sc)) <= B) is
   covered only by Theorem 6(a), (b), not by an exact formula. A probe in which A is not a smooth sum (A built from
   automorphic coefficients in the s variable) was not examined beyond Lemma 3, which applies to it as stated.

## 9. Follow-up

None inside group families. The only place left by Theorem 7 is a family whose epsilon factor has a nontrivial
cocycle of weight 1 and effective order 2 at the same time. Remark 8 says that at a single prime these exclude each
other for characters (genus 0 at r = 2). A candidate would have to be nonabelian (epsilon factors of GL(2) twists,
where the cocycle is a Kloosterman or Jacquet-Langlands local factor); map section 6 item 18 (double cover of GL(3))
is the existing entry of that type.
