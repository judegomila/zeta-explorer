## 1. Re-derivation of the PROVED steps

**Theorem V.** (ii) => (i): `||Theta v||^2 = Q(v) >= 0` on `E^`, then A3 Theorem 1 (refereed). (i) =>
(ii): joint eigenvectors with distinct joint eigenvalues are orthogonal; `g` is defined on a countable
set; `||Theta v||^2 = sum m_gamma |V(i gamma)|^2 = Q(v)` under RH; covariance from A3 Lemma 0.
CONFIRMED. Content: the GNS statement of A3 read backwards; any infinite-dimensional separable
Hilbert space with any self-adjoint operator having infinitely many distinct eigenvalues qualifies.
Casimir and Hecke play no part.

**Theorem H, `GL_2`.** Under a realisation RH holds and `U(a)` is diagonal with `e^(-i a gamma)` (A3
Theorem 1 with the density repair), so (HC) gives `T_p u_gamma = 2 cos(gamma log p) u_gamma`. The joint
eigenspace is closed and commutes with the spectral projections of the Laplacian, so it contains a
Maass form with these eigenvalues at `p` outside `S`; any irreducible constituent `pi` has Satake
parameters `{p^(i gamma), p^(-i gamma)}` there. If `pi` is cuspidal, `L^S(s, pi)` is entire
(`L(s, pi)` entire, inverse local factors are polynomials in `p^(-s)`), while
`zeta^S(s + i gamma) zeta^S(s - i gamma)` has a pole at `s = 1 - i gamma`: the second factor is
`zeta^S(1 - 2 i gamma) != 0` and the removed factors `1 - p^(-1)` do not vanish. CONFIRMED. The
sentence "the finitely many missing Euler factors have no zeros" is not the fact used; what is used
is that their inverses are entire and that the removed zeta factors do not cancel the pole.
Residual spectrum: characters, `|lambda_p| = p^(1/2) + p^(-1/2) > 2`. CONFIRMED.
The statement extends to all of `L^2`: on the Eisenstein part `T_p` is multiplication by
`2 cos(t log p)`, which has no eigenvectors. The file restricts to the discrete spectrum without
saying this.

**Theorem H, quaternionic.** Jacquet-Langlands transfer of infinite-dimensional representations to
cuspidal ones with the same unramified components. CONFIRMED as a citation.

**Theorem H, weight 1/2.** Needs: every genuine cuspidal representation of the metaplectic group not
generated by elementary theta series has a cuspidal Waldspurger lift to `PGL_2` with matching
`T_(p^2)` eigenvalues (Waldspurger 1980; RECALLED). On `Gamma_0(4)` with trivial character there are
no cuspidal elementary theta series (Serre-Stark for the holomorphic case; RECALLED for Maass
forms). Correct as a citation; labelled RECALLED by the author, which is the right label. The file
cites Shimura, Niwa and Katok-Sarnak; the statement actually needed (non-vanishing and cuspidality
of the lift for every form) is Waldspurger's.

**Theorem H, `GL_n`, `n >= 3`. GAP (repairable).** Two holes.
(i) The rule fixes only `lambda_p`, the eigenvalue of the standard Hecke operator. For `n >= 3` this
does not determine the Satake parameters, so `L^S(s, pi) = prod zeta^S(s - i e_k gamma)^(c_k)` does
not follow. Repair: with `Pi` the isobaric sum of the characters, `a_p(pi) conj a_p(Pi) =
|a_p(Pi)|^2 >= 0` and `sum_p |a_p(Pi)|^2 p^(-s) -> inf` as `s -> 1+`; the prime-power terms converge
by Luo-Rudnick-Sarnak; `L(s, pi x Pi~)` is a product of shifted standard L-functions of the cuspidal
`pi`, holomorphic at 1. Contradiction.
(ii) "Discrete spectrum of `GL_n`" contains the residual Speh representations for composite `n`
(Moeglin-Waldspurger). The proof treats only cuspidal ones. Repair: for `Speh(tau, b)`, `b >= 2`,
the rule forces `a_p(tau) = O(p^(-(b-1)/2))`, contradicting the pole of `L(s, tau x tau~)`.
With both repairs the statement is true. As written it is not proved for `n >= 3`.

**Remark on Ramakrishnan (7/8).** Ramakrishnan's refinement compares two cuspidal representations
of `GL_2`. Here one side is Eisenstein. Not applicable as cited; not load-bearing.

**Weyl table.** Re-computed: `SL_2(Z)` main terms, area of `Gamma_0(4)` (`2 pi`), `(2,3,7)` (`1/84`;
this group is arithmetic over the cubic field `Q(cos 2 pi/7)`, not a quotient over `Q`), the
Liouville/Bessel count `(nu/pi) log(2 nu/(e x))` at `nu = T/2`, `x = 2 pi`, and the exotic count from
`theta_a`. CONFIRMED. `K_(it/2)(2 pi)` is not Polya's function (that is
`K_(9/4 + it/2) + K_(9/4 - it/2)` at `2 pi`); the reality of the zeros of `K_(iz)(x)` is Polya's
theorem, so the row stands with a corrected name. "Only one-channel objects have a `T log T` law" is
a reading of thirteen rows, not a theorem.

**Proposition P.** Maass-Selberg: with `phi(1/2+it) = e^(-2i arg xi*(1+2it))`,
`||Lambda^a E||^2 = 2 theta_a'(t) + sin(2 theta_a(t))/t`, and the last term vanishes at an exotic
point. CONFIRMED. (c) CONFIRMED (`arg zeta(1+2it) = O(log log t)`). (d) CONFIRMED
(`xi*(rho - 1) = xi*(2 - rho) != 0`). (a) holds for `1 <= a <= 4 pi e^(-gamma)` by the argument:
a zero of `Z_a` off the line gives an `L^2` eigenfunction of the self-adjoint `Delta_a` with
eigenvalue `s(1-s)`, hence `s` is real; real zeros are excluded in this range (Lagarias-Suzuki;
MEASURED below). The explanation given in (b) is an ERROR (item 1 above). The positivity of the norm
at an exotic point is automatic, because it is a norm; it is not a consequence of the location of
the poles of `phi`. The correct statement of what the square encodes: the existence of the
Eisenstein series (self-adjointness of `Delta_a`) puts every non-real zero of `Z_a` on the line, and
the size of the pole term at `s = 1` decides the real ones.

**Theorem R(a)-(c).** (a) is Aronszajn-Donoghue in Simon's convention. CONFIRMED. (b): a real-analytic
`rho >= 0` has `G(E) < inf` iff `rho(E) = 0`. CONFIRMED; the off-line clause is empty (the operators
are self-adjoint and `rho(gamma) != 0` in general). (c): far part `-m_0/E - m_2/E^3`, near part
`O(E e^(-cE/2))`. CONFIRMED and MEASURED (section 3). (c) uses the exponential decay of `rho` and
`rho'`. It does not transfer to a point evaluation or any period functional outside `L^2`, where the
density grows and `F` needs a subtraction; the file says the automorphic case is described only.

**Theorem R(d).** The identity `Phi = -1/(M + i/alpha)`, `Im Phi = pi rho_alpha`,
`Re Phi(gamma) = 0` is correct, and `M = -Xi'/Xi` is Herglotz iff all zeros of `Xi` are real.
CONFIRMED. Two defects.
(i) Item 2 above: `rho_alpha` is outside the Setting. Its mean on `[300, 400]` is 0.0993 (author's
run; prediction `(1/pi)/(1 + (1/2) log(t/2 pi))` = 0.106), so `int rho_alpha = inf` and also
`int rho_alpha/(1+|t|) = inf`. The object is a deficiency-(1,1) symmetric restriction with a
one-parameter family of extensions, fixed here by evenness. Repair inside the Setting: take
`B(z) = i/alpha + z/m`; then `rho = (1/(pi alpha))/((t/m - Xi'/Xi)^2 + alpha^(-2))` is integrable.
(ii) The "iff" in the claim ("one member carries all iff the weight is `1/|xi + alpha xi'|^2`, iff
RH") is stronger than (d). (d) gives the family `rho = b/(pi |M_c + B|^2)` with arbitrary
`c_gamma > 0` and arbitrary Herglotz `B`; `1/|D|^2` is one member. And for an arbitrary even weight
the statement "every ordinate is an eigenvalue of `A_inf`" does not need RH: the ordinates are real
numbers whether or not the zeros are on the line, and `M_c = sum c_gamma/(gamma - z)` is Herglotz
for any real points. RH enters only through the requirement `rho = |zeta(1/2+it)|^2 |W|^2` with `W`
non-vanishing (then (b) applies), or through the choice `M = -Xi'/Xi`. Also `W` has poles at
multiple zeros of `Xi`, so "exists iff RH" needs "and the zeros are simple" for a non-vanishing
real-analytic `W`.

**Theorem Z.** Algebra re-derived: `Im (rho/2)(1 - rho/2) = (gamma/2)(1 - beta)`; at `s = (1+rho)/4`,
`Im s(1-s) = (gamma/8)(1 - beta)`; `s_rho(1 - s_rho) = (1 - (rho - 1/2)^2)/4`;
`rho(1 - rho) = 1/4 - (rho - 1/2)^2`. CONFIRMED. The coefficient and constant-term formulas are
RECALLED by the author and by me; the theorem is conditional on them and should be labelled
PROVED-from-RECALLED throughout. Two remarks.
(i) Z(b) needs `zeta(4 s_rho - 1) = zeta(2 rho) != 0`, which holds for `beta >= 1/2` only; for the
partner zero with `beta < 1/2` the vanishing of the coefficient is not established.
(ii) "The metaplectic version changes the line" is not a property of the cover. The same shift
occurs in weight 0 at a CM point (the file says so in (b)): it is the difference between poles of
the intertwiner (`zeta(2s)` in a denominator) and zeros of a period (`zeta(s)` or `zeta(2s - 1/2)`
in a numerator). Z(c) is then the statement that `s_rho(1 - s_rho)` is real iff `beta = 1/2`,
which is a reparametrisation of RH. The attribution of the pole vectors to Zagier was not checked
against his paper.

**Proposition C.** `(x^2 - d^2/da^2) f = sum_w e^(aw)`; explicit formula; jumps of `f'` at
`log p^k`; `J + P subset J` with `2 e^P`, `4 e^P`, `3 e^P` prime powers is contradictory. CONFIRMED.
The remark "the density argument alone does not give this" is wrong: a realisation presupposes RH
(A3 Theorem 1), under RH `m(gamma) << log gamma/log log gamma`, so there are `>> T log log T`
distinct ordinates below `T` against `O(T)` lattice points. Unconditionally, a positive proportion
of zeros is simple. Proposition C is correct and not needed.

Consequences for the control statement.

1. "Proposition P fails for DH at a named step" is ARGUED only, as the author says, and the step
   named is wrong. The step given is "the quotient is not inner in `Re s > 1/2`, so phase
   monotonicity (b) fails". For zeta the quotient is not inner either (pole at `s = 1`) and phase
   monotonicity fails on `0 < t < 3`. The step that separates zeta from DH is the existence of the
   Eisenstein series, which makes the Maass-Selberg quantity a norm. That is "not formulable for
   DH", which the map (section 2, Q1) does not count as a quantitative handle.
2. A violation of the conclusion for DH needs a DH zero with real part above 1. With
   `f = c L(s, chi) + conj(c) L(s, conj chi)`, a zero at `sigma > 1` needs
   `sum_{p = +-2 mod 5} +-p^(-s)` real and equal to -1.2940 or +1.8476; at `sigma = 1` the absolute sum
   reaches 1.294 only after the 14 primes up to 97 (leading term; higher prime powers neglected).
   Such a zero requires near-alignment of about 14 prime phases; none is known at a computable height and none was sought here. The claim "DH fails
   Proposition P" is therefore true only at heights nobody has reached, and whether a pole of the
   quotient in `Re s > 1/2` forces an off-line zero of the twin sum is itself only a Rouche
   heuristic.
3. "E_a and T-NR pass" is conditional. Zeros of `xi_E(w)` have real part `Re rho +- a`, which is
   below 1 only if zeta has no zero with `Re rho > 1 - a`; this is not known for any `a < 1/2`. The
   file's bound "at most `1/2 + a`" assumes RH for zeta. `xi_E` also has poles at `1 +- a`, so real
   exceptional zeros of the twin sum occur for large truncation height as they do for zeta. Not run.
4. HEAT, TW, U, Beurling cells: "not formulable" or "no symmetry". Not run by the author or by me.
   R(d) for HEAT was not run.

## 5. Novelty

No online search was run (same as the author). From memory: Theorem H is the classical fact that
Eisenstein Hecke eigensystems do not occur in the cuspidal spectrum. Proposition P is Colin de
Verdiere (1983) with the zero statement of Lagarias-Suzuki (2006) and Ki (2005). Theorem R(a)-(c) is
Aronszajn-Donoghue applied to the Tate density; the automorphic version with interlacing is
Bombieri-Garrett. R(d) is the de Branges structure function of `Xi - i alpha Xi'` (Lagarias).
Theorem Z is Hejhal's account of Haas's values together with Zagier. Proposition C is elementary.
Atlas PR 27 was re-fetched for this review: no entry on pseudo-Laplacians, rank-one perturbations or
multiplicity one. Record as a consolidation of the cases A3 left open, not as a new theorem.

## 6. What the result does not cover

1. Any realisation with no rule at the primes: by Theorem V that class is RH itself, so the lane
   gives a classification of hypotheses, not a no-go, there.
2. Rules at the primes that are not a fixed Laurent polynomial in `p^(i gamma)`, or that hold at a
   set of primes that is not cofinite.
3. `GL_n`, `n >= 3`, and residual spectrum, until the two repairs in section 1 are written.
4. Period-constrained (singular rank-one) operators in the automorphic setting: Theorem R(c) is
   proved only for integrable exponentially decaying densities on `GL(1)`. Whether a Hecke-natural
   period has `F(gamma) = 0` at the zeros is untouched.
5. Compact tori of rank at least 2 without Hecke covariance.
6. Non-covariant, truncated or cone-restricted realisations (as in A3).
7. Any control computation inside the strip for Proposition P: see section 4.
