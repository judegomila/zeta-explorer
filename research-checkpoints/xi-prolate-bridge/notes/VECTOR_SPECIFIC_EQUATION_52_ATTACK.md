# Vector-specific equation (5.2) attack

## 0. Claim boundary and outcome

Equation (5.2) of `DIRECT_RH_ATTACK.md` controls the complete `L2` angle
between the projected prolate state and the finite Weil ground vector.  The
projective bridge in `notes/PROJECTIVE_LOW_MODE_DETERMINANT_BRIDGE.md` shows
that this is stronger than locally uniform convergence of the characteristic
functions requires.

This note gives the corresponding exact resolvent reduction.  There are four
conclusions.

1. Normalize the ground **line** by its coefficient on the distinguished
   prolate vector, rather than by unit norm.  Its correction is exactly

   \[
     -(A-\mu I)^{-1}b.
   \]

2. At a projective cutoff `M`, it is enough to make the low observable

   \[
     \sup_{s\leq U}\|P_M(A-sI)^{-1}b\|
   \]

   of order `lambda^(-1/2)`.  The complete shifted solve need only grow
   slowly enough to be killed by the Cauchy-transform tail.  For
   `M=ceil(lambda)`, bounded or polylogarithmic growth is sufficient.

3. The low observable has a one-inverse, vector-specific bound

   \[
   \sup_{s\leq U}\|P_M(A-sI)^{-1}b\|^2
   \leq
   \| (P_M|_{\mathcal K})(A-U I)^{-1}(P_M|_{\mathcal K})^*\|
   \langle b,(A-U I)^{-1}b\rangle .
   \]

   Both factors have exact quotient-source pseudoinverse formulas.  No lower
   singular value of the physical source synthesis occurs.

4. An even weaker, compact-set graph criterion controls the Mellin transform
   of the correction directly and does not require any complete `L2` solve
   bound.  It uses one inverse on the forcing and one inverse on the actual
   Mellin evaluation vector.

These are conditional reductions.  The required shifted signs, low
leverage/forcing product, and slowly growing complete cyclic solve are not
proved on an unbounded schedule.  The current source-triangle version of
high-Fourier coercivity cannot supply the sign at `M=ceil(lambda)`; an exact
inequality below quantifies that failure.  This note proves neither equation
(5.2), locally uniform determinant convergence for the Weil matrices, nor a
Riemann-hypothesis conclusion.  The project state remains `P0_INVALIDATED`.

## 1. Exact cyclic Feshbach normalization

Fix one finite pair `(lambda,N)`.  In the inversion-even sector, let

\[
 \kappa={P_Nk_\lambda^+\over\|P_Nk_\lambda^+\|},\qquad
 a=\|P_Nk_\lambda^+\|>0,
\]

\[
 \mathcal K=H_+\cap\kappa^\perp,\qquad
 q=\langle\kappa,Q\kappa\rangle,\qquad
 b=(Q-qI)\kappa\in\mathcal K,
\]

and let

\[
 A=P_{\mathcal K}Q|_{\mathcal K}.                         \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus, relative to `C*kappa direct-sum mathcal K`,

\[
 Q_+=\begin{bmatrix}q&b^*\\b&A\end{bmatrix}.             \tag{1.2}
\]

Let `U` be any rigorous variational upper bound for the lowest even
eigenvalue.  Assume the two strict, finite signs

\[
 B:=A-U I\succ0,\qquad Q_- - U I\succ0.                   \tag{1.3}
\]

No uniform lower bound in (1.3) is assumed.

### Theorem 1.1 (ground-line Feshbach formula)

Under (1.3), `Q` has a unique global lowest eigenvalue `mu<=U`, its unit
ground vector `xi` is inversion even, and

\[
 c=\langle\kappa,\xi\rangle\ne0.                           \tag{1.4}
\]

After choosing the phase so that `c>0`, the coefficient-normalized ground
line is

\[
 \boxed{
 {\xi\over c}=\kappa-z_\mu,\qquad
 z_\mu=(A-\mu I)^{-1}b.}                                  \tag{1.5}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{
 q-\mu=\langle b,(A-\mu I)^{-1}b\rangle,}                 \tag{1.6}
\]

and, with

\[
 \tau_U=\|(A-U I)^{-1}b\|,                               \tag{1.7}
\]

one has

\[
 \|z_\mu\|\leq\tau_U.                                   \tag{1.8}
\]

**Proof.**  Cauchy interlacing gives
`lambda_2(Q_+)>=lambda_min(A)>U`, while the variational definition gives a
lowest even eigenvalue `mu<=U`.  Hence that eigenvalue is simple.  The odd
sign in (1.3) makes it the unique global ground eigenvalue.  A ground vector
orthogonal to `kappa` would be an eigenvector of `A` with eigenvalue at most
`U`, contradicting (1.3), so (1.4) holds.

Write `xi=c*kappa+v`.  The complementary block equation is

\[
 (A-\mu I)v=-cb,
\]

which proves (1.5).  Substitution in the first block equation proves (1.6).
Finally, `A-mu I=(A-U I)+(U-mu)I`.  The two resolvents are scalar functions
of the same positive Hermitian matrix, so their squared action on `b` is
ordered term by term in a spectral basis.  This proves (1.8).  \(\square\)

The correction lives in a smaller exact space.  Define

\[
 \mathcal C_b=\operatorname {span}\{b,Ab,A^2b,\ldots\}.    \tag{1.9}
\]

Because `A` is Hermitian, `mathcal C_b` reduces `A`; its orthogonal
complement also reduces `A` and is uncoupled from `kappa`.  Thus (1.5)--(1.8)
depend only on the cyclic spectral measure of `A` generated by `b`.  Tiny
eigenvalues in `mathcal C_b^perp` affect the strict ground-state comparison,
but they do not enter the correction or `tau_U`.

## 2. Projective low-resolvent criterion

Let `P_M` be the centered log-Fourier projection onto `|n|<=M`, with
`1<=M<N`, and write

\[
 T_M=P_M|_{\mathcal K}:\mathcal K\longrightarrow E_M.      \tag{2.1}
\]

For the unit ground vector, choose the allowed nonzero projective scalar

\[
 s={1\over c}.                                             \tag{2.2}
\]

Then (1.5) gives the exact error

\[
 d=s\xi-\kappa=-z_\mu.                                    \tag{2.3}
\]

Define the low resolvent observable

\[
 \Lambda_M(U)=
 \sup_{s\leq U}\|T_M(A-sI)^{-1}b\|.                      \tag{2.4}
\]

The supremum is finite because `A-U I` is positive.  It cannot in general
be replaced by the value at `U`: projection by `T_M` destroys the spectral
monotonicity of the vector norm.

### Theorem 2.1 (vector-specific projective bridge)

Put `L=2*log(lambda)` and let `e=||(I-P_N)k_lambda^+||`.  For a fixed compact
rectangle

\[
 K_{D,\eta}=\{z:|\operatorname {Re}z|\leq D,
                    |\operatorname {Im}z|\leq\eta\},
 \qquad 0\leq\eta<\tfrac12,
\]

assume the cutoff condition of Theorem 2.1 in
`notes/PROJECTIVE_LOW_MODE_DETERMINANT_BRIDGE.md`.  The entire function

\[
 F_{\lambda,N}(z)={4a\over c}\widehat\xi(z)               \tag{2.5}
\]

has only real zeros and satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{z\in K_{D,\eta}}
 |F_{\lambda,N}(z)-4\widehat{k_\lambda^+}(z)|
 \leq{}&4\sqrt L\,\lambda^\eta e\\
 &+4a\sqrt L\,\lambda^\eta\Lambda_M(U)\\
 &+{8\sqrt2\over\pi}a\lambda^\eta
       \sqrt{L\over M}\,\tau_U .                         \tag{2.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

**Proof.**  The source determinant theorem applies to every nonzero scalar
multiple of the unique simple even ground vector, so (2.5) has the same real
zero set as `widehat(xi)`.  By (2.3),

\[
 \|P_Md\|\leq\Lambda_M(U),\qquad
 \|(I-P_M)d\|\leq\|z_\mu\|\leq\tau_U.
\]

Insert these two bounds in equation (3.8) of the projective bridge note.
This proves (2.6).  \(\square\)

On any deterministic schedule with `a=O(1)`, Theorem 2.1 gives the following
sufficient replacement for the complete rate in equation (5.2):

\[
 \sqrt L\,\lambda^\eta e\longrightarrow0,                 \tag{2.7}
\]

\[
 \sqrt L\,\lambda^\eta\Lambda_M(U)\longrightarrow0,       \tag{2.8}
\]

\[
 \lambda^\eta\sqrt{L/M}\,\tau_U\longrightarrow0          \tag{2.9}
\]

for every fixed `eta<1/2`.  In particular, for

\[
 M=\lceil\lambda\rceil,                                   \tag{2.10}
\]

the targets

\[
 \boxed{\Lambda_M(U)=O(\lambda^{-1/2}),\qquad
        \tau_U=\lambda^{o(1)}}                             \tag{2.11}
\]

are sufficient.  A bounded or polylogarithmic `tau_U` satisfies the second
condition.  The already-proved projection estimate supplies (2.7).  The
conditions in (2.11) are not proved for the Weil matrices.

## 3. One-inverse low leverage bound

The low observable in (2.4) does not require the norm of a right inverse on
the ambient complement.  Define

\[
 \mathcal E_U=\langle b,B^{-1}b\rangle,                    \tag{3.1}
\]

and the low inverse leverage

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Theta_M(U)
 &:=\|T_MB^{-1/2}\|^2\\
 &=\boxed{\|T_MB^{-1}T_M^*\|}\\
 &=\boxed{
   \sup_{0\ne v\in\mathcal K}
   {\|P_Mv\|^2\over\langle v,Bv\rangle}.}                 \tag{3.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

The middle expression is the operator norm on `E_M`; the first is the
operator norm from `mathcal K` to `E_M`; and the last is the graph-norm
embedding constant for `P_M|_(mathcal K)`.  These are equal, not successive
estimates.  The last equality follows by writing `v=B^(-1/2)y`.  Zero
directions of `T_M^*` are harmless.

### Theorem 3.1 (B-weighted vector bound)

For every real `s<=U`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \|T_M(A-sI)^{-1}b\|^2
 \leq\Theta_M(U)\mathcal E_U.}                            \tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{\Lambda_M(U)^2
 \leq\Theta_M(U)\mathcal E_U.}                            \tag{3.4}
\]

**Proof.**  Put `h=U-s>=0`.  Since `A-sI=B+hI`,

\[
 T_M(A-sI)^{-1}b
 =T_MB^{-1/2}\,[B^{1/2}(B+hI)^{-1}b].                     \tag{3.5}
\]

The first factor has squared norm (3.2).  In a spectral basis of `B`,

\[
 \|B^{1/2}(B+hI)^{-1}b\|^2
 =\sum_j{x_j\over(x_j+h)^2}|b_j|^2
 \leq\sum_j{|b_j|^2\over x_j}
 =\mathcal E_U.                                            \tag{3.6}
\]

Equations (3.5)--(3.6) prove (3.3), uniformly in `s`, and hence (3.4).
\(\square\)

In normalization-free form the projective low target is therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 a^2\mathcal E_U\Theta_M(U)=O(\lambda^{-1}).}              \tag{3.7}
\]

This is a product of the actual forcing energy and the actual low-observable
inverse leverage.  It is not a lower bound for the smallest eigenvalue of
`B`.  A tiny direction which is missed by `b`, or by the low observation,
does not enter the corresponding factor.

The cyclic spectral measure `nu_b` of `B` makes the distinction explicit:

\[
 \mathcal E_U=\int_0^\infty{x^{-1}}\,d\nu_b(x),\qquad
 \tau_U^2=\int_0^\infty{x^{-2}}\,d\nu_b(x).                \tag{3.8}
\]

Thus (3.7) uses one inverse, while the relaxed complete solve in (2.11) uses
two inverse powers only along the single cyclic forcing measure.  Neither
quantity sees an uncoupled bad direction.

## 4. Direct compact-set graph criterion

The projective split still retains the complete `L2` quantity `tau_U`,
albeit at a much weaker rate.  It can be removed entirely if the Mellin
evaluation is estimated in the shifted graph norm.

For `z` in a compact rectangle, let `ell_z` be the Riesz vector in `E_N` for
the functional `f -> widehat(f)(z)`, and put

\[
 g_z=P_{\mathcal K}\ell_z.                                \tag{4.1}
\]

Define the compact evaluation leverage

\[
 \mathcal L_{D,\eta}(U)
 =\sup_{z\in K_{D,\eta}}\langle g_z,B^{-1}g_z\rangle.      \tag{4.2}
\]

For each fixed `z`, Hilbert-space duality gives the exact graph formula

\[
 \langle g_z,B^{-1}g_z\rangle
 =\sup_{0\ne v\in\mathcal K}
   {|\widehat v(z)|^2\over\langle v,Bv\rangle},            \tag{4.2a}
\]

while the forcing factor has the parallel identity

\[
 \mathcal E_U
 =\sup_{0\ne v\in\mathcal K}
   {|\langle v,b\rangle|^2\over\langle v,Bv\rangle}.      \tag{4.2b}
\]

Thus (4.3) pairs exactly two graph-dual directions; neither expression is
an ambient inverse norm.

### Theorem 4.1 (one-inverse Mellin graph bound)

Under (1.3),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{z\in K_{D,\eta}}
 |a\,\widehat{z_\mu}(z)|
 \leq
 \left(a^2\mathcal E_U\mathcal L_{D,\eta}(U)\right)^{1/2}.}
                                                                    \tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore the projection condition (2.7) and

\[
 \boxed{
 a^2\mathcal E_U\mathcal L_{D,\eta}(U)\longrightarrow0}   \tag{4.4}
\]

for every fixed `D` and `eta<1/2` imply locally uniform convergence of the
projectively normalized characteristic functions to `Xi`, conditional on
the finite signs (1.3).

**Proof.**  Since `z_mu` lies in `mathcal K`,

\[
 \widehat{z_\mu}(z)=\langle g_z,(A-\mu I)^{-1}b\rangle.
\]

Put `h=U-mu>=0` and factor exactly as in (3.5):

\[
 |\langle g_z,(B+hI)^{-1}b\rangle|
 \leq\|B^{-1/2}g_z\|
       \|B^{1/2}(B+hI)^{-1}b\|.
\]

Equation (3.6) bounds the second factor by `sqrt(mathcal E_U)`, proving
(4.3).  Apply the support bound to `P_Nk_lambda^+-k_lambda^+` and use
(1.5), exactly as in (2.6), to obtain (4.4).  \(\square\)

There is an exact version at the unknown eigenvalue.  With
`C=A-mu I`, equation (1.6) and graph Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
 |a\,\widehat{z_\mu}(z)|^2
 \leq
 a^2(q-\mu)\,\langle g_z,C^{-1}g_z\rangle.                 \tag{4.5}
\]

The forcing factor is the scalar Feshbach energy, while the second factor is
only the leverage of the Mellin evaluation vector.  Equation (4.3) is the
source-checkable upper version obtained by replacing `C` with `B<=C`.

If one discards all orientation and uses `B>=gamma I`, then

\[
 \mathcal E_U\leq{\|b\|^2\over\gamma},\qquad
 \mathcal L_{D,\eta}(U)
 \leq{L\lambda^{2\eta}\over\gamma}.                      \tag{4.6}
\]

The right side of (4.3) becomes
`a*sqrt(L)*lambda^eta*||b||/gamma`, the old scalar
residual--gap fallback.  Thus (4.3) retains exactly the two orientations
which that fallback discards.

## 5. Exact source-quotient reconstruction

Let `X` be any finite source-coordinate space and let

\[
 W:X\longrightarrow\mathcal K                              \tag{5.1}
\]

be onto.  It may have a kernel and may be arbitrarily ill-conditioned in
the source `L2` metric.  Define the complete shifted pullback

\[
 \mathfrak B=W^*BW.                                        \tag{5.2}
\]

Because `B` is positive and `W` is onto,

\[
 \ker\mathfrak B=\ker W.                                  \tag{5.3}
\]

To distinguish the two different remainders, put

\[
 p=P_Nk_\lambda^+=P_NC_\lambda K_{\rm global}=a\kappa,\qquad
 r_{\rm phys}=(Q-qI)p=ab,                                  \tag{5.3a}
\]

and let

\[
 K_{\rm global}:=S\mathcal E(\widetilde h_\lambda),\qquad
 R_{\rm glob}=K_{\rm global}-p                             \tag{5.3b}
\]

be the global radical remainder from
`notes/EXACT_RADICAL_LEAKAGE_IDENTITY.md`.  Here
`mathcal W_Z` is the absolutely convergent signed spectral-tail pairing
defined there, not a positive inner product.  Its exact weak-residual
identity, equation (2.1) of
`notes/WEAK_RESIDUAL_SAMPLING_REDUCTION.md`, is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal W_Z(v,R_{\rm glob})
 =-\langle v,r_{\rm phys}\rangle
 =-a\langle v,b\rangle\qquad(v\in\mathcal K).}             \tag{5.3c}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (quotient inverse identity)

On `mathcal K`,

\[
 \boxed{W\mathfrak B^\dagger W^*=B^{-1}.}                 \tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
 \rho_R\in X
 \quad\hbox{represents}\quad
 x\longmapsto\mathcal W_Z(Wx,R_{\rm glob}),               \tag{5.5}
\]

and

\[
 y_z=W^*g_z,                                               \tag{5.6}
\]

then the two graph factors are reconstructed exactly by

\[
 \boxed{
 a^2\mathcal E_U=\rho_R^*\mathfrak B^\dagger\rho_R,\qquad
 \langle g_z,B^{-1}g_z\rangle
 =y_z^*\mathfrak B^\dagger y_z.}                          \tag{5.7}
\]

The low leverage is likewise

\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta_M(U)
 =\|T_MW\mathfrak B^\dagger W^*T_M^*\|.}                 \tag{5.8}
\]

**Proof.**  Put `C=B^(1/2)W`.  It is onto and
`mathfrak B=C^*C`.  The Moore--Penrose identities give

\[
 C(C^*C)^\dagger C^*=I_{\mathcal K}.
\]

Multiplying on both sides by `B^(-1/2)` proves (5.4).  The exact identity
(5.3c) shows that, up to the harmless inner-product convention,
`rho_R=-aW^*b`.  Equations (5.4), (3.1), (4.2), and (3.2) now prove
(5.7)--(5.8).  \(\square\)

Equations (5.4)--(5.8) are invariant under redundant source coordinates and
under any invertible change of source basis.  They do not use the source
`L2` Gram, a source-whitened coframe floor, or a right inverse of `W`.
However, they do not manufacture sign: positivity of `mathfrak B` on
`(ker W)^perp` is equivalent to positivity of `B` because `W` is onto.  The
complete pole, archimedean, minus-prime, physical-shift, and bridge cross
terms must remain in (5.2).

There is a useful one-power sampling corollary.  Let `G>=0` be the ordinary
paired-zero sampling Gram on `mathcal K`, and suppose

\[
 |\mathcal W_Z(v,R_{\rm glob})|^2
 \leq\epsilon_\lambda\langle v,Gv\rangle,                 \tag{5.9}
\]

as already follows from sequence Cauchy--Schwarz.  If the complete signed
graph comparison

\[
 G\preceq C_\lambda B                                    \tag{5.10}
\]

is proved, then

\[
 \boxed{a^2\mathcal E_U\leq
        \epsilon_\lambda C_\lambda.}                     \tag{5.11}
\]

This uses one inverse of the signed graph form.  The stronger quantity
`||B^(-1)G B^(-1)||`, needed to control the complete Euclidean correction,
does not occur.  Combining (5.11) with either (3.7) or (4.4) isolates the
remaining low-leverage or Mellin-leverage target.  The comparison (5.10) is
not currently proved for the complete Weil form.

## 6. Audit of the lambda-scale projective cutoff

The Cauchy-transform calculation and constants in
`notes/PROJECTIVE_LOW_MODE_DETERMINANT_BRIDGE.md` are correct.  Its assertion
that

\[
 \|P_{\lceil\lambda\rceil}\kappa\|\longrightarrow1        \tag{6.1}
\]

on the locked source schedule has a direct existing proof: equation (5.2)
of `notes/PROJECTED_STATE_NORM_LOWER_BOUND.md` gives convergence of the
extended projected state to the fixed limiting state, while its equation
(2.4), used with `N=ceil(lambda)`, bounds the limiting-state Fourier tail by

\[
 {C_*\log\lambda\over\pi(\lceil\lambda\rceil+1)}
 \longrightarrow0.                                       \tag{6.2}
\]

Together with `a->a_infinity>0`, this proves (6.1).  This citation should be
made explicit when (6.1) is used to show that the minimizing projective
scalar is nonzero.

The existing high-Fourier coercivity theorem cannot prove the shifted high
sign at this cutoff.  Its constants obey, exactly,

\[
 C_a(\lambda)\geq4L(\lambda-1),\qquad
 c_\lambda\geq4L(\lambda-1),                              \tag{6.3}
\]

because the first term is the displayed `R_lambda` and the second is the
displayed prime contribution `2*pi*B_p`.  For
`M=ceil(lambda)` and every locked `lambda>=5/2`, equation (5.1) of
`notes/HIGH_FOURIER_COERCIVITY.md` therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 g_{\lambda,M}
 &\leq\log{\lambda+2\over2}-8L(\lambda-1)\\
 &\leq[1-16(\lambda-1)]\log\lambda<0.                     \tag{6.4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus that theorem supplies only a negative lower bound at the projective
cutoff.  For a nonnegative shift and desired nonnegative high floor, its
advance cutoff (5.5) is at least

\[
 \boxed{
 M_*\geq
 \left\lceil2e^{1/(2\pi)}
       \lambda^{16(\lambda-1)}\right\rceil-1.}             \tag{6.5}
\]

The exact Cauchy representation remains valid; the obstruction is the
source-triangle estimate `C_a+c_lambda`, which has discarded the needed
pole--archimedean--prime cancellation.  Consequently the current
high-Fourier Schur machinery does not prove either (1.3) or the low
projective line estimate at `M=ceil(lambda)`.  A cancellation-preserving
shifted lower form, or the quotient graph comparison (5.10) together with
the leverage bound, is still required.

## 7. Strictness: a high-frequency ground-line model

The projective and graph reductions are genuinely weaker than complete
`L2` angle convergence.  On `[-pi,pi]`, let `phi_0,phi_j` be the centered
orthonormal Fourier modes and, in their ordered basis, put

\[
 Q_j=\epsilon_j
 \begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix},qquad
 \epsilon_j>0.                                            \tag{7.1}
\]

Take `kappa=phi_0`.  Then

\[
 q=0,\quad b=\epsilon_j\phi_j,\quad A=\epsilon_j,
 \quad\mu_j={1-\sqrt5\over2}\epsilon_j.                  \tag{7.2}
\]

Writing `varphi=(1+sqrt(5))/2`, the coefficient-normalized ground line is

\[
 {\xi_j\over\langle\phi_0,\xi_j\rangle}
 =\phi_0-\varphi^{-1}\phi_j.                              \tag{7.3}
\]

Its complete `L2` correction has the fixed norm `varphi^(-1)`.  Nevertheless,

\[
 \widehat{\phi_j}(z)
 ={2\sin(\pi z)\over\sqrt{2\pi}(z-j)},                    \tag{7.4}
\]

which tends to zero uniformly on every fixed compact subset of the complex
plane.  The graph estimate (4.5) is exact here:

\[
 q-\mu_j={\epsilon_j\over\varphi},\qquad
 \langle g_z,(A-\mu_j)^{-1}g_z\rangle
 ={|\widehat{\phi_j}(z)|^2\over\epsilon_j\varphi}.        \tag{7.5}
\]

Their product has square root
`|widehat(phi_j)(z)|/varphi`, precisely the transform of the correction.
The ambient inverse may diverge as `epsilon_j->0`, while the locally observed
ground line still converges projectively.  This is a finite-dimensional
functional-analytic model, not a Weil matrix or an RH approximation.  It
shows only that an ambient inverse norm cannot be a necessary determinant-
convergence condition.

## 8. Remaining exact target

The weakest source-reconstructible route isolated here is:

1. prove the strict shifted signs (1.3) on one unbounded schedule;
2. either prove the projective pair (2.11), with the low factor reduced by
   (3.7), or prove the compact graph product (4.4) directly;
3. retain the complete signed pullback (5.2), including every bridge and
   pole--archimedean--minus-prime cross term; and
4. combine it with the already-proved projection and source-normalized
   prolate limits.

Whole-target source capture may be used in (5.2) without paying a coframe
condition number, but by (5.4) it leaves the physical sign and all graph
quadratic forms unchanged.  The high-tail right-inverse obstruction in
`notes/QUANTITATIVE_HIGH_TAIL_BREAKPOINT_COFRAME_ATTACK.md` therefore does
not obstruct this quotient formulation, and it also does not prove any of
the required signed or leverage estimates.
