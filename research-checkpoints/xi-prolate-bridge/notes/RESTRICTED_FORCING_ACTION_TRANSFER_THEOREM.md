# Restricted-forcing action-transfer theorem

## 0. Exact outcome and claim boundary

This note gives an exact, vector-specific replacement for a global
left-close estimate.  Let `B,Delta>0` on a finite-dimensional physical
space and let `c` be one forcing vector.  The transfer factor

\[
 K_c={\|B^{-1}c\|\over\|\Delta^{-1}c\|}
\]

has square exactly equal to the first moment of the right-singular spectral
measure of `B^(-1)Delta` generated by `Delta^(-1)c`.  Consequently, large singular
values are harmless for this one forcing vector when their weighted
overlaps are sufficiently small.  A uniform cap on the complete cyclic
support is sufficient but strictly stronger.

The theorem is also written in the possibly noninjective normalized source
coordinates `A=WD^(-1/2)`.  If

\[
 J=A^*A,\qquad H=A^*BA,\qquad g=A^*c,
\]

then, on `S=(ker A)^perp`,

\[
 \boxed{
 K_c^2={\langle H_S^{-1}g,J_SH_S^{-1}g\rangle
              \over \langle g,J_Sg\rangle}.}
\]

The source identities retain the metric in the correct order.  In
particular,

\[
 A^*B^{-1}c=J_SH_S^{-1}g,
\]

not `H_S^(-1)g`.

An exact two-dimensional family has the fixed comparison

\[
 \Delta_\varepsilon\preceq B_\varepsilon
 \preceq2\Delta_\varepsilon
\]

while `K_c` diverges like the square root of the source condition number.
Thus a one-sided floor, even supplemented by a fixed two-sided Loewner
comparison, does not imply the Euclidean one-vector transfer bound.  The
usual conditioning loss is sharp in order.

This is an abstract finite-dimensional transfer theorem and implication
countermodel.  It proves no bound for the actual prolate--Weil forcing
vector, no physical forcing estimate, no low/high reducing property, no
high-complement or odd-sector floor, no residual action, no equation (5.2)
of `DIRECT_RH_ATTACK.md`, no determinant limit, no RH conclusion, and no
Lambda bound.  No status, replay, source-lock, artifact, or integration file
is changed.

\[
 A:X\longrightarrow K

 S=(\ker A)^\perp,\qquad

The restriction `J_S=J|_S` is positive definite.  Define the quotient

 \Delta=(AA^*)^{-1}=(AA^*)^\dagger\big|_K.             \tag{1.2}

## 2. Noninjective source-coordinate theorem

### Theorem 2.1 (polar source transfer)

Define

\[
 U=AJ_S^{-1/2}:S\longrightarrow K.                    \tag{2.1}
\]

Then `U` is unitary and

\[
 U^*\Delta U=J_S^{-1},\qquad
 U^*BU=J_S^{-1/2}H_SJ_S^{-1/2}.                        \tag{2.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 U^*(B^{-1}\Delta)U
   =J_S^{1/2}H_S^{-1}J_S^{-1/2}.}                      \tag{2.3}
\]

For the forcing vector,

\[
 \boxed{U^*\Delta^{-1}c=J_S^{1/2}g},                  \tag{2.4}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 B^{-1}c&=AH_S^{-1}g,\\
 A^\dagger B^{-1}c&=H_S^{-1}g,\\
 A^*B^{-1}c&=J_SH_S^{-1}g.
 \end{aligned}}                                       \tag{2.5}
\]

Finally, for every real `mu`,

\[
 \boxed{
 B\succeq\mu\Delta
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H\succeq\mu P_0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H_S\succeq\mu I_S.}                                 \tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof

Since `A|_S` is a bijection onto `K`,

\[
 U^*U=J_S^{-1/2}A^*AJ_S^{-1/2}=I_S
\]

and `UU^*=I_K`.  Thus

\[
 A|_S=UJ_S^{1/2},\qquad
 AA^*=UJ_SU^*.
\]

This proves the first identity in (2.2).  The definition of `H_S` gives

\[
 H_S=J_S^{1/2}U^*BUJ_S^{1/2},
\]

which proves the second identity.  Inverting both positive operators and
multiplying in the displayed order gives (2.3); the product is generally
not self-adjoint.

Since `Delta^(-1)=AA^*` on `K`,

\[
 U^*\Delta^{-1}c
 =J_S^{-1/2}A^*AA^*c
 =J_S^{1/2}g,
\]

which is (2.4).  Moreover

\[
 A^*B(AH_S^{-1}g)=H_SH_S^{-1}g=A^*c.
\]

The map `A^*:K->S` is injective, so the first line of (2.5) follows.
Applying `A^dagger A=P_0` gives the second line, whereas applying `A^*A=J`
gives the third.  The latter two operations must not be confused.

Finally, congruence by the bijection `A|_S`, together with

\[
 \langle Ax,\Delta Ax\rangle=\|x\|^2\qquad(x\in S),
\]

proves (2.6).  On the full source space both `H` and `P_0` vanish on
`ker A`.  This completes the proof.

## 3. Exact one-vector quotient

Define the positive quadratic form

\[
 q_J(x)=\langle x,J_Sx\rangle\qquad(x\in S).           \tag{3.1}
\]

Equations (2.4)--(2.5) give

\[
 \|\Delta^{-1}c\|^2=q_J(g),\qquad
 \|B^{-1}c\|^2=q_J(H_S^{-1}g).                        \tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 K_c^2
 ={\|B^{-1}c\|^2\over\|\Delta^{-1}c\|^2}
 ={q_J(H_S^{-1}g)\over q_J(g)}.}                      \tag{3.3}
\]

For a parameter-dependent family, boundedness of the last quotient is
necessary and sufficient for the one-vector transfer

\[
 \|B^{-1}c\|=O(\|\Delta^{-1}c\|).                     \tag{3.4}
\]

This statement is exact but does not prove (3.4) for the actual forcing
family.  Establishing a uniform bound in (3.3) is new signed orientation
information.

## 4. Basis-safe right-singular spectral measure

Put

\[
 u=\Delta^{-1}c,\qquad T=B^{-1}\Delta.                 \tag{4.1}
\]

Then `Tu=B^(-1)c`, so

\[
 K_c^2={\|Tu\|^2\over\|u\|^2}
       ={\langle u,T^*Tu\rangle\over\langle u,u\rangle}.
                                                               \tag{4.2}
\]

Let `E` be the spectral resolution of the positive operator `T^*T` and
define the probability measure

\[
 \nu_u(\Omega)={\|E(\Omega)u\|^2\over\|u\|^2}.        \tag{4.3}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (exact first-moment criterion)

One has

\[
 \boxed{K_c^2=\int_{[0,\infty)}s\,d\nu_u(s).}         \tag{4.4}
\]

Thus a family has uniformly bounded one-vector transfer if and only if the
first moments in (4.4) are uniformly bounded.

If `sigma` ranges over the distinct singular values of `T`, let

\[
 P_\sigma=1_{\{\sigma^2\}}(T^*T),\qquad
 \omega_\sigma={\|P_\sigma u\|^2\over\|u\|^2}.        \tag{4.5}
\]

Then the basis-safe finite-dimensional formula is

\[
 \boxed{K_c^2=\sum_\sigma\sigma^2\omega_\sigma.}      \tag{4.6}
\]

When a singular value is repeated, the individual right singular vectors
inside its eigenspace are noncanonical.  The projection `P_sigma` and the
combined weight in (4.5) are canonical.  Formula (4.6) must not be replaced
by basis-dependent claims about selected vectors in a repeated singular
subspace.  It also cannot be replaced by an eigenvalue measure of the
generally nonnormal operator `T`.

### Corollary 4.2 (exceptional levels plus a tail cap)

For any set `I` of distinct singular-value levels, suppose

\[
 \sum_{\sigma\in I}\sigma^2\omega_\sigma\leq C_1,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\sigma\notin I}\sigma^2\leq C_2.              \tag{4.7}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{K_c^2\leq C_1+C_2.}                           \tag{4.8}
\]

Indeed, the weights outside `I` sum to at most one.  More generally, the
second condition in (4.7) may be replaced by the direct weighted-tail
bound

\[
 \sum_{\sigma\notin I}\sigma^2\omega_\sigma\leq C_2. \tag{4.9}
\]

Thus finitely many large singular levels can be handled by their actual
weighted overlaps.  A global operator-norm reserve is not logically
necessary for one forcing vector.

## 5. First moment versus cyclic-support cap

Let

\[
 {\cal C}_u
 =\operatorname {span}\{(T^*T)^ku:k\geq0\}.          \tag{5.1}
\]

This is the right-singular cyclic subspace generated by `u`.  It reduces
`T^*T`, and

\[
 \sup\operatorname {supp}\nu_u
 =\|T|_{{\cal C}_u}\|^2.                               \tag{5.2}
\]

A uniform cyclic-support cap implies (4.4), but it is strictly stronger.
For each integer `n>=2`, take

\[
 B_n=I_2,\qquad
 \Delta_n=\begin{pmatrix}n&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad c_n=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}.         \tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
 T_n=\Delta_n,\qquad
 u_n=\Delta_n^{-1}c_n=\begin{pmatrix}1/n\\1\end{pmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{5.4}
\]

The two spectral weights of `T_n^*T_n` are

\[
 \omega_{n}={1\over n^2+1},\qquad
 \omega_1={n^2\over n^2+1}.                           \tag{5.5}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{K_{c_n}^2={2n^2\over n^2+1}<2,}               \tag{5.6}
\]

whereas both components of `u_n` are nonzero and the two eigenvalues are
distinct.  Thus `C_(u_n)=C^2` and

\[
 \sup\operatorname {supp}\nu_{u_n}=n^2\longrightarrow\infty.
                                                               \tag{5.7}
\]

The exact first moment is therefore the correct one-vector condition; a
complete cyclic-support cap discards the small high-singular overlap.

## 6. What a one-sided floor gives

By (2.6), a floor `B>=mu Delta` with `mu>0` is exactly
`H_S>=mu I_S`.  It yields

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_J(H_S^{-1}g)
 &\leq\lambda_{\max}(J_S)\|H_S^{-1}g\|^2\\
 &\leq {\lambda_{\max}(J_S)\over\mu^2}\|g\|^2,
 \end{aligned}
\]

while

\[
 q_J(g)\geq\lambda_{\min}(J_S)\|g\|^2.
\]

Consequently the general floor-only conclusion is

\[
 \boxed{K_c^2\leq{\kappa(J_S)\over\mu^2}.}             \tag{6.1}
\]

The source conditioning cannot in general be removed.

### Countermodel 6.1 (fixed two-sided floor, divergent action)

For `0<epsilon<=1`, set

\[
 J_\varepsilon=
 \begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 A_\varepsilon=J_\varepsilon^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \Delta_\varepsilon=J_\varepsilon^{-1},               \tag{6.2}
\]

and fix

\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}3/2&-1/2\\-1/2&3/2\end{pmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{6.3}
\]

Define

\[
 B_\varepsilon
 =J_\varepsilon^{-1/2}HJ_\varepsilon^{-1/2}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 3/(2\varepsilon^2)&-1/(2\varepsilon)\\
 -1/(2\varepsilon)&3/2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                        \tag{6.4}
\]

Since the eigenvalues of `H` are `1` and `2`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_\varepsilon\preceq B_\varepsilon
 \preceq2\Delta_\varepsilon.}                         \tag{6.5}
\]

Both bounds are exact.  In particular, the optimal lower relative floor is
one.

Take `g=e_1`, so `c=A_\varepsilon^{-*}g=(1/epsilon,0)^T`.
Because

\[
 H^{-1}g=\begin{pmatrix}3/4\\1/4\end{pmatrix},
\]

formula (3.3) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 K_c^2={9\over16}+{1\over16\varepsilon^2}
       ={9\over16}+{\kappa(J_\varepsilon)\over16}.}    \tag{6.6}
\]

Rescaling `c` to the fixed physical vector `e_1` leaves this ratio
unchanged.  Thus its divergence is not caused by the normalization of the
forcing vector.

The transfer operator itself is

\[
 T_\varepsilon=B_\varepsilon^{-1}\Delta_\varepsilon
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 3/4&\varepsilon/4\\
 1/(4\varepsilon)&3/4
 \end{pmatrix}.                                        \tag{6.7}
\]

Its eigenvalues remain `1` and `1/2`, but its largest singular value grows
like `1/(4 epsilon)`.  This simultaneously proves that (6.1) is sharp in
conditioning order and that the ordinary eigenvalues of `T` do not encode
the needed action.

The family is injective and therefore remains a countermodel when
noninjective synthesis is allowed.  It is not asserted to arise from the
actual arithmetic source.

## 7. Final scope

The exact route for one forcing vector is now isolated in three equivalent
forms:

1. the physical quotient `||B^(-1)c||/||Delta^(-1)c||`;
2. the source quotient `q_J(H_S^(-1)g)/q_J(g)`;
3. the first moment of the `T^*T` spectral measure generated by
   `u=Delta^(-1)c`.

The exceptional-level decomposition (4.7) shows precisely how a few large
singular modes may be neutralized by small forcing overlaps.  The cyclic
example proves that controlling their mere presence is stronger than
needed.  Countermodel 6.1 proves that a signed Loewner floor alone supplies
no such overlap theorem.

No such estimate, no physical forcing rate, no high-space or reducing
