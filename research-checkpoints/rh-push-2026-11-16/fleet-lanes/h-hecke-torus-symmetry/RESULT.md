## 0. Verdict

**Precise obstruction, with seven proved structural statements.**

1. The arithmetic lattice of the bridge field is the rank-one lattice `Z` of the theta kernel, with
   parameter `x = e^u` (`u` = the Mellin coordinate of PR 43/56). The elliptic curve `E_r` of the
   torus/Klein charts is not that lattice; `r` is a chart parameter and isogenies of `E_r` act
   trivially on every spectral form (section 1).
2. Hecke correspondences act on the loaded field as elements of the translation group of the bridge
   coordinate: `V_p = p^(-1/2) T_(-2 log p)` in rank one, and `T_p = 1 + p * (heat-time dilation by
   p^2)` on heat traces of rank-two tori (Theorems 1, 2). The Fricke involution is `J` and
   `J V_p J = V_p*`.
3. The theta kernel is a simultaneous eigen-object: `(1 - V_p) kappa` is `p`-primitive for every
   `p`. Davenport-Heilbronn is an eigen-object exactly for the index-two subalgebra generated by
   `V_n`, `n = +-1 mod 5`; for `p = +-2 mod 5` the Hecke operator maps it to a twin function with
   functional-equation sign `-1` (Theorem 3). This is a step DH fails for a structural reason.
4. The bridge form `H_c` is not Hecke-invariant: `V_p* H_c V_p = p^-1 H_(c + 2 log p)`. The defect
   is the window form on an interval of length `2 log p` (Theorem 4).
5. The enlarged symmetry algebra (Hecke plus Fricke) is multiplicity-free on the test space
   (abelian commutant), so an invariant form is one real scalar per joint eigencharacter
   (Theorem 5). The Hecke-invariant part of `H_c` is the Laguerre multiplier
   `(alpha/pi)(f'^2 - f f'')(t)`. Its pointwise sign is necessary for RH and not sufficient.
6. On the zero module (the Krein space of `g-spinor-square-root`) the Hecke operator `V_p` acts
   as `diag(p^-rho)` up to a window term, `sqrt(p) V_p` is `J`-unitary, and
   RH is equivalent to: this Hecke action is tempered (spectrum on the unit circle), equivalently
   the Hecke-invariant bridge form is positive there (Theorem 6). This is the Petersson template,
   and it is circular: the invariant form is the bridge form, whose positivity is the unknown.
7. None of statements 4-6 uses the eigen-property of statement 3. They hold verbatim for DH. The
   eigen-property enters proved zero information only through norm convergence of the product
   `prod (1 - V_p)^-1`, which gives `Re s > 1` and nothing in the strip (Theorem 7). A control with
   the eigen-property at every prime except one, the same functional equation, and off-line zeros
   exists (section 7, NR).

Exact place the sign question lands: the existence of non-real joint Hecke characters
`p -> p^(-1/2)(p^(i gamma) + p^(-i gamma))` in the zero module. Schur's lemma gives a definite sign
(positive) on every joint eigenspace with real character and forces a hyperbolic pair on every
non-real conjugate pair. It cannot decide whether non-real characters occur.

## 2. Where the lattice is (answer to "identify the lattice and the parameter")

User sources: zeta-view `docs/KLEIN-ANNULUS.md` section 1 and 3; PR 56
`annulus_theta_coupling.md` section 3; PR 49 decision note; PR 43 `geometry_and_twistors.md`.

- **Chart torus.** `E_r: eta^2 = (w^2+r^2)(w^2+r^-2)`, `w = (i/r) sn(z|m)`, `m = r^-4`. The loaded
  field is the pull-back `f = xi o s` through the degree-two map `s: E_r -> P^1`. The period
  lattice of `E_r` is `4K Z + 2iK' Z`. It does not occur in `xi`. PROVED (`g-symmetry-schur`
  Theorem 1, `g-torus-modular` P1): every spectral form is independent of `r`. Hence the Hecke
  correspondence of `E_r` itself (sum over the `p+1` tori `p`-isogenous to `E_r`) changes the chart
  and nothing else. The half-period translation `tau` of `g-symmetry-schur` is the one isogeny
  (degree 2) that is a symmetry.
- **Arithmetic lattice, rank one.** `F(z) = xi(1/2+z) = int kappa(u) e^(zu/2) du`,
  `kappa(u) = Phi(u/4)`, and with `x = e^u`

      kappa(u) = sum_{n>=1} (2 pi^2 n^4 x^(9/4) - 3 pi n^2 x^(5/4)) exp(-pi n^2 x).

  This is a derivative of the theta series of the lattice `Z subset R` at modular parameter
  `tau = i x`. The parameter is `x = e^u`; the bridge coordinate `u` is the logarithm of the
  position on the geodesic `i R_+` of the theta group.
- **Arithmetic lattice, rank two (twin ladder).** `g-torus-modular` P2: the rectangular torus
  `Z + i y Z` has heat trace `theta(t/y) theta(t y)`; the double Mellin transform in `(t, y)` is
  `2 xi*(s+w) xi*(s-w)`. The bridge shear `w` is the Mellin dual of the modulus `y`; the heat time
  `t` is dual to `s`.

## 3. Hecke action and the eigen-statement

Notation: `(T_h a)(u) = a(u - h)` on `L^2(R, du)`; `J a(u) = a(-u)`;
`V_n = n^(-1/2) T_(-2 log n)`, so `V_m V_n = V_mn`, `V_n* = n^(-1/2) T_(2 log n)`, `||V_n|| = n^(-1/2)`,
and the Fourier multiplier of `V_n` (variable `z = it`) is `n^(-1/2 - z)`.

**Theorem 1 (rank one). PROVED.** Let `g0(u) = (2 pi^2 e^(9u/4) - 3 pi e^(5u/4)) exp(-pi e^u)`.

(a) `kappa = sum_{n>=1} V_n g0`, absolutely convergent pointwise, and
`F(z) = zeta(1/2+z) ghat0(z)` for `Re z > 1/2`.
(b) For each prime `p`: `(1 - V_p) kappa = kappa^(p) := sum_{p not| n} V_n g0`.
(c) `J V_n J = V_n*`, and `J kappa = kappa` (functional equation). Hence also
`(1 - V_p*) kappa = J kappa^(p)`.
(d) `V_n` corresponds to the unique sublattice `nZ` of index `n` in `Z`. In rank one each index
has one sublattice, so the Hecke correspondence is a single dilation `x -> n^2 x`.

Proof. (a) `n^4 x^(9/4) = n^(-1/2) (n^2 x)^(9/4)` and `n^2 x^(5/4) = n^(-1/2)(n^2 x)^(5/4)`, so the
`n`-th term is `n^(-1/2) g0(u + 2 log n)`. Termwise Mellin transform gives the factor
`n^(-1/2-z)`. (b) Remove the terms with `p | n`. (c) Conjugating a translation by reflection
inverts it. QED. MEASURED: seed-sum error `0`, evenness `4e-21`, `F = zeta * ghat0` relative error
`3e-22` at `z = 1.3 + 2.1i`.

Reading. `kappa` is the orbit sum of one seed over all sublattices, and "simultaneous eigen-object"
means exactly (b): the `p`-part of the orbit sum is the geometric series `(1 - V_p)^-1`. Equivalent
classical form (Shimura 1973, CLASSICAL): `theta` is an eigenform of every `T(p^2)` with eigenvalue
`1 + 1/p`.

**Theorem 2 (rank two). PROVED.** For a lattice `L subset C` put
`Theta_L(t) = sum_{v in L} exp(-pi t |v|^2)`. Then for every prime `p`

    sum over the p+1 sublattices L' of index p of  Theta_L'(t)  =  Theta_L(t) + p Theta_L(p^2 t).

So on heat traces the Hecke correspondence `T_p` equals `1 + p D_(p^2)`, with `D_c` the dilation of
heat time, for every lattice. Consequently the Epstein zeta `Z_L(s) = sum' |v|^(-2s)` satisfies
`T_p Z_L(s) = (1 + p^(1-2s)) Z_L(s)` as a function on lattices, for every `s` and every `L`.

Proof. A vector `v in L` lies in all `p+1` sublattices if `v in pL` and in exactly one otherwise
(the sublattices correspond to the lines of `L/pL`). So the sum is
`Theta_L + p Theta_(pL)`, and `Theta_(pL)(t) = Theta_L(p^2 t)`. Mellin transform in `t`. QED.
MEASURED: identity exact to working precision for `tau = 0.31 + 1.17i`, `p = 2, 3, 5`.

**Corollary 2a (what the rank-two eigen-property does and does not say). PROVED + CLASSICAL.**
The eigen-property of Theorem 2 holds in the lattice variable for every torus. It does not give an
Euler product in `s` at a fixed torus. The Dirichlet series of one torus has an Euler product only
when the torus is itself an eigenvector of the Hecke correspondences restricted to its isogeny
class. For CM tori of discriminant `-20` (class number 2, forms `x^2+5y^2` and `2x^2+2xy+3y^2`):
the Hecke operator at a prime `p` that splits into non-principal ideals maps torus 1 to torus 2;
the sums `Z_1 +- Z_2` over class-group characters are eigen-objects; `Z_1` alone is not.
MEASURED (`n <= 3000`): `p = 3, 7`: `T_p r_1 = 2 r_2` exactly, single torus not eigen;
`p = 29, 41` (principal): single torus eigen; class-character sums eigen at all five primes tested.
CLASSICAL (Davenport-Heilbronn 1936): Epstein zeta functions of class number > 1 have zeros in
`Re s > 1`. So the rank-two analogue of the DH control is a single torus in a class group of order
> 1, and the step it fails is "the torus is a Hecke eigenvector on its isogeny class".

Remark (twin ladder). Of the `p+1` neighbours of the rectangular torus `Z + iyZ`, two are
rectangular (`y -> p y`, `y -> y/p`); they are `V_p` and `V_p*` in the shear variable. The other
`p - 1` leave the `r`-geodesic. An average over `r` only (PR 56, `g-torus-modular` section 2) is
therefore not Hecke-stable.

## 4. Davenport-Heilbronn: the defect

`a(n) = 1, k, -k, -1, 0` for `n = 1..5 mod 5`, `k = 0.2840790438...`;
`a = c chi + conj(c) conj(chi)`, `chi(2) = i`, `c = (1 - ik)/2`. Twin sequence
`b = i(c chi - conj(c) conj(chi))`, values `k, -1, 1, -k, 0`.
`kappa_DH = sum a(n) V_n g5`, `kappa_tw = sum b(n) V_n g5` with the weight-3/2 seed `g5`.

**Theorem 3 (DH defect). PROVED.**
(a) For every `m = +-1 mod 5`: `a(mn) = chi(m) a(n)` with `chi(m) = +-1`. So
`(1 - chi(p) V_p) kappa_DH` is `p`-primitive for every prime `p = +-1 mod 5`: DH has the standard
Euler factor at those primes.
(b) For `p = 2 mod 5`: `a(pn) = b(n)`, `b(pn) = -a(n)`. For `p = 3 mod 5`: `a(pn) = -b(n)`,
`b(pn) = a(n)`. So the coefficient Hecke operator `U_p` acts on the real plane
`span{kappa_DH, kappa_tw}` as a rotation by `+-90` degrees: `U_p^2 = -1`. No real or complex
multiple of `kappa_DH` is an eigenvector; the eigenvectors are `theta_chi`, `theta_conj(chi)`
with eigenvalues `+-i`.
(c) `kappa_DH` is Fricke-even and `kappa_tw` is Fricke-odd (functional-equation sign `-1`).
Hence on this plane `U_p` commutes with the Fricke involution for `p = +-1 mod 5` and
anticommutes with it for `p = +-2 mod 5`.
(d) DH is an eigen-object of the subalgebra generated by `{V_n : n = +-1 mod 5}`, of index two in
the Hecke semigroup algebra, and of no larger subalgebra containing a `V_p`, `p = +-2 mod 5`.

Proof. (a), (b): `a(pn) = c chi(p) chi(n) + conj(c) conj(chi(p)) conj(chi(n))`; insert
`chi(p) = +-1` or `+-i`. (c): `Lambda(s, chi) = eps Lambda(1-s, conj chi)` and DH's `c` is chosen
with `c eps = conj(c)`, which gives `Lambda_a(1-s) = Lambda_a(s)`; the twin has coefficient
`ic` in place of `c`, and `(ic) eps = i conj(c) = -conj(ic)`, which gives
`Lambda_b(1-s) = -Lambda_b(s)`. (d) follows. QED.
MEASURED: (a), (b) exact on `n < 60`; `Lambda_a(1-s)/Lambda_a(s) = 1 + 2e-16 i`,
`Lambda_b(1-s)/Lambda_b(s) = -1 - 6e-17 i` at `s = 0.3 + 4.2i`.

Reading. For zeta the Hecke module of the kernel is one-dimensional, and Hecke and Fricke commute
on it (`J V J = V*`, both fix the line). For DH the module is two-dimensional; the Fricke-fixed
vector and the Hecke eigenvectors are different vectors. The defect is the anticommutation in (c).

## 5. The bridge form under the Hecke algebra

`A = h_+ *`, `A* = h_- *`, `h_+- = (1 +- alpha u/2) kappa`, `Q_c = 1_(c, inf)`,
`H_c = A* Q_c A - A Q_c A*` (PR 43 (2.1)). For an interval `I` put `W_I = A* 1_I A - A 1_I A*`.

**Theorem 4 (covariance and defect). PROVED.**
(a) `T_h* H_c T_h = H_(c-h)`. Hence `V_p* H_c V_p = p^-1 H_(c + 2 log p)`.
(b) `[H_c, T_h] = T_h (H_(c-h) - H_c) = T_h W_(c-h, c]`, and
`H_c - p V_p* H_c V_p = W_(c, c + 2 log p]`.
(c) With `T_p := V_p + V_p*` (self-adjoint, multiplier `2 p^(-1/2) cos(t log p)`):
`T_p H_c T_p = p^-1 [ H_(c+l) + H_(c-l) + T_(2l) H_(c+l) + H_(c+l) T_(-2l) ]`, `l = 2 log p`.
(d) `H_c` commutes with no `V_p`. The only operators of the algebra generated by
`{V_n, V_n*}` that commute with `H_c` are scalars.

Proof. (a) `A`, `A*` are convolutions and commute with `T_h`; `T_h* Q_c T_h = Q_(c-h)`.
(b) Algebra from (a), and `H_(c') - H_c = W_(c', c]` for `c' < c`. (c) Expand, use (a).
(d) If `T_h` (`h != 0`) commuted with `H_c`, then `H_c = H_(c - nh)` for all `n`, and
`H_c -> 0` weakly as `c -> +inf`, so `H_c = 0`; but `tr H_c > 0` (`g-symmetry-schur` referee).
A finite combination `sum c_j T_(h_j)` commuting with `H_c` has Fourier kernel
`C(t,s)(m(t) - m(s)) = 0`; `C` is real-analytic and not identically zero off the diagonal, so the
trigonometric polynomial `m` is constant. QED.

All four statements use only that `A` is a convolution. They hold for every kernel, including DH.

**Theorem 5 (Schur analysis for the enlarged algebra). PROVED.**
Let `G = {2 log q : q in Q_+^x}`, a dense subgroup of `R`, and let `M` be the von Neumann algebra
on `L^2(R, du)` generated by `{T_h : h in G}` (equivalently by all `V_p`, `V_p*`) and `J`.
(a) The commutant of `{T_h : h in G}` is the algebra of Fourier multipliers `L^inf(R_t)`, which is
maximal abelian. The commutant of `M` is the even multipliers `{m(t) = m(-t)}`. It is abelian, so
`M` is multiplicity-free.
(b) The decomposition is the direct integral over `t > 0` of the two-dimensional irreducible
representations `span{e^(itu), e^(-itu)}` of `R x| Z/2`, each occurring once. Under the Hecke
algebra alone the joint eigencharacters are `p -> p^(-1/2 - it)`; `log 2 / log 3` irrational
makes `t` determined by the values at `p = 2, 3`.
(c) A bounded Hermitian form invariant under `M` is a real even multiplier `m(t)`. It is
nonnegative iff `m(t) >= 0` for almost every `t`: one scalar per joint eigencharacter.
(d) Invariant forms present in the program, with their scalars:
   - convolution by `kappa`: `m(t) = f(t) = xi(1/2+it)`. Indefinite; its sign changes are the
     on-line zeros of odd order (Hardy).
   - the invariant mean of the bridge form: `int_R <a, H_c a> dc = 2 pi int |ahat(t)|^2 C(t,t) dt`
     with `C(t,t) = (alpha/pi)(f'(t)^2 - f(t) f''(t))`, the Laguerre expression (the phase
     derivative of `S = N/D` times `|D|^2`).
(e) `H_c` itself has Fourier kernel `C(t,s) e^(-i(t-s)c/2)` up to normalization, with
`C(t,s) != 0` for `t != s`: it couples distinct Hecke eigencharacters. Hecke invariance
constrains only its diagonal.

Proof. (a) `h -> T_h` is strongly continuous, so the commutant of a dense subgroup equals the
commutant of the whole group, which is `L^inf` of the dual (CLASSICAL). `J` acts on multipliers by
`t -> -t`. (b), (c) follow from (a). (d) `H_c = T_c H_0 T_c*`, so the kernel is
`C(t,s) e^(-i(t-s)c')`; integrating over `c` gives `2 pi delta(t-s) C(t,t)`. Direct check:
`int <a,H_c a> dc = int r (|f|^2 - |g|^2) dr` with `fhat = D ahat`, `ghat = conj(D) ahat` on the
axis, and `Im(conj(D) D') = alpha (f'^2 - f f'')` for `D = f - i alpha f'`. QED.

Comparison with `g-symmetry-schur` Theorem 2: there, two isotypic components of infinite
multiplicity and no sign from Schur. Here, a continuum of irreducibles each of multiplicity one,
and a pointwise sign test for every invariant form. The gain is real but applies only to
invariant forms, and the bridge form is not invariant.

Status of the invariant sign `f'^2 - f f'' >= 0` on the critical line: implied by RH (CLASSICAL);
not sufficient (`g-renderer-learnings` Theorem B: an even entire control with an off-line quartet
and positive Laguerre expression on the whole line); false for DH near `t = 85.7` and `114.2`
(`g-symmetry-schur` and its referee: `C_+(t,t)` and `C_-(t,t)` both negative there, and
`C_+ + C_- = 2 C(t,t)`).

## 6. Self-adjointness and the square-root operator

`g-spinor-square-root` (3.1): `<a, H_c a> = alpha sum_gamma y_gamma conj(y_(conj gamma))`,
`y_gamma(a;c) = int_c^inf (kappa*a)(r) e^(i gamma (r-c)/2) dr`, `gamma` over zeros of
`f(t) = xi(1/2+it)`, `rho = 1/2 + i gamma`, `(Jy)_gamma = y_(conj gamma)`, `D_c a = sqrt(alpha) y`.

**Theorem 6 (Hecke action on the zero module). PROVED given (3.1).**
(a) `D_c V_p = p^(-rho) (D_c - R_(c,p))`, where `p^(-rho) = diag(p^(-1/2 - i gamma))` and
`(R_(c,p) a)_gamma = sqrt(alpha) int_c^(c + 2 log p) (kappa*a)(r) e^(i gamma (r-c)/2) dr`.
On tests with `kappa * a = 0` on `(c, c + 2 log p)` the intertwining `D_c V_p = p^(-rho) D_c` is
exact.
(b) `sqrt(p) V_p` acts on the zero module by `U_p = diag(p^(-i gamma))`, which is `J`-unitary:
`U_p^dagger J U_p = J`. `T_p = V_p + V_p*` acts by
`diag(p^(-1/2)(p^(i gamma) + p^(-i gamma)))`, which is `J`-self-adjoint.
(c) Joint eigenspaces of `{T_p}` on the zero module are finite-dimensional: the character
determines `{gamma, -gamma}`, and the dimension is twice the multiplicity of the zero (the
multiplicity itself after the parity split).
(d) The bridge form is the Hecke-invariant Hermitian form of this module. Eigenspaces `E_lambda`,
`E_mu` are orthogonal for it unless `mu = conj(lambda)`. On a real character (on-line zero) the
form is positive definite on the eigenspace. On a non-real pair `{lambda, conj lambda}` (off-line
pair) each eigenspace is isotropic and the pair is hyperbolic.
(e) The following are equivalent: RH; `U_2` and `U_3` have spectrum on the unit circle; `T_2` and
`T_3` have real spectrum on the zero module; the invariant form is positive definite.

Proof. (a) `(kappa * T_h a)(r) = (kappa*a)(r - h)`; substitute in `y_gamma` with `h = -2 log p`.
(b) `conj(p^(-i conj gamma)) p^(-i gamma) = p^(i gamma) p^(-i gamma) = 1`. (c) Theorem 5(b) and
`N(T) < inf`. (d) Standard for `J`-self-adjoint operators, plus the rank-one form of (3.1).
(e) `p^(-1/2)(p^(i gamma) + p^(-i gamma))` real with `gamma = a + ib`, `b != 0`, requires
`sin(a log p) = 0`; for `p = 2` and `3` this forces `a = 0`, and `xi > 0` on the real axis. QED.

Answers to question (4).
- Petersson template: `T_p` is self-adjoint because a positive definite Hecke-invariant form exists
  a priori. Then eigenvalues are real.
- Here the Hecke-invariant form on the zero module exists and is unique up to the data of (3.1):
  it is the bridge form, i.e. the Krein form `J`. The Hecke operators are self-adjoint for it
  unconditionally. Its positivity is not known a priori; it is the statement to be proved. With an
  indefinite invariant form, self-adjointness allows non-real eigenvalues in conjugate pairs, one
  pair per negative square (CLASSICAL, Pontryagin).
- So the square-root operator `D_c` is exactly the map to the module on which Hecke is
  `J`-self-adjoint, and RH is the statement that `J`-self-adjoint can be replaced by self-adjoint.
  In Hecke language RH is temperedness of the action `U_p = p^(-i gamma)` on the zero module.
- Real Hecke eigenvalues in the classical sense (theta: `1 + 1/p`; Eisenstein:
  `p^(it) + p^(-it)`) constrain the spectral parameter of the automorphic object, not the zeros.
  Zeros are poles of the scattering quotient, i.e. resonances, and the cutoff `Q_c` that defines
  them does not commute with Hecke (Theorem 4(d)).

## 7. Where the eigen-property produces zero information, and controls

**Theorem 7. PROVED (classical content, stated in Hecke form).** On
`L^2(R, e^(-2 delta u) du)` one has `||V_p|| = p^(-1/2 - delta)`. For `delta > 1/2` the product
`prod_p (1 - V_p)^-1` converges in operator norm to an invertible operator `Z = sum V_n`, so no
character `n -> n^(-s)` with `Re s > 1` annihilates `Z`: `zeta(s) != 0` for `Re s > 1`. For
`delta <= 1/2`, `sum_p ||V_p|| = inf` and the argument stops. For DH,
`Z_DH = c Z_chi + conj(c) Z_conj(chi)` is a sum of two invertible operators and is not invertible
for some `delta > 1/2` (DH has zeros with `Re s > 1`).

So the simultaneous eigen-property, used through norm convergence, gives the half-plane `Re s > 1`
and the same edge as `g-torus-modular` (shift `1/2`) and `g-passive-cascade` (Theorem 1). Inside
the strip the eigen-property is used by no proved inequality of this lane.
