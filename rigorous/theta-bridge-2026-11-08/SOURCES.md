# Primary sources and attribution

The derivations in this checkpoint are written out and are not presented as
newly published theorems. These sources supply the established background.
The preservation of a research route does not imply independent verification
of every claim in a referenced preprint or another repository checkpoint.

1. **NIST DLMF, §25.4, Reflection Formulas.**
   <https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4>
   Completed xi normalization and functional equation.
2. **NIST DLMF, §25.10, Zeros.**
   <https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10>
   Location of nontrivial zeros in the critical strip.
3. **NIST DLMF, §§19.8, 22.2, 22.4.**
   <https://dlmf.nist.gov/19.8>, <https://dlmf.nist.gov/22.2>,
   <https://dlmf.nist.gov/22.4>
   Landen transformations, theta/elliptic period identities, and Jacobi periods.
4. **George Csordas, Fourier transforms of positive definite kernels and the
   Riemann xi-function, arXiv:1309.0055v2 (2014).**
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.0055>
   In particular Theorem 4.2(b), the strict concavity of
   \(\log\Phi(\sqrt t)\); our normalization is \(\kappa(u)=\Phi(u/4)\).
   Theorems 3.5–3.7 and 4.5–4.6 provide associated-kernel and Laguerre context.
5. **Jonathan Sondow and Cristian Dumitrescu, A monotonicity property of
   Riemann's xi function and a reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis (2010).**
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1104>
   Horizontal-modulus monotonicity and its RH equivalence.
6. **Masatoshi Suzuki, Chains of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces generated
   by unimodular functions, arXiv:2012.11121.**
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11121>
   Hermite–Biehler criterion for \(\xi+\xi'\); unimodular boundary data versus
   the additional conditions needed for a positive canonical system.
7. **Chris Guiver, Hartmut Logemann and Mark R. Opmeer, Operator-valued
   multiplier theorems for causal translation-invariant operators with
   applications to control theoretic input-output stability (2024).**
   <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00498-024-00387-4>
   The standard bounded-analytic multiplier/causal \(L^2\) operator theorem.
   Their usual time/Laplace convention is reversed in our plus-Fourier sign.
8. **Braden, charge-two monopole spectral-curve analysis (2021).**
   <https://doi.org/10.1007/s11005-020-01349-y>
   The normalized monopole curve, its real structure, and line-bundle conditions.
9. **Braden and Enolski, arXiv:1708.09660.**
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.09660>
   Charge-two Nahm data and metric formulas, especially equations (6.3), (6.7).
10. **Gerald Teschl, Mathematical Methods in Quantum Mechanics, 2nd ed.**
    <https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~gerald/ftp/book-schroe/schroe2.pdf>
    Green/resolvent, quadratic-form, and short-range scattering background.
11. **Karlheinz Gröchenig, Schoenberg's Theory of Totally Positive Functions
    and the Riemann Zeta Function, arXiv:2007.12889.**
    <https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12889>
    The classification used in the explicitly limited PF-infinity exclusion.
12. **Suvrit Sra, On some positivity properties of the Riemann Xi function,
    arXiv:2611.02944v1, 2 November 2026.**
    <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02944>,
    <https://suvrit.github.io/papers/riem-xi/>
    Preprint context: positive reciprocal-transform laws and finite positivity
    claims. Its full large-degree certificate was not audited here. The notes
    distinguish the Bernstein property from the stronger Stieltjes property.
13. **LMFDB, zeros of the Riemann zeta function.**
    <https://www.lmfdb.org/zeros/zeta/>
    Reference for the first positive zero ordinate exceeding 14. The Rayleigh
    certificate itself establishes the candidate-operator upper bound below 14.

Repository context: [Math Explorer PR #40](https://github.com/judegomila/mathexplorer/pull/40)
preserves a different theta-Fisher/zeta-sphere campaign. Its results are not
