THETA BRIDGE: A SINGLE-PULSE CRITERION, WEIGHTED DOMAINS, AND OPERATOR AUDITS
Research session: November 7, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles)

STATUS
======
This note records deductions and checks from the current research session.
It does not prove RH. The new sufficient/equivalent pulse estimate below is
unproved. The weighted-resolvent theorem and the exclusions are proved for
the explicitly stated operators. No novelty claim relative to the full
literature is made.

1. DEFINITIONS
==============
F(z)=xi(1/2+z), with xi(s)=s(s-1) pi^(-s/2) Gamma(s/2) zeta(s)/2.
theta(t)=sum_(n in Z) exp(-pi n^2 t).
H(u)=exp(u/4) theta(exp(u)); kappa(u)=H''(u)-H(u)/16.
F(z)=integral_R kappa(u) exp(zu/2) du.

Fix alpha>0. Set D=F+alpha F', N=F-alpha F', S=N/D.
F is even and real on the real axis, so N(z)=D(-z).
Our Fourier convention is f_hat(t)=integral f(u) exp(itu/2) du.
The inverse convention has factor 1/(4*pi).
Put h_+(u)=(1+alpha*u/2)kappa(u), h_-(u)=(1-alpha*u/2)kappa(u).
Their bilateral Laplace transforms are D and N.

On the imaginary axis |S(it)|=1 almost everywhere. Common boundary zeros
are removable and have no effect on the L2 multiplier. Thus C0, defined by
(C0 f)_hat(t)=S(it) f_hat(t), is an unconditional unitary on L2(R).

The desired pointwise inequality is
1-|S(x+it)|^2 = 4 alpha Re(F'(x+it) conjugate(F(x+it)))/|D(x+it)|^2 >=0.
For this F, global contractivity in Re z>0 is equivalent to RH.
It is unconditionally known on Re z>=1/2, since Re(xi'/xi)(s)>0 for Re s>=1.

2. ONE FIXED PULSE AND ONE EXPONENTIAL MOMENT SUFFICE
==================================================
Let f0=1_[0,1], and let g0=C0 f0 be the ACTUAL boundary multiplier output.
Then, for every fixed X>=1/2,

  RH  <=>  integral_R exp(Xu)|g0(u)|^2 du < infinity.

Under RH the stronger statements hold:
  support(g0) is contained in (-infinity,1];
  integral exp(Xu)|g0|^2 <= (exp(X)-1)/X.

In particular, the endpoint target is
  integral exp(u/2)|C0 1_[0,1](u)|^2 du <= 2(exp(1/2)-1).
Even FINITENESS, without the numerical bound, suffices for RH.

Proof of necessity of RH from the moment:
The pulse transform V(z)=2(exp(z/2)-1)/z has no zeros in Re z>0.
If g0 is in both L2 and L2(exp(Xu)du), its bilateral Laplace transform Y is
analytic and in the Hardy space of 0<Re z<X. Cauchy-Schwarz gives absolute
convergence at intermediate real parts; interpolation bounds the L2 norms.
D and N are bounded on this strip, using the exponential moments of kappa.
The boundary multiplier identity gives D(it)Y(it)=N(it)V(it). Hardy uniqueness
then yields DY=NV throughout the strip.
Since V never vanishes there, S=N/D has no genuine poles in the strip.
All possible right-half-plane poles are in 0<Re z<1/2, because D is nonzero
on Re z>=1/2. The case X=1/2 therefore covers every possible pole as well.
The canonical-product argument in Section 3 shows that pole-free S is inner
for this particular xi family. An F-zero in Re z>0 would force S=-1 there,
after removable cancellation, contradicting strict interior modulus <1.
Evenness of F then gives RH.

Conversely, under RH, S is an inner right-half-plane function. With our Fourier
sign, its convolution kernel is supported in u<=0. Consequently C0 cannot
move support toward larger u, and support(g0) lies below 1.
For P_a=1_(-infinity,a], Q_a=I-P_a, causality and unitarity give
  ||Q_a C0 f|| <= ||Q_a f||.
Integrate this inequality squared against X exp(Xa) da. Tonelli gives
  ||exp(Xu/2)C0 f||^2 <= ||exp(Xu/2)f||^2.
For f0, the right side is (exp(X)-1)/X.

This reduces the target; it does not establish the moment. An exact,
unconditional physical-space equation for the required response is
  h_+ * g0 = h_- * 1_[0,1],
with g0 in ordinary L2 and ||g0||=1. The required exponential integrability
of this solution has not been proved.

3. WHY POLE-FREE S IS INNER HERE
===============================
This uses xi growth, not a generic maximum-modulus argument.
For sufficiently large Re z, Re(F'/F)(z)>1/alpha uniformly in Im z, by the
gamma asymptotic and the Euler product. Thus D(-z)=F(z)-alpha F'(z) is nonzero
there. The zeros of D lie in a bounded vertical strip. D has order one.

Cancel all common canonical factors of D(z) and N(z)=D(-z). Common factors
can be chosen even. If S has no genuine poles in Re z>0, the remaining
denominator zeros lie in a bounded LEFT vertical strip, with boundary common
zeros also removed. They obey sum |d_n|^(-2)<infinity. Bounded real parts
give the Blaschke sum and absolute convergence of sum |Re(1/d_n)|.

Grouping conjugate factors in the Hadamard product gives
  S(z)=exp(c*z) B(z), c real,
where B is a right-half-plane Blaschke product. No bounded-type assumption on
D is needed for this direct factorization. Bounded real parts of its zeros
and the Blaschke sum give log|B(x)|/x ->0 as x->+infinity.
But F'(x)/F(x)->+infinity and S(x)->-1. Hence c=0 and S=B is inner.
It is nonconstant. If F had a right-half-plane zero of multiplicity m, the
cancelled quotient would satisfy S(z0+h)=-1+2h/(alpha*m)+O(h^2), impossible
for a nonconstant inner function at an interior point. This handles multiple
zeros without assuming simplicity.

4. THE PRECISE DOMAIN OBSTRUCTION
================================
Let D0 be the span of all translates h_+(u+2b). Each such vector is mapped
by C0 to h_-(u+2b). This span is dense in ordinary L2 and separately in each
L2(exp(Xu)du). Those separate statements do not prove simultaneous density.

The correct common norm is H_X=L2((1+exp(Xu))du).
If D(p)=0 and 0<sigma=Re p<X, then
  ell_p(f)=integral f(u) exp(pu/2) du
is a bounded functional on H_X with
  ||ell_p||^2 = pi/[X sin(pi*sigma/X)].
It annihilates every feature translate, since
  ell_p(h_+(u+2b))=exp(-pb)D(p)=0.
But ell_p(f0)=V(p) is nonzero. Thus
  dist_H_X(f0, closure(D0))
  >= |V(p)| sqrt[X sin(pi*sigma/X)/pi] >0.
A zero of multiplicity m supplies m derivative-evaluation annihilators.
No assertion that these exhaust all possible core defects is required here.

For X>=1/2, closing the feature operator on a single vertical line legitimately
gives the bounded contractive Fourier multiplier S(X+it). For arbitrary X the
same argument gives the maximal closed multiplier, which can be unbounded.
Its graph norm on Fourier core
vectors D(X+it)p(t) is L2 of (|D|^2+|N|^2)dt; finite exponential sums are
dense for that finite measure. But identifying that closure with the literal
product exp(Xu/2) C0 exp(-Xu/2) on arbitrary compact tests requires convergence
in the simultaneous norm. This is exactly where the preceding functional
can obstruct the argument.

Simple model: S(z)=(z+p)/(z-p), p>0. The unitary boundary multiplier has
kernel delta(u)-p exp(-pu/2)1_(u>0). Above the support of compact f, its output
is -p exp(-pu/2)ell_p(f). Weight X>p makes this output nonintegrable whenever
ell_p(f) is nonzero. Yet the continued vertical symbol S(X+it) is bounded;
its kernel is delta(u)+p exp((X-p)u/2)1_(u<0). To compare in the same
coordinates, put v(u)=exp(Xu/2)f(u). The continued multiplier applied to v
agrees with exp(Xu/2)C0 f precisely on the original-input cancellation core
ell_p(f)=0, equivalently ell_(p-X)(v)=0. Generic compact original inputs do
not satisfy that condition. The kernels are not being compared on an identical
unweighted input with the conjugations omitted.
This model illustrates the domain issue; it is not a claim that xi has poles
of this kind or a claim that the toy outer multiplier is contractive.

5. A GENUINE WEIGHTED CONTRACTION, AND ITS FAILED MATCH
====================================================
The companion file weighted_theta_resolvent_20261108.txt gives the proof.
Let c=1/4, q=sqrt(kappa/H), and
  B_a(u,v)=q(u)q(v)exp(a(u-v)-c|u-v|)/(2c).
For |a|<=c, B_a is compact and accretive, and T_a=(I+B_a)^(-1) is contractive.
Its Hermitian part is the sum of two positive exponential kernels with rates
c-a and c+a. An explicit positive Gram factorization of I-T_a^*T_a follows.
Thus weighted contractivity itself can be proved throughout the critical
strip tilt a=x/2, including its endpoints.

However T_a is I+compact and cannot map ALL required translated h^+ features
to h^- features. Translates tend weakly to zero, so the compact correction
vanishes, leaving the exact nonzero residual limit
  lim_|b|->infinity ||T_a h^+_(x,b)-h^-_(x,b)||
  = sqrt[(alpha/2)integral u^2 exp(xu)kappa(u)^2 du] >0.
Every lambda*I+compact candidate has the same structural problem. This does
not exclude arbitrary noncompact scattering or canonical-system constructions.

The companion also computes explicit nonzero source terms when the fixed
theta state H at energy -1/16 is replaced by a Mellin-tilted trial state at
another energy. A spectral energy identity cannot omit those terms.

6. CERTIFIED EXCLUSION OF A CONFINING SPECTRAL CANDIDATE
=====================================================
Take psi=kappa^2 on u>=0 and L=-d^2/du^2+psi''/psi with Neumann boundary at 0.
Ground-state factorization makes L nonnegative, with ground state psi and
eigenvalue 0. Its confining tail has the attractive Riemann zero-count leading
terms for frequencies 2*sqrt(lambda_n). This does not fix its individual modes.

Use the trial vector psi(u)*(u^2-m2/m0), orthogonal to the ground state, where
  m_j=integral_0^infinity u^j kappa(u)^4 du.
Ground-state transformation gives the exact Rayleigh quotient
  R=4*m2/(m4-m2^2/m0), hence lambda_1<=R.

The accompanying mpmath interval script directly encloses the three moments
on 2000 rational cells covering [0,3], using theta terms n=1,...,4. It uses no
floating-point quadrature error estimate. For n>=5 and u>=0, t=exp(u)>=1:
  sum omitted kappa terms
  <=2*pi^2*625*exp(-25*pi)/(1-exp(4/5-10*pi)) < 1e-30.
This follows from n=5+j, n^4<=625 exp(4j/5), n^2>=25+10j and monotonicity
of t^(9/4)exp(-25*pi*t) for t>=1.

For u>=0, kappa(u)<=3*pi^2 exp(9u/4-pi exp(u)). For n>=2 use
  sum n^4 exp(-pi(n^2-1)t)
  <=16 exp(-3*pi*t)/(1-exp(2-4*pi*t)) <1/2.
For u=3+v and j=0,2,4, use exp(v)>=1+v and (3+v)^j<=3^j exp(jv/3).
The moment tail is at most
  81*pi^8*3^j*exp(27-4*pi*exp(3))/(4*pi*exp(3)-9-j/3) <1e-90.

The interval result is
  R in [36.1949358864,46.5141964830],
  2*sqrt(lambda_1) <= 13.640264 <14.
The first positive xi ordinate exceeds14 (approximately14.13472514). Thus
the specific candidate, with only its zero ground state removed and the stated
frequency normalization, cannot have exactly the xi spectrum.

Independent noncertified finite-difference values at spacing0.0005, domain
endpoints3 and4, gave 12.76447152,18.64503901,23.45170810 for the first three
positive frequencies. These numbers illustrate the exclusion; the interval
Rayleigh inequality is the certificate. No general spectral route is excluded.

7. OTHER ROUTES AUDITED
======================
For fixed x>0, the exact integrated kernel has asymptotic
  M_x(b) ~ 2*pi^3*x exp(3|b|-2*pi*exp(|b|)).
Laplace's method uses a=O(exp(-|b|/2)) in its defining a-integral.
This tail proves M_x/M_x(0) cannot be an infinitely divisible characteristic
function or a positive Gaussian scale mixture: either would have a Gaussian
lower bound, contradicted by this double-exponential decay. Schoenberg's
classification also excludes M_x itself as a PF_infinity density, since its
bilateral Laplace transform is entire and would force the Gaussian case.
Ordinary one-dimensional positive definiteness remains the possible target.

Suvrit Sra's November2,2026 preprint studies DIFFERENT, reciprocal-transform
densities. It obtains a positive decreasing heat-trace kernel for B(s)=
log[xi(1/2+sqrt(s))/xi(1/2)], using finite verified zero data. Hence B is
Bernstein. Our impedance is F'/F(z)=2z B'(z^2). A positive Stieltjes
representation for B', equivalently complete monotonicity of that heat trace,
would give the required positive-real impedance. This stronger property is
RH-equivalent for xi; the preprint recognizes this distinction. Its much longer
huge-finite-PF certificate was not independently audited in this session.

An elementary obstruction to the shortcut is
  h0(s)=(1+s)(1+s/(100+i))(1+s/(100-i)).
Its logarithmic derivative has positive decreasing Laplace kernel
  exp(-t)+2 exp(-100t)cos(t).
Thus log h0 is Bernstein and h0^(-alpha) gives self-decomposable laws, but
h0(z^2) has off-axis zeros. A positive reciprocal law alone is insufficient.
