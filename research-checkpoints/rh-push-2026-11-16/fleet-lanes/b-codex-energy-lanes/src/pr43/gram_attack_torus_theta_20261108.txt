EXACT SIGNED GRAM AND CERTIFIED SEVEN-POINT OBSTRUCTION
2026-11-08. This is not a proof or disproof of RH.

Definitions
-----------
theta(t) = sum over n in Z of exp(-pi*n^2*t).
kappa(u) = (d^2/du^2 - 1/16)[exp(u/4)*theta(exp(u))].
The full kernel is even. For u >= 0 it has the positive series

kappa(u) = sum_{n>=1} [2*pi^2*n^4*exp(9u/4)
                         -3*pi*n^2*exp(5u/4)] exp(-pi*n^2*exp(u)).

R_x(b) = integral_R exp(x*a)*kappa(a+b)*kappa(a-b) da.
M_x(b) = dR_x(b)/dx
       = integral_R a*sinh(x*a)*kappa(a+b)*kappa(a-b) da.

The target is positive definiteness of M_x for every x>0.

1. Exact signed Gram
-------------------
Let f_x(u)=exp(x*u/2)*kappa(u) and g_b(t)=f_x(t+2b).
Then R_x(b-c)=<g_b,g_c> in L^2(R), and
d_x g_b(t)=(t+2b)*g_b(t)/2.

For any alpha>0 put
h_b^+ = (g_b + alpha*d_x g_b)/sqrt(2*alpha),
h_b^- = (g_b - alpha*d_x g_b)/sqrt(2*alpha).

Direct polarization gives exactly
M_x(b-c) = <h_b^+,h_c^+> - <h_b^-,h_c^->.

A positive factorization follows if one independently constructs a contraction
C_x satisfying C_x h_b^+ = h_b^- for all b. The resulting positive feature is
(I-C_x^* C_x)^(1/2)h_b^+. Formally defining C_x by a Fourier quotient does not
prove it is a contraction; that is precisely the missing assertion.

For a finite sample, let A_+ c=sum_j c_j h_(b_j)^+ and similarly A_-.
The derivative Gram matrix is P-N, P=A_+^*A_+, N=A_-^*A_-.
It is the Schur complement of the block matrix
[[P, A_-^*], [A_-, I]]. Thus the previous E-B^*D^(-1)B issue is present
exactly, with E=P, B=A_-, D=I.

2. An unconditional two-point theorem
------------------------------------
kappa is strictly log-concave. A primary source is George Csordas,
Fourier transforms of positive definite kernels and the Riemann xi-function,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0055, Theorem 4.5 and stronger Theorem 4.2(b).
His kernel Phi satisfies kappa(u)=Phi(u/4).

Write ell=log(kappa). For b>0,
d_b[kappa(a+b)*kappa(a-b)]
 = kappa(a+b)*kappa(a-b)*[ell'(a+b)-ell'(a-b)] < 0.
Since a*sinh(x*a)>0 for a != 0, x>0, M_x(b)>0 and M_x'(b)<0 for b>0.
Consequently each two-point derivative Gram matrix is positive definite for
distinct points: its eigenvalues are M_x(0)+M_x(d) and M_x(0)-M_x(d).

The moment expansion of M_x gives the associated-kernel hierarchy in Csordas,
Theorems 3.5--3.7. Proving all its coefficient kernels positive definite is an
already known RH-equivalent problem. Example 3.12 shows positivity at one
hierarchy level need not imply positivity at the next.

3. Certified obstruction to a Gram factorization separately at each a
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Put h_a(b)=kappa(a+b)*kappa(a-b). Although
M_x(b)=2*integral_0^infinity a*sinh(x*a)*h_a(b) da
has a positive integration weight, h_a need not itself be positive definite.

Take b_j=3j/20 for j=0,...,6 and c=(-2,9,-19,24,-19,9,-2).
The provided interval-arithmetic script proves, for a=1/20,

sum_{i,j} c_i*c_j*h_a(b_i-b_j)
  lies in (-0.000908036036924, -0.000908036036922).

For a=0 the same quadratic form lies in
(-0.001226418753079, -0.001226418753076).

These are negative finite Gram forms for the FULL theta kernel, with a proved
series remainder. They rule out the proposed factorization at each fixed a.
They do not disprove positive definiteness after integration over a. Any
successful positive construction must use information mixing midpoint values.

4. Complete error bounds for the finite certificate
--------------------------------------------------
Use the first eight positive terms to evaluate kappa at |u|, exploiting the
exact modular evenness. Since pi>3 and 2*pi^2<32, for u>=0, n>=9,

0 < kappa_n(u) < 32*n^4*exp(9u/4)*exp(-3*n^2*exp(u))
               <= 32*n^4*exp(-3*n^2).

The last inequality follows because the logarithmic derivative of the
u-dependent factor is 9/4-3*n^2*exp(u)<0. The ratio of consecutive tail terms
is at most (10/9)^4*exp(-57). Therefore

0 < kappa(u)-kappa_8(u)
  < 32*9^4*exp(-243)/(1-(10/9)^4*exp(-57)) < 10^(-99).

The derivative bounds below in particular prove kappa(u)<=2 and
kappa_8(u)<=2. Thus each product error is <4*10^(-99).
Since (sum_i |c_i|)^2=84^2=7056, the full quadratic-form tail is
<28224*10^(-99)<3*10^(-95). All retained terms are evaluated with 40-digit
interval arithmetic, including input fractions and transcendental functions.
The executable adds this full tail interval before checking negativity.

5. Independent Fourier certificate and its bounds
------------------------------------------------
The script also proves
integral_R kappa(u)^2*cos(12u) du
  lies in (-3.56317771*10^(-5), -3.06562926*10^(-5)).

On [0,3], composite Simpson with 1000 subintervals is applied to
f(u)=2*kappa_8(u)^2*cos(12u).
Let t=pi*n^2*exp(u), and write
kappa_n^(j)(u)=exp(u/4)*p_j(t)*exp(-t),
p_0(t)=2t^2-3t,
p_(j+1)(t)=t*p_j'(t)+(1/4-t)*p_j(t).

The five polynomials are:
p_0 = 2t^2-3t;
p_1 = -2t^3+(15/2)t^2-(15/4)t;
p_2 = 2t^4-14t^3+(165/8)t^2-(75/16)t;

p_4 = 2t^6-33t^5+(647/4)t^4-266t^3+(15465/128)t^2-(1875/256)t.

For a monomial c*t^k, put q=k+1/4. Its absolute contribution is at most
|c|*t^q*exp(-t), since (pi*n^2)^(-1/4)<1. For n=1 and t>=3, its maximum is
max(3,q)^q*exp(-max(3,q)). For n>=2, t>=3*n^2>=12>q, so each maximum is
(3*n^2)^q*exp(-3*n^2). The successive-n ratio is at most
(3/2)^(25/2)*exp(-15)<1/20000. Hence the entire n>=2 contribution is bounded
by 12^q*exp(-12)/(1-1/20000).

Summing absolute polynomial coefficients, the script interval-verifies the
global bounds |kappa^(j)| <= (2,9,53,376,3245)_j, j=0,...,4. The same applies
to the truncated positive-axis kernel. Leibniz yields
|(2*kappa_8^2*cos(12u))^(4)| <= 1842772.
Simpson error <= (3/180)*(3/1000)^4*1842772 = 0.0000024877422.
The theta-tail integral on [0,3] is <24*10^(-99)<10^(-97).

For u>=3, the positive theta series gives
