    W_F(g) = sum_p (abs(ghat(ip))^2 + abs(ghat(-ip))^2) + (1/2 pi) int w(t) abs(ghat(t))^2 dt - sum_n n^(-1/2) (c(n) h(log n) + conj c(n) h(-log n)),
    w(t) = 2 log Q + sum_j (Re psi(z_j + it/2) - log pi),   h(u) = int g(x) conj g(x - u) dx.

## 3. Combination 1: free prime phases

### 3.1 Definition and three facts

For theta in the torus of the primes p < e^(2a): c_theta(n) = c(n) exp(i sum_p v_p(n) theta_p), H_F(theta) the Hermitian form on L^2(-a, a; C) with these coefficients and with the gamma factor, constant and pole of F unchanged; Lam_F(theta) = lambda_min H_F(theta). theta = 0 is F. Two variants: "full" (pole terms kept) and "pf" (pole vectors projected out; the form T_a[sigma_2] of the source in the L^2 normalisation, so signs agree with the source and values do not: the source value 0.582 at ln 3 corresponds to 9.45e-4 here).

F1 (PROVED). Lam_F(-theta) = Lam_F(theta) for real c(n) (complex conjugation of g). So theta = 0 is a critical point of Lam_F.

F2 (PROVED). On the orbit theta_p = tau log p the coefficients are c(n) n^(i tau). The substitution g -> e^(i tau x) g turns H_F(theta) into the form with the coefficients of F, the archimedean symbol w(t + tau) and the pole functionals at +-i p + tau. So Lam_F on the orbit is lambda_min of "the coefficients of F against its own gamma factor and pole displaced by tau", which is the unimodular-twist control of the campaign. The numbers log p are linearly independent over Q, the orbit is dense in the torus (Kronecker), and Lam_F is continuous in theta (finitely many coefficients enter), hence

    inf over the torus of Lam_F = inf over tau in R of Lam_F(tau log p),     same for sup.

The torus statistic is a statistic of the one-parameter family of unimodular twists. This holds at every support.

F3 (PROVED). For T-NR with q > e^(2a): H_TNR(theta) = H_zeta(theta) + 2 log q. Same G, same minimiser, same maximiser.

## 4. Combination 2: Gaussian-smoothed cell matrices

### 4.1 Definition and what it is

H_(h,m) = Toeplitz(q_d), q_d = K_F tested against the hat function of width h at d h (cell matrix of the Weil form on step functions of mesh h; refinement identity U^T H_(h/2,2m) U = H_(h,m), CLASSICAL). Heat deformation as specified: each prime event n^(-1/2) c(n) delta(x -+ log n) is replaced by the Gaussian of variance 2t; for consistency the archimedean part is smoothed by the same kernel and the pole term 2 cosh(p x) acquires the factor e^(p^2 t). For t < 0 the Gaussian cannot be removed from a hat function; the exact inverse that exists on the list q_0, .., q_M is the lattice heat step q(t) = e^(t Delta_h) q(0), whose symbol multiplier exp(-4 t sin^2(phi/2) / h^2) is positive and bounded; it is used for t = s h^2, s = -0.3, -1, -3, -6 (beyond s = -6 the multiplier e^(4 abs s) exhausts float64).

H1 (PROVED). For t >= 0, H_(h,m)(t) is the Gram matrix of W_F on the cells convolved with the Gaussian of variance t: K * G_(2t) tested on c_i * c~_j equals K tested on (c_i * G_t) * (c_j * G_t)~. Hence the refinement identity holds for every t >= 0 (measured: 9e-13), and H(t) >= 0 for all h, m, t >= 0 whenever W_F >= 0 on Schwartz functions.

H2 (CLASSICAL, explicit formula). On the zero side the smoothing multiplies the term of a zero 1/2 + i gamma by exp(-t gamma^2) (complex gamma for an off-line zero). It does not move any zero. The de Bruijn-Newman flow moves the zeros of H_t and has no prime side for t != 0. So the parameter t of the test is not the de Bruijn-Newman time, and Lambda_(h,m)(F) has no reason to approach Lambda(F).

H3 (ARGUED). An off-line quadruple contributes an indefinite 2 x 2 block to the form at every t; smoothing scales it by exp(-t (gamma_0^2 - beta^2)) and rotates its phase. So for a window that resolves the quadruple the form is indefinite for every t, and Lambda_(h,m) = +infinity; for a window that does not, the form is non-negative at t = 0 and smoothing can only bring in negativity through the wider effective support (m h plus a few sqrt(2t)).

## 5. Combination 3: skew sections

### 5.1 Definition and three facts

A_F on L^2(0, 2a): (A f)(u) = int (v - u) K_F(v - u) f(v) dv. Cell sections: skew Toeplitz(a_d), a_d by the hat rule for the point masses and a 12-point Gauss rule for the smooth part.
