# What the certified low-divisor cancellation does and does not support

Independently reviewed analytic PASS. This uses the independently certified N1 arithmetic outputs, not a new positive-pivot assumption.

The first two arithmetic terms have directed interval bounds
 U1 upper < -19.52168949,
 U2 upper < 18.44826269.
Including their already-paid spatial integral tails, this gives
 U1+U2 < -1.07.
Thus even grouping the first two divisor indices together does not restore positivity. The first three lower endpoints give
 U1+U2+U3 > -22.43416588+17.64558566+10.86757987 >6.07,
again after the negligible paid tails. This identifies where aggregate cancellation first succeeds in the N1 diagnostic.

There is no arithmetic closure principle established for blocks of three. Divisor grouping by k=nm is exact because it records the log-ratio variance; grouping adjacent k afterward has no accompanying algebraic identity that would propagate this observed sign. In particular this finite observation is not a monotonicity law across degree.

A three-term recurrence for the actual prefix polynomials would introduce mixed products between neighboring polynomials in each arithmetic block. Those block forms cannot be assumed positive semidefinite: the certified negative first-block value on the actual P1 already disproves such a premise. Cauchy–Schwarz in an auxiliary positive profile would not pay that actual signed block feedback.

The surviving rigorous mechanisms are the exact divisor decomposition, the degree-only eventual sign, and the paid finite-core criterion. The missing step is a signed aggregate inequality for the low-divisor core, not choosing a favorable-looking fixed block size from the N1 result.
