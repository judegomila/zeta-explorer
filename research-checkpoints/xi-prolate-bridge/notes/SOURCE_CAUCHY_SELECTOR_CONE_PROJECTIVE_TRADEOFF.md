# Source-Cauchy selector cone and projective tradeoff

## 0. Exact outcome and claim boundary

This note classifies the finite source-Cauchy perturbations whose inversion-
even block has rank one, identifies the positive selector cone generated by
those perturbations, and gives a quantitative coordinate-selection theorem
for a multiple lowest even eigenspace.

Let `A=Q_+`, let `mu=min spec(A)`, and let

\[
 E=\ker(A-\mu I),\qquad P=P_E,\qquad m=\dim E.       \tag{0.1}
\]

Besides the boundary selector `eta` and the coordinate-shell selectors, there
is an exact one-parameter family of Cauchy-kernel selectors.  In the normalized
even parity basis it is, projectively,

\[
 w_\tau=e_0^++\sum_{n=1}^N
 {\sqrt2\,\tau\over \tau+n^2}e_n^+.                \tag{0.2}
\]

The regular finite parameters are
`tau in R minus {-1^2,...,-N^2}`.  The value `tau=0` is the central
coordinate, `tau=infinity` is the boundary vector, and the projective limit
at `tau=-j^2` is the `j`-th even shell coordinate.
Every positive rank-one even block in this class is one of these directions.

For a unit projected prolate state `kappa`, a low projector `Pi`, and

\[
 p_M=\Pi\kappa,\qquad
 {\cal L}=\operatorname {Ran}\Pi\cap p_M^\perp,
 \qquad P_{\cal L}=P_L,                             \tag{0.3}
\]

put

\[
 y=P\kappa,\qquad Y=\|y\|,
 \qquad T=\operatorname {tr}(P_LP).                \tag{0.4}
\]

If `Y!=0`, some coordinate-shell selector
`x_j=Pe_j^+/||Pe_j^+||` obeys, when `T>0`,

\[
 {\|P_Lx_j\|^2\over|\langle\kappa,x_j\rangle|^2}
 \le {2T\over Y^2},
 \qquad
 |\langle\kappa,x_j\rangle|^{-2}
 \le {2m\over Y^2}.                                \tag{0.5}
\]

When `T=0`, one may choose a selector with zero low perpendicular leakage
and overlap inverse squared at most `m/Y^2`.  Thus a sufficient replacement
for the multiple-ground alignment hypotheses in checkpoint 0.73 is

\[
 T=O(Y^2/\lambda),
 \qquad {\sqrt m\over Y}=\lambda^{o(1)}.           \tag{0.6}
\]

Equation (0.6) is conditional.  It is not proved for the pinned arithmetic
matrices on an unbounded schedule.  An exact diagonal source-Cauchy family
below has zero residual and zero projection tail but `T=2`, and its prolate-
direction fixture stays a fixed projective distance from every cross-root
selector.  Consequently the finite classification does not establish
alignment decay, equation (5.2) of `DIRECT_RH_ATTACK.md`, locally uniform
convergence, or the Riemann hypothesis.  The project state remains
`P0_INVALIDATED`.

No source lock, arithmetic matrix formula, or prolate normalization is
changed here.  The auxiliary perturbations below are finite source-Cauchy
matrices, not new pinned Weil matrices.

## 1. Source-Cauchy parity block

Retain the locked centered modes and inversion

\[
 V_n(t)={(-1)^n\over\sqrt L}e^{2\pi int/L},
 \qquad JV_n=V_{-n},
 \qquad -N\le n\le N.                              \tag{1.1}
\]

A real inversion-symmetric source-Cauchy perturbation `H` has parameters

\[
 H_{nn}=a_n,
 \qquad H_{nm}={b_n-b_m\over n-m}\quad(n\ne m),
 \qquad a_{-n}=a_n,\quad b_{-n}=-b_n,\quad b_0=0.  \tag{1.2}
\]

Use the normalized parity bases

\[
 e_0^+=V_0,
 \qquad e_n^+={V_n+V_{-n}\over\sqrt2},
 \qquad e_n^-={V_n-V_{-n}\over\sqrt2}.            \tag{1.3}
\]

Direct substitution into (1.2) gives the complete normalized even block:

\[
 H^+_{00}=a_0,
 \qquad H^+_{0n}={\sqrt2\,b_n\over n},             \tag{1.4}
\]

\[
 H^+_{nn}=a_n+{b_n\over n},
 \qquad
 H^+_{nm}=2{nb_n-mb_m\over n^2-m^2}\quad
 (n\ne m,\ n,m\ge1).                              \tag{1.5}
\]

The parameters `a_0,...,a_N,b_1,...,b_N` are recovered uniquely from this
block: the first row gives every `b_n`, and the diagonal then gives every
`a_n`.  Hence the real vector space of even source-Cauchy blocks has
dimension

\[
 (N+1)+N=2N+1=2d-1,
 \qquad d=N+1.                                    \tag{1.6}
\]

## 2. Classification of rank-one even blocks

### Theorem 2.1 (all positive rank-one even source blocks)

Assume `N>=1` and

\[
 H^+=|w\rangle\langle w|,
 \qquad
 w=w_0e_0^++\sum_{n=1}^Nw_ne_n^+\ne0.            \tag{2.1}
\]

Then exactly one of the following holds.

1. `w` is a coordinate direction `e_j^+`, for some `0<=j<=N`.
2. `w_0!=0`, every `w_n!=0`, and for a unique
   `rho in R` one has

   \[
    w_n={\sqrt2\,w_0\over1+\rho n^2}.             \tag{2.2}
   \]

   With `tau=1/rho`, this is (0.2).  The case `rho=0` is
   `tau=infinity` and gives the boundary direction.

Conversely, every direction in these two classes has a unique full
source-Cauchy extension.

**Proof.**  Suppose first that `w_0!=0`.  Comparing the `0,n` entries in
(1.4) with (2.1) gives

\[
 b_n={n w_0w_n\over\sqrt2}.                       \tag{2.3}
\]

For distinct positive `n,m`, equation (1.5) becomes

\[
 \sqrt2w_0\,{n^2w_n-m^2w_m\over n^2-m^2}=w_nw_m. \tag{2.4}
\]

If one positive `w_n` vanishes while another does not, (2.4) is impossible.
Thus either every positive coordinate vanishes, giving `e_0^+`, or every
positive coordinate is nonzero.  In the latter case divide (2.4) by
`w_nw_m` and rearrange:

\[
 {\sqrt2w_0/w_n-1\over n^2}
 ={\sqrt2w_0/w_m-1\over m^2}=\rho.                \tag{2.5}
\]

For `N=1`, define `rho` by the single left side of (2.5); for `N>=2`,
equation (2.5) proves that the value is independent of the positive shell.
This proves (2.2).  A denominator `1+rho*n^2` cannot vanish for the
noncoordinate vector under consideration.

If `w_0=0`, equation (1.4) forces `b_n=0` for all `n`.  Equation (1.5) then
forces `w_nw_m=0` for every distinct positive pair.  At most one positive
coordinate is nonzero, so `w` is an even shell coordinate.  This proves the
classification.  The formulas in Section 3 prove the converse and
uniqueness.  \(\square\)

### 2.2 Compactified parameter and pole limits

For finite regular `tau`, normalize the central coordinate to one as in
(0.2).  The limiting directions are

\[
 [w_0]=[e_0^+],
 \qquad [w_\infty]=[\eta],
 \qquad
 \lim_{\tau\to-j^2}[w_\tau]=[e_j^+].             \tag{2.6}
\]

Here brackets denote a projective line, so a diverging common scalar is
irrelevant.  Clearing the denominators in (0.2) makes every coordinate a
homogeneous polynomial of degree `N` in the two homogeneous parameters for
`tau`.  Thus (2.6) is a single continuous rational-normal-curve
compactification; the pole directions are not omitted failure cases.

## 3. Unique full extension and its rank

Fix a regular finite `tau`, and let `gamma>0` be the squared central
coordinate.  Define

\[
 b_n={\gamma n\tau\over\tau+n^2},
 \qquad a_0=\gamma,
 \qquad
 a_n={\gamma\tau(\tau-n^2)\over(\tau+n^2)^2}.
                                                               \tag{3.1}
\]

The full raw matrix, including its diagonal, is

\[
 \boxed{
 H_{nm}={\gamma\tau(\tau-nm)
 \over(\tau+n^2)(\tau+m^2)}.}                    \tag{3.2}
\]

At `n=m`, (3.2) is exactly `a_n`.  For `n!=m`, the identity

\[
 {n\tau/(\tau+n^2)-m\tau/(\tau+m^2)\over n-m}
 ={\tau(\tau-nm)\over
   (\tau+n^2)(\tau+m^2)}                          \tag{3.3}
\]

proves the source-Cauchy formula.  Substitution into (1.4)--(1.5) gives

\[
 H^+=|w_\tau\rangle\langle w_\tau|,
 \quad
 (w_\tau)_0=\sqrt\gamma,
 \quad
 (w_\tau)_n={\sqrt{2\gamma}\,\tau\over\tau+n^2}.
                                                               \tag{3.4}
\]

Define raw even and odd vectors

\[
 u_n={\sqrt\gamma\,\tau\over\tau+n^2},
 \qquad
 v_n={\sqrt\gamma\,n\over\tau+n^2}.              \tag{3.5}
\]

Then `u_(-n)=u_n`, `v_(-n)=-v_n`, and (3.2) factors as

\[
 \boxed{H=|u\rangle\langle u|
             -\tau|v\rangle\langle v|.}          \tag{3.6}
\]

Thus every regular atom has even rank one and full rank at most two.  For
`N>=1`, finite `tau!=0`, and no pole, both parity vectors are nonzero, so
the full rank is exactly two.  The special rays are as follows.

- `tau=infinity`: `a_n=gamma`, `b_n=gamma*n`, and
  `H=gamma*|eta><eta|`, of full rank one.
- `tau=0`: `H=gamma*|e_0^+><e_0^+|`, of full rank one.
- `tau=-j^2`: after projective normalization, the full extension is the
  paired raw diagonal shell

  \[
   \gamma\bigl(|V_j\rangle\langle V_j|
        +|V_{-j}\rangle\langle V_{-j}|\bigr),     \tag{3.7}
  \]

  whose even and odd blocks both have rank one.

For the pole statement, put `tau=-j^2+delta` and choose the scale
`gamma=delta^2/(2*tau^2)`.  Then the even `j`-shell coordinate in (3.4) has
unit magnitude, while

\[
 a_j={\tau-j^2\over2\tau}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad b_j={\delta j\over2\tau}\longrightarrow0, \tag{3.8}
\]

and all other source parameters tend to zero.  This proves (3.7) without
silently evaluating a singular displayed fraction.

### Corollary 3.1 (full rank one)

Up to a nonzero real scalar, the only nonzero inversion-symmetric full
rank-one source-Cauchy perturbations are the boundary matrix
`|eta><eta|` and the central coordinate `|V_0><V_0|`.

**Proof.**  A full rank-one matrix commuting with inversion is supported in
one parity sector.  If its even block is nonzero, Theorem 2.1 and (3.6)
show that the odd block vanishes only at `tau=0` or `tau=infinity`.
If only the odd block were nonzero, its central row would give `b_n=0`,
while inversion pairs every nonzero odd shell with its negative shell; the
off-diagonal source formula would then contradict rank one.  \(\square\)

## 4. The positive selector cone

Let `S_N` be the `2d-1` dimensional real vector space of even blocks in
(1.4)--(1.5), and define

\[
 {\cal C}_N=\{R\in S_N:R\succeq0\}.               \tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (finite moment-cone representation)

Every `R in C_N` is a finite positive sum of the rank-one rays in Theorem
2.1.  One may use at most `2d-1` atoms.  Conversely, every such positive sum
belongs to `C_N` and has a unique full source-Cauchy extension.  A sum of
`k` regular or limiting atoms has full extension of rank at most `2k`.

**Self-contained justification.**  Put `x_j=j^2` for `0<=j<=N`.  Ignoring
the fixed invertible diagonal change from raw even coordinates to the
normalized parity basis, a regular selector has coordinates

\[
 r_j(\tau)={1\over\tau+x_j}.                       \tag{4.2}
\]

Multiplying all coordinates by
`prod_(k=0)^N(tau+x_k)` replaces (4.2) projectively by

\[
 q_j(\tau)=\prod_{k\ne j}(\tau+x_k).              \tag{4.3}
\]

The `q_j` form a basis of the real polynomials of degree at most `N`:
evaluation at `tau=-x_i` isolates `q_i`.  Hence an invertible real matrix
maps the curve `(q_0,...,q_N)` to the ordinary homogeneous moment curve

\[
 (s^N,s^{N-1}t,\ldots,t^N),
 \qquad [s:t]\in\mathbb {RP}^1.                  \tag{4.4}
\]

The span of the outer products of (4.4) is the space of `d by d` Hankel
matrices: entry `(i,j)` depends only on `i+j`.  It has dimension `2d-1`.
Every selector outer product lies in `S_N`, which also has dimension
`2d-1` by (1.6), so the invertible congruence identifies `S_N` with this
Hankel space.

It remains to identify the positive cone, not merely its linear span.  A
positive-semidefinite Hankel matrix defines a linear functional `Lambda` on
real homogeneous binary forms of degree `2N` such that

\[
 \Lambda(g^2)\ge0                                  \tag{4.5}
\]

for every real form `g` of degree `N`.  Every nonnegative real homogeneous
binary form of even degree is a sum of two squares of real forms: factor it
over the complex numbers, pair conjugate nonreal factors, and split the
resulting complex degree-`N` form into its real and imaginary parts.  Real
roots have even multiplicity and can be divided equally between the two
factors.  Therefore (4.5) makes `Lambda` nonnegative on every binary form
that is pointwise nonnegative on `RP^1`.

In finite dimension, the dual cone of pointwise nonnegative forms is the
closed conic hull of their point evaluations on `RP^1`.  This follows
directly from separation: a functional outside that conic hull is separated
by a form which is nonnegative at every projective point, contradicting the
preceding paragraph.  The projective line is compact after normalizing its
representatives, so this conic hull is closed.  Consequently the Hankel
matrix is a positive sum of moment-curve outer products.  Mapping back by
the invertible congruence gives the asserted Cauchy-kernel and coordinate
atoms.  Linear-dependence reduction in a `2d-1` dimensional space leaves at
most `2d-1` atoms: if more atoms occur, their outer products are linearly
dependent; adjust the positive weights along such a dependence until at
least one weight becomes zero, and repeat.  A nontrivial dependence among
nonzero positive-semidefinite atoms has coefficients of both signs, so the
adjustment preserves nonnegativity.  This proves the theorem without an
external moment-problem citation.  \(\square\)

### 4.2 Low-rank selection inside a multiple ground space

Let `H^+ in C_N` and set

\[
 K=P H^+P|_E.                                      \tag{4.6}
\]

If the largest eigenvalue of `K` is simple with unit eigenvector `x`, then
the lowest even eigenvector of

\[
 A_\varepsilon=A-\varepsilon H^+                 \tag{4.7}
\]

converges to `x` as `epsilon` decreases to zero at fixed `N`.  This is the
standard finite degenerate perturbation calculation: the ground-space
compression splits at first order as `-epsilon*K`, while the positive gap
from `E` to `E^perp` controls the off-ground component.  A rank-one atom
gives exactly

\[
 x={Pw_\tau\over\|Pw_\tau\|}                     \tag{4.8}
\]

when the numerator is nonzero.

There is no loss in taking the selector block positive semidefinite.  Given
any Hermitian source-Cauchy selector `G^+`, add `cI` with `c` large enough
that `G^++cI` is positive semidefinite.  This remains source-Cauchy and adds
only `cI_E` to its ground-space compression, so it changes no selected
eigendirection.  Thus Theorem 4.1 describes all first-order selector
directions through finite positive mixtures of the atoms.

Every simple lowest even auxiliary vector is cross-rooted by the already
proved finite source-Cauchy theorem.  Coefficientwise closure makes every
limit selected by (4.7) cross-rooted as well.  This restriction is decisive
in the countermodel of Section 6.

## 5. Exact coordinate projective tradeoff

Let `e_0^+,...,e_N^+` be the normalized even coordinate basis.  Put

\[
 p_j=\|Pe_j^+\|^2,
 \qquad
 x_j={Pe_j^+\over\sqrt{p_j}}\quad(p_j>0),          \tag{5.1}
\]

\[
 \alpha_j=\langle\kappa,x_j\rangle,
 \qquad A_j=\|P_Lx_j\|^2.                         \tag{5.2}
\]

The frame identities are

\[
 \sum_jp_j=m,
 \qquad
 \sum_jp_j|\alpha_j|^2=Y^2,
 \qquad
 \sum_jp_jA_j=T.                                  \tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed, `sum_j |e_j^+><e_j^+|=I` on the even space.  Sandwiching this
identity by `P`, by `|y><y|`, and by `P_L` gives the three formulas.

### 5.1 Multiplicity-safe derivative-cofactor serialization

The projector quantities in (0.4) need not be reconstructed from cached
eigenvectors.  Define the even characteristic polynomial and cofactor matrix
by

\[
 p(z)=\det(zI-A),
 \qquad C(z)=\operatorname {adj}(zI-A).            \tag{5.4}
\]

If the lowest even eigenvalue \(\mu\) has multiplicity \(m\), then \(m\) is
the first derivative order for which

\[
 p^{(j)}(\mu)=0\quad(0\le j<m),
 \qquad p^{(m)}(\mu)\ne0.                          \tag{5.5}
\]

The exact spectral projector is

\[
 \boxed{
 P_E={m\,C^{(m-1)}(\mu)\over p^{(m)}(\mu)}.}       \tag{5.6}
\]

To verify the sign and factorial, diagonalize \(A\) only for the proof.  On
a \(\mu\)-eigenvector, the numerator cofactor and the characteristic
polynomial contain respectively \(m-1\) and \(m\) factors of \(z-\mu\);
their derivatives contribute \((m-1)!\) and \(m!\).  On every other
eigenspace the cofactor retains at least \(m\) factors and its
\((m-1)\)-st derivative vanishes.  Equation (5.6) follows.  The verifier
itself can form every entry of \(C(z)\) from signed minors and differentiate
the resulting polynomials.

Put

\[
 k_{00}=\|p_M\|^2>0,
 \qquad
 P_L=\Pi-{|p_M\rangle\langle p_M|\over k_{00}},
 \qquad D=C^{(m-1)}(\mu).                           \tag{5.7}
\]

Then the two inputs to the selector tradeoff have the independently
reconstructible forms

\[
 \boxed{
 Y^2={m\,\langle\kappa,D\kappa\rangle\over p^{(m)}(\mu)},}
                                                               \tag{5.8}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 T={m\over p^{(m)}(\mu)}
 \left\{\operatorname {tr}(\Pi D)
 -{\langle p_M,Dp_M\rangle\over k_{00}}\right\}.}  \tag{5.9}
\]

These are multiplicity-safe finite polynomial identities.  A replay must
reconstruct \(A,p,C,\mu\), and the first nonzero derivative order from the
source parameters.  Producer booleans, cached eigenvectors, and cached
spectral projectors are not inputs to (5.5)--(5.9).  Certifying an exact or
ball-enclosed multiple root is a separate finite verification task; these
identities do not infer multiplicity from decimal eigenvalue clustering.

### Theorem 5.2 (delta tradeoff)

Assume `Y!=0`.  For every `delta>0`, at least one index with
`p_j>0` and `alpha_j!=0` satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 {A_j+\delta\over|\alpha_j|^2}
 \le {T+\delta m\over Y^2}.}                      \tag{5.10}
\]

**Proof.**  For the indices with `alpha_j!=0`, use the nonnegative weights
`p_j|alpha_j|^2`.  Their sum is `Y^2`.  Their weighted numerator for the
left side of (5.10) is

\[
 \sum_{\alpha_j\ne0}p_j(A_j+\delta)
 \le T+\delta m.                                  \tag{5.11}
\]

At least one ratio is no larger than this weighted average.  \(\square\)

If `T>0`, choose `delta=T/m`.  The selected index then obeys

\[
 {A_j\over|\alpha_j|^2}\le {2T\over Y^2},
 \qquad
 |\alpha_j|^{-2}\le {2m\over Y^2},                \tag{5.12}
\]

because the second term `delta/|alpha_j|^2` is also bounded by (5.10).

If `T=0`, every term `p_jA_j` in (5.3) vanishes.  Choose an index maximizing
`|alpha_j|`.  The first two identities in (5.3) give

\[
 A_j=0,
 \qquad
 |\alpha_j|^{-2}\le {m\over Y^2}.                 \tag{5.13}
\]

The same family can be searched through the regular Cauchy parameter.  For
an atom with `Pw_tau!=0`, define

\[
 R_\delta(\tau)=
 {\|P_LPw_\tau\|^2+\delta\|Pw_\tau\|^2
  \over|\langle y,w_\tau\rangle|^2}.             \tag{5.14}
\]

Coordinate shells are pole limits of this family, so minimizing (5.14) over
the compactified real parameter can only improve the coordinate choice.
All entries of (5.14) are finite rational functions of `tau` once `P` and
`y` are fixed.  This is an exact finite selector optimization, not an
asymptotic estimate for the arithmetic projector.

At the natural cutoff in checkpoint 0.73, (5.12) supplies the required
conditional low projective rate and admissible normalization scale if
(0.6) is proved.  Neither source-Cauchy displacement nor inversion implies
(0.6).

## 6. Exact separation countermodel

Fix `N>=2`, `G>0`, and a real `mu`.  Set every Cauchy coefficient to zero
and take

\[
 b_n=0,
 \qquad
 a_n=\begin{cases}
 \mu,&|n|\le2,\\
 \mu+G,&3\le|n|\le N.
 \end{cases}                                      \tag{6.1}
\]

This is an exact diagonal source-Cauchy family.  Its lowest even eigenspace
is

\[
 E=\operatorname {span}\{e_0^+,e_1^+,e_2^+\},
 \qquad m=3.                                      \tag{6.2}
\]

Use the unit vector

\[
 \kappa={e_0^+-\sqrt2e_1^++\sqrt2e_2^+\over\sqrt5}.
                                                               \tag{6.3}
\]

Its raw even coefficients are projectively `(x_0,x_1,x_2)=(1,-1,1)`, the
same direction forced by the admitted fixed-mode source-normalized prolate
asymptotic.  This use of the direction does not turn the synthetic diagonal
matrix (6.1) into the pinned arithmetic matrix.

Take any low projector containing the first three even coordinates.  Then
`P*kappa=kappa`, the residual and projection tail are exactly zero, and

\[
 Y^2=1,
 \qquad
 T=\operatorname {tr}(P_LP)=2.                   \tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the sufficient trace rate in (0.6) fails uniformly despite exact
ground membership.  More strongly, no source-Cauchy auxiliary selector can
approach this direction projectively.

For raw coefficients `(z_0,z_1,z_2)`, the exact polynomial is

\[
 p_z(y)=Ay^2+By+C,                                \tag{6.5}
\]

\[
 A=z_0+2z_1+2z_2,
 \qquad B=-5z_0-8z_1-2z_2,
 \qquad C=4z_0.                                   \tag{6.6}
\]

When `N>2`, the complete raw polynomial of a vector in (6.2) is (6.5)
times `prod_(n=3)^N(y-n^2)`.  These additional factors are real-rooted, so
they neither repair nor obscure a nonreal root of the quadratic factor.

At `(1,-1,1)`,

\[
 p_\kappa(y)\ \parallel\ y^2+y+4,               \tag{6.7}
\]

whose discriminant is `-15`.  The raw even Hilbert norm is

\[
 \|(z_0,z_1,z_2)\|_+^2=z_0^2+2z_1^2+2z_2^2.     \tag{6.8}
\]

Relative to this norm, the squared dual norms of the three coefficient
functionals in (6.6) are

\[
 \|A\|_*^2=5,
 \qquad \|B\|_*^2=59,
 \qquad \|C\|_*^2=16.                             \tag{6.9}
\]

If `||delta z||_+<1/sqrt(59)`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 A>1-\sqrt{5/59}>2/3,
 \qquad |B|<2,
 \qquad C>4-4/\sqrt{59}>24/7.                    \tag{6.10}
\]

Therefore

\[
 B^2<4<{64\over7}<4AC,                            \tag{6.11}
\]

so every polynomial in that ball still has negative discriminant.  The
cone of real-rooted raw polynomials is consequently at least
`1/sqrt(59)` from the unnormalized vector `(1,-1,1)`, whose norm is
`sqrt(5)`.  For every unit cross-root vector `x`,

\[
 1-|\langle\kappa,x\rangle|^2\ge {1\over295}.     \tag{6.12}
\]

At the full low cutoff, `A_x=1-|<kappa,x>|^2`, and hence

\[
 \boxed{{A_x\over|\langle\kappa,x\rangle|^2}
 \ge {1\over294}.}                                \tag{6.13}
\]

Every limit selected by a source-Cauchy auxiliary is cross-rooted by
Section 4, so (6.13) applies to arbitrary-rank selectors, not only to the
rank-one family.  Boundary visibility does not help: the canonical boundary
selector in `E` has overlap `1/5`, perpendicular energy `24/25`, and ratio
`24`.

This is a structural non-implication theorem.  It is not the pinned Weil
matrix, and fixed `N=2` is not a projection-convergent diagonal schedule.
It proves that the projector trace rate and cross-root proximity required by
the conditional route are genuinely new actual-source obligations.

## 7. Remaining finite and asymptotic obligations

The finite selector cone gives three exact alternatives:

1. choose one Cauchy-kernel or coordinate atom by minimizing (5.14);
2. choose a positive low-rank mixture and prove that its ground-space
   compression has a simple top eigenvector with the required prolate
   alignment; or
3. prove the coordinate-averaged conditions (0.6).

All three avoid assuming a pre-existing simple even ground.  None supplies
the necessary arithmetic estimate.  On one common unbounded actual-source
schedule, one must still prove nonvanishing of the relevant ground
projection, quantitative projector orientation, the admitted projection
tails, and locally uniform convergence to the correctly normalized limit.
The source-Cauchy cone classification alone proves none of these statements.
