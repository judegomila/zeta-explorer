# Why the distant positive tail cannot pay an absolute core deficit uniformly in order

2026-11-02. Consequence of the independently certified negative pointwise H5 fiber and the explicit H_J→∞ outer thresholds. This concerns one particular absolute-budget strategy, not RH.

The pointwise oddH5 matrix at h=0 has negative determinant while its lower principal minors are positive, so it has a negative direction. Choose one fixed polynomial coefficient vector p of degree≤5 in that negative direction. By continuity, the corresponding original pointwise quadratic form stays strictly negative on [0,ε] for some ε>0. Multiplication by the bridge-limit weight2h² gives a scalar integrable density b_p(h) which is negative on (0,ε]. Thus
c_p:=∫_0^ε max(0,−b_p(h))dh>0.

Embed this same vector by zero coefficients in every order J≥5. For any thresholds H_J→∞,
A_J[p]:=∫_0^(H_J)max(0,−b_p(h))dh≥c_p
for all sufficiently large J. But by full-theta decay,
T_J[p]:=∫_(H_J)^∞b_p(h)dh→0.
The new outer theorem ensures this tail is nonnegative at its own explicit thresholds. Consequently there is no finite degree-independent q with
A_J[p]≤q T_J[p]
for every J.

Therefore a proof that separates the entire core into an absolute negative deficit and pays it exclusively from the remote positive outer region cannot close the all-order inequality with a uniform budget. Positive contributions inside the core must remain coupled to its negative contributions. This is exactly what the successful integratedH5 tail-matrix certificate retained; it did not discard all favorable core terms.
