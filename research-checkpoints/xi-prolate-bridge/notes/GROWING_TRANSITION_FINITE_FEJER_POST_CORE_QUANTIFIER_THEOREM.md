# Growing-transition finite-Fejer post-core quantifier theorem

## 0. Exact result and claim boundary

Use the locked schedule

\[
 \lambda={p\over2},\qquad p\geq5\text{ odd},\qquad
 A=\lambda^2={p^2\over4}\notin\mathbb Z,\qquad
 c=2\pi A,\qquad L=\log A.                              \tag{0.1}
\]

and the standard, unnormalized sampler Fejer weights

\[
 \omega_M(m)=\left(1-{m\over M+1}\right)_+,
 \qquad m\geq1.                                         \tag{0.1a}
\]

This note proves that the proposed all-cutoff post-core quantifier is
incorrect.  At a finite sampler cutoff, the exact center coefficient is
not `2 log N-Lambda(N)`.  It is

\[
 \boxed{C_M(N)=2\omega_M(1)\ell_M(N)
              -\omega_M(1)^2\Lambda(N),}
 \qquad
 \ell_M(N)=\sum_{n\mid N}\Lambda(n)\omega_M(N/n).       \tag{0.2}
\]

Only in the ordered limit `M -> infinity`, with `A` fixed, does (0.2)
become

\[
 C_\infty(N)=2\log N-\Lambda(N)
 =2L+\{2\log(N/A)-\Lambda(N)\}.                         \tag{0.3}
\]

In particular, `M=1` gives

\[
 \boxed{C_1(N)={1\over4}\Lambda(N).}                    \tag{0.4}
\]

Thus subtracting the unweighted constant `2L` core at every finite `M`
leaves a coefficient of size `L` already on the exact center.  The target

\[
 \sup_{M\geq1}\varepsilon_M=o(1)                        \tag{0.5}
\]

cannot be the scalar hypothesis for that subtraction.  There are two
honest replacements:

1. retain the complete, variable operator generated by `C_M(N)`, together
   with its midpoint trace rows, inside a finite-cutoff assembly `PA_M`;
   or
2. take the sampler limit first and ask for

   \[
    \boxed{\varepsilon(L)=\limsup_{M\to\infty}
      \varepsilon_M(L)=o(1)}.                            \tag{0.6}
   \]

The first replacement does not make `C_M` diagonal or positive.  The
identity `S_1=-mathsf X/4` applies to the constant unweighted alias after
the ordered limit; it does not diagonalize the variable finite-`M`
coefficient (0.2).

The note also records the exact midpoint half-row and the minimal
Stieltjes correction forced by divisor-mask jumps.  It does **not** prove
the ordered-limit scalar estimate (0.6), the complete physical Plemelj
assembly, the ordinary transition-shell estimate, the hard-edge
Christoffel--Darboux scalar estimate, a complete central floor, or RH.  The
formal project state remains `P0_INVALIDATED`.

## 2. Exact weighted unrestricted and both-miss coefficients

At fixed total product `N`, define the weighted unrestricted coefficient

\[
 \boxed{
 G_{N,M}=\sum_{n\mid N}\Lambda(n)
          \sum_{ml=N/n}\omega_M(m)\omega_M(l).}         \tag{2.1}
\]

For

\[
 y_N=\log(N/A),\qquad z=e^\rho,\qquad
 w=e^{y_N-\rho},
\]

the weighted both-miss coefficient is

\[
 B_{N,M}(\rho)=
 \sum_{\substack{n\mid N\\n<A}}\Lambda(n)
 \sum_{ml=N/n}\omega_M(m)\omega_M(l)
 {\bf1}_{m>z}{\bf1}_{l>w}.                              \tag{2.2}
\]

Consider the open center

\[
 0<\rho<y_N,\qquad z<2,\qquad w<2.                       \tag{2.3}
\]

Here `m>z` and `l>w` are exactly `m,l>=2`.  Conversely, if
`m,l>=2`, then

\[
 n={N\over ml}\leq {N\over4}<A,                         \tag{2.4}
\]

because (2.3) implies `N/A=zw<4`.  Hence

\[
 B_{N,M}(\rho)=\sum_{n\mid N}\Lambda(n)
       \sum_{\substack{ml=N/n\\m,l\geq2}}
       \omega_M(m)\omega_M(l).                          \tag{2.5}
\]

For `q=N/n`, inclusion--exclusion of the two boundary layers `m=1`
and `l=1` gives

\[
 \sum_{ml=q}\omega_M(m)\omega_M(l)
 -\sum_{\substack{ml=q\\m,l\geq2}}\omega_M(m)\omega_M(l)
 =2\omega_M(1)\omega_M(q)
  -{\bf1}_{q=1}\omega_M(1)^2.                            \tag{2.6}
\]

Summing (2.6) against `Lambda(n)` proves the finite-cutoff center identity

\[
 \boxed{G_{N,M}-B_{N,M}(\rho)=C_M(N)}                   \tag{2.7}
\]

with `C_M` as in (0.2).  The correction at `q=1` is essential: the two
descriptions `m=1` and `l=1` are the same ordered factorization there.

For each fixed `N`, monotone finite support and
`sum_(n|N) Lambda(n)=log N` give (0.3).  For `M=1`, only `q=1` survives,
so `ell_1(N)=Lambda(N)/2` and (0.4) follows.

## 3. Midpoint convention and the exact half-row

Let a strict mask be assigned value `1/2` at equality.  On the left end
`rho=0`, provided `N<2A`, the only equality inside the adjacent center is
`m=z=1`.  The other inequality is

\[
 l={N\over n}>w={N\over A}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n<A.
\]

Thus the midpoint value of `B` exceeds its inward open-center value by
one half of

\[
 \boxed{
 E_{N,M}=\omega_M(1)
   \sum_{\substack{n\mid N\\n<A}}
     \Lambda(n)\omega_M(N/n)
 =\omega_M(1)\{\ell_M(N)-\omega_M(1)\Lambda(N)\}.}     \tag{3.1}
\]

The last equality uses `A<N<2A`: if a proper divisor `n` of `N` has
`N/n>=2`, then `n<=N/2<A`, so the only divisor omitted by `n<A` from the
displayed boundary layer is `n=N`.  The right end `rho=y_N` has the
same formula with `l=1`.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 (G_{N,M}-B_{N,M})_{\rm midpoint}
 =C_M(N)-{1\over2}E_{N,M}.}                              \tag{3.2}
\]

There is no simultaneous equality: it would force the integer prime
variable `n` to equal the noninteger number `A=p^2/4`.  Formula (3.2) is
an endpoint trace identity, not a change to the correlation integral.
The half-row must travel with the retained Plemelj boundary rows.  At a
true mask transition such as `z=2`, the analogous `m=2` layer is likewise
split in half and belongs to the ordinary transition channel.

## 4. Why the all-cutoff core subtraction fails

The physical fixed-product fold uses `-2(G-B)`.  In the unweighted center,
the decomposition (0.3) contains the constant `2L`; its paired first alias
is the frozen reciprocal core

\[
 (-2L)S_1={L\over2}\mathsf X.                            \tag{4.1}
\]

At finite `M`, however, the exact coefficient before this extraction is
`C_M(N)`.  At `M=1`, subtracting `2L` from it leaves

\[
 C_1(N)-2L={1\over4}\Lambda(N)-2L.                       \tag{4.2}
\]

For `A<N<2A`, `0<=Lambda(N)<=log N<=L+log 2`, and hence

\[
 |C_1(N)-2L|\geq {7\over4}L-{1\over4}\log2.             \tag{4.3}
\]

This is an exact center coefficient, before any transition-shell estimate.
Consequently an all-`M` seminorm for the remainder obtained by subtracting
the unweighted core cannot tend to zero.  Normalizing the Fejer weights to
make `omega_M(1)=1` would not repair the missing factorizations at small
`M`; the ordered limit or an exact `PA_M` allocation is still necessary.

The whole operator generated by `C_M(N)`, not merely a guessed constant
part of it, must remain in `PA_M`.  Because `C_M` depends on `N`, (4.1)
does not orient its sign.

## 5. Exact convergence rate on the center collar

Suppose `M>=N` and put `t=M+1`.  Every divisor quotient `N/n` then lies in
the linear part of (0.1a), so

\[
 \ell_M(N)=\log N-{S_N\over t},\qquad
 S_N=N\sum_{n\mid N}{\Lambda(n)\over n}.                \tag{5.1}
\]

Substitution into (0.2) gives the exact mismatch

\[
\boxed{
 C_M(N)-C_\infty(N)
 ={ -2\log N-2S_N+2\Lambda(N)\over t}
  +{2S_N-\Lambda(N)\over t^2}.}                         \tag{5.2}
\]

Since every `n` with `Lambda(n)!=0` is at least two,

\[
 0\leq S_N\leq {N\over2}\sum_{n\mid N}\Lambda(n)
 ={N\over2}\log N.                                     \tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently, uniformly for `A<N<4A` and `M>=4A`,

\[
 \boxed{|C_M(N)-C_\infty(N)|\leq C{AL\over M}.}        \tag{5.4}
\]

For example, the diagonal choice `M=A L^2` makes this center-coefficient
mismatch `O(1/L)`.  More generally `M/(AL)->infinity` is sufficient for
coefficientwise `o(1)` on the whole center.  This rate does **not** by
itself prove the scalar post-core seminorm: the infinite product tail,
action derivatives, and the sum of ordinary transition channels still
need their own uniform majorants.  The ordered limit (0.6) avoids claiming
such a joint cutoff rate.

## 6. The corrected Stieltjes-BV scalar gate

The fixed-product mask is a step function.  After pullback to a pairwise
action coordinate, a regulated scalar channel `q` generally has

\[
 Dq=q_1(y)\,dy+\nu,
 \qquad q_1\in BV[0,1],\qquad
 \nu=\sum_s J_s\delta_s.                                \tag{6.1}
\]

If `nu!=0`, the expression `Var(q')` is not defined as the variation of a
function: the distributional derivative contains atoms.  One Stieltjes
integration by parts gives, for `Delta!=0`,

\[
 \int_0^1q(y)e^{i\Delta y}\,dy
 =\left[{q(y)e^{i\Delta y}\over i\Delta}\right]_{0}^{1}
 -{1\over i\Delta}\int_0^1e^{i\Delta y}q_1(y)\,dy
 -{1\over i\Delta}\widehat\nu(-\Delta).                \tag{6.2}
\]

The absolutely continuous term admits the second Stieltjes integration by
parts and is controlled by

\[
 |q_1(0+)|+|q_1(1-)|+\operatorname {Var}q_1.             \tag{6.3}
\]

The atomic term in (6.2) has only one action-gap denominator.  Total
variation of `nu` alone supplies no second denominator.  For example,
`q=1_(s,1]` and `Delta=2 pi k` with `s=1/2`, `k` odd, has size
`2/|Delta|`, not `O(|Delta|^(-2))`.

Thus a correct ordered-limit scalar hypothesis must contain, after the
outgoing and `PA` endpoints have been removed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \varepsilon_{\rm ac}(L)=\limsup_{M\to\infty}
 \sum_{\mathfrak c}\bigg[&
  \sup_j\int_0^1|q^M_{\mathfrak c,jj}(y)|\,dy\\
 &+\sup_{j\ne k}\{|q^M_{1,\mathfrak c,jk}(0+)|
       +|q^M_{1,\mathfrak c,jk}(1-)|
       +\operatorname {Var}q^M_{1,\mathfrak c,jk}\}
 \bigg]=o(1),                                           \tag{6.4}
\end{aligned}
\]

together with a jump-channel estimate.  Either of the following is
sufficient:

* the direct gap-weighted operator estimate

  \[
   \limsup_{M\to\infty}\left\|
    \left[\widehat\nu^M_{\mathfrak c,jk}(-\Delta_{jk})
       {\bf1}_{j\ne k}/\Delta_{jk}\right]_{j,k}
       \right\|=o(1),                                    \tag{6.5}
  \]

  summed over transition channels; or
* a scalar decay estimate, for some fixed `eta>0`,

  \[
   |\widehat\nu^M_{\mathfrak c,jk}(-\Delta_{jk})|
   \leq \epsilon_{\mathfrak c}(L)
        (1+|n_j-n_k|)^{-\eta},\qquad
   \sum_{\mathfrak c}\epsilon_{\mathfrak c}(L)=o(1).   \tag{6.6}
  \]

The gap bracket then makes the row sum converge.  Ordinary
mask-transition curvature may be used to prove (6.6), typically with
`eta=1/2`, but the curvature estimate and the summation of its channel
constants are still missing.  A short-interval Brun--Titchmarsh estimate
may control the reciprocal collar prime mass; it is not used or silently
assumed here.

Indeed, the locked action gaps obey
`|Delta_jk| >= (3 pi/2)|n_j-n_k|`.  Therefore (6.6) and the Schur row-sum
test give the explicit dimension-free estimate

\[
 \left\|\left[\widehat\nu^M_{\mathfrak c,jk}(-\Delta_{jk})
       {\bf1}_{j\ne k}/\Delta_{jk}\right]_{j,k}\right\|
 \leq {4\zeta(1+\eta)\over3\pi}
       \epsilon_{\mathfrak c}(L).                       \tag{6.7}
\]

Thus any positive decay power is sufficient; no fixed-number-of-labels
argument is being used.

Finally, the hard-end Hankel--Christoffel--Darboux scalar error must obey
the analogous ordered-limit endpoint-plus-variation bound.  Equations
(6.4)--(6.6), that hard-end bound, and the collar/transition estimates are
the sharp remaining scalar gate.  Replacing them by `sup_M Var(q'_M)`
both uses the wrong cutoff quantifier and omits the atomic mask channels.
