# Cross-root even-sector projective-alignment reduction and barrier

## 0. Exact outcome and claim boundary

This note returns to the checkpoint-0.71 cross-root route.  That route does
not require the selected finite line to be the ground of the full parity
matrix.  It requires only a lowest line of the inversion-even compression
and projective convergence of that line to the projected prolate anchor.

There are two exact versions.

1. On the simple-line branch, let `u` be the lower two-vector Ritz value
   generated by the unit projected anchor `kappa`.  The even-only gate

   \[
   \boxed{
   P_{\kappa^\perp\cap H_+}(Q_+-uI)
   P_{\kappa^\perp\cap H_+}\succ0}                 \tag{0.1}
   \]

   makes the lowest eigenvalue of `Q_+` simple and gives its eigenvector a
   nonzero `kappa` overlap.  No odd-sector comparison occurs.

2. On the multiplicity-safe branch, let `E` be the complete lowest even
   eigenspace and `P=P_E`.  If

   \[
   v=P\eta\ne0,\qquad \langle\kappa,v\rangle\ne0, \tag{0.2}
   \]

   the canonical vector `v/||v||` is cross-rooted.  Its exact normalization
   and low-mode statistics are

   \[
   \boxed{
   {\|v\|^2\over|\langle\kappa,v\rangle|^2}
      =\lambda^{o(1)},\qquad
   {\|P_{{\cal L}_M}v\|^2
      \over|\langle\kappa,v\rangle|^2}
      =O(\lambda^{-1}),}                            \tag{0.3}
   \]

   where `M=ceil(lambda)` and
   `${\cal L}_M=Ran(Pi_M) intersect (Pi_M*kappa)^perp`.

For either branch, the second quantity in (0.3), with the selected line in
place of `v`, is the minimal positive projective statistic

\[
 \boxed{{\cal A}_{N,M}=\|P_{{\cal L}_M}X\|^2,}
 \qquad \langle\kappa,X\rangle=1.                 \tag{0.4}
\]

The convenient natural-cutoff target is

\[
 \boxed{{\cal A}_{N,\lceil\lambda\rceil}
        =O(\lambda^{-1}).}                         \tag{0.5}
\]

Under the admitted prolate tail estimate it is equivalent to the signed
response target `|S_M-k_(00)|=O(lambda^(-1))`.  The exact weaker analytic
condition is

\[
 L\lambda^{2\zeta}{\cal A}_{N,M}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\hbox{for every fixed }0\leq\zeta<\tfrac12. \tag{0.6}
\]

The checkpoint-0.71 finite theorem and its cross-Hurwitz lemma show that
the odd primitive determinant `Delta_N`, the odd part of the shifted margin
`Sigma_N`, the true-even reserve statistic `Theta_N`, and every quantitative
boundary-denominator reserve are not hypotheses of this route.  On the
simple branch, even qualitative nonvanishing of `<eta,xi>` is unnecessary:
the frozen source-Cauchy closure handles the zero-boundary case.  The
condition `P_E eta!=0` in (0.2) is instead a canonical selector for a
multiple lowest space, not the old simple-line boundary denominator.

The remaining projective estimate does not follow from those reductions.
An exact padded positive diagonal source-Cauchy family below has endpoint
scalar zero, a strict even complement, bounded Ritz ratio, nonzero canonical
visibility, and a uniform anchor overlap, while

\[
 \boxed{{\cal A}_{N,M}=S_M-k_{00}=\tfrac12}        \tag{0.7}
\]

for every `M>=1`.  Thus even all those favorable inputs do not imply (0.5).

Checkpoint 1.53 supplies one projected-prolate endpoint/row quotient;
checkpoint 1.54 retunes that same scalar to an odd half-grid and proves a
large low-row nullspace with the wrong minimax direction; checkpoint 1.55
reduces parity and true-reserve questions to mixed cofactors and
negative-Sobolev ground statistics.  None controls (0.1), (0.3), or (0.5).
The saturation-divergence theorem controls sampling energy, explicitly not
a Hilbert-space angle.  These are nonimplications, not actual-zeta failures.

This is a post-checkpoint-1.55 theorem/barrier pair.  It changes none of the

## 2. The exact simple-line even gate

Let `Q_+=Q_+^*` act on the finite even sector, let `kappa` be unit, and put

\[
 q=\langle\kappa,Q_+\kappa\rangle,\qquad
 K=\kappa^\perp\cap H_+,\qquad
 b=P_KQ_+\kappa,\qquad r=\|b\|.                   \tag{2.1}
\]

Suppose first that `r>0`.  Let `u` be the smaller eigenvalue of the
compression of `Q_+` to
`span{kappa,b/r}` and put `d=q-u`.  The precise checkpoint-0.71 gate is

\[
 B_u=P_K(Q_+-uI)P_K\succ0.                        \tag{2.2}
\]

Let `nu_1=lambda_min(P_KQ_+P_K)` and let
`mu_1<=mu_2<=...` be the even eigenvalues.  Equation (2.2) says
`nu_1>u`.  The Ritz principle and codimension-one interlacing give

\[
 \mu_1\leq u<\nu_1\leq\mu_2.                     \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus `mu_1<mu_2`; the lowest even line is simple.  If its unit vector `xi`
were orthogonal to `kappa`, it would belong to `K` and would satisfy
`mu_1>=nu_1>u`, contradicting (2.3).  Hence

\[
 \alpha=\langle\kappa,\xi\rangle\ne0,\qquad
 X={\xi\over\alpha},\qquad
 \langle\kappa,X\rangle=1.                       \tag{2.4}
\]

This proof uses no odd eigenvalue.  The logically weaker scalar condition
`mu_2>u` already proves simplicity and nonzero overlap: a ground vector
orthogonal to `kappa` is also orthogonal to `b`, so its orthogonal
two-vector Ritz space would force `u>=mu_2`.  The stronger condition (2.2)
is the gate used by the frozen Ritz graph bound.  If `d/r` is bounded, that
bound also gives

\[
 \|X-\kappa\|=O(1).                               \tag{2.5}
\]

If `r=0`, do not form `d/r`.  The fail-closed branch requires

\[
 P_K(Q_+-qI)P_K\succ0.                            \tag{2.6}
\]

Then `kappa` itself is the simple lowest even vector, `X=kappa`, and every
projective error below is zero.

The frozen checkpoint-0.71 theorem applies to `xi` using only its
extremality in `Q_+`.  It gives

\[
 Z(\widehat\xi)\subset\mathbb R\cup i\mathbb R.  \tag{2.7}
\]

It neither assumes nor concludes that `mu_1` is the lowest eigenvalue of
the full matrix.

## 3. The exact projective response

Let `Pi=Pi_(M,+)` be the even restriction of the centered cutoff
`|n|<=M`, and define

\[
 p=\Pi\kappa,\qquad k_{00}=\|p\|^2,
 \qquad {\cal L}_M=\operatorname {Ran}\Pi\cap p^\perp,
 \qquad R=I-\Pi.                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

Assume `k_(00)>0`, as holds eventually on the admitted prolate schedules.
For any coefficient-normalized line `X` as in (2.4), put

\[
 {\cal A}_{N,M}=\|P_{{\cal L}_M}X\|^2,
 \qquad h_M=\langle R\kappa,R(X-\kappa)\rangle,
 \qquad S_M=\|\Pi X\|^2.                          \tag{3.2}
\]

Since `<kappa,X-kappa>=0`, the low anchor component of `X-kappa` is
`-h_M p/k_(00)`.  Orthogonal decomposition along
`C*p direct-sum cal L_M` gives the exact identities

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Pi(X-\kappa)\|^2
 ={\cal A}_{N,M}+{|h_M|^2\over k_{00}},}          \tag{3.3}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 S_M-k_{00}
 ={\cal A}_{N,M}+{|h_M|^2\over k_{00}}
                  -2\operatorname {Re}h_M.}       \tag{3.4}
\]

At `M=ceil(lambda)`, the frozen Gevrey estimate makes `||R*kappa||`
superpolynomially small.  Equation (2.5), or the subpolynomial
normalization in Section 4, then makes `h_M` superpolynomially small.
Consequently

\[
 |S_M-k_{00}|=O(\lambda^{-1})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 {\cal A}_{N,M}=O(\lambda^{-1})                  \tag{3.5}
\]

on either declared branch.  This is an equivalence after the stated tail
and normalization inputs; it is not a new estimate for either side.

The rate (0.5), the projected-state norm bounds, frequency separation, and
the admitted tails make the scaled axis-rooted transforms converge locally
uniformly to `Xi` on every fixed closed substrip `|Im z|<=zeta<1/2`.
The cross-Hurwitz lemma then excludes limiting zeros in the open quadrants,
while the elementary positivity `Xi(iy)>0` for `|y|<1/2` excludes the open
imaginary segment.  No odd shifted sign enters this argument.

## 4. The multiplicity-safe canonical projector

Let

\[
 \mu=\min\operatorname {spec}(Q_+),\qquad
 E=\ker(Q_+-\mu I),\qquad P=P_E,
 \qquad v=P\eta.                                  \tag{4.1}
\]

If `v!=0`, the frozen boundary-lowering theorem proves that

\[
 \xi_{\rm can}={v\over\|v\|}                     \tag{4.2}
\]

is cross-rooted, even if `E` is multiple.  Put

\[
 \alpha_{\rm can}
 ={\langle\kappa,v\rangle\over\|v\|}.           \tag{4.3}
\]

The exact weakest direct overlap condition is: for some
`epsilon_lambda>=0` tending to zero,

\[
 \boxed{
 |\langle\kappa,P\eta\rangle|^2
 \geq\lambda^{-2\epsilon_\lambda}
       \langle\eta,P\eta\rangle.}                \tag{4.4}
\]

It is equivalent to `|alpha_can|^(-1)=lambda^(o(1))`.  The
coefficient-normalized canonical vector and its low statistic are exactly

\[
 X_{\rm can}={P\eta\over\langle\kappa,P\eta\rangle},
 \qquad
 \boxed{
 {\cal A}^{\rm can}_{N,M}
 ={\|P_{{\cal L}_M}P\eta\|^2
   \over|\langle\kappa,P\eta\rangle|^2}.}         \tag{4.5}
\]

Thus (4.4) and

\[
 {\cal A}^{\rm can}_{N,\lceil\lambda\rceil}
 =O(\lambda^{-1})                                 \tag{4.6}
\]

are the complete multiplicity-safe even-sector targets.  They use neither
a spectral gap above `E` nor an odd block.  Merely proving `P*eta!=0`, a
large `||P*kappa||`, zero residual from `E`, or a gap above the complete
space does not imply the mixed coefficient in (4.4).

## 5. Why the parity and boundary gates disappear

The deletion is logical rather than heuristic.

1. The frozen finite theorem proves (2.7) for a simple lowest eigenvector
   of `Q_+` without a sign on `Q_--mu*I`.  Negative odd directions merely
   produce imaginary finite zero pairs.
2. Its source-Cauchy perturbation and coefficientwise closure prove the
   same cross-root conclusion when `<eta,xi>=0`.  Thus no simple-line
   boundary denominator is required.
3. On a multiple lowest space, the separate rank-one boundary lowering
   selects (4.2) and proves it cross-rooted.  Only visibility and mixed
   overlap remain.
4. Axis-rooted locally uniform approximants are sufficient: Hurwitz handles
   the open quadrants, and `Xi(iy)>0` handles the open imaginary segment.

Therefore the following quantities from the later full-parity finite-gate
program are not premises of the cross-root bridge:

\[
 \Delta_N>0,\qquad
 \mu_1^- -u>0,\qquad
 \Theta_N\leq1-\rho,\qquad
 D_N^{\rm St}>0.                                  \tag{5.1}
\]

The even component `mu_2^+-u>0`, or the stronger complement gate (2.2),
remains relevant on the simple-line route.  Equation (5.1) does not claim
that the deleted quantities are unnecessary for other finite theorems.
It says only that they are absent from the checkpoint-0.71 implication.

## 6. Exact padded diagonal countermodel

Fix `N>=2` and positive real numbers `U,epsilon,G`.  In raw centered modes
`-N,...,N`, set

\[
 b_n^{\rm C}=0,\qquad
 a_0=U,\qquad a_{\pm1}=U+\varepsilon,
 \qquad a_{\pm n}=U+G\quad(2\leq n\leq N).       \tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
 Q_{nm}={b_n^{\rm C}-b_m^{\rm C}\over n-m}=0
 \quad(n\ne m),                                  \tag{6.2}
\]

so this is an exact positive inversion-symmetric source-Cauchy matrix.
Its parity blocks are

\[
 Q_+=\operatorname {diag}(U,U+\varepsilon,U+G,\ldots),
 \qquad
 Q_-=\operatorname {diag}(U+\varepsilon,U+G,\ldots). \tag{6.3}
\]

Choose the exact unit even anchor

\[
 \kappa={-2V_0+V_1+V_{-1}\over\sqrt6}
 =c e_0^+ +s e_1^+,
 \qquad c=-\sqrt{2\over3},\qquad s={1\over\sqrt3}. \tag{6.4}
\]

Because `eta=e_0^++sqrt(2)*sum_(n>=1)e_n^+`, the endpoint scalar is

\[
 \boxed{\langle\eta,\kappa\rangle=c+\sqrt2s=0.}  \tag{6.5}
\]

The positive energy is

\[
 q=\langle\kappa,Q_+\kappa\rangle
   =U+{\varepsilon\over3}>0.                     \tag{6.6}
\]

Thus any endpoint-over-positive-energy eta quotient is exactly zero.  The
positive source-Cauchy moment-cone representation also realizes `Q` as a
finite positive paired real-evaluation Gram.  This is a structural eta-zero
model, not an actual zeta divisor.

Explicitly, for every finite nonnegative sampling constant `S_0`,

\[
 \boxed{
 (\eta_{\rm model}^{\rm pr})^2
 :={|\langle\eta,\kappa\rangle|^2S_0\over q}=0.} \tag{6.6a}
\]

Let

\[
 w=s e_0^+-c e_1^+.                               \tag{6.7}
\]

Then `(kappa,w)` is orthonormal and spans `(e_0^+,e_1^+)`.  Direct
calculation gives

\[
 \langle w,Q_+\kappa\rangle=-\varepsilon cs
 ={\sqrt2\over3}\varepsilon,
 \qquad r={\sqrt2\over3}\varepsilon.            \tag{6.8}
\]

The lower two-vector Ritz value is the exact ground value `u=U`, and

\[
 d=q-u={\varepsilon\over3},
 \qquad \boxed{{d\over r}={1\over\sqrt2}.}        \tag{6.9}
\]

On `kappa^perp` the active vector `w` has compressed value
`U+2*epsilon/3`, while every padded even direction has value `U+G`.
Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda_{\min}\!\left(
 P_{\kappa^\perp}(Q_+-uI)P_{\kappa^\perp}
 \right)=\min\left({2\varepsilon\over3},G\right)>0.} \tag{6.10}
\]

The even ground is `xi=e_0^+`, so

\[
 E=\mathbb C e_0^+,
 \qquad P_E\eta=e_0^+\ne0,
 \qquad |\langle\kappa,\xi\rangle|^2={2\over3}. \tag{6.11}
\]

Both the simple gate and the canonical visibility/overlap therefore pass
with fixed margins.

For every `1<=M<N`, both `kappa` and `xi` lie in `Ran(Pi_M)`.  The
coefficient-normalized ground is `X=xi/c`, and

\[
 k_{00}=1,\qquad h_M=0,
 \qquad S_M=\|X\|^2={1\over c^2}={3\over2}.       \tag{6.12}
\]

Moreover `<kappa,X>=1`, so `X-kappa` lies in `${\cal L}_M` and

\[
 \boxed{
 {\cal A}_{N,M}=\|X-\kappa\|^2
 ={1\over c^2}-1={1\over2},
 \qquad S_M-k_{00}={1\over2}.}                   \tag{6.13}
\]

Padding `N` arbitrarily and allowing `lambda` to grow does not change any
of (6.5)--(6.13).  The family therefore disproves every abstract
implication from endpoint eta decay, strict even comparison, bounded Ritz
ratio, boundary visibility, or nonzero overlap to the projective rate.
It does not disprove a new arithmetic theorem using the actual prime
inventory and actual prolate coefficients.

## 7. Checkpoint-1.53 through checkpoint-1.55 audit

 \qquad (\eta_N^{\rm pr})^2=O((\log L)^{-2}).     \tag{7.1}

### 7.2 Half-grid and low-row nullity

Checkpoint 1.54 retunes the selected row to an odd half-grid and an exact
varying-density critical ceiling.  It explicitly transports no finite Gram
or ground data.  At the same critical density, Riemann--von Mangoldt gives
a low-row evaluation nullspace of dimension `Theta(N/L)`.  Minimizing over
that constrained nullspace gives a value greater than or equal to the
unconstrained true ground.  It therefore has the wrong minimax direction
for either (2.2) or (4.5).

The row-deletion barrier records `Delta_N`, the two-sided `Sigma_N`, and
`Theta_N` because its target is a full parity/reserve gate.  For the
cross-root route only the even part of `Sigma_N`, or (2.2), survives; the
barrier proves neither.

### 7.3 Saturation, mixed cofactors, and negative Sobolev norms

The saturation theorem proves that one prolate derivative has exponentially
small sampling-energy share in a bottom cluster.  It expressly does not
bound the ordinary Hilbert projection onto that cluster.  Hence it cannot
be read as an upper or lower bound for `<kappa,P_E eta>` or (4.5).

Checkpoint 1.55 rewrites the odd primitive sign as a mixed adjugate
cofactor and gives sufficient negative-Sobolev inequalities for odd parity
and the true-even reserve.  Those are exact reductions for the full gate,
but they neither enter the axis-rooted Hurwitz argument nor control the
even projective statistic.  Their own positive evaluation-Gram models also
show that zero counts and source-Cauchy positivity do not orient an unknown
ground.

Thus no checkpoint-1.53, checkpoint-1.54, or checkpoint-1.55 theorem
currently proves an item in the following exact cross-root target:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{either (2.2) with }d/r=O(1),
 \text{ or }P\eta\ne0\text{ and (4.4), together with }
 {\cal A}_{N,\lceil\lambda\rceil}=O(\lambda^{-1}).} \tag{7.2}

## 8. Fail-closed stopping line

1. restores an odd shifted sign to the checkpoint-0.71 hypothesis list;
2. treats `P_E eta!=0` as a quantitative mixed overlap;
3. infers (4.4) from `||P_E kappa||>0`, a residual, or a gap above `E`;

8. identifies the padded diagonal family with the pinned arithmetic matrix
   or its anchor with the projected prolate state; or
9. claims determinant convergence, an unconditional zeta-zero theorem,

canonical mixed projector together with (0.5).  No such proof is supplied
