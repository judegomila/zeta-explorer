# s2-semilocal-local-factor-swap: screening result (2026-11-19)

Screening arm, four combinations on the semilocal (Route I) objects: prolate operator `A_S = P_1 F_S P_1`, trace-side symbol, cusp allocation of the Sonine remainder, rigid-profile lemma, and the `D_S <= L_S` pencil. The test in every case: replace the local factor at the primes of S by the local factor of each control and see whether zeta passes and the controls fail.

Verdict: **0 survivors out of 4.** Three size-independent reasons close combinations 1 to 3, and combination 4 is verified height in another form. Two by-products are recorded: a short proof of the no-modulus-1 statement for every finite S (engine node `no-modulus-1-rigid-profile`, listed as open), and the closed form of the cusp law for a general local factor.

Labels: PROVED (here; elementary; no referee pass), ARGUED, MEASURED (float64; convergence stated). Nothing here moves a bound.

All numbers are in `tables.md` (generated by `mk_tables.py` from the JSON files in `out/` (server) and `out_local/` (local)).

## 1. Setup shared by the four tests

Local factor at p: `L_p(s) = A(z)/B(z)`, `z = p^-s` (`lf.py`). The semilocal Fourier transform `F_S` has Mellin multiplier on the critical line

    mu_S(t) = m_inf(t) * prod_{p in S} rho_p(t),   rho_p(t) = L_p(1/2+it) / conj(L_p(1/2+it))   ( = L_p(s)/L~_p(1-s) ),

with `m_inf(s) = 2 (2 pi)^-s Gamma(s) cos(pi s/2)`. For zeta, `rho_2 = (1 - 2^(s-1))/(1 - 2^-s)` (the Tate factor of the source `tmp/sonine-fft-mellin.py`). In Laurent form `rho_p = sum_n c_n z^n`, and the cosine kernel is `K(t) = 2 sum_n c_n cos(2 pi p^n t)`; zeta at p has `c_-1 = -1/p`, `c_n = 1 - 1/p` for n >= 0.

Controls as local factors:

| code | local factor at p | role |
|---|---|---|
| zeta | `1/(1 - z)` | reference |
| sign | `1/(1 + z)` | sign-twisted primes at p; identical to the local factor of a real Dirichlet character with chi(p) = -1 (chi_5 at 2 and 3; chi_13 at 2 only) |
| uni | `1/(1 - e^{i} z)` | unimodular twist; A is not self-adjoint, singular values are computed |
| ntp, ntm | `1/(1 - alpha z)`, alpha = p^0.2, p^-0.2 | non-tempered one-sided factor (finite modification) |
| ea | `1/((1 - p^0.2 z)(1 - p^-0.2 z))` | E_a = zeta(s+a) zeta(s-a), a = 0.2 |
| tnr | zeta factor times `2 cosh((s-1/2) log p) + 2.1` | T-NR at q = p |
| dh | p-part of the Davenport-Heilbronn Dirichlet series: `(1 + kappa z)/(1 + z^2)` at 2, `(1 - kappa z)/(1 + z^2)` at 3 | DH has no Euler product; this is the only local object it offers |
| l2 | `1/(1 + z^2)` | local factor of L(s,chi) L(s,chibar) at a prime with chi(p) = i: positive comparison for dh |
| heat | none | the backward heat deformation has no local factor and no Dirichlet coefficients: not applicable to any of the four tests |

Three facts decide combinations 1 to 3 at every size.

**F1 (unitarity). PROVED.** `abs(rho_p(t)) = 1` for every local factor (ratio of a number and its conjugate), so `F_S` is unitary for every swap and `norm(A_S) <= 1`. An eigenvalue of modulus above 1 is impossible for any control. The trace `Tr(theta(g*g~) P Phat_S P)` is non-negative for the same reason.

**F2 (T-NR is invisible). PROVED.** T-NR multiplies `L_q` by a function symmetric under `s -> 1-s` and positive on the critical line, so `rho_q` is unchanged: `A_S`, its spectrum, the symbol and the remainder `delta_S` of T-NR are identical to those of zeta for every S and every size. Checked numerically: `max abs(rho_tnr - rho_zeta) = 2.5e-16`, and the spectra agree to all printed digits (`tnr@2`: 0.99997308, -0.99270220, 0.62020122; `tnr@2+zeta@3` = `zeta@2+zeta@3`). T-NR has zeros off the line. Hence no statistic built from the semilocal operator, with any finite S, separates zeta from T-NR.

**F3 (twists restricted to a finite S are Dirichlet characters).** A sign pattern on a finite set S is the restriction of a real Dirichlet character (CLASSICAL, Chinese remainder theorem plus quadratic reciprocity; here chi_5 = (-,-) and chi_13 = (-,+) on {2,3}); a unimodular pattern is a limit of characters of growing order. So on a finite S the sign twist and the unimodular twist are positive comparisons, not controls: a test that fails them fails Dirichlet L-functions.

**Lemma A (no eigenvalue of modulus 1, every finite S, every factor). PROVED.** Let `A_S f = lambda f`, `abs(lambda) = 1`, `f` in `L^2(0,1)`. Unitarity gives `norm(P F_S f) = norm(F_S f)`, so `F_S f` is supported in [0,1]. With `(D_p g)(w) = g(p w)`, the identity `B_p(z) A~_p(1/(pz)) rho_p = A_p(z) B~_p(1/(pz))` reads (z acts as `D_p`, `1/(pz)` as `(1/p) D_{1/p}`)

    prod_p B_p(D_p) A~_p((1/p) D_{1/p})  F_S f   =   prod_p A_p(D_p) B~_p((1/p) D_{1/p})  fhat .

For zeta at one prime this is `F_S f(w) - F_S f(pw) = fhat(w) - (1/p) fhat(w/p)`. The left side vanishes for `w > prod p^{deg A_p}`. The right side is an entire even function (`fhat` is the cosine transform of a function on [0,1]), so it vanishes identically. Writing `fhat(w) = sum c_k w^{2k}`:

    c_k * prod_p A_p(p^{2k}) * conj(B_p)(p^{-2k-1}) = 0     for every k >= 0.

So `fhat = 0` and `f = 0` unless some `L_p` has a zero at `s = -2k` or a pole at `s = 2k+1`. None of the factors above has one (for `1/(1 - alpha z)` the condition is `alpha = p^{2k+1}`). The same argument gives `norm(A f) < norm(f)` for the non-self-adjoint unimodular case. For S = {inf,2,3} and zeta this settles the residual of imp-96164 (`g(v) = C/v` at the boundary, sub-gap SG-96164-boundary): `C = 0`, because the candidate must be entire, which the tail-interior analysis did not use. The identity was checked on the FFT-Mellin grid for zeta, sign, ntp, ntm at p = 2, 3, 5 with a Gaussian (`check_cov.py`: max difference 5e-8 to 8e-8, the interpolation error of the shift).

## 3. Combination 1: spectrum and trace-side symbol

Finite consequence tested: for each swap, `max abs(spec(A_S)) < 1` and `Lhat_S(t) >= 0`. Survival would need zeta to pass and every control to produce an eigenvalue of modulus >= 1 or a negative symbol.

Outcome: by F1 and Lemma A the fail criterion cannot be met by any control with a local factor, and by F2 the T-NR operator is the zeta operator. The measured values are consistent (T1 and T2 of `tables.md`):

- Validation: archimedean operator 0.9999711, -0.9794864, 0.5241011, -0.0589802 (Connes-Consani values 0.9999711, -0.9795, 0.5242, -0.0590); zeta at p = 2: 0.99997308, -0.99270220, 0.62020122 (source: 0.9999731, -0.9927022, 0.6202012).
- Server and local runs of the same 21 configurations at delta = 1e-5 agree to 7e-15 in the three largest eigenvalues. The top eigenvalue changes by less than 2e-7 between delta = 2e-5, 1e-5 and 5e-6 (largest change: `ea@2+ea@3`, 1.7e-7 and 8.4e-8).
- `1 - max abs(lambda)`: zeta 2.69e-5 (S = {inf,2}), 2.69e-5 ({inf,3}), 2.83e-5 ({inf,5}), 3.70e-4 ({inf,2,3}), 2.26e-3 ({inf,2,3,5}). The top eigenvalue of zeta moves away from 1 as S grows (imp-96143 expected the opposite). ARGUED explanation: `m_inf(s) = zeta(1-s)/zeta(s)`, so `mu_S = zeta_S(1-s)/zeta_S(s)` with `zeta_S = zeta * prod_{p in S}(1 - p^-s)`, and the formal limit over all primes is the multiplier 1, for which `P_1 F P_1 = 0`. For the character-type factors the gap stays small: sign 7.19e-5 (p=2), 4.69e-5 (p=3), 9.01e-5 ({2,3}), 5.90e-5 (sign, sign, zeta at 5); unimodular 1.02e-5, 3.5e-6 ({2,3}). This trend reads the functional equation with conductor 1 and is identical for T-NR, so it is not a lever either.
- Symbol: the formula `Lhat = 2 theta' + 2 Re L_p'/L_p + deltahat` with `delta_S(rho) = rho^{1/2} * 2 sum c_n c_m [si(rho p^n + p^m) + si(abs(rho p^n - p^m))]` gives zeta +0.0649 (Gauss-Legendre 12, ratio 2) and +0.0653 (order 16, ratio 1.5) at p = 2 (source: +0.0650) and +0.0514 at p = 3 (source: +0.0504). The quadrature uncertainty is about 5e-4 at p = 2 and 1e-3 at p = 3. Every swap is positive on [0,60]; the smallest value is +0.0514 (zeta at p = 3). For the non-tempered factors the two quadratures give +0.0709 and +0.0711 (ntp at p = 2, minimum over t) and +0.1416 and +0.1408 (E_a at p = 2).
- A first version of the quadrature (`symbol.py`, plain float offsets) returned negative values for E_a at p = 2 (-0.35 to -9.6 depending on the cut-off). This was an artefact: mesh points collapse onto the spikes for rho above 2^36 and the non-tempered resonant sum is then evaluated at eps = 0. The exact-offset version (`symbol2.py`) removes it. The sentence of the source memory "controls (flip p = 2 sign) go negative" refers to changing `m_2` without changing `delta_S`; with the remainder of the swapped operator the sign twist is positive (+0.0587).

Classification: CLOSED. Reason does not depend on size: unitarity of every swap, and exact invariance under T-NR.

## 4. Combination 2: cusp out-fraction

Finite consequence tested: at `rho = p^k (1 + eps)`, the increments per step of `log_p(1/eps)` of `delta_S` and of `delta_in(rho) = rho^{-1/2} br(1/rho)` have ratio `1/rho` in the limit, i.e. `f_out = 1 - 1/rho`, with error below 1e-4, for zeta only.

**Lemma B (cusp law for a general factor). PROVED for the resonant part; the smoothness of the rest is MEASURED.** The pairs with `rho0 p^n = p^m` contribute `R(x) = 2 sum_n c_n c_{n+k} si(p^n x)` evaluated at `x = p^k eps` in `br(rho)` and at `x = eps/(1+eps)` in `br(1/rho)`. So `delta_in^res(eps) = rho^{-1} delta_S^res(eps p^{-k}) (1 + O(eps))`: the two singular parts are the same function at arguments that differ by the factor `rho`. If `c_n c_{n+k}` has a non-zero mean (`abs(alpha) = 1`), `R` is `a log(1/x)` plus a bounded term and the ratio of log coefficients is exactly `1/rho`. If `c_n` grows or decays like `alpha^n`, `R(x)` scales like `x^{-2 log_p abs(alpha)}` and the ratio is `(abs(alpha)^2/p)^k`. For S = {inf,2,3} the same holds with `log^2` in place of `log`.

Measured (T3, T4):

- zeta: 0.5, 0.75, ..., 0.99609375 at p = 2, k = 1..8, deviations at most 5e-14 (source: 1.1e-7); p = 3 and p = 5 likewise (at most 7e-13).
- sign twist: same values, deviations at most 8e-13. The cusp coefficient changes sign with k and the fraction does not.
- non-tempered and E_a: `f_out = 1 - (abs(alpha)^2/p)^k` to 2e-9 (one-sided) and 2e-8 (E_a) (for example p = 2, k = 1: 0.340246045 against 0.5). For E_a the larger root p^0.2 decides.
- DH 2-part and 3-part: even k follows the law (deviation at most 2e-11); at odd k the mean of `c_n c_{n+k}` is zero, there is no log cusp, and the bounded oscillation has ratio `-1/rho` (so "f_out" = 1 + 1/rho). The degree-2 Euler factor `l2 = 1/(1 + z^2)` has no cusp at odd k either (increment below 1e-11). The odd-k behaviour is a property of the pair of Satake parameters (i, -i), which a genuine L-function also has.
- S = {inf,2,3}, 66 S-units up to 1e4: the fraction of the log^2 coefficient is `1 - 1/rho` to 3.2e-7 (maximum) and 1.0e-8 (mean) for zeta, chi_5 signs and chi_13 signs. The increment ratio at fixed eps, which is what the source extrapolated, still deviates by 1.1e-3 at eps = 1e-150 (mean 1.7e-4): the correction is of relative size `log rho / log(1/eps)`, which is why the source's Aitken extrapolation had mean error 5.9e-2. With one non-tempered factor at 2 the law fails at every S-unit, including pure powers of 3 (0.4823 against 0.6667 at rho = 3).
- The non-resonant part was checked by full quadruple sums at rho0 = 2, 3, 6, 12: its increments fall to below 1.5e-6 at eps = 2^-22.

Classification: CLOSED. The law is the statement `abs(alpha_p) = 1` at each prime of S, read from the local factor. It does separate E_a and the one-sided non-tempered factor from zeta for a reason that involves no zero, but (i) it is a condition at single primes, (ii) the sign twist, the unimodular twist and T-NR satisfy it exactly, (iii) DH is not distinguished from a degree-2 Euler product. It is the temperedness input in another form, not a new statistic.

## 5. Combination 3: rigid profile

Finite consequence tested: for swapped factors the tail equation becomes `g(v) = (p/alpha_p) g(p v)`; the test asked whether a modulus-1 eigenfunction appears and whether the cubic tail of the ladder model stays finite.

- Modulus-1 eigenfunction: excluded for every factor by Lemma A (section 1), including zeta on S = {inf,2,3}. The density argument of imp-96164 shows which swaps keep a formal profile on the tail interior: a continuous solution of `g(2^a 3^b v) = (alpha_2/2)^a (alpha_3/3)^b g(v)` on a dense set of dilations exists only if `alpha_p = p^w` for one complex w at both primes. Zeta (w = 0) and the unimodular shift keep `C v^{-1+w}`; a sign at 2 alone, or a non-tempered factor at 2 alone, admits none. So on this test the controls are more rigid than zeta, not less.
- Measured sup of abs(lambda), S = {inf,2,3}, delta = 5e-6 or 1e-5: zeta 0.99962996, chi_5 signs 0.99990992, sign at 2 only 0.99996490, unimodular 0.99999647 (singular value), DH parts 0.99997138, ntp at 2 0.99636432, E_a 0.98279728, T-NR identical to zeta. All below 1.
- Cubic tail of the ladder model (imp-96143 arithmetic, ARGUED, not a measured spectrum): with block norm `abs(c_n) p^{-n/2}` and multiplicity `C p^n` the tail is `(C/8)(G_2 G_3 - 1)` with `G_p = 1/(1 - abs(alpha_p)^3 p^{-1/2})`: 1.769529 for zeta, the same for sign, unimodular and T-NR, finite for DH parts, divergent when `abs(alpha_p) >= p^{1/6}` (ntp and E_a with exponent 0.2). The Lanczos runs resolve one Ritz value per cluster and cannot test the multiplicity; the count of converged values above 0.3 is 10 (zeta@2), 10 (sign@2), 28 (ntp@2), 30 of 30 (ea@2), which agrees in direction.

Classification: CLOSED. The no-modulus-1 statement is true for every control; the tail sum reads `abs(alpha_p)` only (same information as combination 2).

## 6. Combination 4: the pencil as a function of the coefficient at 2

Setting (`pencil.py`): `g` real in `L^2(-a,a)`, `h = g * g~`, orthonormal Legendre basis. `Arch(h) = -(gamma + log pi) h(0) + 2 int_0^inf [e^{-2u} h(0) - e^{-u/2} h(u)]/(1 - e^{-2u}) du`, `P_n(h) = h(log n) + h(-log n)`, `Pole(h) = 2 ghat(i/2) ghat(-i/2)`. The autocorrelation matrix `H(u)` is a polynomial in u, so all integrals are Gauss-Legendre without singular meshes. Since `L_S - D_S = Arch - (log 2/sqrt 2) P_2` on the pole-free subspace, `mu_max(D_S; L_S) < 1` with the coefficient `x log 2` is the positivity of `W_x = Arch - x (log 2/sqrt 2) P_2`.

Validation: `W_1` on the pole-free subspace loses positivity between 2a = 1.1300 and 1.1316 (N = 128, 256), the source crossing ln 3 + 0.033 = 1.1316.

Results (T5, T6, T7):

- Admissible interval on the pole-free subspace (the pencil of the node), N = 256: every x for 2a <= ln 2; [-3.848, 1.904] at 0.8; [-1.600, 1.0635] at 1.0; **[-0.70175, 1.006354] at ln 3**; x = 1 leaves the interval at 1.1316. Converged in N (N = 64: [-0.70216, 1.006363]). The interval does not shrink to {1} as 2a -> ln 3.
- With the pole term on the whole space (`Pole + Arch - x P_2 >= 0`, the form that equals the sum over zeros) the interval is narrow: `x - 1` in [-2.0e-2, 2.0e-3] at 0.9, [-1.2e-3, 7.4e-5] at 1.0, **[-8.0e-5, 3.1e-6] at ln 3**. The width tracks the smallest eigenvalue of zeta's own form (1.6e-5, 9.3e-7, 5.5e-8).
- Joint statement with the coefficients at 2, 3, 4 (pole-free, N = 192): at 2a = ln 5 the ranges are `x2 - 1` in [-5.0e-8, 8.6e-11], `x3 - 1` in [-8.8e-9, 1.1e-10], `x4 - 1` in [-1.7e-6, 1.5e-7]; smallest eigenvalue at (1,1,1) 2.95e-12 (3.26e-12 at N = 64, 3.09e-12 at N = 128). Each range has non-empty interior at every finite support; it is a point only in the limit of infinite support.
- Controls with their own Weil form: chi_5 and chi_-4 pass at every 2a <= ln 5. **DH passes at every 2a <= ln 5** (smallest eigenvalue 0.209 at ln 3, 6.0e-3 at ln 5); its first off-line zero is at height 85.7. The sign twist at 2 (zeta's gamma factor and pole kept) fails from 2a = 1.07 (-0.084 at ln 3); the unimodular twist at 2 fails at 1.05 for phases 0.5 to 2.0; T-NR at q = 2, c = 2.1 fails from 2a = ln 4 (off-line zeros at height 4.53) and at q = 3, c = 2.2 from 1.45 (height 2.86); E_a fails from 0.85, 0.95, 1.05 for a = 0.4, 0.2, 0.1.

Why this is verified height in another form. The width of the admissible set is `lmin(W_zeta) / P(g_min)`, and `lmin(W_zeta) = min sum_gamma abs(ghat(gamma))^2 / norm(g)^2` is small because a function of support 2a can nearly vanish at the first zeros (the degrees of freedom `a/pi` per unit height exceed the zero density up to height about 30 at 2a = ln 5). The minimiser encodes those ordinates. A perturbed coefficient at 2 with zeta's gamma factor is the function `zeta(s)(1 - 2^-s)^(1-x)`, whose singularities lie on Re s = 0 at heights 0, 4.53, 9.06; E_a moves the first zero pair (height 14.13) off the line; T-NR adds zeros at heights 4.53 and 2.86. Each of these fails once the support resolves heights of that order, and DH, whose first off-line zero is at 85.7, does not fail in the range reached. The Dirichlet comparison passes with its own conductor term, so the failure of the sign twist is a mismatch between the coefficient at 2 and conductor 1 with a pole, not a property of the sign.

Classification: CLOSED. The survivor condition (interval equal to {1} at ln 3, joint statement at ln 5) fails; the narrowing that is observed is the known pattern.
