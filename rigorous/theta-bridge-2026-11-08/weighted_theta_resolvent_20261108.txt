WEIGHTED THETA RESOLVENT: POSITIVE THEOREM AND EXACT INTERTWINING DEFECT
Research session: November 7, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles). All statements below are independent of RH.

Definitions
-----------
c=1/4; H(u)=exp(u/4)*theta(exp(u)); kappa=H''-c^2 H.
Q=kappa/H>0; q=sqrt(Q); A=-D^2+c^2.
B=q A^(-1) q; T=(I+B)^(-1).
The free Green kernel is A^(-1)(u,v)=exp(-c|u-v|)/(2c).
Q is positive, even, bounded, integrable, and decays faster than every exponential.

1. A proved weighted-contraction theorem
----------------------------------------
For real a define the bounded integral operator

B_a(u,v)=q(u)q(v) exp(a(u-v)-c|u-v|)/(2c).

For |a|<=c this is Hilbert-Schmidt: the exponential factor is <=1, so
||B_a||_HS <= (integral Q)/(2c). Its Hermitian part has kernel

(B_a+B_a^*)(u,v)
 =q(u)q(v)/(2c)*[exp(-(c-a)|u-v|)+exp(-(c+a)|u-v|)].

Both exponential kernels are positive definite, including exp(0)=1 at the
endpoints. Therefore B_a is accretive, I+B_a is invertible, and

T_a=(I+B_a)^(-1), ||T_a||<=1, for every |a|<=1/4.

The inverse exists because I+B_a and its adjoint are bounded below by 1 in
real part; hence its range is both closed and dense. The inverse norm is <=1.

This is the bounded extension of exp(au) T exp(-au). To justify the otherwise
unbounded conjugations, use
T=I-q(A+Q)^(-1)q and the positive Green-kernel bound
0< (A+Q)^(-1)(u,v) <= A^(-1)(u,v).
Conjugating the compact correction gives a Hilbert-Schmidt kernel for |a|<=c.
The resolvent identity identifies that bounded extension with (I+B_a)^(-1).

In the original Mellin tilt a=x/2, this proves weighted contractivity throughout
|x|<=1/2. The theorem itself does NOT identify T_a with the desired C_x.

2. Explicit positive Gram for this contraction defect
-----------------------------------------------------
For r>0 define
(V_r f)(t)=sqrt(2r)*integral_t^infinity exp(-r(u-t))*q(u)*f(u) du.
Then V_r^*V_r has kernel q(u)q(v)exp(-r|u-v|).
For r=0 take V_0 f=integral_R q(u)f(u)du, a scalar-valued map.
Put G_a=(V_(c-a) direct-sum V_(c+a))/sqrt(2c).
Thus G_a^*G_a=B_a+B_a^* and

I-T_a^*T_a
 =T_a^*(B_a+B_a^*+B_a^*B_a)T_a
 =(G_a T_a)^*(G_a T_a)+(B_a T_a)^*(B_a T_a).

This is an actual positive Gram factorization, proved from elementary kernels.

3. Quantitative obstruction to the required intertwining
-------------------------------------------------------
Define f_x(u)=exp(xu/2)kappa(u),
h_(x,b)^+/- (t)=[1 +/- alpha(t+2b)/2]*f_x(t+2b)/sqrt(2alpha), alpha>0.
These are ordinary translates of h_(x,0)^+/-.

B_a is compact, hence T_a=I+compact. Translates of any L2 vector tend weakly
to zero as |b| tends to infinity; compact operators send them to zero in norm.
It follows exactly that

lim_(|b|->infinity) ||T_a h_(x,b)^+ - h_(x,b)^-||
 =||h_(x,0)^+ - h_(x,0)^-||
 =sqrt[(alpha/2)*integral_R u^2 exp(xu)kappa(u)^2 du] >0.

Therefore this weighted resolvent cannot be the desired C_x on all translates.
More generally no operator lambda*I+compact can do it: the corresponding
residual limit is ||lambda h_(x,0)^+ - h_(x,0)^-||>0, because the two linear
polynomials 1+alpha*u/2 and 1-alpha*u/2 are not scalar multiples.

At x=0, b=0 there is also a direct parity defect. Since T preserves parity,

||T h^+ -h^-||^2
 =||(I-T)kappa||^2/(2alpha)+(alpha/8)||(I+T)(u*kappa)||^2 >0.

The required boundary operator C_0 is unitary on the translate span, whereas
this positive T already has a nonzero contraction defect on those features.

4. Explicit scattering-state / energy-shift defect
--------------------------------------------------
Let L=-D^2+Q, so (L+c^2)H=0. Simply changing the growth rate by setting
Y_p(u)=exp((p-c)u)H(u) gives the exact defect

(L+p^2)Y_p=2(p-c)exp((p-c)u)[cH-H'].

For the theta H, the missing source is explicitly

cH(u)-H'(u)
 =2pi exp(5u/4)*sum_(n>=1) n^2 exp(-pi*n^2*exp(u)) >0.

On the half-line, this trial also violates the even/Neumann boundary condition:
Y_p'(0)=(p-c)H(0).

One can remove that boundary defect, but a bulk defect remains. Put
Z_p(u)=cosh(pu)H(u)/cosh(cu) and r=H'/H. Then Z_p'(0)=0 and

(L+p^2)Z_p
 =-2Z_p [r-c*tanh(cu)] [p*tanh(pu)-c*tanh(cu)].

The first bracket is strictly positive for u>0, because with U=2cosh(cu),

H'U-HU'=integral_0^u kappa(v)U(v)dv>0.

For real p>c the second bracket is also strictly positive. Thus the residual
is strictly negative and cannot be discarded through modular symmetry.
For 0<p<2c, let H_p be the true even solution of (L+p^2)H_p=0, normalized to
leading growth exp(pu) at +infinity. Then Z_p-H_p decays at infinity and

Z_p-H_p=(L_N+p^2)^(-1)[(L+p^2)Z_p],

where L_N is the nonnegative half-line Neumann operator. In particular H_p>Z_p
for c<p<2c. (For p>=2c the trial state has a slower growing correction, so this
ordinary L2 resolvent formula is not asserted.) This is an exact correction formula between a tilted theta state and
an energy-dependent scattering state, not an unspecified error term.

5. Optional scope distinction about stationary scattering
---------------------------------------------------------
The compactness argument above does not apply to a full stationary scattering
map, which is a noncompact multiplier. But a separate high-energy obstruction
excludes the standard scattering map of this particular short-range Q from
being the desired Cayley multiplier with its usual spectral parameter.

For an integrable short-range potential, the stationary scattering matrix tends
to the free matrix at large real frequency (Jost Volterra estimates give
transmission=1+O(1/k), reflection=O(1/k)). By contrast
S_0(y)=[Xi(y)+i*alpha*Xi'(y)]/[Xi(y)-i*alpha*Xi'(y)]
takes value -1 at real Xi zeros (after cancelling common multiplicity factors)
and +1 at the nonzero extrema between consecutive real zeros. Infinitely many
real Xi zeros are known without RH. Thus S_0 has no high-frequency limit.

This does not exclude another spectral operator with a nontrivial background,
or an independently justified scattering normalization. It excludes only the
direct standard short-range scattering identification for Q=kappa/H.

The weighted theorem and explicit defects above are direct derivations.
