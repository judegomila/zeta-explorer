# s5-growth-class-fejer-sympow-right-edge: screening of four combinations (2026-11-20)

Screening arm. Four combinations of idea-graph nodes were turned into finite tests and run on zeta, on Dirichlet L-functions and on the controls. Labels: PROVED (in this file or in the cited source), CLASSICAL, MEASURED. Nothing here is a statement about the zeros of zeta beyond the sizes reached.

**Verdict: 4 combinations tested, 0 survivors.**

- Combination 1 (difference operator with growth cap): the resolvent is a rank-one function of the object; its norm is bounded in M for every object, the M-dependence is carried by the pole term of the eigenfunctions and is the same for zeta and for T-NR, and the norm is large only within a horizontal distance of the line comparable to the distance of a zero. The growth-class separation of the source statement does not exist for pole-free objects (L(chi), Davenport-Heilbronn): their eigenfunctions decay for every s.
- Combination 2 (primon-gas susceptibility): the peak sits at sigma = 1/2 for every object in every window, including windows of Davenport-Heilbronn and of the heat member that contain off-line zeros. The profile to the right of 1/2 equals the prime-side mean square of the object's own coefficients (mean value), which is the same for zeta, sign twists and unimodular twists.
- Combination 3 (symmetric-power margin at s = 1): random Sato-Tate angles pass (margins 0.47 to 1.04, against 0.95 to 1.12 for Delta and the level-11 newform) and their partial products converge as fast; the sign twist gives identical values (exact); the only sequences that fail are those whose angle distribution has a non-zero mean of U_2j, and this set contains an automorphic form (the CM newform: sym^4 has a pole).
- Combination 4 (Fejer / de la Vallee Poussin inequality): it is evaluated at sigma > 1 and uses Lambda_F(n) >= 0 on prime powers (or unimodular Satake parameters closed under powers). E_a, the Liouville sign twist, a unimodular twist and T-NR at q = 1000003 pass it; Davenport-Heilbronn fails at n = 3 and at the first ordinate sampled; T-NR at q = 3 fails at n = 9 and, on the function side, at the ordinates of its off-line zeros divided by k.

## 2. Combination 1: difference operator with a growth cap

**Definitions (source: node bbm-growth-class-implication).** For an object with Dirichlet coefficients a_n put psi_z(x) = - sum_{n >= 1} a_n (n + x)^(-z), continued in z; psi_z(0) = -F(z). For zeta, psi_z(x) = -zeta(z, x+1) and psi_z(x) - psi_z(x-1) = x^(-z). For periodic a_n (L4, L5, DH) psi_z(x) = -q^(-z) sum_a c_a zeta(z, (a + x)/q), and for T-NR psi_z(x) = -[zeta(z, 1+x) + c q^(1/2-z) zeta(z, 1 + x/q) + q^(1-2z) zeta(z, 1 + x/q^2)]: exact Hurwitz combinations. The operator H acts by H psi_z = z psi_z; the boundary condition psi(0) = 0 selects the zeros of F.

**Resolvent (PROVED, one line).** On the domain psi(0) = 0,

    R(s) psi_z = [psi_z - (F(z)/F(s)) psi_s] / (z - s),

because (H - s) of the right side is psi_z and its value at x = 0 is [-F(z) + F(z)]/(z - s) = 0. The object enters R(s) through psi_z and through the single scalar 1/F(s); the second term is rank one.

**Finite test.** Inputs g = int ghat(tau) psi_{sigma0 + i tau} dtau with ghat in L^2(t - 60, t + 60), norm of ghat; outputs in L^2([0, M], (1+x)^(-2-2c) dx) (functions of growth below x^(1/2+c)); sigma0 = 1/2 + c; s = sigma0 + it. N_M(s) = operator norm of R(s). Discretisation: tau grid of step 0.125 with s at midpoints, 96-point Gauss-Legendre panels of width <= 0.25 in log(1 + x); N_M^2 is the largest eigenvalue of the Gram matrix (960 x 960 per s). Survival would need: N_M bounded in M for zeta on every c, and growth in M for a control on a line to the right of its off-line zeros or at heights more than 20 from them.

**Why it cannot survive (before computing; PROVED for this realisation).** psi_z(x) = A x^(1-z)/(1-z) + O(x^(-sigma)) with A the residue of F at 1. The map ghat -> int ghat(tau) x^(1/2 - c - i tau)/(1 - z) dtau is a Fourier transform in log x, so the output norm on [0, M] is bounded uniformly in M by Plancherel (the weight contributes x^(-4c) d log x), for every object, as long as F(s) != 0. The increments in M come from the pole term only and decay as M^(-4c). Nothing in this depends on the coefficients beyond A and F(s).

**Validation.** Hurwitz evaluator (Euler-Maclaurin, float64) against Arb: worst relative error 3.0e-12 (36 points, alpha from 0.2 to 9.9e4, abs Im z up to 260). abs F at the tabulated off-line zero of DH: 1.7e-14; at the first zero of zeta: 1.7e-14; at a zero of the T-NR factor: 5e-17. Difference relation psi(x) - psi(x-1) = x^(-z): 6e-15. Gram eigenvalue against a direct SVD of the kernel matrix (4704 x 960): equal to all printed digits (DH, c = 0.3, t = 85.75: 36.505975; zeta, c = 0.03, t = 14.125: 74.552530 against 74.552517 with the tau step halved). Arb re-check of 525 kernel entries per case, 225 of them on the 15 largest components of the top left and right singular vectors (`out_local/t1_arbcheck.txt`): worst absolute difference 3.5e-13 (largest entry of order 1) for DH at 0.8 + 85.75i, zeta at 0.6 + 111i, TNRQ at 0.8 + 111.25i. These are re-checks of entries and of the discretisation; the norms are float64 values, not interval certificates.

## 3. Combination 2: primon-gas susceptibility

**Finite test.** chi(sigma) = sum_t w(t) abs(log F(sigma + it))^2 with Hann weights on [T, T+H], log F = log abs F + i arg F, the argument continued from the right along the horizontal line (principal value at sigma = 1.2, unwrapped on a grid of step 0.01 in sigma). Reported at sigma - 1/2 in {0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, 0.07, 0.1, 0.15, ..., 0.5, 0.6, 0.7}: location of the maximum; "cusp" = (chi_abs(1/2) - chi_abs(0.51))/(0.01 log 100); the ratio chi/D with D(sigma) = sum over prime powers of abs(b(p^k))^2 p^(-2k sigma), b the coefficients of log F (for zeta: sum_p sum_k p^(-2k sigma)/k^2; p = 2 removed for L4 and p = 5 for L5; primes <= 1e5 for the truncated products; + c^2 q^(-2(sigma - 1/2)) for TNRQ). Survival would need a control whose maximum leaves 1/2 in windows free of its off-line zeros.

**Validation.** Euler-Maclaurin evaluator of the Hurwitz combinations against Arb at T = 1000 and 20000: worst relative error 3.8e-10 (zeta), 5.7e-11 (L4), 1.4e-11 (DH). Riemann-Siegel evaluator against Arb at 1e6: 7.2e-14 (and the s2 validation table to 1e12). Truncated Euler product (P = 1e5) against log zeta at sigma = 1.2 and 2: 7.2e-5 and 4.8e-9 (the prime tail). Known answer: for zeta at T = 1e8, chi/D = 1.001, 1.002, 1.001, 1.001 at sigma = 0.6, 0.75, 0.9, 1.0 (the Bohr-Jessen mean value; CLASSICAL), and zeta computed by the two evaluators at 1e4 gives chi(1/2) = 3.39 and 3.36 .. 3.49 on neighbouring windows.

## 4. Combination 3: symmetric-power margin at s = 1

**Definitions (sources: imp-2437, imp-2459).** c_m = prod_{j=1}^{m} L(1, sym^{2j} f) = Res_{s=1} L(s, sym^m f x sym^m f); h_m = 1 - log c_m/log Q_m with log Q_m = m log N + sum_{j=0}^{m} log(1 + abs((m - 2j)(k - 1)/2)) (the conductor function of the source with the level term added). Partial products: log L_P(1, sym^{2j}) = sum_{p <= P} [-log(1 - 1/p) - sum_{i=1}^{j} log(1 - 2 cos(2 i theta_p)/p + 1/p^2)]. Survival would need: h_m bounded below for the automorphic forms and a failure (negative drift or non-convergence) for every control sequence.

**Why it cannot survive (PROVED parts).** (a) Only cos(2 theta_p) enters, so x_p -> -x_p (the sign twist of the Satake parameters) leaves every factor unchanged. (b) For independent angles with E U_2j(cos theta) = 0 the sum over p of U_2j/p converges almost surely (variance sum 1/p^2), so Sato-Tate random angles give finite c_m. (c) The partial product diverges as (E U_2j) loglog P when the mean is not zero; for a CM form E U_2j = (1 + (-1)^j)/2, so L(s, sym^4), sym^8, ... have a pole at 1 and c_m is infinite for m >= 2.

**Validation.** tau(p) for p = 2..29 and a_p(11a) for p = 2..29 against the known values (asserted in `t3_series.py`); all abs x_p <= 1 (Deligne, Hasse); the CM values a_p^2 against point counts on y^2 = x^3 - x for 16 primes; L(1, sym^2 Delta) = exp(-0.45919) = 0.631795 against 0.631793 = (pi/2)(4 pi)^12 <Delta, Delta>/11! with the Petersson norm 1.035362057e-6 (CLASSICAL identity); class numbers h(-23), h(-47), h(-71), h(-163), h(-20), h(-84), h(-5923) = 3, 5, 7, 1, 2, 4, 7.

## 5. Combination 4: Fejer-kernel zero-free inequality

**Definitions (source: Route J / imp-96058).** P_n(theta) = abs(sum_{j=0}^{n} sin((j+1) pi/(n+2)) e^{ij theta})^2 = sum a_k cos(k theta) >= 0 has a_1/a_0 = 2 cos(pi/(n+2)), the largest possible ratio at degree n (Fejer-Riesz). R_floor(n) = 1/(2(1 - (a_0/a_1)^(1/2))^2). For zeta, sum_n Lambda(n) n^(-sigma) P(t log n) = sum_k a_k Re(-zeta'/zeta)(sigma + ikt) >= 0 for sigma > 1, and the region is Re s > 1 - 1/(R log t).

## 7. Three weakest steps

2. 3.
